The study of hypotheses under which centered and scaled random quantities have Gaussian limiting distributions, including versions for sums, arrays and processes.

**Scope.** The centering, scale, dependence assumptions and state space are part of each result. This subject is not a synonym for all probability limit theorems: stable non-Gaussian limits and laws of large numbers are different tasks. A central limit result by itself supplies neither a quantitative approximation rate nor a pathwise identity. Functional versions need additional tightness and topology conditions.
