The study of homology invariants of spaces and pairs, their functorial and axiomatic properties, and concrete constructions for computing them.

**Scope.** Ordinary simplicial, singular and cellular constructions require their own definitions and comparison theorems. Coefficients, reduced versus unreduced conventions, and relative pairs matter. Generalized homology theories require additional axiomatic and coefficient data; the elementary chain quotient is not asserted as a universal definition of every generalized theory. Homology is not homotopy or cohomology by another spelling.
