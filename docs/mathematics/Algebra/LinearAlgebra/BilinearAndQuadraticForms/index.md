The study of bilinear forms and quadratic forms on vector spaces over specified commutative fields, including their restrictions, equivalences and maps that preserve the forms.

**Scope.** A bilinear form is linear separately in its two arguments. A quadratic form has degree-two scalar homogeneity and a bilinear polar form. Its relation to a symmetric bilinear form must retain the field characteristic; recovering a form by division by two is not a characteristic-two convention. A concrete homogeneous quadratic polynomial can warrant this subject without using a particular proof assistant's bundled form type. State dimension, field, symmetry, nondegeneracy and any order or positivity hypotheses explicitly. Positivity is additional data over a suitable ordered field, not part of an arbitrary field. A form-preserving map need not be a constructed orthogonal group. Coordinate matrices require the relevant basis and dimension. Sesquilinear Hermitian forms, general rings, infinite-dimensional operator norms and analytic estimates require separately qualified scopes.
