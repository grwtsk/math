# Distribution of prime numbers

[Multiplicative number theory](../MultiplicativeNumberTheory/index.md) · [Subjects](../../../index.md) · [Taxonomy](../../../../index.md)

## Definition

The study of the abundance, locations, spacing and counting behavior of positive rational primes subject to specified size or arithmetic restrictions.

## Scope

A prime is an integer at least two with no positive divisors other than one and itself. The subject includes counting functions and error questions, not merely deciding primality of one input. The range, constants and any uniformity in varying parameters must be stated for a distributional claim. No prime-gap bound or unproved hypothesis is silently supplied.

## Browse

Selected division criterion: Restriction on the primes being counted; arithmetic progressions are the selected child.

Selected narrower subjects: [Prime distribution in arithmetic progressions](PrimesInProgressions/index.md).

## Example and boundary

There are four primes at most 10: 2, 3, 5 and 7. This small exact example illustrates a counting function and proves no general density or gap law.
