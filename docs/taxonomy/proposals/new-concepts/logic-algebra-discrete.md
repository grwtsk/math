# Logic, algebra and discrete subjects: proposed additions

[Taxonomy](../../index.md) · [Subjects](../../subjects/index.md) · [Registry](../../registry.md)

## Problem and affected identities

The previous candidate provides analysis, probability, history and education entry points but not the subjects in this batch. Add fifteen established subjects as original local definitions, not as new mathematics or imported MSC entries. The affected identifiers above name the additions; all fourteen preceding concept payloads remain unchanged. This is an explicit batch, not a claim to cover all foundations, algebra or discrete mathematics.

## Evidence and closest concepts

Open Logic Project supplies a primary project scope for the logic families; Judson supplies an algebraic and finite-group context; Diestel supplies graph and coloring definitions; Stanley supplies enumerative scope; Levin supplies a broader discrete-mathematics context. Editions and limitations are in the source register. The sources are complementary warrants, not independent review of this local placement. Broader field definitions require further disciplinary review, and international terminology remains incompletely assessed.

## Alternatives and recommendation

Keep mathematical logic, algebra, combinatorics and discrete mathematics as distinct entry subjects. Discrete mathematics is a broad organizing field, not an alias for combinatorics. Do not infer that mathematical logic is synonymous with every foundation or type-theory topic. The original namespace collision proposal remains intact: none of the six source roots is renamed, merged or assigned a concept from spelling alone.

Place chromatic-polynomial theory under finite proper vertex coloring and also under enumerative combinatorics. An alias to either parent loses its narrower question. A polynomial-object facet does not replace the subject of studying that invariant. A crosswalk would connect schemes, not supply the second parent in this scheme. Alternatives are one semantic parent plus an associative link or different subject granularity; independent review must compare them.
