the general case of Vojta’s inequality for curves. We will not review [Moc10] here and refer to
the excellent exposition [Mat13] but just state that [Moc10] relies on the result of Mochizuki’s
Noncritical Beyli Maps paper [Moc04].

Gubler [BG06, Theorem 14.4.16]. See also Frankenhuysen [VF02].
   The basic idea of [Moc21c, Corollary 2.2], is to realize U as a parameter space for elliptic
curves of the form y 2 = x(x−1)(x−λ) then convert the Arakelov degrees in (1.1) into heights
and the Szpiro-like upper bounds given in [Moc21c, Theorem 1.10] to the log-conductor and
log-different terms that appear in Vojta’s inequality. The main claim then is that for each
K of a certain type there exists a finite subset S ⊂ K such that for every λ ∈ K \ S of
bounded degree d, every Eλ given by y 2 = x(x − 1)(x − λ) can be put into initial theta data
in such a way that the estimates of [Moc21b, Corollary 3.12] apply with uniform parameters.
This placement into initial theta data then implies Vojta’s conjecture a compactly bounded
subset of U (and hence ABC).

      2. Global Multiplicative Subspaces, Initial Theta Data, and E11a1
  In this section we explain the meaning of the “Initial Theta Data” parameters

                                       (F, l, E, S, V , M , ϵ),

appearing in Mochizuki’s theory. At a 0th order approximation, these are just constants
satisfying enough hypotheses so that a zoo of anabelian interpretations apply simultaneously.
Our exposition breaks the tuple (F, l, E, S, V , M , ϵ) into two parts: (F, l, E, S) and (V , M , ϵ).
   The first chunk, (F, l, E, S), which we call “pre theta data” consists of an elliptic curve E
over a field F together with a rational prime l and a collection of places of bad multiplicative
reduction S. These parameters are then asked to satisfy some conditions related to the
l-torsion that allow Mochizuki’s anabelian machine to run. These should be thought of
“non-isotriviality conditions over the field with one element”.
   The second part of the tuple, (V , M , ϵ), serves as a simulation for a “global multiplicative
subspace” and “global canonical generator” (the precise definitions are given in §2.7). For
Mochizuki, these serve as a fix to a failure of his approach to the ABC conjecture in his
Hodge-Arakelov paper [Moc99]. There, his inequalities suffered from so-called “Gaussian
poles” rendering them useless.
   Finally, after the definition of initial theta data is given, we construct an explicit tuple
(F, l, E, S, V , M , ϵ) where E is a base change of the elliptic curve over Q with Cremona label
E11a1.

2.2. Fricke Involutions. In the process of unpacking Mochizuki’s “initial theta data” we
make use of the classical theory of the Fricke involution, which we now recall. Let Y0 (l)
be the moduli stack2 whose S-points are elliptic curves with cyclic subgroup of order l; by
“cyclic subgroup of order l” we mean a finite flat subgroup scheme over S that étale locally
is isomorphic the constant group scheme Z/lZ. If S = Spec(L) is the spectrum of a number
field then the cyclic subgroup M ⊂ E in the moduli problem is equivalent to the cyclic
subgroup M (L) ⊂ E[l](L) of order l which is stable under G(L/L).

Definition 2.2.1. The Fricke involution is the endomorphism Y0 (l) → Y0 (l) is given by

                              (E, M ) 7→ (E, M ) := (E/M, E[l]/M ).

isogeny or Fricke cover. Note that E/M = (E/M )/(E[l]/M ) = E/E[l] = E as the cokernel

   What about determining ℓ? Checking everything except for surjectivity of the Galois
representation over Q(E[30], i) is practical and one might hope that one could test surjectivity
of the the mod ℓ representation over Q or Q(i) and that it would imply surjectivity of the

theta data). Unfortunately, the interaction between E[a] and E[b] for a, b ∈ Z≥1 can be
complicated due to “entanglements”; the image of the mod ℓ representation over Q(i, E[30])
may be smaller than image of the the mod ℓ representation over Q(i); in fact there is a large
program to classify such entanglements [DLRM23].

of Galois representations of elliptic curves which allows us to resolve our issues with en-

suggested to us by David Zureick-Brown.
