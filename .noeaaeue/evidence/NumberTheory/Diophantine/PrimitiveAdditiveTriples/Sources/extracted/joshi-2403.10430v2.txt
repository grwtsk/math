1 Introduction
1.1     The goal of this paper
[This is still a work in progress!] This paper completes (in Theorem 7.1.1) the remarkable proof
of the abc-conjecture announced by Shinichi Mochizuki in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] (see § 1.5
for an outline).
     Unfortunately, [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] is quite opaque and labyrinthine. In the decade
between the release of Mochizuki’s preprints in 2012 and its publication in 2021, Mochizuki’s
claims have remained poorly understood; and following the release of [Scholze and Stix, 2018]
(and [Scholze, 2021]), many came to the conclusion that Mochizuki’s theory was vacuous.
     My work, [Joshi, 2019], and the present series of papers ([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a,
2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019]), commenced amidst this backdrop, provides an independent
construction of the Theory of Arithmetic Teichmuller Spaces which includes, as a special case,
Mochizuki’s Theory and shows that many impressions readers may previously have had about
Mochizuki’s Theory must be reconsidered ([Joshi, June, 2024]).
     The most important discovery of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c] is that the arithmetic of a fixed
number field is itself deformable, there is a Frobenius morphism of a number field (known
as the log-Link in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c]), and, in fact, there is a Teichmuller Theory of a
fixed Number Field1 . Each of these assertions, especially the assertion that a number field is
deformable (just as a Riemann surface is deformable), has turned out to be an extremely subtle
and challenging one, and to be sure, Mochizuki does not provide any quantification of this,
even though his theory rests upon it.
     One of the original contributions of the present series of papers is to provide a precise quan-
tification, of the idea that an apparently rigid object such as a number field has topological
deformations, and also of the other assertions mentioned above. The above assertions are now
robustly established in [Joshi, 2023a], where the notion of a deformation of number field is cap-
tured in the notion of an arithmeticoid. Each distinct arithmeticoid presents a quantifiably dif-
ferent avatar of a fixed number field, there exists topologically non-isomorphic arithmeticoids
and the deformability is established by constructing, in analogy with classical Teichmuller The-
ory, a metrisable topological space which parameterizes arithmeticoids (so it makes sense to
talk of proximate arithmeticoids).

     At any rate, the importance of Mochizuki’s assertion should not be underestimated: it turns
on its head the absolute rigidity of Arithmetic which has dominated number theory since the
introduction of number fields at the hands of Gauss, Kummer and Dedekind and it also brings
the analogy between Riemann Surfaces and number fields to yet another level by bringing it on
par with Teichmuller’s refinement of Riemann’s Moduli Theory.
     In particular, the point of view of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c] is that one can now treat the
Arithmetic of a fixed number field as a dynamical variable and average quantities of Diophan-
tine interest over aggregates of Arithmetics of a fixed number field. The breath-taking novelty
and the scope of this idea should not be lost on us.
     As an exhibit of what this does for us, Mochizuki offers a proof of the abc-conjecture in
[Mochizuki, 2021d]. While the proof of the abc-conjecture (see § 1.5 for an outline) described
in this paper is based on Mochizuki’s proof in [Mochizuki, 2021d], my approach to it is based
on the present series of papers ([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a, 2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019])
and at this juncture, readers need not have any familiarity with [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] to
understand the claims established in the present series of papers ([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a,
2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019]). However, for readers who wish to compare the two theo-
ries, I have provided detailed references to [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] and a ‘Rosetta Stone’ for
transliterating the assertions of the two theories.
     I have also organized the proof (given in [Mochizuki, 2021d]) from my point of view for
greater transparency, filled in details and provided additional proofs (for [Mochizuki, 2021d]
and [Mochizuki, 2010]) which I felt were necessary for completeness. My approach to the
proof of the abc-conjecture demonstrates quantitatively the idea of “comparing and averaging
over distinct arithmeticoids or avatars of the familiar arithmetic of integers” (see [Joshi, 2023a],
[Joshi, 2024c]).
     Mochizuki’s vision (in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c]) is quite different from mine: he has viewed
his proof as changing of mathematical universes and comparing arithmetic in independent set
theories ([Mochizuki, 2021a, Page 26]) and hence the name Inter-Universal Teichmuller The-
ory. [Readers should bear in mind that unfortunately, Mochizuki does not offer a precise quan-
tification of this assertion in his papers.] My theory, on the other hand, is rooted in a fixed
mathematical universe, within which one can quantify precisely the existence of topologically
distinct arithmetics of integers (pluralized ‘arithmetics’ is intended) and the inter-relationships
between them which bears all the features of classical Teichmuller Theory (hence its name:
The Theory of Arithmetic Teichmuller Spaces). My work allows one to precisely quantify
Mochizuki’s ideas as well.
     The theory presented in my work so closely mirrors Classical Teichmuller Theory that it is
tempting to dream that it will allow us to weave, in Arithmetic Geometry, a tapestry of ideas
and theorems, as rich as the one that has emerged in the last hundred years with the invention
of classical Teichmuller Theory.

1.2    Why is this paper needed?
My role in this complicated saga (see [Castelvecchi, 2015], [Castelvecchi, 2020]) surround-
ing [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] has been to address the foundational and existential questions


                                                 5
which have engulfed [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c]. This has allowed me to establish a complete
and independent proof of [Mochizuki, 2021c, Corollary 3.12] which is central to the claims of
[Mochizuki, 2021d].
    It is clear to me that there is a valid basis for concern about [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d],
Mochizuki’s methodology, and the approach he has adopted to persuade his readership (e.g.
[Mochizuki, 2022]). However, it must also be emphasized that limited experience, among
the arithmetic geometry and the anabelian geometry communities, with classical Teichmuller
Theory has played a central role in the unfortunate debacle that has surrounded [Mochizuki,
2021a,b,c,d].
    Let me discuss some important examples of issues with [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d]. The cen-
tral difficulty one faces in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] is how to disambiguate objects within the
isomorphism class of, say, a smooth hyperbolic curve over a p-adic field. In the archimedean
case, Classical Teichmuller Theory of Riemann surfaces solves this problem by using quasi-
conformal mappings between Riemann surfaces in place of conformal mappings used in Rie-
mann’s moduli theory. While Mochizuki constructed his theory in analogy with classical Te-
ichmuller Theory (this is why Teichmuller Theory appears in the title of Mochizuki’s papers),
unfortunately [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] does not provide any way of disambiguating objects
used in his theory. This difficulty was first highlighted by [Scholze and Stix, 2018] (to be clear,
my work shows that the theory does need distinct objects). They declared the impossibility
of disambiguation, but my work shows that this is incorrect. Mochizuki, on the other hand,
dismissed the disambiguation problem altogether in [Mochizuki, 2022]. [My discussion of
[Scholze and Stix, 2018], [Scholze, 2021] is in § 1.9.]
    Here is another important example of inadequacies of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d]. Some
anabelian geometers (Mochizuki included) have asserted that the collection of algebraically
closed complete valued fields {Cp }p∈VQ is adequate for establishing [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c].
This claim is incorrect because

  (1) Mochizuki’s Key Principle of Inter-Universality [Mochizuki, 2021a, § I3] (which accord-
      ing to Mochizuki lies at the foundation of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d]) requires one to work
      with arbitrary geometric base-points for tempered fundamental groups,

  (2) the definition of geometric base-points for tempered fundamental groups given in [André,
      2003, Section 4.1] (Andre’s paper lays the foundation of the theory of tempered funda-
      mental groups), and

  (3) [Joshi, 2021a, Lemma 3.2.1],

taken together, imply that arbitrary algebraically closed perfectoid fields naturally enter [Mochizuki,
2021a,b,c,d]. On the other hand, as discussed in [Joshi, June, 2024], [Joshi, 2024c], arbitrary
algebraically closed perfectoid fields cannot be recovered using Mochizuki’s group-theoretic
methods of [Mochizuki, 2012, 2013, 2015] (on which [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] is based) and
this leads to a whole range of mathematical issues in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d].
    In late 2019–early 2020, I came to the conclusion (which is documented in [Joshi, October 2020]–
current version is [Joshi, 2022]) that the introduction of algebraically closed perfectoid fields
provides a canonical solution to the disambiguation problem which is fully compatible with

                                                6
Mochizuki’s anabelian ideas, his Key Principle of Inter-Universality and also fully compatible
with our understanding of the role of analytic functions in the classical case, thus making it clear
that there exists a canonical Teichmuller Theory, in a stronger sense than has been asserted by
Mochizuki, even in the p-adic context.
    A more central and rather troublesome difficulty encountered with Mochizuki’s approach
using Frobenioids (or monoidal data) is this: it is exceedingly difficult to mathematically estab-
lish, using only the methods of [Mochizuki, 2012, 2013, 2015] and [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d],
that modifications to a Frobenioid or a monoid necessarily arise from an intrinsic variation
of ring structures. Much of the criticism, by Scholze-Stix, myself and others, of [Mochizuki,
2021a,b,c,d] has been about the lack of precise answers to such technical questions. On the
other hand, my work makes it clear from the very beginning how modifications of monoidal
data lead to distinct arithmetic holomorphic structures (see [Joshi, 2019], [Joshi, 2021a], [Joshi,
2023a]).
    Another important question regarding [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c] is what is the significance of
Mochizuki’s Three Indeterminacies ([Mochizuki, 2021c, Pages 576–577]) which are central to
the formulation of [Mochizuki, 2021c, Theorem 3.11] and hence to [Mochizuki, 2021c, Corol-
lary 3.12]. My work provides (see [Joshi, 2024c, § 8.11]) a canonical geometric description of
Mochizuki’s Three Fundamental Indeterminacies Ind1, Ind2, Ind3–in particular this eliminates
doubts about their existence asserted in [Scholze and Stix, 2018].
    There are more subtle phenomena which were discovered or claimed by Mochizuki (e.g.
Galois action is used to change arithmetic holomorphic structures in [Mochizuki, 2021c, The-
orem 3.11]), but whose proofs are now available only through the present series of papers
([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a, 2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019]). Notably, by [Joshi, 2023a],
there exists a Teichmuller Theory of number fields as asserted by Mochizuki.
    Hence I believe that, without addressing the concerns raised about [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c]
and without their resolution as presented in my work, [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] and its proof
of the abc-conjecture unfortunately stands incomplete.
    The present series of papers ([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a, 2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019])
detail a solution to the issues raised about [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d], which is fully compatible
with Mochizuki’s key ideas and especially, fully compatible with his Key Principle of Inter-
Universality [Mochizuki, 2021a, § I3] (for a detailed discussion see the ‘Rosetta Stone’ pro-
vided in [Joshi, 2024c, § 8]). The solution which I have found, independently of Mochizuki’s
ideas, goes well beyond the original confines of the theory presented in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c],
and extends to higher genus and even higher dimensions, and provides us with a new and radical
way of thinking about arithmetic of number fields.

1.3      Archival Note
[A detailed time-line of events is given in [Joshi, June, 2024].] Starting with my sabbatical
visit to RIMS, Kyoto (Spring 2018), Mochizuki and I actively discussed matters related to
[Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] during 2018–2020. Unfortunately, Mochizuki stopped correspond-
ing with me after the release of [Joshi, October 2020]. I reached out to him on a number of
occasions during 2020–2024 but my emails went unanswered.
    After [Joshi, 2024c] was released on the arxiv, I wrote to Mochizuki suggesting that it
would be best that we write this paper as a joint paper, revisiting [Mochizuki, 2021d] and settle,
once and for all, the issues which have surrounded his remarkable proof (once again, I offered
to explain my work in Zoom meetings/lectures). But neither Mochizuki nor the other anabelian
geometers who have been involved with [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d], responded to my e-mails.

1.5    A brief outline of the proof
This paper (in Theorem 7.1.1) completes the remarkable proof of the abc-conjecture asserted
by Mochizuki in [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d].
    The central novel idea of the proof of the abc-conjecture claimed by Mochizuki in [Mochizuki,
2021a,b,c,d] is that arithmetic of integers (generally encapsulated in the notion of a Number
Field) is not quite as rigid as has been previously believed and one may prove the abc-conjecture
by averaging over a suitably chosen set of deformations of a fixed number field. In [Joshi,
2023a], a deformation of the arithmetic of a number field L (assumed to have no real embed-
dings for simplicity) is captured in the notion of an arithmeticoid [Joshi, 2023a, Definition
5.1.1] and I have robustly demonstrated (in [Joshi, 2023a]) that there is in fact a metrisable
parameter space YL of such deformations of arithmetic (of a fixed number field). Notably,
it makes perfect sense to talk of deformations of a fixed Number Field L, as is claimed in
[Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d].
    A holomorphoid, hol(X/L)y of a quasi-projective variety X/L [Joshi, 2023a, Definition
6.2.3] (also [Joshi, 2024c, Definition 2.1.2]) consists, among other data which I ignore for
this discussion, of a deformation of arithmetic i.e. an arithmeticoid arith(L)y ([Joshi, 2023a,
Definition 5.1.1]) given by a point y ∈ YL . By [Joshi, 2024c, Definition 2.1.2] a holomor-
phoid hol(X/L)y also includes various Berkovich analytic spaces associated to the variety. In
general (for X projective) two holomorphoids hol(X/L)y and hol(X/L)y′ have isomorphic
tempered fundamental groups (and isomorphic geometric tempered fundamental subgroups)
[Joshi, 2021a, Theorem 3.10.1] but need not provide isomorphic analytic spaces. However,

                                                  10
the schemes underlying these holomorphoids are isomorphic to X/L ([Joshi, 2024c, Theorem
2.3.1], [Joshi, 2024c, Remark 2.3.2]).
     In particular, one can think of a holomorphoid hol(X/L)y as representing a quasi-conformal
equivalence class of X/L (see [Joshi, 2023a, § 1.1.1]). Hence the term holomorphoid serves
as a reminder that working with the category of holomorphoids provides an analog of classical
Teichmuller Theory.
     Now let me come to the principal reason why this theory is of interest in Diophantine Geom-
etry. It has been well-understood in Diophantine Geometry that if one had a global Frobenius
morphism for a number field, then many conjectures in Diophantine Geometry could be settled
using it (see the survey [Mochizuki, 2020, § 2.4, Page 15] for an accessible discussion of this
point). But, unfortunately, no global Frobenius morphism exists for a number field.
     However, the central point of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] ([Mochizuki, 2020, § 2.4]) is that
Mochizuki’s log-Link serves as a proxy for global Frobenius morphism on number fields. In
[Joshi, 2023a], I demonstrate that, in fact, there is a global Frobenius morphism (which has
all the properties of Mochizuki’s log-Link (and more)) on the natural parameter spaces of de-
formations of a fixed number fields (in particular, my work demonstrates the existence and the
canonical nature of Mochizuki’s log-Links) and this can be exploited in Diophantine Geometry
(as Mochizuki’s work suggests).
     Specifically, the parameter spaces YL , YLmax , YLR (constructed in [Joshi, 2023a]) are each
equipped with a global Frobenius morphism ϕ : YL → YL (and similarly for YLmax , YLR ) and
also equipped with other global and local symmetries which topologically transform a given
deformation of arithmetic. [As established in [Joshi, 2024c, § 8.9], ϕ corresponds to a log-
Link in [Mochizuki, 2021c], and hence iterates of Mochizuki’s log-Link correspond to global
Frobenius iterates {ϕn }n∈Z .]
     An important consequence of [Joshi, 2023a, Theorem 6.2.1] is that arithmetic (and geome-
try) provided by the holomorphoids hol(X/L)y and hol(X/L)y′ (in general), and hol(X/L)y
and hol(X/L)ϕ(y) (in particular), occur at intrinsically different valuation scales–this scaling
is analogous to the scaling behavior seen in quasi-conformal mappings between complex do-
mains or Riemann surfaces (where this scaling behavior is encapsulated in the theory of the
Beltrami differential or the Beltrami coefficient [Imayoshi and Taniguchi, 1992, Section 1.4.1]).
[Mochizuki’s discussion of the importance of this valuation scaling aspect for his theory is in
[Mochizuki, 2021c, Remark 1.5.3, Page 461].] It is precisely the existence of such intrinsic dif-
ferences in valuation scales which the averaging techniques considered here (and in [Mochizuki,
2021a,b,c,d]) exploit.
     In the context of Szpiro’s conjecture, each holomorphoid hol(X/L)y of any elliptic curve
X/L provides a Tate parameter at a prime of bad semi-stable reduction and so the Tate parame-
ter may be considered as a function (which should be elevated to a sheaf using Grothendieck’s
function sheaf dictionary but this is left for other writers) on the parameter space YL .

the closest analog of what one does here occurs in [Mirzakhani, 2007] (see the survey [Wright,
2019]). An example of such an averaging (or integration) is discussed in the genus one case

gcd(a, b, c) = 1) satisfying

one has                                                       Y

where the product is over all the prime number p dividing a · b · c.

2.3    Functions of bounded discrepancy class
Let X be a set (in the main context of this paper X = P1 − {0, 1, ∞} − (Q̄)). Let f, g : X → R
be two real valued functions. Then I will say that f, g have the same bounded discrepancy class,
and write f ≈ g, if |f − g| : X → R is a bounded function (equivalently f (x) = g(x) + O(1)
holds on X). It is easy to see that ≈ is an equivalence relation on the set of real valued functions
on X. I will say that f (x) . g(x) if f (x) − g(x) ≤ C for some constant C (as in pointed out in
[Mochizuki, 2021d, Remark 2.3.1(ii)], readers should beware that the definition of f (x) . g(x)
given in [Mochizuki, 2010, Definition 1.2(ii)] suffers from an obvious misprint). The definition
of f & g is then self-evident.

2.9    A brief history
  (1) In [Szpiro, 1981], Szpiro proved the geometric Szpiro Inequality and this led him to con-
      jecture the inequality now know as the Arithmetic Szpiro Inequality (Conjecture 2.2.1).
      Several proofs of the geometric Szpiro Inequality now exist [Szpiro, 1981], [Kim, 1997],
      [Amorós et al., 2000], [Zhang, 2001], [Beauville, 2002].

  (2) Mordell’s Conjecture was proved by Faltings in [Faltings, 1983].

  (3) In [Oesterle, 1988], it was observed that the abc-conjecture (Conjecture 2.1.1) is equiva-
      lent to the arithmetic Szpiro Conjecture (Conjecture 2.2.1).

  (4) In [Elkies, 1991] it was shown that the abc-conjecture (Conjecture 2.1.1) implies Mordell’s
      Conjecture.

  (5) In [Vojta, 1998] conjectured a general inequality, which is now known as Vojta’s Height
      Inequality for Curves (Conjecture 2.5.1), and showed that this inequality implies the abc-
      conjecture (Conjecture 2.1.1).

  (6) The equivalence of the two forms of the abc-conjecture given by Conjecture 2.1.1 and
      Conjecture 2.6.1 is quite elementary.

                                              33
  (7) In an important development, in [van Frankenhuysen, 2002] it was shown that the abc-
      conjecture is equivalent to Vojta’s Height Inequality (Conjecture 2.5.1).
  (8) In [Mochizuki, 2010, Theorem 2.1], Mochizuki refined the method of [van Frankenhuysen,
      2002] and showed that Vojta’s Height Inequality for compactly bounded subsets of (P1 −
      {0, 1, ∞})(Q̄) (Conjecture 2.6.1) implies Vojta’s Height Inequality (Conjecture 2.5.1)
      and by van Frankenhuysen’s Theorem he deduced the abc-conjecture (Conjecture 2.1.1)
      from abc-conjecture for compactly bounded subsets of a fixed curve.

2.10     The Main Theorem
The following assertion of [Mochizuki, 2021d], together with the present series of papers
([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a, 2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019]) or [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c]
together with the corrections and changes suggested in the ‘Rosetta Stone’ of [Joshi, 2024c,
§ 8], establish:
Theorem 2.10.1. Let (X, D)/L be a geometrically connected, smooth, projective curve over
a number field L and let D ⊂ X be a reduced divisor over L. Let d ≥ 1 be an integer. Then
for every ε > 0, Vojta’s Height Inequality
                          htωX (D) . (1 + ε) (log-diffX + log-conD )
holds on U(Q̄)≤d = (X − D)(Q̄)≤d .
   As a consequence the following assertions are also true:
  (1) The strong abc-conjecture (Conjecture 2.6.1) (and abc-conjecture).
  (2) The Arithmetic Szpiro Inequality (Conjecture 2.2.1) for elliptic curves over number fields.

3.1    Anabelomorphisms
Throughout this paper a p-adic field means a finite extension of Qp for some prime number p.
All valuations on any valued field considered here will be rank one valuations.
    Throughout the paper I will freely use the notion of anabelomorphisms of schemes, fields
etc. and the related notion of amphoric quantities, properties and structures associated to
anabelomorphic schemes, fields introduced in [Joshi, 2020a, § 2]. Some basic results can be
found in [Joshi, 2020a, § 2.3].

                                              34
3.2    Averaging over additive structures of a number field
It is asserted in [Mochizuki, 2021d] that proof of the abc-conjecture (and Arithmetic Szpiro
Conjecture) presented therein rests on averaging over distinct additive structures i.e. one aver-
ages over distinct avatars of the arithmetic of a fixed number field. In [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c],
the existence of distinct avatars is asserted without adequate and convincing proof while its
theory rests, centrally on a precise quantification of this notion.
     The Theory of Arithmetic Teichmuller Spaces of the present series of papers ([Joshi, 2020a,
2022, 2023b,a, 2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019]), allows me to demonstrate this central missing
point of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] in exquisite detail, and with far greater precision than that
can be achieved by the methods of [Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d].
     My work provides a topological space YL (constructed in [Joshi, 2023a, § 4]) which param-
eterizes the many inequivalent avatars of a fixed number field. The central idea which emerges
is that the arithmetic structure of a number field is bendable while its multiplicative structure
of the number field remains fixed, but the additive structures and the intertwining between the
additive and multiplicative structures may differ in a precise topological sense. The differences
between two such structures move in a manner compatible with the differences between two
such structures at completions at each prime of the number field [Joshi, 2023a, § 4].
     The question of great importance, which arises is this:

      How does one measure the differences between two additive structures of a number
        fields, p-adic fields?

    Understanding the answer to this question, holds the key to the proof of [Mochizuki, 2021d],
([Mochizuki, 2021a,b,c,d] does not explicitly provide a direct quantitative answer to this ques-
tion).

4.1      The basic set up
Recall the theory of [Joshi, 2023a]. Let arith(L)y be the standard arithmeticoid of a number
field L. This arithmeticoid is defined using the chosen standard point y0 = (yv )v∈VL ∈ YL′

    The assumptions [Joshi, 2024c, § 3.1, § 3.3] and § 4.1.1, § 4.1.2 are valid for each of these

    Let L be the algebraic closures of L provided by the arithmeticoid of arith(L)y . And let
M/L be a finite extension of L and let L ⊂ M ⊂ L be this extension viewed in the two
arithmeticoids. Let v ∈ VL be a prime and let w|v be a prime of M lying over v. I work with

present series of papers ([Joshi, 2020a, 2022, 2023b,a, 2024c] and [Joshi, 2020a, 2019]) works
simultaneously with a large collection of arithmeticoids and one averages Tate parameters and

4.6    The log-Different+log-Conductor Theorem
A central point in the proof of the main theorem of [Mochizuki, 2010, Theorem 2.1] (abc-
conjecture for compactly bounded subsets implies Vojta’s Height Inequality for Curves) and
of [Mochizuki, 2021d, Theorem 1.10] is a basic assertion [Mochizuki, 2010, Proposition 1.7]
which I call “The log-Different+log-Conductor Theorem.”
    This theorem, stated as [Mochizuki, 2010, Proposition 1.7], is stated in greater generality
for arithmetic surfaces. But the proof is not adequately documented (in my opinion) and cer-
tainly uses facts about differents of fields which are not established in [Serre, 1979]. Moreover,
the proof of this point does not appear in [Tan, 2018], [Yamashita, 2019] or [Dupuy and Hilado,
2020] (but its consequences are used in [Mochizuki, 2010, Theorem 2.1] and in [Mochizuki,
2021d]).
    Also note that a version of [Mochizuki, 2010, Proposition 1.7] already appears as [Bombieri and Gubler,
2006, Proposition 14.4.6] where this is proved (for arithmetic surfaces) using theory of local
height functions.
    Since I do not use the theory of local heights (and nor do [Mochizuki, 2010] & [Mochizuki,
2021d]) I have felt that I should provide a direct proof of this theorem.
    [I have pointed out in [Joshi, 2023a, § 8], that to understand how Arithmetic Teichmuller
Spaces impacts the theory of heights in Diophantine Geometry, one must understand how many
inequivalent local heights functions arise (at all primes) from many distinct local analytic ge-
ometries because of the existence of Arithmetic Teichmuller Spaces.]
    The theorem is as follows.
