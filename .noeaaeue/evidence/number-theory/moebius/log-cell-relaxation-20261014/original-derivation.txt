## 1. Source boundary and relationship to the earlier screw work

of the complete signed response with its full ordered native Gram quadratic.
Its positive semidefiniteness gives a lower bound on the cross term, not the
missing polynomial upper bound. In particular this Gram is not, just by being
positive, Suzuki's arithmetic screw kernel.

`GRWTSK/NumberTheory/Moebius/CriticalIntervalBlocks.lean` supplies the existing

uses an additional `exp(-u)` weight. Here we use the same source law for the
**unweighted** pickup energy consumed by `PolynomialEnergyCriterion.lean`.
We do not claim the arrival-cell source decomposition itself as a new result.

The earlier positive-screw/Hodge notes retain harmonic mass as linear axial
drift, a rank-one positive kernel, and a resolvent pole. Their warning applies
here: a component with zero derivative under a chosen relaxation is not an
absent source component. This document retains the actual cell means and
quantifies the part perpendicular to them.

Suzuki's specific function `g=-Psi` has global screw-kernel positivity equivalent
to RH. An independently positive Hilbert-space construction does not establish
that arithmetic identification. The forward Haar heat below is an auxiliary
source decomposition, not the backwards theta heat or an identified arithmetic

## 2. Exact unweighted source cells

Let a real coefficient sequence satisfy `|a_k| <= B`, with `B >= 0`. Define

\[
 A_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{a_k}{\sqrt{k}},\qquad
 M_n=\sum_{k=1}^n a_k,
\]
\[
 F(t)=2\sum_{1\le k\le e^t}a_k(k^{-1/2}-e^{-t/2}),\quad t\ge0.
\]

For the actual arithmetic source, `a_k=mu(k)` and `B=1`. On the logarithmic
arrival cell `I_n=[log n,log(n+1)]`,

\[
 F(t)=2(A_n-M_ne^{-t/2}).                                           \tag{2.1}
\]

The newly arriving atom at `log(n+1)` has zero response. Thus the formulas from
the adjacent cells agree at the shared endpoint. Endpoint conventions do not
change any of the integrals below, and this argument also explains continuity.
Only finitely many atoms are involved at each finite time.

Put

\[
 h_n=\log(1+1/n),\quad d_n=n^{-1/2}-(n+1)^{-1/2},\quad
 S_n=2d_n,\quad C_n=\frac1n-\frac1{n+1}.
\]

Here `S_n = integral_{I_n} exp(-t/2) dt` and
`C_n = integral_{I_n} exp(-t) dt`. Every integral is of a continuous function on
a compact interval. Define the *actual*, not optimized, mean

\[
 m_n=\frac1{h_n}\int_{I_n}F(t)\,dt
     =2\left(A_n-\frac{S_n}{h_n}M_n\right).                         \tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1: exact mean–variance split

\[
 e_n:=\int_{I_n}F(t)^2dt=h_nm_n^2+v_n,                             \tag{2.3}
\]
\[
 v_n=4M_n^2\left(C_n-\frac{S_n^2}{h_n}\right)\ge0.                \tag{2.4}
\]

**Proof.** Direct integration of (2.1) gives
`e_n=4(h_n A_n^2-2 S_n A_n M_n+C_n M_n^2)`. Completing the square gives
(2.3). Equivalently `v_n=integral_{I_n}(F-m_n)^2`, so it is nonnegative.
Cauchy–Schwarz separately gives `S_n^2 <= h_n C_n`. No unsigned replacement
of `A_n M_n` is made. The denominator-free algebra is

\[
 h e=4(hA-SM)^2+4M^2(hC-S^2).                                    \tag{2.5}
\]

This identity is among the candidate Lean leaves and exact symbolic tests.

## 3. An unconditional, all-height fine-scale budget

### Lemma 3.1: Lipschitz variance

If a real `L`-Lipschitz function is defined on an interval of length `h>0`,

\[
 \int_I(f-\bar f)^2
 =\frac1{2h}\int_I\int_I(f(u)-f(v))^2du\,dv
 \le\frac{L^2h^3}{12}.                                           \tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Expand the double integral and use
`integral f=h*bar f`. Bound the squared difference by `L^2(u-v)^2`.
Direct integration gives `integral integral (u-v)^2=h^4/6`.
These are finite integrals; no convergence hypothesis is omitted.

On `I_n`, `F'(t)=M_n exp(-t/2)`. Thus `L_n^2<=M_n^2/n`.
Because `|M_n|<=Bn` and `h_n<=1/n`,

\[
 0\le v_n\le\frac{M_n^2h_n^3}{12n}
            \le\frac{B^2}{12n^2}.                               \tag{3.2}
\]

The bound uses no arithmetic cancellation.

### Theorem 3.2: bounded detail energy

At `T_N=log(N+1)`, let

\[
 E(T_N)=\int_0^{T_N}F(t)^2dt,\quad
 G_N=\sum_{n=1}^Nh_nm_n^2,\quad R_N=\sum_{n=1}^Nv_n.
\]

Then

\[
 E(T_N)=G_N+R_N,\qquad 0\le R_N\le B^2/6.                        \tag{3.3}
\]

**Proof.** Sum the exact finite cell identities and use
`sum_{n>=1} n^{-2} <= 1+integral_1^infinity x^{-2} dx = 2`.
This also follows by the elementary telescoping comparison for `n>=2`.
No infinite rearrangement of the original arithmetic series has been used.

For an arbitrary finite `T`, partition `[0,T]` into full arrival cells and its
last nonempty partial cell. Compute the partial cell's *own* mean. The same
identity and budget apply. Variance is monotone under interval inclusion:

\[
 \min_c\int_J(f-c)^2\le\int_J(f-\bar f_I)^2\le\int_I(f-\bar f_I)^2
 \quad(J\subseteq I).
\]

This permits use of a bound on all full-cell variances at every partial height.
It does **not** assert that a fixed full-cell mean is orthogonal on a truncated
part of that cell.

### Actual Möbius refinement of the constant

The exact rational script encloses the first 4,096 `v_n` for `a_k=mu(k)`.
It then uses

\[
 \sum_{n>N}v_n\le\frac1{12N}.                                    \tag{3.4}
\]

The recorded upper bound is less than the exact rational number

\[
 \boxed{\frac{533}{25000}=0.02132.}                               \tag{3.5}
\]

The first-4096 variance is approximately `0.021290459251759843`; prefix plus
proved tail has upper bound approximately `0.02131080430384318`. These decimal
strings are displays only. Acceptance uses the rational endpoints in
`reports/prefix-4096.json`, and a strict rational comparison with (3.5).
The uncomputed tail is not asserted equal to its upper bound.

At the same horizon the full energy is about `6.643201838744102`, with mean
energy about `6.621911379492342`. Thus (3.5) is emphatically not an upper bound
on the whole signal energy.

## 4. A positive compact residual Gram, with permitted rearrangements

Define

\[
 b_n=4(C_n-S_n^2/h_n),\quad
 r_n(t)=-2(e^{-t/2}-S_n/h_n)\mathbf1_{I_n}(t).
\]

The functions `r_n` have pairwise disjoint interiors, have zero cell mean, and
`||r_n||_2^2=b_n`. For a coefficient sequence `a`, the residual is

\[
 (Ra)(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}M_nr_n(t)=F(t)-(P_0F)(t),                   \tag{4.1}
\]

where `P_0F` is the full-cell mean on each cell. Formula (3.2) with `M_n=1` gives

\[
 0\le b_n\le\frac{h_n^3}{12n}\le\frac1{12n^4}.                  \tag{4.2}
\]

Hence `R : ell^infinity -> L^2(0,infinity)` is bounded with

\[
 \|Ra\|_2^2=\sum_n b_nM_n^2\le\frac16\|a\|_\infty^2.            \tag{4.3}
\]

This applies to the actual Möbius sequence, which is not assumed to lie in
`ell^2`. Retaining only output cells `n<=N` gives a finite-rank operator `R_N`.
The exact operator-tail allowance is

\[
 \|R-R_N\|_{\ell^\infty\to L^2}\le\frac1{\sqrt{12N}}.            \tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently `R` is compact on this stated domain/codomain pair. A finite-rank
map is compact because its bounded image lies in a bounded subset of a
finite-dimensional space; the uniform operator limit is compact.

The residual Gram on input coordinates is explicitly

\[
 \boxed{K_{ij}=\sum_{n\ge\max(i,j)}b_n.}                          \tag{4.5}
\]

For finite vectors it is a Gram matrix. For bounded infinite vectors, **all
signed pair expansions are absolutely convergent**, since

\[
 \sum_{i,j}|a_i a_j|K_{ij}
 \le\|a\|_\infty^2\sum_n n^2b_n
 \le\frac16\|a\|_\infty^2.                                     \tag{4.6}
\]

The interchange yielding `sum_{i,j}K_ij=sum_n n^2 b_n` involves only
nonnegative terms, so monotone convergence applies. Bound (4.6) then licenses
arbitrary rearrangement of the residual's signed terms. Thus

\[
 \sum_{i,j}a_i a_jK_{ij}=\sum_n b_nM_n^2
\]

without dropping any off-diagonal interaction. The same operation is NOT
licensed for the coarse source or the original prime/Möbius series as a whole.

On `ell^2`, `R` is Hilbert–Schmidt and `K=R^*R` is positive trace class:

\[
 \|R\|_{HS}^2=\operatorname{tr}K
 =\sum_n n b_n\le\frac1{12}\sum_n n^{-3}\le\frac18.              \tag{4.7}
\]

Moreover the `ell^infinity -> L^2` norm squared is **exactly** `sum n^2 b_n`:
the inequality is saturated by the bounded coefficient sequence `a_k=1`.
`reports/residual-operator-4096.json` encloses this norm squared below `0.05245`
and the Hilbert–Schmidt norm squared below `0.02844`, using the analytic tails
`1/(12N)` and `1/(24N^2)` respectively. These are universal residual-operator
bounds, distinct from the sharper actual-Möbius-vector bound (3.5).

## 4A. A genuine bounded positive screw component from the actual residual

The preceding construction supplies an actual source vector, not merely an
arbitrary auxiliary positive matrix. Extend the Möbius residual `r=F-P_0F` by
zero to the real line. Let `U(t)` be the unitary translation group on
`L^2(R)` and set

\[
 b(t)=U(t)r-r,\qquad g_r(t)=-\tfrac12\|b(t)\|_2^2.
\]

Strong continuity of translations gives continuity of `g_r`. The exact
cocycle identity

\[
 b(s+t)=b(s)+U(s)b(t)
\]

and translation invariance of the norm yield

\[
 \mathcal K_r(s,t):=g_r(s-t)-g_r(s)-g_r(-t)+g_r(0)
     =\operatorname{Re}\langle b(s),b(t)\rangle.                  \tag{4.8}
\]

Thus `g_r` is a positive screw function in the stated kernel convention.
Its finite Gram quadratic is a squared norm; complex coefficients can be
handled by the complexification of this real Hilbert space. Moreover

\[
 0\le\mathcal K_r(t,t)=\|b(t)\|_2^2\le4\|r\|_2^2
                  <\frac{533}{6250}=0.08528.                    \tag{4.9}
\]

This is a bounded coboundary screw. It does not require differentiating the
piecewise residual, assuming that it is in the translation generator's domain,
or dividing by a zero spectral value. The coarse native means remain retained
in the source reconstruction `F=P_0F+r`; they are not silently put into this
bounded component. No equality between `g_r` and Suzuki's particular `-Psi`,
and no decomposition of Suzuki's kernel by this formula, is asserted.

## 5. Quartic Haar details and binary refinement

Refine each arrival cell into `2^k` equal pieces in the **logarithmic time**
coordinate. Let `P_kF` be the resulting piecewise mean and `R_k` its total
squared residual. Applying (3.1) on each smaller interval yields

\[
 0\le R_k\le\frac{B^2}{6\,4^k}.                                 \tag{5.1}
\]

This is a uniform bound, not an assertion `R_k=4^{-k}R_0` or even a proof of
`R_k<=4^{-k}R_0`. The exact variance varies with the exponential profile.

The binary merge law for lengths `l,r` and actual child means `x,y` is

\[
 lx^2+ry^2=(l+r)\left(\frac{lx+ry}{l+r}\right)^2
             +\frac{lr}{l+r}(x-y)^2.                             \tag{5.2}
\]

It retains both the merged mean and the detail. Repeated use of (5.2) gives
`G_{k+1}-G_k=R_k-R_{k+1}>=0` at complete-cell horizons.

For a parent subinterval `J=[a,a+ell]` within `I_n`, take the normalized real
Haar function

\[
 \psi_J=\ell^{-1/2}(\mathbf1_{J_L}-\mathbf1_{J_R}).
\]

A direct integral of (2.1) gives the signed coefficient

\[
 \boxed{c_J=\langle F,\psi_J\rangle
 =-\frac{4M_ne^{-a/2}}{\sqrt\ell}(1-e^{-\ell/4})^2.}              \tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{|c_J|^2=
 \frac{16M_n^2e^{-a}}\ell(1-e^{-\ell/4})^4.}                      \tag{5.4}
\]

This is a positive quartic identity, rather than a sign conjecture. The constant
`A_n` cancels from the Haar coefficient; its arithmetic influence remains in
the mean coordinate. This is precisely why a bound on all these fine details
cannot be promoted to a bound on the actual coarse energy.

Completeness requires no assumption about global `F in L^2`: the residual
`F-P_0F` is in `L^2` by (4.3), and its dyadic approximations converge because
(5.1) tends to zero. Thus its Haar expansion is orthogonal, square summable,
and unconditionally convergent in `L^2`. Arbitrary reorderings of this residual
expansion are valid. They do not authorize arbitrary rearrangements of the
coarse means or unregularized arithmetic source.

## 6. Forward relaxation with a retained zero mode

For the actual source, write `g=P_0F` and `r=F-g`. The function `g` need only be
locally square integrable; `r` is globally square integrable. On the Haar detail
space define the positive self-adjoint diagonal operator

\[
 \Delta\psi_J=4^j\psi_J
\]

when `J` is a level-`j` parent in its arrival cell. Extend the operator by zero
on the closed span of full-cell constant functions in `L^2`. Define

\[
 F_\tau=g+e^{-\tau\Delta}r,\qquad\tau\ge0.                       \tag{6.1}
\]

This is an affine construction when `g` is not globally in `L^2`; it is not
an illicit application of a Hilbert-space heat operator to such a `g`.
All native cell means remain unchanged. At a complete-cell horizon,

\[
 0\le E(T_N)-\int_0^{T_N}|F_\tau|^2
 \le\|r\|_2^2<0.02132.                                         \tag{6.2}
\]

For a partially cut cell, the same orthogonality statement with its full-cell
mean is not automatic. Formula (6.2) is intentionally stated at the complete
horizons that suffice for growth transfer.

There is a useful quantitative small-time reconstruction estimate. Put
`w_j=sum_{J at level j}|c_J|^2`. By (5.1),

\[
 \sum_{j\ge k}w_j\le C4^{-k},\qquad C=B^2/6.
\]

For the positive discrete spectral measure `nu=sum_j w_j delta_{4^j}` this
implies `nu((t,infinity))<=C/t` for every `t>0` (and use total mass `<=C`
when `t<1`). Tonelli with the nonnegative derivative of
`f(t)=(1-e^{-tau t})^2` gives

\[
 \|F-F_\tau\|_2^2
 =\int(1-e^{-\tau t})^2d\nu(t)
 \le2C\tau\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-s}-e^{-2s}}s\,ds
 =2C\tau\log2.                                                  \tag{6.3}
\]

The last identity follows directly from
`(e^{-s}-e^{-2s})/s=integral_1^2 e^{-us}du` and another nonnegative interchange.
For `B=1`,

\[
 \boxed{\|F-F_\tau\|_2^2\le(\log2/3)\tau.}                      \tag{6.4}
\]

This prices a genuine zero-time limit on the source's fine-scale residual.
It is NOT a proof of the previously open source-coupled Hodge–Schur zero-time
limit, nor the all-height zeta nonvanishing margin. At finite positive time the
detail multipliers are nonzero, but their inverse is unbounded; no bounded
inverse reconstruction is assumed.

## 7. Why Schur minimization must not replace the native reading

For a cell, regard the quadratic `e(A,M)` in (2.3) as a function of a variable
`A`, holding `M` fixed. It is minimized at `A=S_n M/h_n`, with

\[
 \min_A e(A,M)=b_n M^2=v_n.                                      \tag{7.1}
\]

At the actual source coordinate,

\[
 e(A_n,M_n)-\min_Ae(A,M_n)=h_nm_n^2.                              \tag{7.2}
\]

Thus a small Schur complement is a lower floor, not an upper bound for the
native energy. Relaxing the mean to its minimizing value deletes exactly the
quantity whose growth must still be controlled. Unlike that incorrect surgery,
(2.3), (5.2), and (6.1) retain the mean with exact accounting.

The coefficient family `a_k=1` is a useful hostile control. It satisfies all
unconditional fine-scale estimates. But
`A_n>=integral_1^{n+1}x^{-1/2}dx>=2sqrt(n)-2` and `M_n=n`.
For `n>=16`, throughout `I_n`, `F(t)>=sqrt(n)`, so

\[
 e_n\ge n\log(1+1/n)\ge n/(n+1)\ge1/2.
\]

Consequently `E(log(N+1))>=(N-15)/2` for `N>=16`: exponential growth in the
time variable despite uniformly bounded within-cell variance. This is not a
counterexample for Möbius coefficients or RH. It rules out inferring arithmetic
cancellation solely from the new positive residual geometry.

## 8. Exact surviving arithmetic target and terminal consumer

For the Möbius source, the proposed reduced arithmetic premise is

\[
 \exists C,p\ge0\ \forall N\ge1:\quad
 G_N=4\sum_{n=1}^N h_n\left(A_n-\frac{2d_n}{h_n}M_n\right)^2
 \le C(1+\log(N+1))^p.                                          \tag{8.1}
\]

This premise is **not established here**. It retains the original source,
all Möbius signs, and the weighted-prefix/Mertens coupling.

### Theorem 8.1: exact growth-class reduction

Premise (8.1) is equivalent to a polynomial upper bound on the original
integrated pickup energy `E(T)` for all `T>=0`.

**Proof.** If the full energy has such a bound, `G_N<=E(log(N+1))` proves the
forward sample bound. Conversely, use `E(log(N+1))<=G_N+1/6`.
For a real `T>=0`, put `N=floor(exp T)>=1`. Energy is nondecreasing and

`T<log(N+1)<=T+log2`.

Thus the sample bound gives

\[
 E(T)\le C(1+T+\log2)^p+1/6
 \le[C(1+\log2)^p+1/6](1+T)^p.
\]

Here `p>=0` and `1+T>=1`; every constant is retained. This proves the claimed
equivalence of growth classes. It is not a claim that RH is equivalent to this
particular polynomial-energy premise.

The existing repository theorem `riemannHypothesis_of_pickupEnergyPoly`
consumes the original polynomial energy. Its separate source-identification
and signed-Laplace analytic steps remain the repository's existing conditional
route. Applying that consumer to Theorem 8.1 is a written conditional
composition, not a new Lean admission or a witness of (8.1).

The earlier uniform signed bilinear sequence involving `ell_n` is not identified
with `G_N` by this document. Establishing a bound on one needs its own actual
formula/consumer; common use of the word energy is insufficient.

## 9. Verification and remaining formalization

The exact arithmetic implementation retains the actual signs and squareful
zeros. It uses an integer Möbius sieve, dyadic enclosures of roots computed by
integer arithmetic, and the convergent series

`log(1+1/n)=2 sum_{j>=0} z^(2j+1)/(2j+1), z=1/(2n+1)`.

After term `j`, its positive tail is bounded by
`2 z^(2j+3)/((2j+3)(1-z^2))`. Every arithmetic operation rounds outward with
rational endpoints. Variance enclosures are intersected with the separately
proved nonnegative bound (3.2), never a sampled positivity guess.
The computed roots concern `n^{-1/2^k}`, not zeta zeros.

The test suite includes exact symbolic centering, antiderivatives, Haar and
quartic identities, the `1/12` integration constant, full mixed-pair preservation,
sieve comparison with independent factorization, sign/mean/Schur counterexamples,
and receipt tampering. Normal and optimized Python modes are both run. A 192-bit
recomputation is compared against the 160-bit 4,096-cell result. See the source-
and execution-bound report for precise executed counts and return codes.

The candidate Lean modules establish finite algebra and reuse the original
source formula. They do **not** formalize the integrated source moment adapter,
Lipschitz variance estimate, complete summation, operator compactness, Haar
completeness, or forward-heat spectral bound. The proofs of those steps are
written above. `proofs/Audit.lean` lists the candidate declarations to inspect
when a pinned Lean environment is available; merely printing their axioms is
not the repository's full owned-declaration audit.

Before library admission, compile under the pinned toolchain and Mathlib,
verify dependencies at trust zero with fatal warnings, audit all owned
declarations and consumers, register the subject-based pages, and run the
repository's structural/documentation checks. None of these absent runs is
replaced by the Python results or by this document.
