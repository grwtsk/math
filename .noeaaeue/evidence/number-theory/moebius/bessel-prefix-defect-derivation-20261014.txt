## 1. The moving source prefix, not a degree truncation

For j>=1 put D=2^j, K_j=(2D-1)^2, U_j=floor(cuberoot(K_j)).
For D<=n<2D define U(n)=U_j and a_U(k)=mu(k)1_(k<=U).
Use the SAME source-exact Bessel kernel K_(n,k) from CP3/EI1, with y range
 [n^-4, min(1/n,k^-2)], zero if this interval is degenerate. It retains
(1-y)[1/3-5y^-1/4/(6sqrt(k))] B_n'(y)/(2lambda_n), the full Bessel
frequency n+7/12, parity, normalization and geometric prefactor.

Write
 s_n=sum_(k<=U(n))mu(k)K_(n,k) = T_(1,n),
 v_n=sum_(U(n)<k<=n^2)mu(k)K_(n,k),
 t_n=s_n+v_n.                                                (PD1)

All sums are finite. No replacement of the Bessel kernel or its endpoints
is made. In particular, s_n is not zero and is not silently deleted.

The result below proves, under MertensLogSquareBound,

 ||s||_(ell2(w_n), n>=2) < 78200,     w_n=4n+7/3.              (PD2)

More directly, for the ORIGINAL complete Jacobi coefficients d_n,

 ||(d_n-v_n)_(n>=2)||_(ell2(w_n)) < 78200.                    (PD3)

Thus the finite source before U(n), not just before sqrt(n), can be removed
from the remaining obligation with a paid norm error. This does not assert
that removing it preserves any finite coefficient, sign, or energy exactly.
The previous carry-deletion counterexamples remain correct.

## 2. A uniform truncated-source Jacobi estimate

This section requires only a real source c_k supported on 1<=k<=U with
|c_k|<=1, and integers 2<=U<=n. Define
 M_U(x)=sum_(k<=min(floor(x),U))c_k,
 A_U=sum_(k<=U)c_k/sqrt(k),
 V_U(x)=int_1^x M_U(t)t^-3/2 dt,
 g_U(y)=y^-5/12 V_U(y^-1/2),
 p(y)=y^7/6(1-y), lambda_n=n(n+7/6), P_n=P_n^(0,1/6)(2y-1).
Let pJ_(n,U) be the COMPLETE Jacobi coefficient of this truncated source:

 pJ_(n,U)=sum_(j=0..n) p_(n,j)
            [sum_(k<=U)c_k/k^(2j+2)]/[(2j+3/2)(2j+2)].       (PD4)

This expression is rational for rational c_k. It integrates the truncated
source on the entire half-line; it is NOT the original full Mobius d_n.
The ordinary absolute-convergence source calculation and Jacobi integration
by parts yield
 pJ_(n,U)=(2lambda_n)^-1 int_0^1 p(y)g_U'(y)P_n'(y)dy.         (PD5)

For y<=U^-2, all the short source is already active, giving the EXACT form
 g_U'(y)=-(5/6)A_U y^-17/12+(1/3)M_U(U)y^-7/6.                (PD6)
For every 0<y<=1 we also have |g_U'|<=(4/3)y^-5/3.
The primitive is locally absolutely continuous; we do not differentiate
M_U. The boundary at zero in PD5 is O_n,U(y^3/4), hence zero; the factor
1-y kills the other boundary. No target square-integrability is assumed.

Use the previously written, explicitly derived Jacobi bounds
 b_n=(7/6)_n/n! <=(6/5)n^1/6,
 |P_n'|<=(6/7)lambda_n b_n,
 |P_n'(y)|<=7sqrt(n)y^-5/6(1-y)^-3/4
    when n^-2<=y<=1-n^-2.                                  (PD7)
The last bound follows from the transformed scalar ODE, not an unproved
arithmetic derivative estimate. Its endpoint/weighted polynomial inputs are
DLMF 18.14.2 and 18.14.3, with parameters (0,1/6) in the stated domains;
the derivative endpoint formula uses parameters (1,7/6) in 18.14.2.

Put delta=n^-2, v=U^-2, epsilon=1-delta. Since 2<=U<=n,
 delta<=v<=1/4<=epsilon. Split PD5 into four intervals.

(0,delta): PD6 and the global derivative bound give at most
 (4/7)|A_U| n^-4/3+(6/35)|M_U(U)| n^-11/6.
The integral powers are int y^-1/4=(4/3)y^3/4 and int 1=y.

(delta,v): PD6 and the interior derivative bound give at most
 35|A_U| n^-4/3+7|M_U(U)| U^-1/3 n^-3/2.
Here int_delta^v y^-13/12 <=12n^1/6 and
int_delta^v y^-5/6 <=6U^-1/3. The factor (1-y)^1/4 is bounded by one.

(v,epsilon): the elementary full-source bound gives at most
 14 U^2/3 n^-3/2,
using int_v^epsilon y^-4/3 <=3U^2/3.

(epsilon,1): the endpoint bound gives at most
 (24/35)n^-23/6,
using y^-1/2<2 and int_epsilon^1(1-y)dy=delta^2/2.

Since |M_U(U)|<=U and U<=n, both
 U n^-11/6 <= U^2/3 n^-3/2 and
 n^-23/6 <= U^2/3 n^-3/2 hold. The exact constants satisfy
 4/7+35=249/7<36,
 6/35+7+14+24/35=153/7<22.
We obtain the all-(n,U) theorem

 |pJ_(n,U)| <=36|A_U| n^-4/3+22U^2/3 n^-3/2,
                       integers 2<=U<=n.                   (PD8)

The two source terms in PD6 are retained in the identity. Taking their
absolute values here bounds ONLY the short source, not the positive and
negative masses of the full arithmetic core.

## 3. Actual Mobius cancellation supplies the critical logarithmic gain

Assume explicitly
 |M(x)|<=3x/(log x)^2 for every real x>1.                    (ML)
JLT Theorem A.1(A.5), arXiv:2408.04143v2, has the stronger coefficient
2.91890. Its typeset statement on PDF page index 23 has been inspected.
No finite experiment is used to supply ML and no native Lean import is made.

Together with |M(x)|<=x this gives |M(x)|<=12x/(1+log x)^2 on x>=1.
The source integral then gives
 |V(x)|<=312sqrt(x)/(1+log x)^2.
For completeness put x=e^t and use
 (1+t)/(1+t-u)<=1+u, 0<=u<=t,
 int_0^infinity e^-u/2(1+u)^2du=26.
All compact ranges, including x=1, are covered.

Finite Abel summation, with the closed cutoff, gives exactly
 A_U=M(U)/sqrt(U)+(1/2)V(U).
Consequently

 |A_U|<=168sqrt(U)/(1+log U)^2.                             (PD9)

For the actual shell cutoff we have 2<=U(n)<=n, U(n)^3<=4n^2,
and n^2<(U(n)+1)^3. The upper U<=n follows at D>=4 from
(2D-1)^2<4D^2<=D^3, and at D=2 by the exact value U=2.
Thus
 sqrt(U)<=(4/3)n^1/3,
 U^2/3<=(3/2)n^4/9,
 1+log n <=(5/2)(1+log U).
The last inequality follows from n<(2U)^3/2 and log 2<1, not from
an asymptotic equivalence that might omit small degrees.

It follows from PD9 that
 |A_(U(n))|<=1400 n^1/3/(1+log n)^2.
Therefore the moving-prefix Jacobi coefficients p_n=pJ_(n,U(n)) satisfy

 |p_n| <= 50400/[n(1+log n)^2]+33n^-19/18, n>=2.            (PD10)

This is the literal arithmetic exponent gain in this contribution. The
source cutoff U=O(n^2/3) changes the generic n^-5/6 estimate to the
critical n^-1 term; ACTUAL signed Mertens cancellation supplies log^-2.
The independently bounded remainder is n^-19/18. Neither exponent is
claimed for the full source d_n. Bounded coefficients alone would replace
the first term by a constant times n^-1; that envelope is not summable
in the weighted square norm.

Using w_n<=7n, Minkowski for finite sums and decreasing integral comparisons,

 ||p||_w <= Kp:=50400sqrt(7/3)+33sqrt(63).                    (PD11)

Indeed sum_(n>=2)1/[n(1+log n)^4]<=1/3, and
sum_(n>=2)n^-10/9<=9. The squared budgets before Minkowski are
5927040000 and 68607, respectively. No weighted norm of p is assumed
before deriving these finite bounds and passing to their supremum.

For every integer N>=1 the explicit tail is
 ||(p_n)_(n>N)||_w <=
 50400sqrt(7/3)(1+log N)^-3/2+33sqrt(63)N^-1/18.             (PD12)

## 4. Transfer the prefix estimate without charging an error twice

The prior Jacobi-to-Bessel proof uses only |c_k|<=1 and the source identities.
It applies for EACH degree n to the short source mu(k)1_(k<=U(n)), even
though the source changes with n. The pointwise error allowances do not
depend on U:

 e_n=15n^-7/6+200n^-4/3+50n^-11/6,
 |p_n-s_n|<=e_n,
 ||e||_w <= D=sqrt(4725)+sqrt(420000)+sqrt(10500)<821.        (PD13)

Hence ||s||_w<=Kp+D. The same comparison can be applied DIRECTLY to the
long source mu(k)1_(k>U(n)), whose coefficients also have modulus <=1.
Linearity at each degree says that its Jacobi coefficient is d_n-p_n,
while its finite Bessel coefficient is v_n. Thus
 |d_n-p_n-v_n|<=e_n and ||d-v||_w<=Kp+D, not Kp+2D.
There is no assumption that p, s, or v are coefficients of one fixed
function independent of degree; only the degreewise comparisons and
uniform pointwise error allowances are used.

Let L=Kp+D. The exact rational inequalities sqrt(7/3)<153/100,
sqrt(63)<8, sqrt(4725)<69, sqrt(420000)<649, sqrt(10500)<103 give

 L<50400*(153/100)+33*8+69+649+103=78197<78200.              (PD14)

This proves PD2 and PD3. In particular sum_(n>=2)w_n*s_n^2<6115240000.
This constant is deliberately nonoptimal; it is independent of every
shell, cutoff and degree. The arithmetic premise ML is unchanged.

The corresponding tail for either s or d-v is bounded by
 L(N)=50400sqrt(7/3)(1+log N)^-3/2+33sqrt(63)N^-1/18
       +sqrt(4725)N^-1/6+sqrt(420000)N^-1/3
       +sqrt(10500)N^-5/6, N>=1.                           (PD15)
It tends to zero. This is a short-source/difference tail, not an upper
bound for the remaining full energy tail.

## 5. Two signed defect terms reconstruct exactly the remaining source

Use divisor convolution on positive integers, identity epsilon and
constant-one arithmetic function 1. For the frozen shell prefix define
 eta_U=a_U*1-epsilon.
At 1<=k<=U the full divisor sum of mu is epsilon(k), so eta_U(k)=0.
The cubic compiler, with K_j<(U+1)^3, gives

 mu=a_U-a_U*eta_U+a_U*eta_U*eta_U     on indices 1,...,K_j.   (PD16)

A direct proof notes a_U=mu*(epsilon+eta_U). Multiplication by
 epsilon-eta_U+eta_U^2 gives mu+mu*eta_U^3. The cube of eta_U
vanishes before (U+1)^3. Beyond the strict gate the exact remainder is
-mu*eta_U^3 and must not be dropped. This is a regrouping of the classical
Huxley--Watt/Vaughan/Heath--Brown identity, not new divisor cancellation.

Let L_n be the coefficient functional CP3 applied to a_U*eta_U, and
R_n the same functional applied to a_U*eta_U^2. Then

 v_n=R_n-L_n,     t_n=s_n-L_n+R_n.                          (PD17)

In the earlier three-branch notation,
 L_n=T2_n-T1_n, R_n=T3_n-2T2_n+T1_n,
 v_n=T3_n-3T2_n+2T1_n.
These are exact identities using the same K_(n,k) everywhere. All ordered
multiplier tuples remain, and the strict cubic gate is unchanged.
The long coefficient vector is EXACTLY mu(k) for U<k<=K_j and zero for
k<=U; in particular it does not include an arbitrary reset of V.
The quadratic defect is zero below (U+1)^2. The full long source generally
starts already at U+1 through the linear defect.

The remaining shell energy is
 V_j=sum_(n=2^j..2^(j+1)-1) w_n [R_n^2+L_n^2-2R_n L_n].     (PD18)
The mixed term is required; neither defect term is proved summable alone.
They do NOT satisfy the bounded-coefficient assumptions individually.
For a prime p>U, eta_U(p)=1, so delayed support is not coefficientwise
smallness. Nilpotence at a finite cutoff is not a uniform inverse bound:
N_R=[[0,R,0],[0,0,R],[0,0,0]] obeys N_R^3=0, but
||(I+N_R)^-1 e_3||^2=1+R^2+R^4. This control is not a Mobius counterexample.

## 6. Exact return and the remaining quantified obligation

For finite N>=1, define B_N=sum_(n=2..N)w_n v_n^2. PD3 implies

 |sqrt(Q_N-Q_1)-sqrt(B_N)|<=L<78200.                        (PD19)

Under ML, sup_N B_N<infinity is therefore EQUIVALENT to the original
sup_N Q_N<infinity. In the dyadic form it is sum_(j>=1)V_j<infinity.
A witness B_N<=B gives

 H_(1/3)(T)<=Q_1+(sqrt(B)+L)^2
            <1+(sqrt(B)+78200)^2, all real T>=0.            (PD20)

The original source-identification theorem, not a change to an auxiliary
function, supplies this return. The original signed core consequently
satisfies Z(R)<=8[1+(sqrt(B)+78200)^2]+69c for R>=1, with the
unchanged localized-atom constant c. These are conditional returns;
NO witness B has been supplied. A sufficient shell bound
V_j<=C/(j+1)^(1+eta), eta>0, likewise remains unproved.

No pointwise Mertens power saving, all-positive-damping conclusion or RH
proof follows from this one short-prefix theorem. The finite compiler,
source corrections, and old failed unsigned suppliers are preserved.
