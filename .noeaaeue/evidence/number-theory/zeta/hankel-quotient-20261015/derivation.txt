---------------

 s(w)=(3+w)/(2(1-w)), a(w)=xi'(s(w))/xi(s(w)),

2. AN EXACT QUOTIENT HANKEL IDENTITY
----------------------------------
Put b(w)=1/(1+a(w))=(1-F(w))/2 and B_N=T_N(b).
For EVERY N>=1, without a tail or boundary correction,

 H_N(F)=2 B_N H_N(a) B_N^T.                                    (2.1)

Proof. In the bivariate power-series ring the exact identity is

 (F(x)-F(y))/(x-y)
   =2 b(x)b(y)(a(x)-a(y))/(x-y).

The coefficient of x^i y^j in the divided difference of f is f_(i+j+1).
Taking i,j<N gives (2.1): only multipliers b_0,...,b_(N-1) and
source coefficients through a_(2N-1) enter. No bivariate term of lower degree
is changed by truncating higher terms. Both transposes are ordinary transposes;
the stated norm application is to the real arithmetic jets. The algebra also
holds over commutative characteristic-zero fields, not with an inserted complex
conjugation. The entry (N-1,N-1) really uses index 2N-1.

Define the ONE-SIDED FILTERED SOURCE MATRIX

 K_N=H_N(a) B_N^T.

Since 2B_N=I-T_N(F), (2.1) becomes

 H_N(F)=(I-T_N(F)) K_N,
 beta_N <= ||K_N||.                                           (2.2)

The second inequality follows from ||I-T_N||<=1+||T_N||=A_N.
This uses the existing normalization to pay one inverse factor exactly; it does
not charge two independent denominator inverses. K_N need not be symmetric.
It is a coherently nested principal-section family: each entry is

 K_(i,j)=sum_(l=0)^j a_(i+l+1)b_(j-l),

independent of a chosen containing dimension. Thus a finite norm bound at N
also bounds K_n for 1<=n<=N by principal compression. This is not an all-size
conclusion from one finite computation.

3. COMPLETE COEFFICIENT-RECONSTRUCTION COST
------------------------------------------
Suppose actual and approximate a,c jets obey

 e_a=sum_(k=1)^(2N-1)|a_k-ahat_k|,
 e_c=sum_(k=0)^(N-1)|c_k-chat_k|.

Let Bhat=(I-T_N(chat))/2 and Khat=H_N(ahat) Bhat^T. If
||H_N(ahat)||<=h and ||Bhat||<=b, then

 ||K_N-Khat|| <= e_a b + h e_c/2 + e_a e_c/2.                  (3.1)

Proof. A single Hankel anti-diagonal mask has norm one (it is a partial
permutation); hence ||H(a)-H(ahat)||<=e_a. Each Toeplitz shift has norm at
most one, giving ||B-Bhat||<=e_c/2. Expand the product difference into its
three terms and bound all three. The cross-error is NOT deleted. This is a
sufficient coefficientwise majorant, not a claim that independent errors occur.
The old shared-denominator interval certificate is reused to enclose c itself.

4. THE RESULTING ALL-DEPTH TARGET
--------------------------------
The supplied tensor paper proves A_(2N)<=A_N(1+beta_N), with the terminal
carry retained. By (2.2), a NEW sufficient source inequality is

 ||K_(2^j)|| <= C (2^j)^p for all j>=0, for some C,p>=0.       (4.1)

Indeed log A_(2^J)<=log A_1+sum_(j<J)log(1+C 2^(pj))=O(J^2).
Compression then gives ||T_N||<=exp(O((log N)^2)). The supplied source-specific
strip return, Subexponential arithmetic matrix growth Theorem 3.1, implies RH.
More generally log(1+||K_(2^j)||)=o(2^j) suffices: for any epsilon>0 the late
terms are at most epsilon 2^j, and the dyadic sum is at most epsilon 2^J plus
a fixed finite prefix. Divide by 2^J and let epsilon decrease to zero.

Equation (4.1) is an UNPROVED ARITHMETIC TARGET, not an estimate established
by this extension. It is permitted to grow. No identification with Guth--Maynard's
phase matrix or affine functional has been assumed. This removes one avoidable
inverse cost and identifies the exact matrix to which a future bound must apply.

5. THE PRIME-POWER HANKEL KERNEL IS A REFLECTED LAGUERRE PAIRING
-------------------------------------------------------------
Define directly

 q_0(x)=1,
 q_k(x)=sum_(r=1)^k binom(k-1,r-1)(-x)^r/r! (k>=1),
 L_i(x)=sum_(r=0)^i binom(i,r)(-x)^r/r!.

The elementary generating functions are

 sum q_k(x) w^k = exp(-x w/(1-w)),
 sum L_i(x) w^i = (1-w)^(-1) exp(-x w/(1-w)).

These are the generalized alpha=-1 and ordinary Laguerre forms; DLMF 18.12.13
is the classical generating-function ancestry. Our proof uses the displayed
finite polynomial coefficients and formal series, not orthogonality at alpha=-1.
For every i,j>=0 and x>=0,

 q_(i+j+1)(x)=-x integral_0^1 L_i(tx)L_j((1-t)x) dt.            (5.1)

Proof. Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to the two exponentials in
the divided difference. Since x/(1-x)-y/(1-y)=(x-y)/((1-x)(1-y)), their divided
difference is minus the spectral argument times the product of the two ordinary
Laguerre generating functions, integrated over t. Equate coefficients.
The dummy series variables in this proof are distinct from the nonnegative
spectral argument called x in (5.1). At spectral argument zero, both sides vanish.

For the vector ell(t)=(L_0(tx),...,L_(N-1)(tx)), define

 ell_+=(ell(t)+ell(1-t))/2, ell_-=(ell(t)-ell(1-t))/2.

Reflection has opposite signs on these two channels, and its mixed integrals
vanish under t->1-t. Therefore

 integral ell(t)ell(1-t)^T dt
    = integral ell_+ell_+^T dt - integral ell_-ell_-^T dt.      (5.2)

In the initial half-plane Re s>1, the prime part of a is
- sum Lambda(n)n^(-3/2) exp(-2log(n)w/(1-w)). Thus its Hankel matrix is

 sum_(n>=2) Lambda(n)n^(-3/2) 2log(n)
        [Gram(ell_+) - Gram(ell_-)], x=2log(n).                (5.3)

For every fixed N this converges absolutely, since the features are polynomials
in log(n), Lambda(n)<=log(n), and the power is n^(-3/2).
After the FULL-source B_N has been constructed, multiplying both features by
B_N gives the corresponding decomposition in (2.1), with its factor two.
B_N is not recomputed separately for each prime packet or for the archimedean
part: that would change the nonlinear quotient.

The signed distinction is necessary. At x=1,N=2 the reflected matrix is
[[1,1/2],[1/2,1/6]]. On v=(1,-2) it has value -1/3. Replacing reflection by an
ordinary Gram yields [[1,1/2],[1/2,1/3]], with value +1/3 on the SAME v.
For odd-channel Hodge attenuation ell_- -> r ell_-, 0<=r<=1, the original
pairing equals the relaxed pairing MINUS (1-r^2)Gram(ell_-). This exact signed
debt survives filtering as well. It is not licensed to disappear.
The auxiliary reflection here acts on the Laguerre integration coordinate t;
it is not identified with critical reflection of a zero.

6. CANCEL THE DETERMINISTIC POLE BEFORE TAKING ABSOLUTE VALUES
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let psi_ar(x)=sum_(n<=x)Lambda(n) and

 Delta(x)=psi_ar(x)-x+1, Delta(1)=0.

This is a new notationally explicit use of the ACTUAL prime discrepancy, not
a replacement for the manuscripts' source means. The digamma recurrence gives

 R(s)=[psi_digamma(1+s/2)-log(pi)]/2,
 xi'(s)/xi(s)=R(s)+1/(s-1)-sum_(n>=2)Lambda(n)n^(-s).           (6.1)

The recurrence cancels 1/s against the digamma pole at zero. At the initial
point s=3/2 it does not cross any singularity. The remaining 1/(s-1) must stay
paired with the prime-power series. Its kth composed coefficient is

 integral_1^infty y^(-3/2) q_k(2log y) dy
    = 2 (k=0), and -8(-3)^(k-1) (k>=1).                      (6.2)

Proof. Integrate each finite monomial using
integral_1^infty y^(-3/2)(log y)^r dy=r! 2^(r+1), then apply the binomial theorem.
The continuum Hankel is exactly -8 u_Nu_N^T with u_N=(1,-3,...,(-3)^(N-1)).
Its norm is 9^N-1. This exponentially large deterministic piece is canceled
in the actual source; bounding it separately is an avoidable loss.

For f_k(x)=x^(-3/2)q_k(2log x), Stieltjes integration by parts gives

 a_k=R_k + integral_1^infty Delta(x)x^(-5/2)
       [2q_k'(2log x)-(3/2)q_k(2log x)] dx.                  (6.3)

Here R_k=[w^k]R(s(w)). To check the sign, dDelta=dpsi_ar-dx, so continuum minus
prime is -integral f_k dDelta=integral Delta f_k' dx. The lower boundary is
zero by Delta(1)=0; the upper boundary vanishes. The crude bound
|Delta(x)|<=x(log x+1) suffices for absolute convergence at every FIXED k.
The k=0 weight is -3/2 and is retained, because it affects the denominator b_0.

For the filtered matrix define the true source polynomial

 P_(i,j)(t)=sum_(l=0)^j b_(j-l) q_(i+l+1)(2t).

Equations (2.2) and (6.3) give the EXACT ALL-DEPTH ENTRY IDENTITY

 K_(i,j)=[H(R)B^T]_(i,j)+integral_1^infty Delta(x)x^(-5/2)
                          [P_(i,j)'(log x)-(3/2)P_(i,j)(log x)] dx. (6.4)

This is the concrete source comparison for a future dispersion/large-value
argument. It retains the continuum, every prime power, the archimedean term,
the derivative weight and the full quotient filter. It needs no zero list,
7/8 input, zero-density theorem, or RH to be an identity of the initial jets.
The degree-dependent coefficients and b_j can amplify errors; their norm is not
asserted subexponential. A generic density estimate or a positive feature Gram
alone does not establish (4.1). A finite continuum/prime truncation must include
its boundary term and omitted tail; none has been silently discarded here.
