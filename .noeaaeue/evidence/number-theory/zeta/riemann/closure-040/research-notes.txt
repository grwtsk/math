1. Mathematical advance: the full witness slope family

For R(a,t)=[t+ia,t-ia,-t+ia,-t-ia], with all four list weights retained, let
p_k(x)=x^2+k*a*x+a^2-t^2. Direct evaluation of the complex squares gives

  H_k(a,t) = -4*a^2*[k^2*a^2+(4-k^2)*t^2].

This is a signed trace of p(r)^2, not of |p(r)|^2. It specializes to the
supplied H_1=-4*a^2*(a^2+3*t^2) and to H_2=-16*a^4. Thus the slope-two
witness removes height from the negative margin, including height zero.
The slope-one witness can have a larger negative magnitude at large height;
height-independence is not an assertion of uniformly superior localization
conditioning or smaller analytic error.

Within this fixed family, strict detection for every a!=0 and every real t
holds exactly when 0<k^2<=4. Sufficiency follows because the inner bracket
has a strictly positive first term and a nonnegative second term. Necessity
at k=0 follows from a=1,t=0. If k^2>4, use

  a=1, t=(k^2+1)/(k^2-4),
  H_k = 4*(6*k^2+1)/(k^2-4) > 0.

The simple counterfixture k=3,a=1,t=2 has trace 44. This refutes only
oversized-slope universal detection, not RH and not the admissible slopes.

2. Quantitative and source-extraction consequences

A lower approximation H_2(a,t)>=-epsilon gives 16*a^4<=epsilon. For any
fixed exclusion width delta>0, |a|>=delta and epsilon<16*delta^4 are
incompatible. Arbitrarily small such errors force a=0; one finite positive
error only gives an enclosure, never exact equality.

A generic ordered-real lemma now isolates the sufficient extraction:
for every epsilon>0, obtain w>=0 and scale>0 with

  w <= scale*(x+epsilon).

Then x>=0. Indeed, were x<0, choose epsilon=-x/2; the right side is strictly
negative. An exact decomposition w=scale*x+residual and the one-sided bound
residual<=scale*epsilon suffice. No uniform positive lower bound on scale
is needed, provided the error is controlled relative to that same scale.
This is a generic order lemma, not zeta positivity disguised as an assumption.
The zeta consumer keeps the entire extraction supplier explicit.

A finite test can also exclude a specific hypothetical off-line zero:

  w = scale*H_2(a,t)+residual >= 0,
  scale>0, residual < 16*scale*a^4.

Substitution makes w<0. Thus one adequately sharp source test per hypothetical
off-line zero is sufficient. Constructing that test on the actual zeta source
remains open. Generic positive matrices and arbitrary choices of w do not
supply the required decomposition or residual estimate.

The loss of a scale condition is genuinely dangerous. For x=-1, take
scale_n=1/(n+1), residual_n=1/(n+1), w_n=0. Both scale_n and residual_n tend
to zero and w_n is nonnegative, yet x<0. The relative residual stays 1.
Likewise a positive total (-1)+2 does not make its first component positive.
