1. An explicit pair isolator, not an arbitrary positive model

Let r=t+ib with b!=0. Let S be a finite set of other complex locations,
containing neither r nor conjugate(r). Define

 h(z) = product over w in S of (z-w)(z-conjugate(w)),
 d = i/h(r),
 P(z) = h(z) [Re(d) + (Im(d)/b)(z-t)].

Each factor of h is (z-Re(w))^2+Im(w)^2. Hence P has real coefficients,
degree at most 2|S|+1, and respects conjugation. No assumption that S itself
is closed under conjugation is needed. Exclusion of both selected nodes
makes h(r) nonzero. At r, the affine factor equals d, so

 P(r)=i,  P(conjugate(r))=-i,  P(w)=0 for every w in S.

For arbitrary positive analytic weights m_r,m_conjugate(r), the selected
signed square contribution is exactly -M, where M=m_r+m_conjugate(r)>0.
No equality between these weights is required. Real nodes or both members
of another conjugate pair may produce repeated factors of the TEST
polynomial; these are not claims about analytic source multiplicities.
This construction remains valid when t=0. It excludes b=0 solely because
that is already the desired real-frequency/critical-line case.

ConjugatePair.lean.txt gives product, polynomial evaluation, normalization,
conjugation and finite weighted-trace candidates. All quantities are literal
signed squares. Norms enter only to bound residuals, never as a replacement
for the signed functional.

2. Freeze the polynomial before shrinking the Gaussian

For L>0 set

 G_L(z)=P(z) exp(-L[(z-t)^2+b^2]),
 D(z)=(Re(z)-t)^2+b^2-Im(z)^2.

The exponent is zero at BOTH selected nodes. Therefore G_L(r)=i and
G_L(conjugate(r))=-i for every L; all cancelled values stay zero. Direct
complex-exponential evaluation gives

 |G_L(z)|^2=|P(z)|^2 exp(-2L D(z)).

For |Im(z)|<=1/2 and |Re(z)-t|>=1,

 D(z)-3/4 = [(Re(z)-t)^2-1]+b^2+[1/4-Im(z)^2] >= 0.

Taking L=n+1 yields, for every natural n,

 |G_(n+1)(z)|^2 <= exp(-3n/2) |G_1(z)|^2.              (A)

The closed endpoints are included. All near-window locations other than
the selected pair must have been cancelled first. S, P and all constants
are fixed before n. Allowing an unbounded interpolation polynomial P_n to
change with n would not justify this bound with one fixed summable majorant.
PairLocalization.lean.txt retains the exact norm identity, exponent estimate,
comparison theorem and a finite natural-index selection using exp/log and
exists_nat_gt. It supplies no claim of numerical certificate-search totality.

4. From pointwise decay to the infinite weighted tail

Index DISTINCT actual nontrivial frequency locations by z and retain their
positive finite analytic multiplicities m_z. Separate the selected pair,
other near-window locations, and the far tail. Provided

 C = sum over far z of m_z |G_1(z)|^2 < infinity,        (B)

absolute comparison gives a well-defined complex residual

 R_n = sum over far z of m_z G_(n+1)(z)^2,
 |R_n| <= C exp(-3n/2).                               (C)

The finite cancellation is exact; it introduces no extra analytic weights.
Thus the real part of the FULL actual spectral sum is

 W_n = Re(sum over all z of m_z G_(n+1)(z)^2)
     = -M + Re(R_n).

Choose a natural n with C exp(-3n/2)<M. When C>0 it suffices that
n>(2/3)log(C/M); when C=0, n=0 suffices. Then W_n<0. This provides a
concrete local-pair extraction sequence, including a fixed negative margin
and geometric remainder, rather than asserting positivity of an orbit by
positivity of an unrelated form.

There is a classical route to (B), not an additional RH assumption. Trudgian,
arXiv:1208.5846v2, Corollary 1, gives a multiplicity-counted zero estimate
implying N(T)=O(T log T). Conjugation handles negative heights and compact
finiteness absorbs any bounded central region. For a fixed polynomial P of
degree d and |Im(z)|<=1/2, |P(z)|^2 is bounded by a constant times
(1+|Re(z)-t|)^(2d). Splitting into unit annuli around t bounds (B) by a
constant times sum_(k>=1) (k+2)^(2d+2) exp(-2k^2), which converges by the
ratio test. Constants may depend on this fixed zero and P, not on n.
This is a written consequence of an externally cited counting theorem;
no native counting, analytic-order or moment theorem is supplied here.
Neither finite samples nor discreteness ALONE prove (B).

6. The positivity supplier is still missing

The actual spectral expression has now been specified, not proved positive.
For a real-type transform G, the Weil pairing
G(z)*conjugate(G(conjugate(z))) equals G(z)^2, not |G(z)|^2. To finish this
route one still needs an exact admissibility/explicit-formula bridge to an
actual arithmetic functional and its nonnegativity for these tests, without
RH or an equivalent positivity assertion as an unproved premise.

Connes--Consani, arXiv:2006.13771, Introduction and Appendix C, explain the
Weil criterion with additional finite vanishing conditions; their Theorem 1
is an archimedean, restricted-support result. This is existing literature,
not a new equivalent criterion discovered here. Its positivity theorem is
not a supplier for the full Gaussian family. The inverse Fourier transforms
of nonzero polynomial-Gaussians are not compactly supported. Increasing L
does not preserve a fixed compact support in logarithmic coordinates.
Using that literature requires a proved truncation/continuity extension or
a separate admissibility theorem, with all prime and archimedean terms.

Pole evaluations can be killed algebraically by adjoining i/2 to S: its
paired factor kills both +/-i/2, which are not nontrivial source frequencies
in the open half-strip. This observation does not by itself establish the
Fourier/Mellin normalization, pole-term identities or the positivity theorem.
Those source effects remain explicit integration obligations.

The complete source-level positivity assertion on this rich test class
would imply RH by the above isolation argument. Calling it positivity does
not make it a weaker proven fact. Conversely, on RH the locations are real
and each real-type squared value is nonnegative; this explains exactly
where the unproved arithmetic content remains.
