1. The unchanged source; cancel gamma before numerical extraction
----------------------------------------------------------------
Use Delta(x)=sum_(n<=x) Lambda(n)-x+1, Delta(1)=0, every prime power retained,
s(w)=(3+w)/(2(1-w)), a(w)=xi'(s(w))/xi(s(w)), and
r(w)=[psi_digamma(1+s(w)/2)-log(pi)]/2 from the preceding two derivations.
For k>=0 let

 u_k=integral_0^infinity Delta(exp(t))exp(-3t/2)L_k(2t)dt,
 p_k=a_k-r_k=-(3u_k+u_(k-1))/2, u_(-1)=0.                   (1.1)

Each moment exists absolutely for every FIXED k. Initially on |w|<1/3,

 p(w)=1/(s(w)-1)+zeta'(s(w))/zeta(s(w)).                    (1.2)

Our new coefficient producer uses (1.2) directly. It never independently
subtracts two computed gamma jets, and never inverts 1+a on a large disk.
The only zeta inversion is the triangular local jet at the independently
nonzero value zeta(3/2). The deterministic pole is included before coefficient
composition, norms, or absolute values. The stable inverse in (1.1) is
u_k=-(2p_k+u_(k-1))/3, with all constants and terminal coefficients retained.

The predecessor proves that log(1+D_N)=o(N), D_N=sum_(k<N)|u_k|^2, implies RH
by holomorphic continuation of a and the denominator-free equation
V'=s'aV for V(w)=xi(s(w)). The present work uses that written return, not an
assumption of nonvanishing on the disk. It is compatible with the supplied
Subexponential arithmetic matrix growth, Theorem 3.1: a fixed positive rate
is not subexponential. The new task below is still an arithmetic inequality.

2. The exact finite Dirichlet residual includes the endpoint
-----------------------------------------------------------
Fix a real X>=1, L=log X, and define the entire function

 J_X(s)=integral_0^L Delta(exp(t))exp(-s t)dt.
 P_X(s)=sum_(2<=n<=X) Lambda(n)n^(-s),
 C_X(s)=integral_1^X x^(-s)dx,
 R_X(s)=P_X(s)-C_X(s)-Delta(X)X^(-s).                       (2.1)

Then, for EVERY complex s,

                  R_X(s)=s J_X(s).                        (2.2)

Proof. Stieltjes integration by parts for dDelta=dpsi_ar-dx on (1,X]
gives integral x^(-s)dDelta=Delta(X)X^(-s)+s integral_1^X
Delta(x)x^(-s-1)dx. The lower term is zero by Delta(1)=0. The latter
integral is J_X after x=exp(t). All quantities are entire in s because
the integration range and prime inventory are finite. Thus any calculation
first made away from s=0,1 extends through those values. C_X(1)=log X;
R_X(0)=0. No zeta quotient is evaluated on the critical line in (2.1).
At a prime power exactly equal to X its jump cancels between P_X and the
endpoint in R_X. It is retained in both, not dropped from either inventory.

For any partition 1=x_0<...<x_m=X define

 R_[U,V](s)=sum_(U<n<=V)Lambda(n)n^(-s)-integral_U^V x^(-s)dx
                         -Delta(V)V^(-s)+Delta(U)U^(-s).

It equals s integral_U^V Delta(x)x^(-s-1)dx. Summing shells reconstructs
R_X exactly. Internal endpoints cancel only when the SAME actual Delta is
used; resetting the discrepancy to zero separately in each shell changes it.
The discrete shell convention is open at U and closed at V.

3. A literal Cayley-Fourier adapter, not a renaming of a matrix
-------------------------------------------------------------
Set s_tau=1/2+i*tau and

 omega(tau)=(i*tau-1)/(i*tau+1), |omega(tau)|=1,
 A_X(tau)=R_X(s_tau)/[s_tau(1-i*tau)].

For every k>=0 the finite Laguerre moment satisfies

 u_k^(X)=(1/(2*pi)) integral_R A_X(tau)omega(tau)^(-k)dtau.  (3.1)

Proof. Put f_X(t)=exp(-t/2)Delta(exp(t))1_[0,L](t), extended by zero,
and h_k(t)=exp(-t)L_k(2t)1_[0,infinity)(t). Both belong to L2(R).
In the Fourier convention hat(f)(tau)=integral f(t)exp(-i*tau*t)dt,

 hat(f_X)(tau)=J_X(s_tau),
 hat(h_k)(tau)=(i*tau-1)^k/(i*tau+1)^(k+1)
             =omega(tau)^k/(1+i*tau).

The second equality follows by integrating the finite polynomial term by
term, or from its Laplace transform. Parseval yields (3.1) using (2.2).
The entire source is assembled before this pairing. The factors s_tau and
1-i*tau never vanish on the real tau axis.

At each FIXED X, f_X is compactly supported and of bounded variation. For
Re(z)>0, integration by parts bounds |z integral f_X(t)exp(-zt)dt| by its
endpoint values and total variation, and bounds the integral itself by
||f_X||_1. Thus (z+1)/2 times this transform is bounded in that half-plane.
The corresponding disk function belongs to H-infinity for this finite X.
This justifies its Hardy boundary interpretation without inferring boundedness
from boundary values alone. Its bound can grow with X; no uniform arithmetic
estimate has been proved by this observation.

For an independent normalization check, w=exp(i*theta) gives
s(w)=1/2+i*cot(theta/2), 1/(1-w)=(1+i*tau)/2, and
|dtheta/dtau|=2/(1+tau^2). Thus

 (1/(2*pi)) integral_0^(2*pi) |(1-w)^(-1)J_X(s(w))|^2 dtheta
              =(1/(4*pi)) integral_R |J_X(s_tau)|^2dtau
              =(1/2) integral_1^X |Delta(x)|^2 x^(-2)dx.    (3.2)

The factor one half is essential. This is the norm of the full finite-cutoff
coefficient series, NOT its first-N energy. Its Bessel upper bound is already
known to lose the required scale when used with a fixed power beta>1/2.
No square-integrability of the untruncated f is assumed.

Define the complete phase kernel

 Theta_N(tau,nu)=sum_(k=0)^(N-1)[omega(nu)/omega(tau)]^k.    (3.3)

For tau=nu it is N; otherwise it is the ordinary finite geometric quotient.
The sum, including its terminal exponent N-1, is authoritative at the diagonal.
Then EXACTLY

 D_N^(X)=(1/(4*pi^2)) integral_R integral_R
       A_X(tau) conjugate(A_X(nu)) Theta_N(tau,nu) dtau dnu. (3.4)

This is the promised two-frequency comparison. It contains the literal
Dirichlet phases n^(-i*tau), the continuum, the endpoint, and all their mixed
terms. It is not an estimate for an arbitrary positive Gram. Absolute
convergence follows from Section 4 and |Theta_N|<=N at each finite N.
Equivalently, it is the squared operator norm of the functional

 v -> (1/(2*pi)) integral_R A_X(tau)
                           sum_(k<N) conjugate(v_k)omega(tau)^(-k)dtau

on the Euclidean unit sphere in C^N. The low-degree projection is retained;
replacing it by the full boundary norm reintroduces the coarse Bessel loss.

4. Both infinite tails can now be paid by explicit rational budgets
------------------------------------------------------------------
For all real tau, elementary estimates give

 |R_X(s_tau)| <= 3 sqrt(X)(log X+1)=:B_X.                   (4.1)

Indeed sum_(2<=n<=X)n^(-1/2)<=2sqrt(X), Lambda(n)<=log X,
|C_X(s_tau)|<=2sqrt(X), and |Delta(X)|<=X(log X+1).
For Y>=1, |s_tau| |1-i*tau|>=tau^2 when |tau|>Y. Therefore

 |u_k^(X)-(1/(2*pi))integral_(-Y)^Y A_X(tau)omega(tau)^(-k)dtau|
                         <= B_X/(pi*Y).                    (4.2)

This controls the WHOLE omitted two-sided frequency tail, independently of k.
The length-N Euclidean error is at most sqrt(N)B_X/(pi*Y).

Retain the predecessor's integer prime-label cutoff X_N=2^(16N), and choose
a second, distinct frequency cutoff Y_N=2^(10N). Define v_(N,k) by the finite
integral in (4.2) at X_N,Y_N, and F_N=sum_(k<N)|v_(N,k)|^2. The written
prime-label estimate is

 E_N^2=16N(4N+1)^2 9^(2N-2)/256^N.

Since pi>3 and log 2<1, (4.2) supplies the rational frequency budget

 Q_N^2=N(16N+1)^2/16^N.

Consequently

 |sqrt(D_N)-sqrt(F_N)| <= E_N+Q_N,
 ||u_(<N)-v_N||_2^2 <= 2(E_N^2+Q_N^2)=:B_N.               (4.3)

Both budgets tend to zero exponentially (up to the displayed polynomial
factors). In particular log(1+F_N)=o(N) is equivalent to the preceding scalar
growth target, and would imply RH. This is still an UNPROVED SIGNED HEAD
ESTIMATE. The right side of (3.4) with both integrals restricted to [-Y_N,Y_N]
is a completely finite-domain expression for F_N. No such enormous prime
inventory or frequency integral was evaluated in this contribution.
The finite moment calculations in Section 6 instead use the actual analytic
germ and its rigorous remainder. The two computations must not be confused.

5. What may actually be inserted into Guth--Maynard
---------------------------------------------------
On a prime shell M<n<=V<=2M, M>=1, write

 P_[M,V](s_tau)=A_M sum_(M<n<=2M)b_n n^(-i*tau),
 A_M=log(2M)/sqrt(M),
 b_n=1_(n<=V) [Lambda(n)/log(2M)]sqrt(M/n), |b_n|<=1.        (5.1)

Thus this shell's atomic polynomial really does have the coefficient bound
in Guth--Maynard, arXiv:2405.20552v2 (7 April 2026), Theorem 1.1. Translation
or reversal of a frequency interval multiplies coefficients by unit phases
or conjugates them and preserves that bound. It does NOT remove A_M.
For 1-separated tau-points in an interval of length T, atomic values at
least A_M*V are bounded in count by the theorem's

 C_epsilon T^epsilon (M^2/V^2+M^(18/5)/V^4+T M^(12/5)/V^4)

in its stated asymptotic sense. C_epsilon and the large-T qualification are
not numerically evaluated here. Physical tau-separation is required; angular
Cayley separation cannot replace it without its Jacobian and density cost.
The paper's coefficient-supremum hypothesis cannot be replaced by coefficient
energy alone. This theorem is an external input, not newly proved or imported
as a Lean axiom.

The actual residual is P_[M,V]-C_[M,V]-Delta(V)V^(-s)+Delta(M)M^(-s),
not P_[M,V] alone. Each correction and every cross-shell pairing in (3.4)
remains. Inserting the published upper count for P as though it counted this
complete residual would be invalid. If a discrete layer argument is used,

 sum_i w_i |z_i|^2
   =sum_(j=1)^m (q_j-q_(j-1)) sum_(i: |z_i|^2>=q_j) w_i,

where 0=q_0<q_1<...<q_m are all distinct nonzero squared amplitudes. The
highest layer and the low-value contribution are included. The implementation
checks this identity rather than extrapolating a count beyond its range.

The selected largest prime scale X_N is Y_N^(8/5), so the most expensive
prime shells are LONGER than this frequency window. The published favorable
regime near normalized amplitude M^(3/4) is not obtained merely by substituting
N for M or by choosing a larger bookkeeping clock. Frequency annuli, actual
shell lengths, their endpoint corrections and the weighted Theta_N pairing
need a genuine dispersion estimate. This work now specifies those objects
exactly, but supplies no all-depth bound for their joint correlation.
