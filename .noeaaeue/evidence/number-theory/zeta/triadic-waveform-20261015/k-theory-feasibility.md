# K-theory feasibility for the triadic lattice-reflection system

Version: `TWMK-K/0.1.0`  
Status: feasibility analysis; no formal K-theory implementation and no arithmetic index theorem.

## Validly constructed finite object

Let

\[
L=\{(a,b,c)\in\mathbb Z^3:a+b=c\}\cong\mathbb Z^2,
\]

and let `p_X` be the diagonal projection on `ℓ²(L)` onto the finite set

\[
\Lambda_X^\circ=\{(a,b,a+b):a,b>0,\ (a,b)=1,\ a+b\le X\}.
\]

Then `p_X` is finite rank and

\[
\operatorname{rank}(p_X)=\operatorname{Tr}(p_X)
=\sum_{c=2}^X\varphi(c).
\]

This is the strongest current interpretation of the “jumper who counts”: a finite trace/rank pairing. The standard K-theory statement that a finite-rank projection represents its rank in `K₀(K)` is relevant but is not available as a checked declaration in the pinned repository scope.

## Correct symmetry group

Translations of the complete additive lattice form `Z²`. The involution swapping `a` and `b` acts on `Z²` by swapping coordinates. Thus the full symmetry is

\[
G=\mathbb Z^2\rtimes C_2.
\]

The infinite dihedral group `D_∞` occurs only on the fixed-`c` difference fiber generated by

\[
T_k(a,b,c)=(a+k,b-k,c),\qquad R(a,b,c)=(b,a,c).
\]

The positive primitive cone is not translation invariant. A crossed product on that finite cone would therefore be artificial unless the action is restricted or the space is enlarged.

## Candidate operator algebra

A mathematically natural candidate is

\[
\mathcal A=M_3(C_0(L)\rtimes_r G).
\]

This remains a candidate until the following are supplied:

1. the exact action of `G` on `C₀(L)` and its continuity;
2. a concrete covariant representation;
3. boundedness of arithmetic multiplication and phase operators;
4. domains and affiliation for any unbounded screw generator;
5. a spectral triple or Fredholm module;
6. compactness of commutators/resolvents required by the selected category;
7. a cyclic cocycle or trace with a proved finite pairing;
8. an arithmetic identification of that pairing.

General crossed-product spectral-triple constructions show that such lifts can represent Kasparov products under significant hypotheses. They do not identify this project’s arithmetic operators or produce the missing RH estimate.

## Candidate classes and current disposition

| Candidate | Intended role | Current state |
|---|---|---|
| `[p_X]` | finite primitive-triple count | finite projection and rank formula available; K₀ formalization absent |
| reflection parity projections | even/odd moment channels | exact finite projections available |
| phase unitary `e^{i q₄(A)}` | K₁ transport class | requires self-adjoint source operator and stable homotopy |
| screw evolution `e^{tJQ}` | transport cycle | unitary only under explicit skew-adjointness hypotheses |
| spectral flow | sequence-counting observer | no Fredholm path or arithmetic equality |
| cyclic pairing | moment/count functional | no cocycle/source identification |

## Negative controls

- A projection address or cache record is not a K₀ class theorem.
- The rank of `p_X` does not count zeta zeros.
- Solvability of `G` does not imply source positivity.
- A generic spectral triple on a crossed product is not the repository’s Möbius/Weil source.
- A finite pairing cannot be promoted to all `X` or to RH without an exact limiting theorem.
- `D_∞` is not the full symmetry of the two-dimensional additive lattice.

## Smallest missing theorem

Construct a source-specific Fredholm module `(A,H,D)` and a class `[p_X]` or `[u]` such that the pairing is well defined and

\[
\langle[p_X],[D]\rangle
=\sum_{c=2}^X\varphi(c)
\]

or another independently defined arithmetic count, and then prove that this count controls the analytic source estimate. The first equality alone would still not imply RH.
