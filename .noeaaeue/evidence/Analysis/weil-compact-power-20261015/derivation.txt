0. Why this construction
-----------------------
The predecessor gives an off-line-zero obstruction at support-growth rate
b^2/4, using a Gaussian with a separately paid compact cutoff. That argument
is preserved. Here the test is compact from the beginning. A fixed compact
probability density is convolved with itself n times before applying a fixed
interpolating polynomial. Exact support addition replaces truncation. This
eliminates the cutoff error and improves the necessary growth rate to 2|b|.
The positive probability density does NOT give Weil positivity: its only roles
are the real-axis Fourier bound, an imaginary-axis normalization, and compact
support. The final arithmetic trace still contains signed complex squares.

1. Source, symmetry, multiplicities, and lower bound
--------------------------------------------------
Use Fourier convention hat f(z)=integral f(u)exp(izu)du, and
Q_W(f)=W(f*tilde f), tilde f(u)=conj(f(-u)). The source is Suzuki's complete
Weil distribution for xi, including all prime powers, archimedean and polar
terms; its explicit formula is [S22], Eq.(5.15), and its screw-form adapter is
[S22], Proposition 3.1. No substituted positive measure represents the zeros.
Index distinct actual nontrivial frequency locations z=-i(rho-1/2) by their
positive analytic multiplicities m_z. Critical reflection becomes conjugation,
and |Im z|<1/2. The even completed xi function permits either sign of this
rotation for the whole multiset, not equality of the maps on an individual rho.

For real-type transforms G(conj z)=conj G(z), the complete spectral pairing is
 Q_W(f)=sum_z m_z G(z)conj(G(conj z))=sum_z m_z G(z)^2.            (1)
It is not sum m_z |G(z)|^2. All uses below are absolutely convergent.

Let D_A^0 be smooth compactly supported f with supp f subset [-A,A] and
hat f(i/2)=hat f(-i/2)=0. Define lambda_0(A) as the infimum of Q_W(f)/||f||_2^2
over nonzero f in D_A^0 and kappa_0(A)=max(0,-lambda_0(A)). The full-space
quantities lambda,kappa use all smooth compact tests at the same support.
The prior actual-source estimate gives
 Q_W(f)>=-[11/2+P_A]||f||^2 on D_A^0,
 P_A=2 sum_(n<=exp(2A)) Lambda(n)/sqrt(n).                       (2)
Thus lambda_0 is finite below. D_A^0 is nonempty: apply -D^2+1/4 to a nonzero
smooth bump strictly inside [-A,A]. Integration by parts makes this operator
injective on compact smooth functions. Pole vanishing follows by Fourier
multiplication. The inclusion of domains gives kappa_0(A)<=kappa(A), and both
kappas are nondecreasing in A. We will work on the SMALLER, pole-null domain.
A future finite-dimensional projection must retain both exponential constraints.

2. Complete source summability
------------------------------
HSW [H21], Corollary 1.2, uses both signs of height and analytic multiplicity.
At K=Q its printed bound is
 |N_all(T)-(T/pi)log(T/(2pi e))|<=0.228 log T+27.628, T>=1.
The typeset definition and corollary were rechecked on PDF pp.1-2. Their
numerical companion and theorem were not kernel replayed here.
Since log T<=T, pi>3, T>=1, the upper bound is <30 T^2. The region |Re z|<=1
costs at most30. On 2^j<|Re z|<=2^(j+1), the weight (1+|z|^2)^-2 is at most
2^(-4j), and the count is <=120*4^j. Therefore
 sum_z m_z/(1+|z|^2)^2 <=30+120 sum_(j>=0)4^-j=190.             (3)
No central zero-free statement or simplicity is required. Equally one may
retain an arbitrary proved finite weighted mass C_Z instead of190.

3. A compact base with explicit horizontal decay and near-optimal growth
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix ell>0. Let beta be the probability density 1_[-1/2,1/2]. Choose ONCE a
real, even, nonnegative smooth probability psi supported in [-1,1]. Such a
psi is obtained by normalizing a standard smooth bump; its Fourier transform
Psi satisfies |Psi(x+iy)|<=exp(|y|), |Psi(x)|<=1, and Psi(ib)>=1 for real b.
The last inequality is the integral of cosh(bu) against an even probability.

Put v=beta*beta*psi and
 phi_ell(u)=[v(u-ell)+v(u+ell)]/2,  a0=ell+2.
It is an even nonnegative C_c^infinity probability supported in [-a0,a0]. Its
entire transform is
 H_ell(z)=cos(ell z)[sin(z/2)/(z/2)]^2 Psi(z),                   (4)
with the removable value at0. For real x,
 |H_ell(x)|<=min(1,4/x^2),                                    (5)
where the value1 is used at0. Uniformly in |y|<=1/2,
 |H_ell(x+iy)|<=exp(a0/2) min(1,4/x^2).                        (6)
The first bound comes from the probability integral; the second sine bound
uses |sin(z/2)|<=exp(|y|/2). The cosine costs exp(ell|y|), both boxes together
cost exp(|y|), and psi costs exp(|y|). No large-frequency asymptotic is used.
For b!=0,
 v_b:=H_ell(ib)>=cosh(ell b)>1.                                (7)
The exact v_b, not merely a lower enclosure, normalizes the analytic test.

Choose E>=exp(a0/2) and a positive integer R with R^2>=8E. The program uses
 E=3^ceil(a0/2), R=ceil sqrt(8E),
using e<3 and exact integer square comparisons. It follows that
 |H_ell(z-t)/v_b|<=1/2 whenever |Re z-t|>R, |Im z|<=1/2.        (8)
The shift ell and window R are fixed BEFORE selecting the interpolation
polynomial. A bare cosine without the box/smooth factors has real aliases
of modulus1 at arbitrarily large x and cannot justify (8).

4. Fix the complete near-source inventory, then take powers
----------------------------------------------------------
Suppose an actual nonreal frequency r=t+ib exists, 0<|b|<1/2. The compact
window |Re z-t|<=R contains finitely many actual frequency locations. Adjoin
the two TEST nodes +/-i/2 and retain the distinct support. Use the existing
conjugation-equivariant Lagrange construction to obtain a REAL polynomial P
with
 P(r)=i, P(conj r)=-i, and P(z)=0 at all other nodes of this finite support.
All actual multiplicities m_z stay in the trace, not in the interpolation
support. Pole nodes carry no source mass. They cannot coincide with r because
|b|<1/2. This includes t=0 with no division by height. Let d=deg P and
M=m_r+m_conj(r)>0. P is fixed BEFORE n increases; its coefficient costs can
be very large and are not claimed uniform over source pairs or ell.

For integer n>=1 define
 G_n(z)=P(z)[H_ell(z-t)/v_b]^n,
 f_n(u)=v_b^(-n) P(iD)[exp(-itu) phi_ell^{*n}(u)].              (9)
Finite Fubini and integration by parts give hat f_n=G_n for every complex z.
Every f_n is C_c^infinity and has support in [-na0,na0]. No noncompact input,
cutoff, tail of an inverse transform, or cutoff commutator is present. The
convolution f_n*tilde f_n instead has half-support2na0. Prime powers in its
arithmetic formula can extend to floor(exp(2na0)); these are NOT enumerated.

The selected values are i,-i exactly for every n. All other near-source values
and both pole evaluations vanish exactly. The transform has real type.
Nonnegativity of phi_ell concerns only (4)-(7), not the signed sum (1).

5. Explicit tail after all near terms are cancelled
---------------------------------------------------
For P(z)=sum_j p_j z^j, put S(x)=sum_j |p_j|x^j and
 n0=ceil((d+2)/2), c=|t|+1/2,
 B=E^n0 [1+(|t|+5/2)^2] S(|t|+5/2).                          (10)
For u=|Re z-t|, the polynomial
 [1+(u+c)^2]S(u+c)
has nonnegative coefficients and degree d+2. On u<=2 its value is <= its
value at2. On u>=2 it is <= its value at2 times(u/2)^(d+2). Since2n0>=d+2,
(6), with v_b>=1, proves
 (1+|z|^2)|G_n0(z)|<=B on the entire closed half-strip.         (11)
Thus (3) gives an absolutely convergent baseline tail <=190B^2.
By (8), for every n>=n0,
 |sum_far m_z G_n(z)^2| <=190 B^2 4^(-(n-n0)).                 (12)
A finite sufficient n1 is the first n>=n0 for which this is <M/2. Its
existence follows from a geometric limit; a bounded loop can still exhaust
its resources and is then marked unsuccessful. For EVERY n>=n1,
 Q_W(f_n)=-M+Re sum_far m_z G_n(z)^2 < -M/2.                   (13)
There is no omitted finite interpolation residual; the only spectral error
here is the complete far sum in (12).

6. True L2 normalization with an explicit polynomial cost
--------------------------------------------------------
Put C=S(|t|+2)^2>0 and k=4n0-2d>=4. Plancherel and (5) show for n>=n0
 ||f_n||^2=(1/(2pi)) v_b^(-2n) integral_R |P(x)|^2|H_ell(x-t)|^(2n)dx.
Since |H_ell(x-t)|<=1 for real x, reduce n to n0 inside the integral. On
|x-t|<=2 the integrand is <=C. On |x-t|>=2 it is <=C(2/|x-t|)^k. Consequently
 integral <=C[4+4/(k-1)]<=16C/3.
Because pi>3, the Plancherel prefactor makes the last allowance <C. Hence
 ||f_n||^2 <=C v_b^(-2n).                                    (14)
This is a norm of the ACTUAL compact test, not the coefficient norm alone.
It retains C even when interpolation nodes are extremely close. The test is
nonzero, since its entire Fourier transform takes value i at r.
Combining (13)-(14), with negative numerator and positive denominator,
 lambda_0(na0)<=-M/(2C) v_b^(2n), n>=n1.                     (15)

7. Linear rate and the cofinal finishing target
---------------------------------------------
For any real A>=a0 n1 choose n=floor(A/a0). Support inclusion in the definition
of lambda_0, not a guessed interpolation of eigenvalues, gives
 kappa_0(A)>=M/(2C) v_b^(2floor(A/a0)).                        (16)
It follows that
 liminf_(A->infinity) log(1+kappa_0(A))/A >=2log(v_b)/a0
 >=2log cosh(ell b)/(ell+2)
 >=[2ell|b|-2log2]/(ell+2).                                  (17)
For each fixed ell all windows and polynomials are fixed before taking A large.
Only AFTER obtaining (17) for every fixed ell do we let ell->infinity. Thus

 ANY NONTRIVIAL ZERO rho SATISFIES
 liminf_(A->infinity) log(1+kappa_0(A))/A >=2|Re rho-1/2|.      (18)

For a critical-line zero the right side0 is automatic. The full kappa is >=
kappa_0, so (18) holds there too. This is a necessary source-growth obstruction,
not proof that kappa_0 has a small growth rate or that an off-line zero exists.
It improves the preceding b^2/4 detector rate without rewriting that record.

The executable conservative rate is
 alpha_ell(b)=max(0,2(ell|b|-7/10)/(ell+2)),
using log2<7/10. For 0<=r<2|b|, any ell satisfying
 ell>(2r+7/5)/(2|b|-r)
gives alpha_ell(b)>r. Exact rational selection is provided. It is not a feasible
source-window enumeration claim; R itself grows exponentially with ell.

A future source theorem need only provide a COFINAL sequence A_j->infinity
and nonnegative K_j such that
 Q_W(f)>=-K_j||f||^2 for every f in D_(A_j)^0,
 limsup_j log(1+K_j)/A_j <=alpha.                            (19)
Then (18) implies |Re rho-1/2|<=alpha/2 for EVERY nontrivial zero. In particular
alpha=0 implies RH. The original exact root must still be connected and the
analytic chain natively proved. No witness of (19) is supplied here.
The inequality must concern ALL pole-null tests at each chosen support, not
only a finite basis or our manufactured detectors. A lower bound just for the
specific detectors would suffice separately if its quantifiers and constants
are actually proved, but does not automatically give a spectral infimum bound.
No bounded gaps between A_j are required. A finite prefix is insufficient.
One can prove the comparison directly using f_floor(A_j/a0); it does not require
an attained eigenvalue, a chosen eigenfunction or a spectral limiting model.
The only currently supplied general upper rate is the crude one from (2):
P_A<=8A exp(A), hence kappa_0(A)<=11/2+8A exp(A) and the upper rate is at most1.
Equation(18) then returns only |Re rho-1/2|<=1/2, consistent with the known
critical strip, not a new region. An upper rate strictly smaller than1 would
supply a thinner closed collar; a rate tending to0 is the remaining RH target.
Conversely RH gives Q_W>=0 on all compact tests, hence K_j=0 for any cofinal
sequence. This makes alpha=0 an equivalent obstruction, not a known supplier.
The prior moment-null positive complement may help prove (19), but its full
source Schur coupling must remain, as must the pole constraints under projection.

8. Sharp evaluation scale, without a false spectral equality
-----------------------------------------------------------
Cauchy--Schwarz gives for arbitrary f supported in [-A,A], b!=0,
 |hat f(t+ib)|^2 <=[sinh(2|b|A)/|b|]||f||^2.                  (20)
At b=0 the factor is2A. Consequently any test normalized to value i at r has
||f||^2>=|b|/sinh(2|b|A). A normalized fixed-pair detector cannot have an L2
mass with a faster exponential decay than exp(-2|b|A). Our rates tend to this
scale. This is sharpness of the fixed-pair evaluation scale, NOT an assertion
that the whole source's negative spectral rate equals2|b| for a selected zero.
Other zeros may give a larger obstruction; no upper spectral rate is proved.

9. Research attribution, negative controls, and formal limits
------------------------------------------------------------
[S26] treats the actual localized operator, its form core, small-support
positivity, and lowest-eigenvalue continuity. Its global de Branges limiting
construction is conjectural. [S22] supplies (1) and the exact kernel adapter.
The 2026 numerical paper [K26] discusses exponentially growing INJECTED-zero
residuals in a different observable; those sampled/heuristic claims are not a
uniform lower bound for our source operator and are not used as a lemma here.
The compact convolution/Fourier mechanism is classical analysis. No priority
claim is made for (18), which is proved here from stated source hypotheses.

The pinned Mathlib convolution module was inspected. Its existence/support
and calculus APIs are reusable foundations, not a newly replayed Paley--Wiener
source theorem. The two accompanying Lean files cover only explicit finite
signed-power and scalar Rayleigh/rate implications. They are inert candidates,
not active imports and not proved continuum/root adapters. No new axiom,
placeholder, renamed root, or fulfilled missing positivity premise is asserted.

Six executed profiles use SYNTHETIC spectra: finite Gaussian-rational nodes,
with the listed analytic weights, plus simple real nodes +/-k for every integer
k>=K>R+|t|. Their finite mass is<=20, so N(T)<=20+2T<=30T^2 for T>=1. Pole
nodes occur only in the polynomial. Exact code verifies interpolation, baseline
power, tail budget and rates. It does not evaluate the smooth bump's Fourier
integral or a source Weil form, enumerate zeta zeros in the selected windows,
or compute any spectral bottom. Actual source windows can have MANY more
nodes and much larger constants. The finite divisor is held fixed before n.

An endpoint-only cosine fails because aliases persist arbitrarily far away.
Omitting v_b^n changes the selected negative mass. Replacing G^2 by |G|^2
changes its sign. Counting pole constraints as zeros changes M. A degree bound
without enough initial powers fails integrability. Enlarged test support and
convolution support differ by two. Negative Rayleigh division must use the
actual test norm with the right inequality direction. Strict spectral-tail
separation is required. Invalid inputs or exhaustion produce errors, not proofs.

The remaining substantive task is (19), or an independently proved arithmetic
lower bound on this exact detector family. Exact compactness and the improved
linear rate do not supply it. No new zero-free region is claimed.
