1. Fixed source, finite isolation, and what is actually counted
-------------------------------------------------------------
Use spectral coordinate z=-i(s-1/2). A nontrivial zero has |Im z|<1/2; critical
reflection becomes conjugation. The frequency zero set is the same as Suzuki's
X(z)=xi(1/2-iz), since Xi is even. This equality of full zero multisets must not
be confused with equality of the two coordinate maps on an individual zero.
Use distinct source locations with positive analytic multiplicities m_z. Their
symmetries preserve multiplicity. No repeated interpolation factor creates an
extra analytic zero.

Suppose, for contradiction, a selected nonreal frequency is r=t+ib, b!=0. Put
M=m_r+m_conj(r)>0. The compact zero window |Re z-t|<=1 has finitely many locations.
The earlier finite Lagrange construction supplies a REAL-coefficient polynomial
P with P(r)=i, P(conj r)=-i and P(z)=0 at every other location of that window.
Fix this polynomial and all its coefficients BEFORE varying L. Do not grow a
new P_L without paying the new coefficients. Nothing assumes a numerical list
of unknown off-line zeta zeros. This is a per-hypothetical-zero construction.
It works at t=0 and does not divide by height.

Define, for real L>=1,
 G_L(z)=P(z) exp(-L((z-t)^2+b^2)).
The selected values stay +i,-i, hence contribute -M to the signed square sum.
For |Im z|<=1/2 and |Re z-t|>1,
 |G_L(z)|^2 <= exp(-3(L-1)/2)|G_1(z)|^2.                    (1)
This uses (Re z-t)^2+b^2-(Im z)^2>=3/4, not oscillatory cancellation.
The near-window cancellation and every outside contribution remain explicit.

2. From multiplicity counting to a weighted global source bound
---------------------------------------------------------------
Let N(T)=sum_{|Re z|<=T}m_z. Assume an ACTUAL counting supplier
 N(T)<=C(1+T)^(3/2), for all real T>=0, with 0<C<infinity.  (2)
Then
 S=sum_z m_z/(1+(Re z)^2) <=27 C.                         (3)
Proof: |Re z|<=1 costs at most 2^(3/2)C<3C. On annuli
2^j<|Re z|<=2^(j+1), the contribution is at most
3^(3/2) C 2^(-j/2) <=6C(3/4)^j. Sum the geometric series, costing 24C.
Closed outer boundaries and analytic multiplicities are retained.

For zeta, a finite C exists classically without RH from Trudgian [T12],
Corollary 1 (multiplicity-counted N_+(T)=O(T log T)), zero symmetry and compact
finiteness. This is a cited counting theorem, NOT a native import in this run.
One loose explicit choice is C=48+m_0, where m_0 is the finite total
multiplicity of source zeros at height zero: for T>=3 the printed corollary,
log T<=sqrt T, and its endpoint limit give N_+(T)<=3(1+T)^(3/2).
For T<=3 use monotonicity at 3. Conjugation pays the negative side. No value
of m_0 or simplicity is needed to know this is finite. A bound for this central
inventory can instead be supplied by independently certified source data.
The exact tests below use C=32 for explicitly described SYNTHETIC infinite
spectra; they do not establish that numerical C for the actual zeta source.

Write P(z)=sum_(j=0..d)c_j z^j. For A=1/2 put
 B(u)=sum_j |c_j|(u+|t|+A)^j, a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients.
For every integer k>=0 and real u, |u|^k exp(-u^2)<=k!: if |u|<=1 this is
immediate, and otherwise use |u|^k<=|u|^(2k)<=k! exp(u^2).
Let Q(u)=(1+2t^2+2u^2)B(u)^2=sum q_k u^k and set
 K=2 sum_k q_k k!.
Then
 sup_{|Im z|<=1/2}(1+(Re z)^2)|G_1(z)|^2 <=K,            (4)
since exp(1/2)<2 and exp(-2b^2)<=1. Thus the full far tail is at most
 27 C K exp(-3(L-1)/2).                                  (5)
For integer L, this is at most 27 C K 2^(-(L-1)). The code chooses L so this
is <=M/4. This is a proved scalar schedule, not a certificate-search theorem
about finding off-line zeros. No square-modulus replaces the signed total:
moduli bound only the remainder outside the exactly cancelled window.

3. Exact inverse Fourier transform; its Gaussian is not compactly supported
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Our convention agrees with Suzuki Section 3:
 Fhat(z)=integral_R F(x) exp(i z x) dx.
The inverse Fourier transform of G_L is
 f_L(x)=exp(-L b^2)/(2 sqrt(pi L)) *
        P(iD_x)[exp(-itx-x^2/(4L))].                     (6)
Polynomial-Gaussian Fourier inversion and differentiation follow from the
ordinary Gaussian integral; Mathlib's pinned Gaussian/FourierTransform.lean
has integral_cexp_quadratic and fourierIntegral_gaussian. We inspect and reuse
that source, not a new Fourier normalization. Native replay is still absent.
For nonzero P this is not compactly supported. It is smooth, rapidly decreasing,
and all its derivatives have finite exponentially weighted L1 norms.
Because P is coefficient-real, f_L(-x)=conj(f_L(x)).

For exact coefficient computation, define U_0(x)=1 and
 U_(j+1)=(t-i x/(2L))U_j+i U_j'.
Then P(iD)exp(-itx-x^2/(4L))=exp(-itx-x^2/(4L))*sum_j c_j U_j.
Let A_0=sum_j c_j U_j and
 A_(j+1)=A_j'+(-it-x/(2L))A_j.
Then f_L^(j)=exp(-L b^2)/(2sqrt(pi L))*exp(-itx-x^2/(4L))*A_j.
The factor exp(-L b^2)/(2sqrt(pi L)) is kept in the identity; only for an
UPPER ALLOWANCE with L>=1 may it be replaced by the bound 1.

4. An explicit smooth cutoff and all derivative costs used
----------------------------------------------------------
Let kappa(x)=c exp(-1/(1-x^2)) on |x|<1, zero outside, normalized to integral 1.
This is the standard C_infinity bump. On [-1/2,1/2] its unnormalized value is
at least exp(-4/3)>1/9, so c<9 using e<3. Its derivative obeys
 |kappa'|<=18 sup_(v>=1) v^2 exp(-v)<=18,
since the maximum is 4/e^2<=1. Also |kappa|<=9.
Set kappa_half(x)=2 kappa(2x), and
 chi(x)=integral_{-3/2}^{3/2} kappa_half(x-y)dy.
Then chi is even, smooth, 0<=chi<=1, equals 1 on [-1,1], vanishes outside
[-2,2], and has |chi'|<=36, |chi''|<=144. These follow by differentiating
at the two endpoints, with both endpoint contributions counted.
Let chi_R(x)=chi(x/R) for R>=1, and f_(L,R)=chi_R f_L.
This is genuinely C_c^infinity, supported on [-2R,2R], and is still Hermitian
under x->-x and conjugation. Its transform G_(L,R) is entire and real-type.
It is NOT equal to G_L, including at the selected pair. We pay that change.

5. Gaussian derivative tails with computable constants
-------------------------------------------------------
Fix a strip width A>=0 and R>=max(1,8AL). Put
 J_k(R,L)=integral_R^infinity x^k exp(-x^2/(8L))dx.
Integration by parts gives
 J_0 <= (4L/R)exp(-R^2/(8L)), J_1=4L exp(-R^2/(8L)),
 J_k=4L R^(k-1)exp(-R^2/(8L))+4L(k-1)J_(k-2), k>=2.
Define B_0=4L/R, B_1=4L and B_k=4L R^(k-1)+4L(k-1)B_(k-2).
Let a_j be any upper bound on the coefficient l1 norm of A_j; the executable
uses sum(|real coefficient|+|imaginary coefficient|), an upper bound on the
Euclidean complex coefficient norm. For j=0,1,2,
 T_j(R):=integral_{|x|>=R} exp(A|x|)|f_L^(j)(x)| dx
       <=2 a_j B_(d+j)(R,L) exp(-R^2/(8L)).              (7)
Indeed A|x|<=x^2/(8L) on this region, deg A_j<=d+j, |x|>=1, and the
normalizing factor is <=1. No rounding occurs in B_k. The code safely bounds
exp(-X) by 2^(-floor X) for X>=0. All estimates are for the original f_L.

6. Weighted strip convergence, not just uniform pointwise convergence
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For e=f_(L,R)-f_L and z=u+iy, |y|<=A, direct integration bounds its transform
by T_0. Twice integrating by parts with respect to the REAL frequency u gives
 u^2 |G_(L,R)(z)-G_L(z)|
 <= T_2+(72/R+2A)T_1+(144/R^2+72A/R+A^2)T_0.
The boundary terms at +/-infinity vanish by Gaussian decay. Differentiating
exp(-yx)e produces e''-2ye'+y^2e; every cutoff derivative is retained.
Consequently
 sup_{|Im z|<=A}(1+u^2)|G_(L,R)-G_L| <=delta_R,           (8)
 delta_R=T_2+(72/R+2A)T_1+(1+144/R^2+72A/R+A^2)T_0.
For fixed P,L,t,b,A the explicit bound delta_R tends to zero as R tends to
infinity. The code uses R=4L,8L,16L,... at A=1/2. This does not assert that
R remains bounded when L, P or the source window changes.

For integer L>=1 a global strip bound is
 |G_L(z)|<=B_L:=3^ceil(L/4) sum_k B_k_coeff k!, |Im z|<=1/2,
where B_k_coeff are the coefficients of the polynomial B(u) in Section 2.
This uses exp(L/4)<=3^ceil(L/4). It is deliberately coarse and source-dependent.

If |g-h|<=delta/(1+u^2), |h|<=B, then
 |g^2-h^2|<=delta(2B+delta)/(1+u^2).                      (9)
Therefore the ENTIRE multiplicity-weighted spectral sums satisfy
 |sum_z m_z G_(L,R)(z)^2 - sum_z m_z G_L(z)^2|
 <=27 C delta_R(2B_L+delta_R).                           (10)
Equations (3),(8),(9) prove absolute convergence and permit the passage from
finite sums to the full source. A uniform error without the frequency weight
would not suffice on an infinite divisor. This is not an unpriced invocation
of Fourier continuity, nor a limit in a norm that ignores off-real frequencies.

7. A compact, mean-zero screw test with the signed mass retained
----------------------------------------------------------------
Choose L with (5)<=M/4. Then choose R>=4L with (10)<=M/4. Both choices are
finite because the displayed scalar bounds decrease to zero. The full signed
spectral pairing of the compact test obeys
 sum_z m_z G_(L,R)(z)^2 <=-M+M/4+M/4=-M/2<0.            (11)
The sum is real by source conjugation and real-type G_(L,R).
This proves a quantitative form of the classical direction: an off-line source
pair would produce a negative COMPACTLY SUPPORTED Weil test. It is not a proof
that such an off-line pair exists, and it is not a proof that this pairing is
nonnegative. It supplies the tail/admissibility part of the proposed extraction.

Let tilde f(x)=conj(f(-x)) and theta=f_(L,R)*tilde f_(L,R), so theta is smooth,
supported on [-4R,4R]. Fourier transformation gives
 thetahat(z)=G_(L,R)(z)conj(G_(L,R)(conj z))=G_(L,R)(z)^2.
No absolute square is introduced. For the actual zeta source, Suzuki [S22]
Section 3.2 defines W(theta) as this full zero sum, and Section 3.5 identifies
 W(theta)=integral_R Psi(x) theta''(x) dx.                (12)
Here Psi is EXACTLY formula (1.1), extended evenly: for x>=0,
 Psi(x)=4(exp(x/2)+exp(-x/2)-2)
  -sum_(n<=exp x) Lambda(n)/sqrt(n)*(x-log n)
  +(x/2)(digamma(1/4)-log pi)
  +(1/4)(C0-exp(-x/2)*Phi(exp(-2x),2,1/4)),
 C0=Phi(1,2,1/4)=pi^2+8*Catalan.
All polar, prime-power, digamma, and Lerch terms remain. The support in (12)
permits a finite prime-power range, potentially as large as exp(4R); no cheap
computation or cancellation follows from finiteness. These terms were not
numerically evaluated in this run.

For phi=f_(L,R)', integral phi=0 EXACTLY because f_(L,R) is compactly supported.
Suzuki Proposition 3.1 gives
 W(theta)=<phi,phi>_(G_g,2R), g=-Psi.                     (13)
This identifies the admissible source form with no surrogate Hodge matrix.
It is a cited classical theorem with the correct Fourier convention; its
formal dependency closure remains to be implemented/replayed in Lean.

Cutoff multiplication generally breaks extra exact transform zeros at +/-i/2.
Thus a pole-killing interpolation factor before cutoff does NOT license
omission of pole terms afterward. Equations (12)-(13) keep the complete source.
Restricted-support positivity theorems cannot be applied at arbitrarily growing
R unless their actual hypotheses hold. The Fourier support has not stayed fixed.
