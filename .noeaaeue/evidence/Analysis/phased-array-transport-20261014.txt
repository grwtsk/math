## 1. Sources and the exact phased-array interpretation

The recovered logarithmic pendulum model is a finite exponential sum.
For real sigma,t and a finite integer support A, use

 D_sigma(t) = sum_(k in A) c_k k^(-sigma) exp(-it log k).       (PA1)

The log k are a nonuniform frequency array, and c_k may be signed or
complex. This is a mathematical comparison with an array factor, not a
claim of an electromagnetic realization. Integer modes are not all prime
modes. In the actual finite zeta evaluator, polynomially weighted
Euler--Maclaurin completion terms also occur and remain part of the source.

## 2. What can be steered without changing the problem

Changing t to t0+u in PA1 is exact if c_k is first multiplied by
exp(-it0 log k). The subsequent factor exp(-iu log k) remains. Changing
sigma also changes k^(-sigma). A phase-only model that freezes that
amplitude is not an off-line approximation with zero cost. Every
polynomial amplitude in the completed zeta stack must also be transported.

For a column a and readout w, D=w* a. Under an invertible change a'=Ta,
the faithful readout is w'=(T^-1)*w, so w'*a'=w*a. For unitary T this
preserves vector energy as well. Steering only the coefficients or only
the readout changes D unless a separate reconstruction is proved.
Example: (1,-1) and (1,1) have equal squared coefficient norm 2 but their
sum-readout powers are 0 and 4. Thus phase-only element steering can
remove a genuine null of a different function; it cannot exclude a zero
of the fixed zeta function.

A GLOBAL scalar phase in a modulus certificate is different: it only
chooses a direction in which to prove a lower bound on the same complex
number. The new algorithm uses this latter operation, not elementwise
source steering.

For complex columns a=a0+Eu, ||u||_2<=1, a direct support calculation is

 inf Re(w* a) = Re(w* a0)-||E* w||_2,                       (PA2)

when u ranges over a complex Euclidean ball. With a real uncertainty
parameter the dual term is ||Re(E* w)||_2. An arbitrary uncertainty
ellipsoid inferred from finite samples is not a proof that a whole
analytic cell is contained in it. Our executable instead derives its
whole-cell enclosure algebraically from a Taylor polynomial and its tail.

## 3. New phase-separated whole-rectangle certificate

Let F be the ACTUAL F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), with F(1)=1 by analytic limit.
Retain the unchanged completed Euler--Maclaurin stack E from
scripts/reflected_zeta_cover.py and its disk source error epsilon_EM.
At the exact center c of a rectangle, its complete Taylor coefficients
are a_j, with complex interval enclosures supplied by the same evaluator.
Its existing outer disk radius is R=1/2, Taylor degree d=24.

For z=x+iy, |x|<=hx, |y|<=hy, set r=hx+hy<R. Choose 1<=b<=d and keep
p_b(z)=sum_(j=0..b) a_j z^j. The ENTIRE discarded allowance is

 epsilon = epsilon_EM + M_E (r/R)^(d+1)/(1-r/R)
                  + sum_(j=b+1..d) U_j r^j,               (PA3)

where M_E is the original whole-disk stack bound and U_j encloses |a_j|.
All finite coefficient rounding errors stay in the coefficient intervals.
In particular b=4 does not mean that terms 5..24 have been deleted.

Write x=hx(2u-1), y=hy(2v-1), with 0<=u,v<=1. If the corresponding
power polynomial is sum h_pq u^p v^q, its bidegree-(b,b) Bernstein
coefficients are

 B_ij = sum_(p<=i,q<=j) h_pq * binom(i,p)/binom(b,p)
                                  * binom(j,q)/binom(b,q). (PA4)

The identity follows twice from
 u^p=sum_(i=p..b) [binom(i,p)/binom(b,p)] binom(b,i)
                                              u^i(1-u)^(b-i).
Indeed binom(b,i)binom(i,p)/binom(b,p)=binom(b-p,i-p), so the remaining
sum equals u^p[u+(1-u)]^(b-p)=u^p. The Bernstein basis functions are
nonnegative on [0,1], and their sum is one. Consequently p_b(z) belongs
to the convex hull of the B_ij throughout the entire closed rectangle.
Interval arithmetic may widen this hull but cannot remove true values.

Choose a rational pair q=(q_re,q_im) with |q_re|+|q_im|=1. Its Euclidean
norm is at most one. If L_ij is a lower interval bound for
q_re Re(B_ij)+q_im Im(B_ij), then

 m = min_(i,j) L_ij - epsilon > 0                         (PA5)

implies Re(conj(q)F(c+z))>=m throughout the closed rectangle and hence
|F(c+z)|>=m>0. The proof subtracts at most |q| epsilon<=epsilon from the
projected polynomial bound. A convex combination cannot fall below its
smallest projected control point. This is a whole-region certificate,
not a test at vertices of F or at sampled look directions.

The direction is proposed from the midpoint of a0 and normalized in L1.
It can be chosen differently, but the verifier checks its exact norm
and recomputes PA3-5. Direction search can fail to certify a nonvanishing
cell; failure is not a zero theorem. A nonvanishing image that winds
around zero may have no global separating half-plane, so subdivision
and the existing one-zero Rouche mechanism remain necessary.

Strict improvement is possible even for p(z)=1+20z on
|Re z|<=1/100, |Im z|<=1/10: Re p>=4/5, although the older whole-disk
triangle allowance 1-20(11/100) is negative. This example is a test of
the certificate rule, not a zeta-source identity.

## 5. Coherence, aliases, and conditioning for the arithmetic arrays

For PA1 define a_k=c_k k^(-sigma). Exact integration gives, for T>0,

 (2T)^-1 integral_(-T)^T |D_sigma(t)|^2 dt
 =sum_(m,n) a_m conj(a_n) sinc(T log(n/m)),                 (PA6)

where sinc(0)=1. Signs can be placed consistently on the conjugated
factor since the Gram is real symmetric. This is a genuine finite Gram
identity; its off-diagonal terms are NOT removed by assigning separate
labels to array elements. A finite spectral-frequency average is also
not the original physical-time H_(1/3) energy without its exact adapter.

The two-mode normalized Gram for frequencies 0 and epsilon has eigenvalues
1+/-sinc(T epsilon). In particular

 lambda_min <= 1-sinc(T epsilon) <= T^2 epsilon^2/6.        (PA7)

The last inequality follows by integrating 1-cos(epsilon t)<=epsilon^2
t^2/2. The first inequality allows sinc to be negative. For adjacent
arithmetic modes epsilon=log(1+1/n)<=1/n, lambda_min<=T^2/(6n^2).
Thus a fixed observation aperture does not give a uniform stable inverse
for arbitrary increasingly close log frequencies. A source-specific
restricted estimate could still avoid this worst-case cost; none is
assumed. An observed finite matrix rank is not a uniform frame bound.

Multiplicative characters retain exact dependencies:
 exp(-it log(ab))=exp(-it log a)exp(-it log b).
On support {2^j}, the entire phase vector has period 2pi/log2. Adding 3
removes this exact common period but does not provide a quantitative
small-divisor estimate or independent random phases. The actual phase
trajectory of composite modes lies on the subtorus specified by prime
factorization. This is the relevant alias/coherence constraint for FK
and almost-Mathieu comparisons, not a freely chosen uniform array grid.

If beam transforms satisfy sum_j B_j* B_j=I on a finite source space,
then sum_j ||B_j a||^2=||a||^2: the complete energy is preserved, not
reduced. A discarded beam has a residual cost. A lower frame bound is
needed for reconstruction when the transform is not an isometry.

## 6. Calibration, tapering, and coupled residuals

For real phase errors delta_k and any common phase alpha,

 |sum c_k exp(i(theta_k+delta_k))
       -exp(i alpha)sum c_k exp(i theta_k)|
 <=sum |c_k| |delta_k-alpha|.                             (PA8)

The proof uses |exp(iu)-1|<=|u|. Common phase is harmless for modulus;
differential phase is charged with the actual amplitudes. Repeating an
operation charges every occurrence, and a growing arithmetic readout
can amplify a small phase error. There is no automatic large-n gain.

A taper tau_k gives D=D_tau+D_(1-tau), so
 |D|^2=|D_tau|^2+2Re(D_tau conj(D_(1-tau)))+|D_(1-tau)|^2.  (PA9)

Antenna design may choose tau to create a new pattern. Our source cannot
be replaced in this way without proving that the complete complementary
terms have the required bound. The already paid prefix and Bessel tails
are legitimate such comparisons; no new bound on the remaining Mobius
bilinear sequence follows from a desirable tapered plot.

Similarly, if a coupling operator is replaced by C0 with ||C-C0||<=e,
then |w*(C-C0)a|<=||w||e||a||. Diagonal loading of a Gram is an actual
operator change. For invertible positive G,
 G^-1-(G+lambda I)^-1=lambda G^-1(G+lambda I)^-1.
Dropping this correction cannot turn a collapsing mode into a paid one.
The retained-center, Schur, Newton, and FK rules follow this same
principle: keep the observable and every residual of its transformation.
