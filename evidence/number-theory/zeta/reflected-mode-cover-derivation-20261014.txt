## 1. What the coordinated stacks must certify

A finite Dirichlet mode is k^(-s)=exp(-Re(s)log k)*exp(-i Im(s)log k).
Its phase is a logarithmic oscillator, but its horizontal amplitude is part
of the same analytic function. We do not freeze amplitude while moving
horizontally, replace integer support by prime support, or identify a raw
finite Dirichlet sum with zeta inside the critical strip.

We use the analytically regularized function

 F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s),       F(1)=1.

The value at one is the analytic limit, not a totalized product 0*infinity.
Let

 G(s)=Gamma(1+s/2)*pi^(-s/2).

Then xi(s)=G(s)F(s), including removable values. G is holomorphic and
nowhere zero on Re(s)>-2. Thus F and xi have the same zeros, with the same
multiplicities, in every domain used below. We do not evaluate a gamma
function numerically to infer nonvanishing; the classical analytic theorem
supplies this exact normalizer.

For J(s)=1-conj(s), the classical functional equation and conjugation law give

 xi(J(s))=conj(xi(s)),       xi(conj(s))=conj(xi(s)).          (RC1)

J is the SAME-HEIGHT reflection about Re(s)=1/2, not reflection about a
freely movable axis. It transfers zero statements and multiplicities.
It does NOT imply |F(J(s))|=|F(s)|. A numerical lower bound reported by
this program is for F on its evaluated right-hand cell, not the same
lower bound for xi or for F on the reflected cell.

## 2. Exact source identity with every completion term

For integers N>=2, m>=1, write (s)_q=s(s+1)...(s+q-1), with (s)_0=1,
and define the entire finite stack

 E_(N,m)(s)=(s-1)sum_(k=1..N-1) k^(-s)
        +N^(-s) [N+(s-1)/2
              +(s-1)sum_(r=1..m) B_(2r)/(2r)! (s)_(2r-1)N^(1-2r)].
                                                               (RC2)

The N term and half-endpoint term are retained. The finite source stops
strictly at N-1, while the closed tail starts at N. Replacing the sum by
k<=N requires changing the half-endpoint sign. At s=1, the exact stack has
value one; deleting its completion terms instead gives zero.

Euler--Maclaurin, initially on Re(s)>1 and then by analytic continuation,
gives, on Re(s)>1-2m,

 F(s)-E_(N,m)(s)
   =-(s-1)(s)_(2m)/(2m)! *
       integral_N^infinity Btilde_(2m)(x) x^(-s-2m) dx.       (RC3)

This formula is regular at s=1. One can derive the even-Bernoulli remainder
from DLMF 25.2.9's odd-Bernoulli remainder by integrating once, using
Btilde_(2m+1)'=(2m+1)Btilde_(2m) on integer cells and B_(2m+1)(0)=0.
Conversely integration by parts changes (RC3) back into the printed odd
remainder. We use the slightly smaller domain Re(s)>1-2m, so the even
integral is absolutely convergent. Both the integer boundary and infinity
terms are accounted for. No formal power-series continuation is presumed.

The absolutely convergent Fourier expansion of periodic B_(2m) gives

 sup_x |Btilde_(2m)(x)|/(2m)!
      <= 2 zeta(2m)/(2pi)^(2m) < 4/6^(2m),                 (RC4)

using pi>3 and zeta(2m)<=zeta(2)<2. The last inequality also follows by
comparing sum_(n>=2)n^-2 with integral_1^infinity x^-2 dx.

## 3. Entire-disk bounds, not unverified center-to-neighbor propagation

Let c=sigma+i*t, 1/2<=sigma<=1. Fix outer radius R=1/2 and put

 A=|sigma-1|+|t|+R,         V_j=|sigma+j|+|t|+R.

Every point of the closed complex disk D(c,R) has nonnegative real part.
Thus k^(-Re(s))<=1. Directly from (RC2),

 M_E=A(N-1)+N+A/2
     +A sum_(r=1..m) |B_(2r)|/(2r)! N^(1-2r)
                                  product_(j=0..2r-2) V_j

bounds |E_(N,m)(s)| throughout that disk. From (RC3)-(RC4), the complete
source remainder throughout the SAME disk is at most

 epsilon_EM = 4 A product_(j=0..2m-1)V_j
              /[6^(2m) N^(2m-1)(2m-1)].                  (RC5)

Here we bounded N^(1-Re(s)-2m)/(Re(s)+2m-1) by
N^(1-2m)/(2m-1). The formula is conservative but rational, with no unknown
compact-set maximum and no required zero-free neighborhood.

The executable profile uses N=32, m=12. Uniformly for these centers with
|t|<=32, its disk source remainder is less than 2^(-43). For an exact
standalone check use A<=33 and V_j<=67/2+j, giving

 epsilon_EM <=
 53703147653473060592408040143561202428125 /
 517801261096514918502056314122382776165978964381138944
 < 1/2^43.                                               (RC6)

This is an approximation bound for F, not a lower-modulus estimate.
Increasing the height without changing parameters is not licensed by the
finite cover. The source bound itself remains explicit in t.

## 4. Aggregate the signed mode jet before taking norms

Expand the complete finite stack at c:

 E_(N,m)(c+z)=sum_(k>=0) a_k z^k.

For the mode n^(-s), its coefficients are
 n^(-c)(-log n)^k/k!.
Multiplication by (s-1) and by the actual rising-factorial polynomial in
(RC2) is performed BEFORE combining the modes and estimating a_k.
This is not sum of individual absolute Dirichlet-mode allowances.

The program encloses each coefficient a_k in a complex rectangle, with
rational endpoints, for 0<=k<=d. Write L_k for a proved lower bound on
|a_k| and U_k for a proved upper bound. It uses

 L_k=max(distance(0,real interval),distance(0,imag interval)),
 U_k=max|real interval|+max|imag interval|.

These are respectively L-infinity lower and L1 upper bounds for complex
Euclidean modulus. They do not pretend that the two norms are equal.

For 0<=r<R, Cauchy's coefficient estimate and a geometric series imply

 |F(c+z)-sum_(k=0..d)a_k z^k| <= rho(r),   |z|<=r,
 rho(r)=epsilon_EM + M_E (r/R)^(d+1)/(1-r/R).               (RC7)

The finite coefficient intervals already include every rounding and
transcendental-evaluation error. Those errors are not omitted from L_k or
U_k; no second guessed numerical tolerance is inserted into rho.

The default Taylor degree is 24, with 112-bit outward dyadic rounding.
The outer disk is independently certified by (RC5); overlap of smaller
output disks is never used to assume an outer-disk hypothesis.

## 5. Two different local certificate types

ZERO-FREE DISK. If

 m0=L_0-sum_(k=1..d) U_k r^k-rho(r)>0,                     (RC8)

then |F(s)|>=m0 throughout the CLOSED disk D(c,r), so it has no zeros
there. For an axis-aligned rectangle, choose r as the sum of its two
half-widths. Every corner and every interior/seam point lies in this disk.
The program certifies the interior through this inequality, not merely the
sampled center or a collection of nonzero boundary values.

ONE-ZERO DISK. If r>0 and

 m1=L_1*r-U_0-sum_(k=2..d)U_k r^k-rho(r)>0,                (RC9)

then, on |z|=r, |F(c+z)-a_1 z|<|a_1 z|. Rouche's theorem gives exactly
ONE zero, counting multiplicity, in the open disk, and no zero on its
boundary. In particular that zero is simple. This is a zero-count theorem,
not a Newton iteration convergence assumption.

When Re(c)=1/2, this disk is invariant under J. The nonvanishing factor G
makes its F-zero an xi-zero. By (RC1), J produces another zero in the same
disk. Uniqueness forces J(rho)=rho, hence Re(rho)=1/2. No numeric real part
is projected onto the critical line. The positive radius can be much
larger than the final horizontal conclusion: the latter is EXACT by
symmetry plus a certified count of one.

This is the concrete local locking mechanism requested for coordinated
reflected stacks. It is not the false claim that symmetry alone excludes
an off-line pair. For example (s-1/2)^2-lambda is reflection symmetric, but
at positive lambda its two roots leave the line. Our count-one test fails
on the double-zero collision, and our zero-free test fails on a disk
containing a zero. These controls are tested independently.

## 6. A complete finite cover, including critical-line cells

The saved instance begins with the CLOSED right upper rectangle

 Dplus={s: 1/2<=Re(s)<=1, 0<=Im(s)<=32}.

Starting with that rectangle, bisect its longest side exactly at its
rational midpoint; use the real side when lengths tie. Each bit in a
leaf path selects one of these two closed children. Interiors of the
children are disjoint and the shared seam is included on both sides.

Every leaf has one of two justifications:
 (i) its entire closed rectangle is certified zero-free by (RC8), or
 (ii) every corner is strictly inside an already certified disk of (RC9).
For (ii), convexity and the exact maximum-corner squared distance prove
containment of the entire rectangle. A center sample alone is insufficient.

The verifier builds the full binary trie of leaf paths. A missing child
means an uncovered region and is rejected. Duplicate paths, an ancestor
leaf with descendants, unsupported numeric types, or false claims of
native admission are rejected. Local margins are RECOMPUTED from the
literal source and parameters. Stored positive flags/margins are not
accepted on their own. Resource exhaustion raises an error and cannot
emit a complete certificate.

The instance has 236 leaves: 227 zero-free cells and 9 cells contained in
four one-zero disks. The smallest stored zero-free lower margin for F is
2^(-11). That margin is neither a global lower bound near the zeros nor a
same-modulus claim for xi or reflected F.

The four centers and common radius are

 c1=1/2+(113/8)i, c2=1/2+21i,
 c3=1/2+25i,      c4=1/2+(487/16)i,       r=1/16.

These were proposed seed locations, not imported facts about zeros.
Their independently recomputed RC9 margins are at least
 1/2, 1/2, 1, 1, respectively.
All four disks lie within 0<Re(s)<1 and 0<Im(s)<32, and are pairwise
disjoint. Each contains exactly one zero and its real part is 1/2.

The coverage check excludes any other zero in Dplus. J covers the left
upper half, retaining multiplicity. Conjugation covers negative heights.
No zero lies on height zero (its cells are certified zero-free). Thus the
finite classical conclusion is

 xi has exactly eight zeros in 0<=Re(s)<=1, |Im(s)|<=32;
 all eight are simple and have Re(s)=1/2.                   (RC10)

The four positive ordinates are enclosed, respectively, in
 (225/16,227/16), (335/16,337/16),
 (399/16,401/16), (486/16,488/16).

Generated mirror images are not independent numerical certificates.
The use of the CLOSED cover makes seams, height endpoints and the
critical-line boundary part of the proof. No zero count from a public
zero table is used in RC10. Nor does the argument infer an arbitrary-height
statement from these eight zeros.

## 7. Exact elementary arithmetic and independent controls

Only Python standard-library integers and Fraction enter analytic values.
Real intervals use outward dyadic rounding; complex rectangles use exact
real/imaginary operations. Multiplicative errors and cancellations are
propagated before a final margin is tested.

For log n, reduce n=2^k*u with 1<=u<2 and use
 log u=2 sum_(j>=0) y^(2j+1)/(2j+1), y=(u-1)/(u+1), 0<=y<=1/3.
The omitted positive tail after L terms is at most
 2 y^(2L+1)/[(2L+1)(1-y^2)].
Use the same identity at y=1/3 for log 2. Interval operations retain its
multiplicity k. All cached public inputs are checked before lookup.

For complex exp, halve the argument until its L1 upper bound a<=1/2.
After degree L, the entire omitted tail in complex modulus is at most
 2 a^(L+1)/(L+1)!,
since exp(a)<2. Widen both real and imaginary rectangles by that amount,
then square with outward rounding for each halving. There is no floating
point trigonometric oracle, ordinary numeric root finder, or library zeta
call in the certified calculation.

Bernoulli coefficients are exact rational values from their defining
recurrence. The jet is independently checked against a distinct Euler
cutoff/order, shifted-center evaluation, the pole value F(1)=1, elementary
series identities and negative normalization controls. Agreement of two
interval evaluations is a consistency check, not the analytic source law.
RC3 supplies the source law.

All new normal/optimized unit tests and all saved-cover local margins are
replayed in separate fresh processes. The execution receipt binds their
actual exit codes, source hashes and complete certificate bytes.
A proposed false one-zero disk, deleted leaf, enlarged margin, missing
completion term, invalid outer domain, wrong source name, or fabricated
all-height/native claim is rejected in the hostile tests.

## 8. What remains for a critical-strip exhaustion

For any delta>0 and finite T, independently zero-free boxes covering
 {1/2+delta<=Re(s)<=1, |Im(s)|<=T}
would reflect to cover the corresponding left region. If such finite
certified covers exist for every required delta and T, every hypothetical
off-line zero is eventually excluded. This is a conditional exhaustion
theorem; its analytic witnesses have NOT been constructed here.

The covering formulation does not require a positive global modulus
margin uniform near every critical zero. Separate cells can have distinct
positive margins, and no sum of all cell energies is required merely to
unite their certified domains. An iterative solver still owes its own
error and conditioning bounds. No uniform success/termination theorem is
inferred from our finite atlas.

The demonstration used simple-zero disks to cover the line as well. This
does not make simplicity of all zeta zeros a prerequisite for RH. The
weaker off-line-cover route needs no universal simple-zero assertion.

No FK equilibrium, positivity, invertible Hessian or source-specific KAM
estimate is assumed. The present contribution completes the analytic
realization and finite reflected coverage step for literal logarithmic
modes. An FK continuation could help only after proving its source map,
phase/neutral-mode compatibility, inverse losses and output error. No
freely chosen coupling, reflection axis, counterterm or projection is
allowed to change xi.

No boundedness result for the existing ell_n, H_(1/3), signed core, or
all-positive-damping family follows from RC10. The large-height portion
of the arithmetic/spectral problem remains. MertensLogSquareBound is not
used here and is not discharged for its previous consumers.
