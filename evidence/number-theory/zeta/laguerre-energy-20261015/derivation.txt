1. The unchanged linear arithmetic source
-----------------------------------------
Let Delta(x)=sum_(n<=x) Lambda(n)-x+1, x>=1, with every prime power present.
Delta(1)=0. Define s(w)=(3+w)/(2(1-w)),
 a(w)=xi'(s(w))/xi(s(w)),
 r(w)=[psi_digamma(1+s(w)/2)-log(pi)]/2,
 p(w)=a(w)-r(w),
 u_k=integral_0^infinity Delta(exp t) exp(-3t/2) L_k(2t) dt.

The original source identity, initially on |w|<1/3, is
 p(w)=1/(s(w)-1)+zeta'(s(w))/zeta(s(w))
     =-(3+w) sum_(k>=0) u_k w^k /2.
Thus p_k=-(3u_k+u_(k-1))/2, with u_(-1)=0. The inverse has Euclidean
operator norm at most one on every finite prefix; no global Xi-quotient inverse
is needed. The prior written proof gives
 log(1+sum_(k<N)|u_k|^2)=o(N) => RH.
It does not establish the antecedent. Subexponential growth is the inherited
endpoint; a fixed positive exponential allowance is not enough.

The new producer evaluates p directly as follows. At s=3/2+h, enclose the
coefficients of zeta by the existing rational Euler--Maclaurin evaluator,
including its Cauchy-priced remainder, form zeta'/zeta, and ADD 1/(1/2+h)
BEFORE composition with h=2w/(1-w). Apply the stable two-term inverse last.
Only the initial zeta value is inverted, and its lower endpoint is positive.
The separate path assembles the original a and r jets and then subtracts them.
Both paths share the zeta evaluator; their overlap is a consistency check, not
an independent proof of its analytic remainder. The constant and terminal
coefficients are retained.

2. A smaller all-depth prime cutoff, proved before enumeration
---------------------------------------------------------------
For X>=1, let L=log X and
 u_k^(X)=integral_0^L Delta(exp t) exp(-3t/2) L_k(2t) dt.
Use only |Delta(exp t)|<=exp(t)(t+1), valid by Lambda(n)<=log n.

The standard ordinary Laguerre generating function is
 sum_(k>=0) L_k(2t) z^k=(1-z)^(-1) exp(-2tz/(1-z)), |z|<1.
It is DLMF 18.12.13 at alpha=0 and follows directly from the finite binomial
coefficient formula used by the scripts. For |z|=r<1,
 Re(z/(1-z))>=-r/(1+r).
One exact verification, writing x=Re z and |z|=r, is
 (1+r)|1-z|^2 [Re(z/(1-z))+r/(1+r)]=(1-r)(r+x)>=0.
Cauchy's coefficient estimate therefore gives, t>=0,
 |L_k(2t)| <= r^(-k)/(1-r) exp(2rt/(1+r)).

Unlike replacing every polynomial coefficient by its magnitude, this bounds
one completed Laguerre polynomial. It does not assume cancellation of primes.
For 0<r<1/3 put alpha=(1-3r)/(2(1+r))>0. Exact tail integration yields

 |u_k-u_k^(X)| <= r^(-k)/(1-r) X^(-alpha)
                         [(log X+1)/alpha+1/alpha^2].          (2.1)

Take r=1/12, alpha=9/26, and the INTEGER cutoff X_N=2^(11N). The exact
integer inequality
 14^26=629983141281877223603213172736
       <633825300114114700748351602688=2^99
implies X_N^(-alpha)<14^(-N). Also log2<7/10. For k<N this proves

 |u_k-u_k^(X_N)| <= (91N/45+910/891) (6/7)^N,
 sum_(k<N)|u_k-u_k^(X_N)|^2
             <= N(91N/45+910/891)^2(36/49)^N =: E_N^2.         (2.2)

The error tends to zero exponentially up to its displayed polynomial factor.
This reduces the prior sufficient cutoff 2^(16N) to 2^(11N), by a factor
2^(5N), while retaining a proved vanishing tail. The earlier tail at the larger
cutoff has a faster convergence rate; no claim is made that (2.2) improves
that rate. At cutoff 2^(11N) the old absolute-polynomial majorant would have
squared exponential ratio 81/sqrt(2^11)>1, whereas the completed-polynomial
bound above decays. The enormous prime range is not enumerated by the program.

The new large-cutoff finite head satisfies
 |sqrt(D_N)-sqrt(D_N^(X_N))|<=E_N,
where D_N=sum_(k<N)|u_k|^2. Hence the same subexponential criterion is equivalent
to subexponential growth of D_N^(X_N). This improves truncation, not the
remaining all-depth arithmetic estimate.

The coefficient count N, prime cutoff X_N, zeta ordinate, and Tao heat time
are different variables. No conversion between them is asserted here.

3. Complete finite-head evaluation at actually enumerated cutoffs
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For fixed k write P_k(t)=L_k(2t). For lambda>0 define the finite polynomial
 A_(k,lambda)(t)=sum_(j>=0) P_k^(j)(t)/lambda^(j+1).
It satisfies lambda A-A'=P_k and
 J_(k,lambda)(a)=exp(-lambda a) A_(k,lambda)(a)
               =integral_a^infinity exp(-lambda t) P_k(t)dt.

On the finite interval [0,L], direct substitution of Delta gives

 u_k^(X)=sum_(n<=X) Lambda(n)[J_(k,3/2)(log n)-J_(k,3/2)(L)]
          -[J_(k,1/2)(0)-J_(k,1/2)(L)]
          +[J_(k,3/2)(0)-J_(k,3/2)(L)].                       (3.1)

Every summand at n=X is exactly zero, but that touching prime power remains
in the inventory. The subtracted common endpoint in each prime contribution
must NOT be removed. The two continuum differences include the original +1.
At X=1 the interval is empty and every u_k^(1) is exactly zero.

The program uses elementary rational logarithm and square-root enclosures,
finite polynomial evaluation, and complete sums over primes AND higher powers.
An independent trial-factorization pass checks each integer through X. It
computes (3.1) through k=7 for X=256 and X=1024. These finite heads are not
silently substituted for the complete u_k; they are recorded separately.
There is no floating-point acceptance or numerical integration in this route.

The first test run caught interval dependency widening at X=1: equal rational
antiderivative endpoint expressions produced a tiny interval around zero.
The producer now uses the exact empty-interval identity at X=1. The test was
not weakened. Original source and failure output are retained under attempts/.

4. An exact dissipative realization of the same Laguerre synthesis
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Let S_N be the lower nilpotent shift and let v=(1,...,1)^T. Put
 A_N=(I+S_N)(I-S_N)^(-1)=I+2S_N+...+2S_N^(N-1).
This is NOT the preceding relative-energy normalization with the same letter;
here A_N is a specified finite GENERATOR. To avoid importing either meaning,
its code name is dissipation_generator.

The full identity is
 A_N+A_N^T=2vv^T.                                             (4.1)
Thus A_N=vv^T+J_N with J_N^T=-J_N. The symmetric part is the Hodge Laplacian
d^*d for d(x)=v^T x; the skew part is retained. A_N itself is not a
self-adjoint Hodge Laplacian, and its semigroup is not identified with Tao heat.

Let Phi_N(t)=exp(-tA_N). For every real or complex vector x,

 d/dt ||Phi_N(t)x||^2=-2|v^T Phi_N(t)x|^2,
 ||Phi_N(t)||<=1,                                             (4.2)
 ||x||^2-||Phi_N(T)x||^2=2 integral_0^T |v^T Phi_N(t)x|^2 dt.

This is a dimension-independent contraction of the EXACT finite Laguerre
propagator, not a sign statement for the arithmetic forcing. The eigenvalues
of A_N are all one, but its symmetric part has a large kernel when N>1.
There is no falsely assigned dimension-independent positive symmetric gap.

The generating function, or direct differentiation of finite polynomials,
proves

 Phi_N(t)v=exp(-t)(L_0(2t),...,L_(N-1)(2t))^T.                (4.3)

More explicitly, after factoring out exp(-t), Phi_N(t) is lower triangular
Toeplitz with coefficient q_k(2t). Multiplication of these polynomial matrices
at t and u gives the same matrix at t+u. No terminal coefficient is omitted.

For f_X(t)=Delta(exp t)exp(-t/2)1_[0,L](t), the actual finite head is

 u_(<N)^(X)=integral_0^L Phi_N(t)v f_X(t)dt.                  (4.4)

A time-reversed driven state x'(tau)=-A_N x(tau)+v f_X(L-tau), x(0)=0,
therefore satisfies x(L)=u_(<N)^(X). Its exact real energy law is

 d/dtau ||x||^2=-2|v^T x|^2+2 Re(f_X(L-tau) overline(v^T x))
              =|f_X(L-tau)|^2/2
                 -2|v^T x-f_X(L-tau)/2|^2.                 (4.5)

The input-state interaction has not been made positive, discarded, or treated
as independent. Integrating (4.5) gives the complete remainder formula

 D_N^(X)=1/2 integral_0^L |f_X(t)|^2dt
           -2 integral_0^L |v^T x(tau)-f_X(L-tau)/2|^2dt.    (4.6)

This identifies exactly which mismatch a coarse Bessel upper bound drops.
When f_X is the prime discrepancy, estimating the difference in (4.6), not
merely each large positive term separately, is still an arithmetic problem.
The scalar subexponential target is not proved by (4.2).

For completeness, stability of the finite triangular matrix gives Phi_N(t)->0.
Differentiating Phi_N(t)Phi_N(t)^T and using (4.1) yields
 integral_0^infinity Phi_N(t)vv^T Phi_N(t)^T dt=I/2.           (4.7)
This agrees with ordinary Laguerre orthogonality. It does not assert f_X has
an untruncated L2 limit. No change of integration measure is hidden.
