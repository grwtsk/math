2. EXPLICIT SOURCE JETS: NO NUMERICAL ZETA OR ZERO TABLE
-----------------------------------------------------
Put s=3/2+h and use the circle |h|=1/4. The standard Euler--Maclaurin identity is

 zeta(s) = sum_(1<=n<M) n^(-s) + M^(1-s)/(s-1) + M^(-s)/2
           + sum_(r=1)^p B_(2r)/(2r)! (s)_(2r-1) M^(1-s-2r)
           + R_zeta(s).

Here (s)_k is the rising factorial. The exact integral remainder is controlled
using |periodic B_(2p)(t)|/(2p)! <= 4/6^(2p). This coarse inequality follows
from its Fourier expansion: 2 zeta(2p)/(2pi)^(2p) <= 4/6^(2p), since pi>3 and
zeta(2p)<2. On the circle, Re(s)>=5/4>1 and |s+j|<=j+2, hence

 |R_zeta(s)| <= 4(2p+1)! / [2p (6M)^(2p)] =: r_zeta.

Indeed the remainder integral of x^(-Re(s)-2p) from M to infinity is at most
M^(-2p)/(2p). The error in the h^k coefficient is at most 4^k r_zeta by Cauchy's
formula. The derivative coefficients are (k+1) times the h^(k+1) coefficients;
that derivative error is explicitly included by generating one extra term.

For the digamma term x=K+3/4+h/2, the recurrence and the Hurwitz-zeta
Euler--Maclaurin constant at its pole give

 psi(3/4+h/2) = log x - 1/(2x)
               - sum_(r=1)^p B_(2r)/(2r x^(2r))
               - sum_(j=0)^(K-1) 1/(j+3/4+h/2) + R_psi(h).

One exact expression for the last remainder is an integral of
periodic B_(2p)(t)/(x+t)^(2p+1). Because Re(x)>=K+5/8 on the circle,

 |R_psi(h)| <= 4(2p-1)! / [6^(2p)(K+5/8)^(2p)] =: r_psi.

Again its kth Taylor error is <=4^k r_psi. Thus no asymptotic expansion is used
without its remainder. The branch of log is the holomorphic one in Re(x)>0.
The source identity is

 xi'/xi = 1/s + 1/(s-1) - log(pi)/2 + psi(s/2)/2 + zeta'/zeta.

The finite interval division for zeta'/zeta needs only the constant Taylor
coefficient to exclude zero; this is verified at s=3/2. It neither assumes nor
proves global nonvanishing. All rational terms, the digamma term, and the zeta
logarithmic derivative remain in the same source before quotient formation.

Elementary constants and rounding:
* Logarithms use 2 atanh((x-1)/(x+1)) after exact reduction to [1,2]. The omitted
  positive tail is bounded by 2 z^(2j+3)/[(2j+3)(1-z^2)].
* pi uses 16 atan(1/5)-4 atan(1/239) with alternating-series endpoint bounds.
* Square roots use integer square roots on an outward binary grid.
* Bernoulli numbers are generated by their rational recurrence.
* Every interval operation rounds outward; zero-containing divisors and reversed
  intervals are rejected. Booleans and floats are not accepted as exact inputs.

Composition h=2w/(1-w) is finite at each coefficient:

3. A CANCELLATION-PRESERVING TRIANGULAR QUOTIENT CERTIFICATE
---------------------------------------------------------
Work in the finite ring R[w]/(w^n). Let D=1+a, and assume a real coefficient box
D_k in [d_k-e_k,d_k+e_k] with e_k>=0 and |d_0|>e_0. Set

 B = d^(-1) mod w^n, cbar = 1-2B,
 chat = any proposed rational coefficient vector,
 V = B(1-chat), Delta=D-d.

The actual quotient c=1-2D^(-1) satisfies the EXACT identity

 (1+B Delta)(c-chat) = cbar-chat + V Delta.                  (Q)

Proof: multiply Dc=D-2 by B, subtract BD chat, and use Bd=1. The common
numerator and denominator are retained. Their uncertainties are not treated
as independent sources.

Define nonnegative coefficient arrays using complete signed convolutions before
absolute values wherever possible:

 q_k = sum_(j=0)^k |B_j| e_(k-j),
 rho_k = |cbar_k-chat_k| + sum_(j=0)^k |V_j| e_(k-j).

Then q_0=e_0/|d_0|<1, and the recursively computed numbers

 u_0=rho_0/(1-q_0),
 u_k=[rho_k+sum_(j=1)^k q_j u_(k-j)]/(1-q_0)

bound |c_k-chat_k| for every k<n. This follows by induction from coefficient k
of (Q). Only q_0<1 is needed. In particular, the sum of the q_k is permitted
to exceed one. A global Banach/Neumann contraction condition is not an
unnecessary finite-stage prerequisite.

For chat=cbar, V=2B^2. Signed convolution in B^2 is performed before taking
absolute values. This is the default implementation. Inversion amplification
remains in the returned u_k; the algorithm does not claim it is subexponential.
The constant denominator is required and checked, not supplied by a positivity
assumption. The test D=1+xw, x in [-2,2], has sum q_k=2 but is correctly enclosed
at every requested finite depth. This controls a finite algebraic recurrence,
not the infinite radius of convergence.

4. LITERAL MATRIX EXTRACTION AND THE RETAINED CUBIC MEAN
-----------------------------------------------------
H_N uses c_1 through c_(2N-1); its terminal coefficient is required. T_N uses
c_0 through c_(N-1). Every Gram entry is computed as its complete signed finite
sum. The program computes Tr(G_N^3) as a complete sum over three matrix indices,
and subtracts (N-1)(Tr(G_N)/N)^3. It never omits the cubic mean or substitutes
an entrywise-positive Gram surrogate.

If l_N <= ||T_N|| <= u_N, then the true denominator A_N^6 is in
[(1+l_N)^6,(1+u_N)^6]. The lower bound comes from ||T_N e_0||, using all first
column entries. The upper bound comes from the absolute row sums of the
literal T_N T_N*. PSD ensures the cubic numerator is nonnegative; intersecting
its enclosure with [0,infinity) is licensed by the supplied spectral identity.
These operations yield a certified interval for J_N, not a floating eigenvalue.

For additional reconstruction at a different proposed coefficient vector, if
|c_k-chat_k|<=epsilon_k, then

 ||T_N-That_N|| <= sum_(k=0)^(N-1) epsilon_k,
 ||H_N-Hhat_N||_F^2 <= sum_(k=1)^(2N-1) min(k,2N-k) epsilon_k^2.

The first inequality follows from the nilpotent-shift representation. The
second counts precisely the multiplicity of each antidiagonal, including the
last one. It shows how the quotient error must enter an arithmetic matrix
