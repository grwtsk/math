## 1. Exact definitions and quantitative result

Use the actual closed Mertens prefix M(x)=sum_(1<=k<=floor(x))mu(k),
V(x)=W(log x)=int_1^x M(v)v^(-3/2)dv, and

  g(y)=y^(-5/12)V(y^(-1/2)),  p(y)=y^(7/6)(1-y),
  P_n(y)=P_n^(0,1/6)(2y-1),
  lambda_n=n(n+7/6),  nu_n=n+7/12,  b_n=(7/6)_n/n!.

The previous exact full coefficient formula is

  d_n=(1/(2lambda_n)) int_0^1 p(y)g'(y)P_n'(y)dy, n>=1. (BE1)

Here g' is the almost-everywhere derivative of the actual primitive;
M is not differentiated. It satisfies |g'| <= (4/3)y^(-5/3).
For n>=2 the bulk r_n from y in [1/n,1] satisfies

  |r_n|<=15 n^(-7/6),
  sum_(n>=2)(4n+7/3)|r_n|^2<=4725.                       (BE2)

For gamma>-1/2 define the entire, normalized Bessel function

  F_gamma(z)=sum_(k>=0) (-z^2/4)^k / (k! (gamma+1)_k),
  F_gamma(0)=1.

For positive z this equals Gamma(gamma+1)(2/z)^gamma J_gamma(z).
Let theta(y)=2 arcsin(sqrt(y)) for 0<y<=1/2. Define the first Bessel
reference for the actual Jacobi normalization by

  B_n(y)=(-1)^n b_n [theta(y)/(2sqrt(y))]^(2/3)
                    (1-y)^(-1/4) F_(1/6)(nu_n theta(y)). (BE3)

The analytic extension at y=0 is B_n(0)=(-1)^n b_n. Retain its exact
frequency nu_n, geometric prefactor, and parity. Do not replace theta by
2sqrt(y), remove the prefactor, or use the frequency n without a new error
estimate. The construction is a classical type of Jacobi--Bessel reference;
its existence or its name does not itself imply an arithmetic bound.

For every n>=2 and 0<y<=1/n, we prove the explicit kernel comparisons

  |P_n(y)-B_n(y)| <=19 b_n y,
  |P_n'(y)-B_n'(y)| <=100 b_n(1+nu_n^2 y).                (BE4)

Define the endpoint coefficient

  beta_n=(1/(2lambda_n)) int_0^(1/n) p(y)g'(y)B_n'(y)dy.

Then the Jacobi-to-Bessel endpoint difference epsilon_n satisfies

  d_n=r_n+beta_n+epsilon_n,
  |epsilon_n|<=200 n^(-4/3),
  sum_(n>=2)(4n+7/3)|epsilon_n|^2<=420000.                (BE5)

Finally define a FINITE-SOURCE coefficient, using the same g rather than a
new inverse-transform representative,

  t_n=(1/(2lambda_n)) int_(n^-4)^(1/n) p(y)g'(y)B_n'(y)dy.
                                                               (BE6)

Its omitted coefficient tail tau_n=beta_n-t_n obeys

  |tau_n|<=50 n^(-11/6),
  sum_(n>=2)(4n+7/3)|tau_n|^2<=10500.                    (BE7)

Consequently d_n-t_n=r_n+epsilon_n+tau_n, with all terms retained, and

  ||(d_n-t_n)_(n>=2)||_(ell2(4n+7/3))
     <=D:=sqrt(4725)+sqrt(420000)+sqrt(10500)<821.         (BE8)

For any integer N>=1, the squared-error tail is bounded through

  ||(d_n-t_n)_(n>N)||_(ell2(4n+7/3))
   <=sqrt(4725)N^(-1/6)+sqrt(420000)N^(-1/3)
                         +sqrt(10500)N^(-5/6).          (BE9)

This is an approximation-difference tail, NOT an upper bound for either
actual arithmetic sequence. No smallness of t_n is inferred.

## 2. Finite physical arithmetic range and all cutoff terms

Changing variables in (BE6), without modifying either source term, gives

  t_n = -1/(2lambda_n) int_(sqrt(n))^(n^2) (1-x^-2)
       [(5/6)x^(-5/2)V(x)+x^(-3)M(x)] B_n'(x^-2) dx.     (BE10)

Only mu(k) with k<=n^2 is needed for this coefficient, but V(x) retains
all the coefficients before the lower endpoint. In particular, replacing
V(x) by V(x)-V(sqrt(n)) changes t_n. The lower endpoint sqrt(n) is not
rounded. On integer cells use M(x)=M_floor(x) and
V(x)=2(A_floor(x)-x^-1/2 M_floor(x)), including the partial first cell and
closed last cutoff. Integer values do not affect the Lebesgue integral, but
the source convention is not changed. At n^2 the touching response is zero
and its coefficient remains in the closed prefix. The original completed
prime-product numerator, its endpoint terms, and unborn-product debt are
not replaced or deleted by this scalar coefficient transformation.

The range [sqrt(n),n^2] is a finite-source theorem for each n, not a claim
that a finite collection of n proves the all-degree estimate. It is not a
complexity or fast-evaluation claim. The current executable evaluates the
reference kernels and rational identities, not these full source integrals.

## 3. A Bessel Green kernel with explicit constants

Put h=2/3, ell=1/3. For theta in (0,pi/2], let

  y=sin(theta/2)^2, w(theta)=y^(1/3)(1-y)^(1/4),
  U(theta)=(-1)^n w(theta)P_n(y), C_n=b_n 2^(-2/3).

Direct substitution in the Jacobi equation gives

  U''+[nu_n^2+2/(9theta^2)+r(theta)]U=0,
  r(theta)=1/[18sin(theta/2)^2]-2/(9theta^2)
                                      +1/[16cos(theta/2)^2]. (BE11)

At zero U(theta)=C_n theta^(2/3)(1+O_n(theta^2)); the analogous derivative
expansion follows from the finite Jacobi polynomial and analytic geometric
factors. This expansion fixes the regular branch, not just the value U(0)=0.

The perturbing potential satisfies

  0<=r(theta)<=11/72<1/6.                                (BE12)

Indeed u=theta/2<=pi/4<1. The elementary sine remainder gives
sin(u)>=u(1-u^2/6), so

  0<=csc(u)^2-u^-2
    <=(1/3-u^2/36)/(1-u^2/6)^2 <=12/25<1/2.

Also cos(u)^2>=1/2. Thus r<=1/36+1/8=11/72. The apparent singularities
in r cancel; no unspecified compact maximum is used.

The normalized Poisson integral for F_gamma is a cosine average against a
positive probability density on [0,1] for gamma>-1/2. Therefore

  |F_gamma(z)|<=1 for real z,
  F_gamma'(z)=-z F_(gamma+1)(z)/(2(gamma+1)).              (BE13)

These statements also follow by normalizing DLMF 10.9.4 and differentiating
the entire power series. Define

  a(theta)=theta^(2/3)F_(1/6)(nu_n theta),
  b(theta)=theta^(1/3)F_(-1/6)(nu_n theta).

They solve the unperturbed equation u''+[nu_n^2+2/(9theta^2)]u=0.
Their Wronskian a b'-a' b is the constant -1/3, obtained from their leading
powers at zero and the absence of a first-derivative term. For 0<s<=theta,

  G(theta,s)=3[b(s)a(theta)-a(s)b(theta)]

has G(s,s)=0 and partial_theta G(s,s)=1. From (BE13),

  |G(theta,s)|<=6theta^(2/3)s^(1/3),
  |partial_theta G(theta,s)|
        <=4theta^(-1/3)(1+nu_n^2 theta^2)s^(1/3).         (BE14)

For clarity, the derivative factors of a and b are bounded by

  |a'| <=(2/3)theta^(-1/3)+(3/7)nu_n^2 theta^(5/3),
  |b'| <=(1/3)theta^(-2/3)+(3/5)nu_n^2 theta^(4/3).

Using s^(2/3)theta^(-2/3)<=s^(1/3)theta^(-1/3) and its corresponding
higher-power version, the derivative constant is at most
3[1+(36/35)nu_n^2 theta^2], dominated by 4(1+nu_n^2 theta^2).
All orders in these bounds lie in the stated Poisson domain.

## 4. Matching the original regular branch

Let v(theta)=C_n a(theta) and D_n(theta)=U(theta)-v(theta).
Variation of constants, with the actual branch normalization, gives

  D_n(theta)=-int_0^theta G(theta,s)r(s)U(s)ds.            (BE15)

This can first be derived on [epsilon,theta] and then passed to zero.
Alternatively, the displayed integral solves the inhomogeneous difference
equation and has order theta^(8/3). The difference from D_n is homogeneous;
both its theta^(1/3) and theta^(2/3) coefficients vanish, so it is zero.
The integral and its first derivative converge by the bounds in (BE14).
Merely imposing a zero endpoint value would not eliminate the theta^(1/3)
homogeneous branch: both unperturbed solutions vanish at zero.

The classical endpoint Jacobi bound gives |P_n(y)|<=b_n. Therefore

  |U(s)| <= b_n (s/2)^(2/3)=C_n s^(2/3).

No Gronwall exponential or spectral bound is needed. Equations (BE12)--(BE15)
now yield, for the complete difference and its derivative,

  |D_n(theta)|<=(C_n/2)theta^(8/3),
  |D_n'(theta)|<=(C_n/3)theta^(5/3)(1+nu_n^2 theta^2).     (BE16)

For example the first follows from 6*(1/6)*int_0^theta s ds=theta^2/2;
the second follows from 4*(1/6)*int_0^theta s ds=theta^2/3.

On 0<y<=1/2, use

  theta<=3sqrt(y), dtheta/dy<=2y^-1/2,
  1/w<=2y^-1/3, |(d/dy)log w|<=1/y, C_n<=b_n.

The first bound follows from arcsin(u)<=sqrt(2)u for u<=1/sqrt(2).
Since P_n-B_n=(-1)^n D_n/w, the value error is at most
3^(8/3)b_n y<19b_n y. Its y-derivative is bounded by

  b_n[(4/3)3^(5/3)(1+9nu_n^2 y)+3^(8/3)]
  <=b_n[85/3+84nu_n^2 y] <=100b_n(1+nu_n^2 y).

Here 3^5<7^3 and 3^8<19^3 certify the root comparisons. This proves (BE4)
for all n>=2; there is no asymptotic onset hidden in the constants.

The reference is not identical to the polynomial. Its exact derivative at
zero satisfies

  B_n'(0)-P_n'(0)=(-1)^n b_n *5/72.                     (BE17)

To verify this, the geometric prefactor divided by (-1)^n b_n has expansion
1+(13/36)y+O(y^2), and F_(1/6)(nu theta)=1-(6/7)nu^2 y+O(y^2).
Use nu^2=lambda+49/144 and P_n'(0)/((-1)^n b_n)=-6lambda/7.
Omitting the shift in nu changes this difference to n+13/36 in modulus,
so even the derivative allowance at zero fails for that wrong reference
when n>100. This is a finite exact control, not a numerical asymptotic claim.

## 5. Coefficient error: an all-degree summable estimate

Subtract the two endpoint integrals before estimating. By (BE4) and |g'|,

  |epsilon_n|
  <= (200 b_n/(3lambda_n))
               [2n^-1/2+(2/3)nu_n^2 n^-3/2].           (BE18)

For n>=2, b_n<=(6/5)n^(1/6), lambda_n>=n^2, and nu_n^2<=2n^2.
Consequently the right side is at most

  160 n^-7/3+(320/3)n^-4/3
  <=(560/3)n^-4/3 <200 n^-4/3.

The normalization bound b_n<=(6/5)n^(1/6) follows from
log b_n<=H_n/6<=(1+log n)/6 and exp(1/6)<6/5. The latter follows by
comparing the exponential series with the geometric series.
Since 4n+7/3<=7n and sum_(n>=2)n^-5/3<=int_1^infinity u^-5/3du=3/2,
the total squared comparison cost is at most 7*200^2*(3/2)=420000.
This proves (BE5), independent of MertensLogSquareBound.

For (BE2), the prior interior derivative bound gives, from y=1/n to
1-n^-2, a contribution at most 14n^-7/6. The endpoint derivative estimate
on the last interval contributes less than n^-7/6. Thus |r_n|<=15n^-7/6
and 7*15^2*int_1^infinity u^-4/3du=4725 pays its squared sum. The source
terms are unchanged from (BE1). No target energy premise is used.

## 6. The far physical tail also has an explicit summable cost

For any real X>=sqrt(n), (BE4) and the endpoint derivative bound give

  |B_n'(y)|<=b_n[(6/7)lambda_n+100+100nu_n^2 y],
                                   0<y<=X^-2<=1/n.

Hence the part of beta_n arising from physical x>X has modulus at most

  T_n(X)=(4b_n/(3lambda_n))
       [((6/7)lambda_n+100)/X+100nu_n^2/(3X^3)].          (BE19)

This is a scalar coefficient-tail allowance, not the target half-line
energy tail. It bounds the original future source by its elementary
amplitude; it does not set future coefficients to zero.

Take X=n^2. With nu_n^2/lambda_n<2 and n>=2,

  T_n(n^2) <= b_n n^-2 [8/7+100/3+50/9]
           <= (5044/105)n^-11/6 <50n^-11/6.

Squaring with weight <=7n gives 7*50^2*int_1^infinity u^-8/3du=10500.
This proves (BE7). The same integral comparisons from N rather than one
prove the three terms of (BE9).

## 7. Exact remaining arithmetic obligation

For integer N>=1 let Bcal_N=sum_(n=2..N)(4n+7/3)t_n^2, an empty sum for
N=1. Let Q_N be the exact full Jacobi partial energy and Q_1 its first two
terms. Finite weighted triangle inequalities give

  |sqrt(Q_N-Q_1)-sqrt(Bcal_N)|<=D<821.                   (BE20)

Thus the original all-degree bound, and hence the original one-third
finite-window energy bound, is equivalent to

  exists B>=0 forall N>=1: Bcal_N<=B.                    (BE21)

No witness for (BE21) is obtained. If it were supplied, the earlier exact
moment norm identity would give

  H_(1/3)(T)<=Q_1+(sqrt(B)+D)^2
             <1+(sqrt(B)+821)^2, for all real T>=0.      (BE22)

Indeed pi>3, d_0=2/pi^2 and
 d_1=-7/(3pi^2)+195/(14pi^4)
show Q_1<28/243+(19/3)(7/27)^2=1183/2187<1.
Conversely H_(1/3)<=B gives Bcal_N<=(sqrt(B)+D)^2.
The previously retained core comparison then gives
 Z(R)<=8[Q_1+(sqrt(B)+D)^2]+69c.
These are conditional returns only. No new zero-free region is inferred
without the missing arithmetic witness.

For a genuinely weaker sufficient route than a coefficientwise envelope,
one may bound dyadic blocks of Bcal. For example a proved statement

  exists C>=0,epsilon>0 forall k>=1:
    sum_(2^k<=n<2^(k+1))(4n+7/3)t_n^2<=C/(k+1)^(1+epsilon)

would supply a finite B after accounting for the finite initial block.
Neither this nor a pointwise subcritical power bound is proved. All signs
remain inside t_n before its square. Bessel names, oscillation, and the
summable comparison error do not supply that cancellation.

## 8. Why generic Bessel bounds do not complete the argument

The counterexample W_*(t)=exp(t/3)-1, which is not the Mobius source,
corresponds to V_*(x)=x^(1/3)-1 and M_*(x)=x^(5/6)/3. It satisfies the
same elementary amplitude and derivative assumptions used in this transfer.
The previous exact coefficients obey

 d_n^*=(-1)^n/2 [1/(n+7/12)-(5/12)_n/(3/4)_(n+1)],
 H_N^*-6<=Q_N^*<=H_N^*+45/2,
 H_N^*=sum_(n=0..N)1/(n+7/12).

Thus Q_N^* diverges. By (BE20), its corresponding finite-source Bessel energy
also diverges. Neither oscillation of the reference kernel nor the presence
of finitely many source values at each n proves (BE21). This refutes a
source-independent inference, not the actual arithmetic target. In
particular, Bessel's inequality in an unrelated Hilbert space cannot be
applied to an input whose required norm has not been established, or to a
family made orthogonal only by changing the source or normalization.

The proved progress is a source-exact passage to an explicit Bessel kernel,
with a summable perturbation and a summable finite-source truncation. The
arithmetic obstruction is not claimed eliminated; it is confined to (BE21).
