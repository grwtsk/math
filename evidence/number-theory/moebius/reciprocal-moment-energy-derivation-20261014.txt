## 1. Exact source and unconditional scalar moments

Retain the literal definitions

\[
M(x)=\sum_{1\le n\le\lfloor x\rfloor}\mu(n),\quad
W(t)=\int_0^t e^{-u/2}M(e^u)\,du\quad(t\ge0),
\]

\[
h_n(t)=2(n^{-1/2}-e^{-t/2})1_{[\log n,\infty)}(t),
\qquad W(t)=\sum_{n\le e^t}\mu(n)h_n(t).
\]

The elementary bound is |W(t)| <= 2(e^(t/2)-1). For z>1/2, nonnegativity of each h_n and direct integration give

\[
\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}h_n(t)dt
=\frac{n^{-(z+1/2)}}{z(z+1/2)},\qquad
\sum_n\int_0^\infty e^{-zt}h_n(t)dt
=\frac{\zeta(z+1/2)}{z(z+1/2)}<\infty.
\]

Absolute Fubini therefore applies to the complete signed Möbius sum. Only in this absolute-convergence domain use the classical reciprocal Dirichlet series. Define, for integers j>=0,

\[
z_j=2j+\frac32,\qquad s_j=z_j+\frac12=2j+2,
\]

\[
\boxed{m_j:=\int_0^\infty e^{-z_jt}W(t)dt
=\frac1{(2j+3/2)(2j+2)\zeta(2j+2)}.}\tag{RM1}
\]

Every m_j exists unconditionally; its zeta argument is at least two. This does not require any continuation of the reciprocal into the critical strip, nor any half-line square integrability at the target damping. These are actual moments, not moments assigned to an auxiliary inverse transform.

## 2. A fixed arithmetic vector for all positive target dampings

Fix 0<sigma<=1/2 and put

\[
a=\frac32-\sigma>0,\quad \alpha=a-1=\frac12-\sigma>-1,
\quad f_\sigma(t)=e^{-\sigma t}W(t),\quad y=e^{-2t}.
\]

For every j>=0, h_j^sigma(t)=e^(-(a+2j)t) satisfies

\[
\int_0^\infty f_\sigma(t)h_j^\sigma(t)dt=m_j.
\]

Thus the arithmetic vector m is the SAME for every sigma. Only its Gram metric changes:

\[
\boxed{A_N(\sigma)_{ij}
=\langle h_i^\sigma,h_j^\sigma\rangle
=\frac1{2i+2j+3-2\sigma},\qquad 0\le i,j\le N.}\tag{RM2}
\]

The functions are linearly independent: multiplication by e^(at), followed by y=e^(-2t), reduces this to independence of distinct polynomial powers on (0,1). Hence A_N is positive definite. The finite Cauchy determinant is

\[
\det A_N(\sigma)
=\frac{\prod_{0\le i<j\le N}[2(j-i)]^2}
{\prod_{i,j=0}^N(2i+2j+3-2\sigma)}>0.
\]

This is a genuine Gram, not the indefinite compressed divisor kernel used elsewhere. It does not imply an upper bound on the arithmetic quadratic below.

## 3. Orthogonalization with no numerical matrix inversion

Let

\[
P_n(y)=P_n^{(0,\alpha)}(2y-1)=\sum_{j=0}^n p_{n,j}y^j.
\]

The Jacobi normalization is fixed by p[n,0]=(-1)^n product_(k=1..n)(alpha+k)/k and the rational recurrence

\[
p_{n,j+1}=p_{n,j}
\frac{(-n+j)(n+\alpha+1+j)}{(\alpha+1+j)(j+1)}.
\tag{RM3}
\]

Standard Jacobi orthogonality, after shifting from (-1,1), reads

\[
\int_0^1 y^\alpha P_n(y)P_k(y)dy
=\frac{\delta_{nk}}{2n+\alpha+1}.
\]

The substitution y=e^(-2t) contributes a factor 1/2. Consequently

\[
e_n^\sigma(t)=\sqrt{4n+3-2\sigma}\ e^{-at}P_n(e^{-2t})
\]

is an orthonormal sequence in L2(0,infinity). Define the fully signed finite scalar

\[
d_n(\sigma)=\sum_{j=0}^n p_{n,j}m_j,
\]

and the nonnegative partial quadratic

\[
\boxed{Q_N(\sigma)
=\sum_{n=0}^N(4n+3-2\sigma)d_n(\sigma)^2
=m_{[0,N]}^T A_N(\sigma)^{-1}m_{[0,N]}.}\tag{RM4}
\]

The arithmetic cancellation occurs inside each d_n before it is squared. No positive/negative Möbius masses are bounded separately. Packet/complement mixed terms are not lost; the whole W determines every m_j.

For arbitrary finite data m, the right side of (RM4) is exactly the minimum L2 norm squared among functions with the first N+1 specified moments. Its minimizer is

\[
g_N(t)=\sum_{n=0}^N (4n+3-2\sigma)d_n(\sigma)e^{-at}P_n(e^{-2t}).
\]

Indeed it has those moments by triangular change of polynomial basis, and any other matching function differs by a vector orthogonal to the finite span. This finite minimization does NOT suppose the actual f_sigma belongs to L2.

For a proposed constant B, Schur complementation gives the exact finite condition

\[
Q_N(\sigma)\le B\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{pmatrix} B&m_{[0,N]}^T\\m_{[0,N]}&A_N(\sigma)\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\tag{RM5}
\]

Its determinant is det(A_N)*(B-Q_N). A finite set of such inequalities does not prove the all-N assertion.

## 4. The actual-source converse, without assuming the conclusion

The key statement is the extended identity

\[
\boxed{\sup_{T\ge0}H_\sigma(T)
=\sum_{n=0}^\infty (4n+3-2\sigma)d_n(\sigma)^2
=\sup_N Q_N(\sigma)\quad\hbox{in }[0,\infty].}\tag{RM6}
\]

Here H_sigma(T)=integral_0^T exp(-2sigma t)W(t)^2 dt. The left side means the nonnegative extended integral, not a totalized ordinary integral used without integrability.

First suppose f_sigma is in L2. The orthonormal sequence is complete. To see this, let q in L2 be orthogonal to all e_n. Then all integrals of q(t)e^(-at)e^(-2jt) vanish. The signed measure q(t)e^(-at)dt has finite total variation by Cauchy--Schwarz. Push it forward by y=e^(-2t) to (0,1], extend it to [0,1] with zero mass at zero, and use polynomial density in C[0,1]. All of its moments vanish, so the measure is zero and q=0 almost everywhere. Parseval proves (RM6) in the finite-energy case.

For the noncircular reverse direction, suppose sup_N Q_N=B<infinity, WITHOUT assuming f_sigma is in L2. The orthonormal series with coefficients sqrt(4n+3-2sigma)*d_n has an L2 sum g with norm squared B. Each h_j lies in the first j+1 basis span, so g has moments m_j.

The actual f_sigma has those same moments by (RM1). Moreover

\[
\int_0^\infty |f_\sigma(t)|e^{-at}dt
=\int_0^\infty e^{-3t/2}|W(t)|dt
\le2\int_0^\infty(e^{-t}-e^{-3t/2})dt=\frac23.
\]

Also integral |g|e^(-at) <= sqrt(B)/sqrt(3-2sigma). Therefore (f_sigma-g)e^(-at)dt is a finite signed measure, with total variation at most 2/3+sqrt(B)/sqrt(3-2sigma), and all polynomial moments of its pushforward vanish. The same density argument identifies f_sigma=g almost everywhere. In particular, it is the actual pickup that belongs to L2; no alternative causal representative is substituted. Parseval then proves equality of both finite values in (RM6).

If the left side is infinite, a finite right side would contradict this reverse direction. Hence (RM6) is also valid when both sides are infinite. This proves the equivalence of bounded partial moment quadratics and the literal uniform finite-window bound, with the SAME constant B.

This proof uses no critical-strip contour, reciprocal boundary growth assertion, or zero-free premise. The missing all-degree bound is still arithmetic, not granted by the existence of the individual moments.

## 5. One-third specialization and the return to the signed core

At sigma=1/3, alpha=1/6 and

\[
Q_N=\sum_{n=0}^N(4n+7/3)d_n^2.
\]

Let x=pi^(-2). Euler's even-zeta formula gives each d_n as a polynomial in x with rational coefficients. The first three are

\[
d_0=2x,\qquad d_1=-\frac73x+\frac{195}{14}x^2,
\]

\[
d_2=\frac{91}{36}x-\frac{1235}{28}x^2+\frac{16625}{88}x^3.
\]

In particular Q_0=28/(3pi^4). The general exact formula used by the code is

\[
\frac1{\zeta(2k)}=\frac{2(2k)!}{|B_{2k}|(2\pi)^{2k}},\qquad k\ge1.
\tag{RM7}
\]

This reduction to even reciprocal-zeta data is not claimed as a new general concept; reciprocal-value criteria such as Riesz/Báez-Duarte are classical. The present derivation binds a specific orthogonal metric and the exact norm to this repository's completed pickup, including the no-prior-L2 converse.

The prior written sector bounds give Z(R)<=Gamma_(1/3)(log R)+69c and Gamma_(1/3)<=8H_(1/3). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\sup_N Q_N(1/3)\le B
\ \Longrightarrow\ H_{1/3}(T)\le B\ (T\ge0),
\quad Z(R)\le8B+69c\ (R\ge1).}\tag{RM8}
\]

Conversely the previous signed-core supplier sup_J Z(2^J)<=C gives Q_N(1/3)<=123(C+123c/2) for every N. Thus (RM4)'s all-degree upper bound is equivalent to the original one-third question, not an additional prerequisite.

More generally the same m vector, with the rational metric at sigma=1/q for each integer q>=2, reaches every positive damping by monotonicity and the existing range above 1/2. This would satisfy the existing all-positive-damping consumer; no witnesses for that family are supplied here. The B constants may depend on q. No uniform-in-q bound and no zero-damping bound are imposed.

At finite degree the metric also has the correct damping direction. The variational quotient defining Q_N has denominator integral exp(-(3-2sigma)t)|sum_j v_j exp(-2jt)|^2 dt. Increasing sigma increases that denominator, so Q_N decreases. One cannot infer a smaller-damping upper bound from a larger-damping one.

## 6. Exact physical cutoffs and quantified moment errors

For R>=1, N=floor(R), and z=z_j, s=s_j, the actual truncated moment is

\[
\boxed{m_j(R)=\int_0^{\log R}e^{-zt}W(t)dt
=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^N\mu(n)n^{-s}}{zs}
-\frac{2A_N R^{-z}}z+\frac{2M_N R^{-s}}s.}\tag{RM9}
\]

Here A_N=sum_(n<=N)mu(n)/sqrt(n), and M_N is the closed Mertens prefix. Both endpoint sums survive. At an integer R=N the touching coefficient is retained in all three terms, whose changes cancel. At R=1 the entire result is zero even though the coefficient term is nonzero.

The elementary original-source envelope pays the moment tail explicitly:

\[
|m_j-m_j(R)|\le
2\left[\frac{R^{-(z-1/2)}}{z-1/2}-\frac{R^{-z}}z\right].
\tag{RM10}
\]

This is a tail estimate for these strongly damped scalar moments, NOT a target-energy tail estimate. It is valid for every real R>=1 and uses no future coefficients. An independent coefficient-truncation allowance for m_j is

\[
\frac{N^{1-s}}{(s-1)zs},
\]

obtained from sum_(n>N)n^(-s) <= integral_N^infinity x^(-s)dx. Tests compare the two organized calculations with the even-zeta values. No cutoff is inferred from a floating-point logarithm.

No prime-product representation is changed by this argument. The target is the original W and its original H, so the existing completed numerator, both transform endpoint terms, and unborn-product debt remain intact. In particular, (RM1) is not an estimate for a raw finite Euler product.

## 7. Failed shortcuts and the next arithmetic inequality

A finite moment family cannot bound the full norm. For any N, the vector e_(N+1) is orthogonal to the first N+1 h_j. Adding an arbitrarily large multiple to a matching L2 function preserves these moments and increases its squared norm. This is a counterexample to a generic inference from finite constraints, not an alternative Möbius source.

Even the natural scalar size m_j=O((j+1)^(-2)) is insufficient. Take the continuous comparison source W_*(t)=2(e^(t/2)-1). It has moments

\[
m_j^*=2\left(\frac1{2j+1}-\frac1{2j+3/2}\right)
=\frac1{(2j+1)(2j+3/2)},
\]

all explicit positive rationals with that size. Yet its one-third energy diverges because exp(-2t/3)W_*(t)^2 grows like 4exp(t/3). The same moment argument therefore forces Q_N^* to be unbounded. For exact controls one has

\[
\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{p_{n,j}}{2(j+\lambda)}
=\frac{(-1)^n}{2}\frac{(7/6-\lambda)_n}{(\lambda)_{n+1}}
\quad(\lambda=1/2,3/4),
\]

verified generally by the shifted Rodrigues formula and n integrations by parts. Individual scalar magnitude control has discarded the finite differences responsible for the needed cancellation.

The smallest remaining statement in this formulation is

\[
\boxed{\exists B\ge0\ \forall N\in\mathbb N:\quad
\sum_{n=0}^N(4n+7/3)
\left[\sum_{j=0}^n\frac{p_{n,j}}
{(2j+3/2)(2j+2)\zeta(2j+2)}\right]^2\le B.}\tag{RM11}
\]

A stronger sufficient coefficient supplier, not assumed necessary, is |d_n|<=C(n+1)^(-1-epsilon) for n>N0, epsilon>0. Since 4n+7/3<=4(n+1), it would give

\[
\sup_T H_{1/3}(T)\le Q_{N0}
+\frac{2C^2}{\epsilon}(N0+1)^{-2\epsilon}.
\tag{RM12}
\]

The critical logarithmic alternative |d_n|<=C/[(n+1)(1+log(n+1))^(1/2+epsilon)] gives tail at most (2C^2/epsilon)(1+log(N0+1))^(-2epsilon). Neither coefficient supplier was proved. The computed Q_128<1 verifies only a finite portion of the proposed bordered-matrix bound with B=1. It supplies no tail estimate and cannot be promoted to (RM11).

## 8. Reproducible finite calculation and evidence status

`reciprocal_moment_energy.py` uses exact Fraction arithmetic, rational Jacobi coefficients, rational Bernoulli recurrence, and outward dyadic intervals. The Machin formula pi=16atan(1/5)-4atan(1/239) is evaluated using signed alternating-series remainders. Its branch is checked by tan(4atan(1/5))=120/119 and tan(4atan(1/5)-atan(1/239))=1, with that angle in (0,pi/2). This is classical elementary trigonometry, not a call to an unverified pi constant.

Work precision includes 12(N+1)+64 guard bits beyond requested output precision. This heuristic allocation is not silently trusted: the final coefficient and partial-energy interval widths must satisfy the explicit requested budget or the calculation raises an error. No small computed coefficient is declared zero because of cancellation. The norm uses complete signed sums before squaring.

The degree-128 calculation uses zeta(2), zeta(4), ..., zeta(258), with 96-bit outward output and 1708 internal work bits. Each displayed partial energy interval has width 2^(-96). The numerical partial energies are approximately Q_0=0.095815834377053795, Q_8=0.214182026772796460, Q_32=0.286869559459135045, Q_64=0.31177865597617854, Q_128=0.3287696715670429. They are finite LOWER approximations to the full norm, not upper estimates for it. N=128 denotes polynomial degree, not a physical cutoff or time horizon.

Twenty-five tests passed in normal and optimized Python. They include exact orthogonality for degrees 0..10 at five dampings, independent rational Gaussian elimination and Cauchy determinants, actual reciprocal Möbius series and positive zeta-series checks, integer/noninteger finite cutoffs, both endpoint corrections, exact moment-invisible modes, a divergent smooth comparison source, and rejection at inadequate precision.
