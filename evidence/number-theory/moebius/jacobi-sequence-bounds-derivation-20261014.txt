## 1. Statements, with the missing conclusion distinguished

For alpha=1/6 let P_n(y)=P_n^(0,1/6)(2y-1)=sum_j p[n,j] y^j and

  m_j = 1 / ((2j+3/2)(2j+2) zeta(2j+2)),
  d_n = sum_(j=0..n) p[n,j] m_j,
  Q_N = sum_(n=0..N) (4n+7/3) d_n^2.

The following written bounds hold:

  d_0 = 2/pi^2;
  |d_n| <= 16 n^(-5/6)                         (n>=1).          (AS1)

Assuming the explicitly named arithmetic premise

  MertensLogSquareBound:
  |M(x)| <= 3x/(log x)^2 for every real x>1,

the stronger all-degree bound is

  |d_n| <= 6400 n^(-5/6)/(1+log n)^2            (n>=1).          (AS2)

Theorem A.1(A.5) of Johnston--Leong--Tudzi, arXiv:2408.04143v2,
provides this premise as classical published mathematics, with the smaller
constant 2.91890. The theorem is not a native Lean supplier here. Thus AS1
requires only the literal source and |mu(n)|<=1; the formal version of AS2
must retain the premise until its supplier is admitted.

The constants are deliberately nonoptimal. In particular AS2 does not
assert a uniform tail bound in the requested norm: its squared majorant
has order n^(-2/3)/(1+log n)^4 after multiplication by 4n+7/3, and that
series diverges. Failure of this majorant is not divergence of actual Q_N.

There is also an all-degree quadratic comparison. If 1/3<=sigma<=1/2
and H_sigma(T)<=B_sigma for all T>=0, then

  Q_N <= (97/25) B_sigma (N+1)^(2sigma-2/3)
      <= 4 B_sigma (N+1)^(2sigma-2/3).                         (AS3)

Using the written Mertens-log boundary return B_(1/2)=16 gives

  Q_N <= 64 (N+1)^(1/3), for every N>=0.                     (AS4)

This is a bound for every degree, but its constant is not independent of
N. AS3 at sigma=1/3 would require the unproved target bound as input and
cannot be used to supply itself.

## 2. Actual-source Jacobi integration by parts

Keep the literal closed prefix M(x)=sum_(1<=k<=floor(x)) mu(k),
V(x)=W(log x)=integral_1^x M(v)v^(-3/2)dv. Put

  g(y)=y^(-5/12)V(y^(-1/2)),  0<y<=1,
  p(y)=y^(7/6)(1-y),  lambda_n=n(n+7/6).

The exact moment substitution y=exp(-2t) gives

  d_n = (1/2) integral_0^1 y^(1/6) g(y) P_n(y)dy.             (AS5)

It is the full signed d_n that is paired with the polynomial. The source
is locally absolutely continuous. Differentiating V, but NOT the step
function M, gives almost everywhere

  g'(y)=-(5/12)y^(-17/12)V(y^(-1/2))
        -(1/2)y^(-7/6)M(y^(-1/2)).                          (AS6)

The elementary bounds |M(x)|<=x, |V(x)|<=2(sqrt(x)-1) imply

  |g(y)|<=2y^(-2/3),   |g'(y)|<=(4/3)y^(-5/3).              (AS7)

The shifted Jacobi equation is

  -(p P_n')'=lambda_n y^(1/6) P_n.

For n>=1, integration by parts gives the exact full-source identity

  d_n=(1/(2lambda_n)) integral_0^1 p(y)g'(y)P_n'(y)dy.         (AS8)

This is first done on [epsilon,1]. At zero, the boundary term is
O_n(y^(1/2)); at one the factor 1-y vanishes. The remaining integrand is
O_n(y^(-1/2)) near zero, so passage to the limit is absolutely justified.
Integer arrival points produce no additional terms: V is continuous and
locally absolutely continuous, and M has not been differentiated. Every
historical prefix is retained in AS6. No target L2 premise is used.

In the physical coordinate this is equivalently

  d_n = -1/(2lambda_n) integral_1^infinity (1-x^(-2))
        [(5/6)x^(-5/2)V(x)+x^(-3)M(x)] P_n'(x^(-2)) dx.      (AS9)

The two signed terms remain together before estimates. AS9 is an improper
scalar coefficient integral whose absolute convergence follows from AS7
for each fixed n; it is not the unproved energy-tail integral.

## 3. Explicit Jacobi bounds, including endpoints

Write b_n=(7/6)_n/n!. The classical endpoint and Bernstein-type Jacobi
inequalities (NIST DLMF 18.14.2 and 18.14.3) imply

  |P_n(y)|<=b_n,
  b_n<=(6/5)n^(1/6)                                      (n>=1),
  y^(1/3)(1-y)^(1/4)|P_n(y)|<=(6/5)n^(-1/2)               (n>=1).
                                                                  (AS10)

For the middle inequality, log b_n<=H_n/6<=(1+log n)/6 and
exp(1/6)<=6/5. In the last inequality the DLMF constant is
Gamma(n+7/6)/(sqrt(pi)n!(n+7/12)^(2/3)); use Gamma(7/6)<=1,
which follows by concavity of t^(1/6) under the exponential probability
measure, and pi>1. All parameter domains of the cited inequalities hold.

The derivative formula and the endpoint bound for parameters (1,7/6) give

  |P_n'(y)|<=(6/7)lambda_n b_n  for 0<=y<=1.                (AS11)

An interior estimate needed below is

  |P_n'(y)|<=7 sqrt(n)y^(-5/6)(1-y)^(-3/4)
  for n>=2 and n^(-2)<=y<=1-n^(-2).                        (AS12)

Here is a proof of AS12, avoiding an unstated derivative asymptotic.
Set y=sin(theta/2)^2 and

  u(theta)=y^(1/3)(1-y)^(1/4)P_n(y).

Direct substitution in the Jacobi equation gives

  u''+q(theta)u=0,
  q(theta)=(n+7/12)^2+1/(18y)+1/(16(1-y)).                 (AS13)

AS10 bounds |u| by U=(6/5)n^(-1/2). For the specified y, both
sin(theta/2) and cos(theta/2) are at least 1/n. On the interval
[theta,theta+1/(2n)], each is at least three quarters of its original
value, using the Lipschitz constant one of sin and cos. Thus on that
interval

  q <= [(19/12)^2+8/81+1/9]n^2=(3521/1296)n^2<3n^2.

The interval lies strictly inside (0,pi). Taylor's formula with integral
remainder, d=1/(2n), yields

  |u'(theta)| <= 2U/d+(d/2)*3n^2 U
               =(19/4)nU=(57/10)sqrt(n).

Differentiate P_n=u*y^(-1/3)(1-y)^(-1/4). The u' term has bound
(57/10)sqrt(n)y^(-5/6)(1-y)^(-3/4). The two weight-derivative terms add
at most (2/5+3/10) times the same majorant, because y and 1-y are at least
n^(-2). The total constant is 32/5<7. This proves AS12.

## 4. Proof of the unconditional full-sequence bound

For n>=2 let delta=n^(-2), and split AS8 at delta and 1-delta.
From AS7 and AS11, the initial part is at most

  (4/7)b_n integral_0^delta y^(-1/2)(1-y)dy
  <=(8/7)b_n/n <=(48/35)n^(-5/6).

From AS7 and AS12, the middle part is at most

  (14sqrt(n)/(3lambda_n))
       integral_delta^(1-delta) y^(-4/3)(1-y)^(1/4)dy
  <=14n^(-5/6).

The last part, using (1-delta)^(-1/2)<2, is at most

  (4/7)b_n delta^2 <=(24/35)n^(-23/6)
                    <=(3/35)n^(-5/6).

Their sum is bounded by (541/35)n^(-5/6)<16n^(-5/6).
For n=1, AS5 and the unconditional scalar variation bound
integral_0^infinity exp(-3t/2)|W(t)|dt<=2/3 give
|d_1|<=(7/6)*(2/3)=7/9<16. This proves AS1 at EVERY degree n>=1.
No individual |m_j| has been substituted into sum_j |p[n,j]m_j|.

One consequence, without any Mertens decay premise, is

  Q_N <= 1+5376 N^(1/3)  for every integer N>=1.             (AS14)

Indeed Q_0=28/(3pi^4)<1, 4n+7/3<=7n, and
sum_(n=1..N)n^(-2/3)<=3N^(1/3). This still grows with degree.

## 5. All-degree logarithmic refinement for the actual Mobius source

Assume MertensLogSquareBound. Combining it with |M(x)|<=x gives globally

  |M(x)|<=12x/(1+log x)^2,  x>=1.                          (AS15)

For log x<=1 use the elementary bound; for log x>=1 use
3/(log x)^2<=12/(1+log x)^2. There is no missing compact range.
Writing x=exp(t), the inequality
(1+t)/(1+t-v)<=1+v for 0<=v<=t gives

  |V(exp(t))| <=12exp(t/2)/(1+t)^2
                *integral_0^infinity exp(-v/2)(1+v)^2dv
              =312exp(t/2)/(1+t)^2.                       (AS16)

The evaluated integral is 26. AS6 now yields for every 0<y<=1

  |g'(y)|<=544y^(-5/3)/(1+log(1/y))^2.                    (AS17)

For n>=2 split AS8 into [0,n^(-2)], [n^(-2),n^(-1)],
[n^(-1),1-n^(-2)], and [1-n^(-2),1]. On the first two parts,
1+log(1/y)>=1+log n. The calculations in Section 4, with 4/3 replaced
by 544, bound their combined modulus by

  [36*544/35+21*544/2] n^(-5/6)/(1+log n)^2.

Use only the elementary AS7 on the last two parts. Their total is at most
15n^(-7/6). The elementary maximum calculation

  sup_(t>=0) (1+t)^2 exp(-t/3)=36exp(-5/3)<7

implies n^(-7/6)<=7n^(-5/6)/(1+log n)^2. The strict constant follows,
for example, from sum_(k=0..5)(5/3)^k/k!=7663/1458>36/7.
Consequently the combined constant is

  36*544/35+21*544/2+105 =223179/35 <6400.

The n=1 control above covers the remaining degree. This proves AS2 for
EVERY n>=1, with the same constant and no asymptotic onset.

AS2 does establish a weaker infinite norm, for example

  sum_(n>=1) n^(-1/3)(4n+7/3)d_n^2 <=(28/3)*6400^2.         (AS18)

Use sum_(n>=1)1/[n(1+log n)^4]<=1+1/3. The extra n^(-1/3) is
essential to this proof. It cannot be dropped from the claimed conclusion.

## 6. A summable part of the full coefficient sequence

For n>=2 split AS8 as d_n=a_n+r_n at y=1/n, with a_n the integral over
(0,1/n) and r_n over [1/n,1]. The previous calculation proves

  |r_n|<=15n^(-7/6),
  sum_(n>=2)(4n+7/3)|r_n|^2<=4725.                        (AS19)

The first assertion is a direct bound for the complete signed source
integral over the latter region; it is not obtained by setting any moments
to zero. In physical coordinates a_n uses x>=sqrt(n), and r_n uses
1<=x<=sqrt(n). The squared-sum constant follows from
7*225*sum_(n>=2)n^(-4/3)<=7*225*3.

Thus an infinite portion of the coefficient calculation has a summable
budget. The other contribution a_n is retained. No weighted l2 bound for
that remaining full sequence has been obtained. The split alone supplies
no estimate on its cancellation with r_n and no uniform Q_N bound.

## 7. Finite polynomial comparison, valid for every degree

Let 1/3<=sigma<=1/2, delta=sigma-1/3, alpha=1/6 and beta=alpha-delta.
For every polynomial R of degree at most N the following inequality holds:

  integral_0^1 y^beta R(y)^2dy
  <=(97/25)(N+1)^(2delta) integral_0^1 y^alpha R(y)^2dy.     (AS20)

Proof: Expand in the orthogonal P_k. Cauchy--Schwarz and AS10 give

  |R(y)|^2<= ||R||_alpha^2
       *sum_(k=0..N)(2k+7/6)P_k(y)^2
    <= (72/25)(N+1)^(7/3)||R||_alpha^2.

The same upper bound on P_k holds at k=0 if n^(1/6) is replaced by
(k+1)^(1/6). Put epsilon=(N+1)^(-2). On [epsilon,1], y^(-delta)
is at most (N+1)^(2delta). On [0,epsilon] integrate the last displayed
constant times y^beta. Since beta>=0, its contribution is at most
(72/25)(N+1)^(2delta)||R||_alpha^2. Addition proves AS20.
The case N=0 is included; the second interval is then empty.

If the actual f_sigma=exp(-sigma*t)W(t) has L2 norm squared B_sigma,
Cauchy--Schwarz for sum_j v_j*m_j has test-function norm
(1/2)integral y^beta R^2. The variational formula for Q_N has denominator
(1/2)integral y^alpha R^2. The two factors 1/2 cancel, and AS20 proves AS3.

Under MertensLogSquareBound the finite Volterra proof gives H_(1/2)<=16:
the damped source e^(-u)M(exp u) has squared integral at most
1+9*integral_1^infinity u^(-4)du=4, and the Volterra factor is 4.
This gives AS4. No new native admission of that premise is asserted.

If one instead uses the earlier written conditional zero-free transfer,
AS3 gives Q_N<=4B_sigma(N+1)^(2sigma-2/3) for every sigma>3/8 in that
transfer's domain, retaining its complete B_sigma and zero-free hypothesis.
It yields exponents arbitrarily close to 1/12 from above, NOT zero.
Neither letting sigma tend to 3/8 nor letting the degree increase removes
that positive exponent or supplies a uniform bound.

## 8. A critical-rate control: O(1/n) still does not suffice

For the comparison source W_*(t)=exp(t/3)-1 (NOT the Mobius source), the
same complete Jacobi transform has the exact coefficients

  d_n^*=(-1)^n/2 * [1/(n+7/12)-s_n],
  s_n=(5/12)_n/(3/4)_(n+1).                               (AS21)

This follows from Rodrigues and finite integration by parts:

  integral_0^1 y^(lambda-1)P_n(y)dy
    =(-1)^n(7/6-lambda)_n/(lambda)_(n+1), lambda>0.

Use lambda=7/12 and 3/4. All moments of W_* are positive rational numbers
1/(2j+7/6)-1/(2j+3/2), individually of order j^(-2).
The exact recurrence for s_n implies the finite telescope

  sum_(n=0..N)s_n=3[1-(N+7/4)s_(N+1)]<=3.                 (AS22)

Also s_n<=3(n+1)^(-4/3). One elementary proof writes
s_n=(4/3)product_(k=0..n-1)(1-(4/3)/(k+7/4)), uses log(1-u)<=-u and
the decreasing-integral comparison. The resulting constant
(4/3)(7/4)^(4/3) is less than 3 since 2401/108<27.

With H_N^*=sum_(n=0..N)1/(n+7/12), the complete squared sum therefore
satisfies the ALL-N bounds

  H_N^*-6 <= Q_N^* <= H_N^*+45/2.                          (AS23)

Indeed its exact summand is 1/(n+7/12)-2s_n+(n+7/12)s_n^2. The negative
correction sums to at most 6, while the positive correction sums to at most
9*sum_(n>=0)(n+1)^(-5/3)<=45/2.
In particular Q_N^* diverges logarithmically, despite d_n^*=O(1/n).
This refutes an inference from a critical individual coefficient bound,
not the actual Mobius target. No numerical zero or asymptotic extrapolation
is used for the divergence.

## 9. Remaining target and evidence status

The required sum is still sum_(n>=0)(4n+7/3)d_n^2, without an extra
negative power of n. AS1, AS2, AS3 and AS19 do not bound it. They are
genuine all-degree statements with written proofs, but not the requested
uniform constant. A sufficient improvement would be the actual signed
bound |d_n|<=C/[n(1+log n)^(1/2+epsilon)] with epsilon>0; the weaker exact
quadratic-summability target remains primary. Neither is proved here.

The companion Python test checks finite rational Jacobi differential and
beta identities, exact scalar constants, and the critical-rate control.
