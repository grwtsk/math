## 1. Main mathematical statements

Keep the actual closed Mertens prefix, pickup, and completed transform:

 M(x)=sum_(1<=k<=floor(x)) mu(k),
 V(x)=W(log x)=integral_1^x M(u)u^(-3/2)du,
 d_n=sum_(j=0..n) p_(n,j)/[(2j+3/2)(2j+2)zeta(2j+2)],

Here P_n(y)=P_n^(0,1/6)(2y-1)=sum_j p_(n,j)y^j has the previously fixed
Jacobi normalization. The earlier written source-identification theorem
states sup_T H_(1/3)(T)=sup_N Q_N in the extended nonnegative reals.
Its reverse direction does not assume target L2 membership. We reuse that
proof at its written grade, not as a new kernel admission.

For each integer n>=2 set

 U_n=ceil(n^(3/5)),            H_n=U_n^2.                      (NR1)

Ceiling is implemented as the unique integer U with
 (U-1)^5 < n^3 <= U^5.
This U is degree-specific, not the old frozen cubic-shell cutoff.
Let K_(n,k) be the UNCHANGED finite Bessel atom functional from CP3/EI1.
It retains its full prefactor, parity, frequency n+7/12, endpoints and both
terms from the actual source derivative. Define

 ell_n=sum_(a,b<=U_n) mu(a)mu(b)
              sum_(r>=1; U_n<a*b*r<=U_n^2) K_(n,a*b*r).       (NR2)

All a,b and r are positive integers; ordered pairs and repeated products
are counted. The inner kernel is zero outside its original atom support.
The exact coefficient identity is

 ell_n = -sum_(U_n<k<=U_n^2) mu(k)K_(n,k).                  (NR3)

The main all-degree conclusion is

 ||(d_n+ell_n)_(n>=2)||_(ell2(w_n)) <= C_* < 1936,            (NR4)

 C_*=(456/5)sqrt(105)+30sqrt(35)
                   +sqrt(4725)+sqrt(420000)+sqrt(10500).

In particular, the comparison requires NEITHER MertensLogSquareBound nor
a zero-free premise. It does not remove those premises from unrelated old

For every integer N>=1 the difference tail has the explicit allowance

 C_*(N)=(456/5)sqrt(105)N^(-1/30)+30sqrt(35)N^(-1/10)

## 2. What is adapted from signed covariance, and what is not

For an invertible real matrix A, positive amplitudes u_i>=u_*>0, and a
residual r, put delta_i=-(A^-1 r)_i/(2u_i). Exact expansion gives

 A(2u odot delta)=-r,
 r+A[(u+delta)^(odot2)-u^(odot2)]=A delta^(odot2).            (NR6)

The entire correction square is retained. In Euclidean norm it is at most
 ||A|| ||A^-1||^2 ||r||^2/(4u_*^2).
The executable has a rational squared budget using Frobenius norms:
 ||A delta^(odot2)||^2 <= ||A||_F^2 ||A^-1||_F^4
                                      ||r||^4/(16u_*^4).
It rejects singular matrices and nonpositive amplitudes. Tests explicitly
exhibit amplification rather than contraction when u_* is too small.
Thus the matrix solve alone is NOT assigned to the Möbius source.

The source-preserving arithmetic realization instead works in the divisor
convolution ring. Write epsilon for its identity, 1 for the constant-one
arithmetic function, a=mu restricted to 1..U, and eta=a*1-epsilon.
The Newton inverse update is

 a_new=a*(2epsilon-a*1)=a-a*eta.

Using commutativity and associativity, but no norms, proves

 a_new*1-epsilon=-eta^2,
 mu-a_new=mu*eta^2.                                        (NR7)

The second identity uses the ACTUAL inverse relation mu*1=epsilon. Since
eta(k)=0 at k<=U, its square vanishes at k<(U+1)^2. Therefore

 mu(k)=2a(k)-(a*a*1)(k),       k<(U+1)^2.                  (NR8)

For U<k<=U^2 the term a(k) is zero, yielding (NR3).
This is the classical order-two Möbius/Vaughan-type identity in a Newton
form, not a new divisor identity. Huxley--Watt (1.6) provides the general
order formulation; the finite proof above does not need an analytic import.

The gate is strict. For U=1, k=4, the first update is -1 while mu(4)=0.
The exact residual mu*eta^2 equals 1 at four. A touching zero response must
not be used to certify the wrong coefficient. Nor does nilpotence at a
finite cutoff establish a norm contraction. Both facts are tested.

We use the update only on its proven range U<k<=U^2. All omitted physical
source is paid by (NR12), not changed by a chosen amplitude. In particular
we do not assign a Newton residual-squaring rate to the target norm.

## 3. Regular Volterra inversion bounds the small-argument kernel

For real c>-1 and continuous f on [0,Y], use the regular weighted mean

 W_c[f](y)=integral_0^1 t^c f(ty)dt,
 V_c[f](y)=y W_c[f](y).

These definitions include y=0 without dividing by y. Dominated convergence
on the fixed interval gives continuity, and
 |W_c[f](y)|<=sup_(0<=s<=y)|f(s)|/(c+1).
Also V_c[f](0)=0 and V_c[f]'(0)=f(0)/(c+1); for y>0 its integrating-factor
formula yields V_c[f]'+(c/y)V_c[f]=f. The written real-exponent form is
needed at c=1/6. The inspected upstream Lean radial inverse has c:Nat, so
we do not claim to instantiate it directly at 1/6. The Python polynomial
specialization computes exact rational coefficients f_j/(c+j+1) and tests
the equation at the axis and away from it.

Use the ACTUAL Bessel reference from BE3:

 B_n(y)=(-1)^n b_n A(y)^(2/3)(1-y)^(-1/4)
                       F_(1/6)(2nu_n sqrt(y) A(y)),
 A(y)=asin(sqrt(y))/sqrt(y), A(0)=1,
 b_n=(7/6)_n/n!,   nu_n=n+7/12,   lambda_n=n(n+7/6).

F is the normalized even entire Bessel function with F(0)=1. DLMF 10.9.4
provides its real cosine-probability representation, so |F(z)|<=1 on the
real axis. A(y)=integral_0^1 (1-y t^2)^(-1/2)dt is analytic for |y|<1.
Thus on 0<=y<=1/4,

 1<=A(y)<=(1-y)^(-1/2),
 |B_n(y)|<=b_n(1-y)^(-7/12)<=(4/3)b_n.

The exact differential equation already derived for this Bessel reference
is

 (y^(7/6)(1-y)B_n'(y))'=-(lambda_n-r(y))y^(1/6)B_n(y),
 r(y)=(1-A(y)^(-2))/(18y)+1/[16(1-y)],
 r(0)=35/432.                                             (NR9)

This is not the unperturbed Jacobi equation: r(y) is retained.
The bounds on A show 0<=1-A(y)^(-2)<=y, so
 0<=r(y)<=1/18+1/12=5/36 on [0,1/4].
Since lambda_n>=19/3 for n>=2, |lambda_n-r|<=lambda_n there.
The analytic branch has bounded derivative at zero, whence its integrating
constant y^(7/6)(1-y)B_n' tends to zero. Integrating NR9 and scaling gives

 B_n'(y)=-(1-y)^(-1)
             W_(1/6)[(lambda_n-r)B_n](y).                 (NR10)

It remains valid at zero by continuity. In particular B_n'(0) is
 -(6/7)(lambda_n-r(0))B_n(0), not a zero or an arbitrarily selected branch.
Applying the regular weighted-mean norm, |B_n|<=(4/3)b_n and
(1-y)^(-1)<=4/3 yields the all-n estimate

 |B_n'(y)| <= (32/21)lambda_n b_n,
            n>=2, 0<=y<=n^(-2).                           (NR11)

This is the useful Volterra adaptation: no large-argument asymptotic,
unknown compact maximum, or changed Bessel frequency is used. Exact Taylor
coefficients from the existing integral evaluator satisfy NR10 as an
independent coefficient-by-coefficient diagnostic.

## 4. High source indices have a summable remainder after the right cutoff

The original atom is
 K_(n,k)=(2lambda_n)^(-1) integral_(n^-4)^min(1/n,k^-2)
     (1-y)[1/3-5y^-1/4/(6sqrt(k))] B_n'(y)dy,
with zero for an empty or degenerate interval. For k>=n and k<=n^2, its
whole interval lies in [0,n^-2]. On y<=k^-2,
 5y^-1/4/(6sqrt(k))-1/3 >=1/2.
Consequently, integrating an explicit positive scalar majorant gives

 |K_(n,k)| <= [(32/21)b_n/2]
       integral_0^(k^-2) [5y^-1/4/(6sqrt(k))-1/3]dy
     = (16/27)b_n/k^2 <= b_n/k^2.                         (NR12)

We used 0<=1-y<=1 only after identifying the sign of the scalar bracket.
The two scalar source terms were NOT separately dropped. The equality in
the majorant is (10/9-1/3)k^-2=(7/9)k^-2. It introduces neither future
coefficients nor a change to the physical atom cutoff.

For any integer H with n<=H<=n^2, define the actual high-source tail
 h_(n,H)=sum_(H<k<=n^2) mu(k)K_(n,k).
Using |mu|<=1, b_n<=(6/5)n^(1/6), and sum_(k>H)k^-2<=1/H gives

 |h_(n,H)|<=b_n/H.                                       (NR13)

This is a scalar source-coefficient tail, not a tail of the target norm.
For H=U_n^2 its decay becomes adequate across degrees. Notice that the
weighted Dirichlet exponent two comes from the proved atom bound NR12.
We do NOT move a zeta contour or invoke reciprocal-zeta growth at 5/6.

## 5. Balance prefix and residual costs rather than demand either vanish

First use the earlier written truncated-source Jacobi estimate PD8:
for every source |c_k|<=1 supported on 1..U and 2<=U<=n,

 |pJ_(n,U)| <=36|sum_(k<=U)c_k/sqrt(k)|n^(-4/3)
                         +22U^(2/3)n^(-3/2).             (NR14)

This is a statement about a FULL signed Jacobi combination. Its proof
splits the exact source-derivative integral at n^-2, U^-2, and 1-n^-2.
In the fully active interval the derivative equals
 -(5/6)A_U y^-17/12+(1/3)M_U y^-7/6.
The first two interval costs in |A_U| are (4/7)+35<36; all other costs
are 6/35+7+14+24/35<22 after using |M_U|<=U and U<=n. The boundary terms
vanish. The interior derivative estimate is the previous explicit Jacobi
ODE estimate, not an assumed smoothness theorem for M. That proof and its
classical Jacobi inputs remain at written grade. It uses no ML premise.

We now use only |A_U|<=sum_(k<=U)k^-1/2<=2sqrt(U), not logarithmic Möbius
cancellation. If U behaves like n^theta and the high cutoff is U^2, the
three coefficient exponents beyond n^-1 are

 delta_short=1/3-theta/2,
 delta_bulk=1/2-2theta/3,
 delta_high=2theta-7/6.

Both leading errors become summable when 7/12<theta<2/3. Balancing the
short and high exponents gives theta=3/5 and delta=1/30. The bulk exponent
is then 1/10. This is an approximation/cutoff balance; it is not a new
cancellation exponent for the full Möbius source or for ell_n.

All floors and small n are paid as follows. Let x=n^(3/5), U=ceil(x).
For n>=2, x>3/2 since 2^3>(3/2)^5. If U=2 then U<(3/2)x. If U>=3,
x>U-1>=(2/3)U, so again U<=(3/2)x. Since n is an integer and x<=n,
U<=n. Also U>=x>=sqrt(n) and U>=2. Hence

 n<=U^2<=n^2,
 sqrt(U)<=(5/4)n^(3/10),
 U^(2/3)<=(4/3)n^(2/5),
 U^2>=n^(6/5).

The rational comparisons behind the last two constants are
 3/2<25/16, and (3/2)^2<(4/3)^3.
Thus for the complete Jacobi coefficient p_n of the short actual source,

 |p_n|<=90n^(-31/30)+30n^(-11/10),                         (NR15)

and the actual high-source coefficient h_n=h_(n,U_n^2) satisfies

 |h_n|<=(6/5)n^(-31/30).                                 (NR16)

Neither statement requires MertensLogSquareBound. They are proved for
every n>=2, not by finite regression or an unspecified eventual onset.

## 6. Return to the actual full source and sum only the paid errors

Apply the previous Jacobi--Bessel comparison to the degreewise LONG source
 mu(k)1_(k>U_n), whose coefficients are bounded by one. Its full Jacobi
coefficient is d_n-p_n; its finite Bessel coefficient is the actual sum
 over U_n<k<=n^2. By NR3 that finite sum is -ell_n+h_n.
Thus, with no alteration of mu,

 d_n+ell_n=p_n+h_n+e_n,
 |e_n|<=15n^(-7/6)+200n^(-4/3)+50n^(-11/6).               (NR17)

This comparison is applied once to each long source; there is no double
charge for the short/long transformation. The source may change with n
because the uniform pointwise estimate is used at each degree before
summing. It is not asserted that p_n is the coefficient sequence of one
fixed truncation independent of n.

From NR15--17,

 |d_n+ell_n| <= (456/5)n^(-31/30)+30n^(-11/10)
                    +15n^(-7/6)+200n^(-4/3)+50n^(-11/6).

For finite degree sums use Minkowski, w_n<=7n, and
 sum_(n>N)n^(-1-delta)<=N^(-delta)/delta, delta>0.
The first two squared budgets have constants 105 and 35; the last three
are the existing 4725, 420000, 10500. This proves NR4 and NR5 after taking
the monotone supremum of the finite error sums. No target L2 assumption
is used to pass to that supremum.

For the explicit final constant,
 sqrt(105)<41/4, sqrt(35)<6,
 sqrt(4725)<69, sqrt(420000)<649, sqrt(10500)<103,
so C_*<(456/5)(41/4)+180+69+649+103=9679/5<1936.

This adapts the support-local summation principle without importing the
fluid proof's unverified-for-this-source smoothness and all-orders residual
hypotheses. Only the explicitly proved powers in NR17 are summed.

## 7. Exact remaining target and quantitative consumers

Set J_N=sum_(n=2..N)w_n ell_n^2, with the empty sum zero. For every N>=1,

 |sqrt(Q_N-Q_1)-sqrt(J_N)|<=C_*<1936.                    (NR18)

Therefore sup_N J_N<infinity is equivalent to the original sup_N Q_N<infinity,
WITHOUT ML. A witness J_N<=B for all N>=1 would give

 H_(1/3)(T)<=Q_1+(sqrt(B)+C_*)^2
                  <1+(sqrt(B)+1936)^2, every real T>=0.   (NR19)

Q_1<1 is the already evaluated exact even-zeta bound. It can also be checked
from d_0=2/pi^2, d_1=-(7/3)pi^-2+(195/14)pi^-4 and pi>3, giving
Q_1<=1183/2187<1. No numerical infinite-n extrapolation is used.
The original core consequently has
 Z(R)<=8[1+(sqrt(B)+1936)^2]+69c for all R>=1,
with the unchanged localized-atom constant c. These are conditional
consumers. NO witness B is supplied by this adaptation.

The remaining sufficient arithmetic theorem can be stated directly as
 sum_(n>=2)(4n+7/3)|sum_(a,b<=U_n)mu(a)mu(b)
                   sum_(r; U_n<abr<=U_n^2)K_(n,abr)|^2 < infinity.
All ordered cross interactions inside the finite bilinear sum survive.
No positive/negative Möbius splitting, independent packet budget, or
P04 premise is imposed. A finite number of intervals or successful Newton
coefficient updates cannot establish this infinite arithmetic bound.

The earlier conditional prefix constant 78200 and cubic-shell formulation
remain valid recorded alternatives. We have not proved the earlier ML
supplier or altered its native status. The new target uses a different
cutoff and a different remainder; 1936 is not a retroactive claim for the
old d-v sequence. This is essential when propagating the new bound.

corrections with matrix-condition costs, regular Volterra polynomial
primitives for c>-1, divisor-convolution Newton updates, and explicit finite
budget composition. The matrix correction is not pretended to be an
arithmetic source map. The arithmetic map is NR7--8.
