1. Research result and the distinction from the preceding quartic work

Nathanson [N19], Lemmas 1-2 and Theorem 1, proves Hermite-Sylvester by
Lagrange interpolation. [N20] extends the same mechanism to every positive
even power. These are published mathematical inputs, not claimed new results.
The pinned Mathlib Lagrange module supplies interpolation and its evaluation,
degree and uniqueness theorems. No new interpolation axiom is needed.

The repository already has a single-orbit quadratic witness and a height-uniform
slope variant. Repeating their algebra would not advance the source issue.
Here the useful step is finite DIVISOR isolation: other roots can conceal the
negative value of a fixed test. An interpolating test annihilates every other
point in the selected finite support before taking a signed trace.

This is finite polynomial interpolation, not analytic isolation of an orbit
from the infinite zeta zero divisor. It supplies one reusable part of Q4, not
Q3's source positivity and not the remaining analytic tail/admissibility proof.

2. Exact finite selector, including all multiplicities

Let Lambda be a finite distinct support, closed under complex conjugation,
with r=|Lambda|. Attach a positive mass m(z) to every node; for an actual divisor
of a real analytic function these are analytic multiplicities and are equal
on conjugate nodes. Do not replace these weights by one when deduplicating.
Let z0 in Lambda be nonreal. Define the Lagrange basis

 L_z(X) = product_(u in Lambda, u!=z) (X-u)/(z-u).

For ANY complex eta define

 q_eta(X) = eta L_z0(X) + conjugate(eta) L_conjugate(z0)(X).       (FI1)

This polynomial has degree <r and evaluates to eta at z0,
conjugate(eta) at conjugate(z0), and zero at every other distinct node.
Its conjugate-coefficient polynomial has the same values at all r nodes;
uniqueness of degree-<r interpolation proves its coefficients real.
No imaginary coefficient is rounded or projected away.

For k>0 the complete weighted trace satisfies

 sum_(z in Lambda) m(z) Re(q_eta(z)^k)
   = [m(z0)+m(conjugate(z0))] Re(eta^k).                         (FI2)

The formula needs only positive weights and conjugation-stable SUPPORT.
When weights also respect conjugation, the unsimplified complex sum is real.
The exact-rational producer uses this latter, actual-real-divisor profile.
For k=2 choose eta=i, giving -[m(z0)+m(conjugate(z0))].
For k=4 choose eta=1+i, giving -4[m(z0)+m(conjugate(z0))].

The test must change with the power. Retaining eta=i and merely replacing a
square by a fourth power gives POSITIVE pair mass, because i^4=1. The fourth
power version is not positivity by taking |q|^4; it is the signed q^4 sum.

It follows for k=2 and, separately, k=4 that

 (every coefficient-real polynomial q of degree <r has nonnegative trace)
       iff (every node of Lambda is real).                       (FI3)

Proof of the reverse implication is positivity of real even powers. For the
forward implication a nonreal node would supply FI1-FI2 with a strictly
negative trace. The empty support is harmless; no nonempty assumption is
needed in the formal iff. The finite numerical producer rejects empty input
because its task is to PRODUCE a nonreal-pair witness.

An indexed list with coinciding symmetry slots is represented by its distinct
support together with its original counts. The t=0 orbit [ia,-ia,ia,-ia]
has pair mass four; FI2 at k=2 gives -4, not -2. On the critical axis only real
nodes remain and the trace is nonnegative, possibly singular. No claim about
actual zeta multiplicities follows from a repeated formal slot.

3. Complete moment contraction and conditioning

If q(X)=sum_(j=0..r-1)c_j X^j and S_l=sum_z m(z)z^l, then

 T_2(q)=sum_(i,j=0..r-1)c_i c_j S_(i+j).                         (FI4)

Let the corresponding true real moments and proposed moments satisfy
|S_l-Shat_l|<=epsilon_l through 2r-2. Then

 |T_2(q)-That_2(q)| <=sum_(i,j)|c_i c_j|epsilon_(i+j)
                    <=epsilon (sum_j|c_j|)^2                    (FI5)

when epsilon is a uniform error. More sharply, combine repeated monomials
first: if q^k=sum_l d_l X^l, the exact budget is

 |T_k(q)-That_k(q)| <=sum_l |d_l|epsilon_l
                    <=epsilon (sum_j|c_j|)^k.                   (FI6)

These inequalities are ordinary finite triangle inequalities and polynomial
coefficient convolution. The candidate MomentPerturbation module proves the
quadratic finite-sum version and its strict-negative consumer. The executable
also implements FI6 for k=4; no claim of a native fourth-power error theorem
is made.

One complete conditioning allowance is available. Assume |z|<=R for every
node and |z-u|>=d>0 for distinct nodes. The coefficient l1 norm of each
(X-u) is <=1+R. Submultiplicativity and the denominator product give

 ||L_z||_(coeff,1) <= ((1+R)/d)^(r-1),
 ||q_eta||_(coeff,1) <=2|eta|((1+R)/d)^(r-1).                   (FI7)

This norm is on polynomial coefficients, not the value at one root. For exact
Gaussian rational inputs the implementation bounds Euclidean radius above by
max(|Re z|+|Im z|), separation below by min max(|Re(z-u)|,|Im(z-u)|), and
|eta| above by |Re eta|+|Im eta|. Thus every factor in its allowance is rational.
The exact coefficient norm is often much smaller and is retained separately.
FI7 is a written bound with finite checks; not a native Lean theorem here.

Consequently normalized witnesses can lose their negative margin as roots
approach one another. For k=2, after division by coefficient norm squared,
the negative magnitude is M/||q||_(coeff,1)^2, where M is pair mass.
A small source-moment error cannot be compared to M without its coefficient
factor. The examples with imaginary displacements 1/16,1/256,1/4096 show that
factor explicitly. No uniform separation is assumed for zeta zeros.

4. Exact finite examples and nontransfer controls

Take Lambda={2+i/4,2-i/4,-2+i/4,-2-i/4,10,-10}, unit weights.
The old fixed even quadratic X^2+1/16-4 has negative trace on the four
nonreal orbit slots, but trace 2361857/128>0 after the two real nodes are
included. This is a concrete masking example, not a zeta configuration.
FI1 targeting 2+i/4 gives a real degree-five polynomial with whole trace -2.
Using eta=1+i yields whole fourth-power trace -8. Repeating the target pair
three times gives -6 and -24 respectively; other nodes still contribute zero.
The same i-interpolant has fourth trace +2, exposing the wrong-power shortcut.

All reported inputs are synthetic exact finite divisors, NOT certified zeta
zeros. The profile evaluates every node and contracts every required moment.
Finite-case checks are not used to infer FI1-FI7; the written proofs above do
that work. The formal proof bodies still need native elaboration and auditing.

Another scope control is the real-node proportion M/(M+2) in a configuration
of M real nodes and one nonreal conjugate pair. It tends to one while the
nonreal pair remains at every stage. Even a density-one conclusion need not
exclude sparse exceptions. This is a comparison family, not a claim about
zeta zero counts.

5. Source-specific frontier and exact root consumer

The candidate FiniteDivisorCriterion refers to the repository's existing
NontrivialZetaZero (zeta zero, trivial-zero exclusion, pole exclusion) and the
unchanged _root_.RiemannHypothesis. Its ordinate is exactly -i(s-1/2).
Its conditional premise says that every such zero is contained in a finite,
conjugation-stable positive-mass family whose complete square traces are
nonnegative for every degree-<card coefficient-real polynomial.
FI3 then makes its ordinate real and gives Re(s)=1/2.

NO witness of that premise is supplied. The proof text neither constructs a
positive replacement Gram nor uses a comparator challenge containing a proof
placeholder as mathematical evidence. This is a candidate conditional edge,
not an admitted edge in the proof hypergraph.

A concrete use for FI1 in the proposed analytic route would be an admissible
actual-source test f with

 W(f)=lambda T_2(q)+R,  W(f)>=0,  lambda>0,
 |R| < lambda [m(z0)+m(conjugate(z0))].                         (FI8)

If approximate moments are used, their additional FI5/FI6 cost is included in
R in the SAME coefficient basis. FI2 makes FI8 contradictory for a nonreal
selected pair. Every term outside the selected finite divisor, test-function
admissibility, cutoff, coefficient approximation and multiplicity must remain.
A polynomial annihilating finitely many nodes need not define an admissible
Weil test or control its values at the infinitely many remaining zeros.

Required next inputs: (i) actual source nonnegativity for the selected tests
without an RH-equivalent assumption, (ii) a complete finite/infinite-divisor
comparison with a paid remainder, (iii) enough source-selected regions to
cover the target zero domain. The finite algebra solves none of those by
