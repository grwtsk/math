1. Research context and fixed source
-----------------------------------
Suzuki [S26], Theorems 1.1, 1.3 and 1.4, treats the localized actual Weil
form through a self-adjoint operator, proves continuity of its lowest
eigenvalue, and proves positivity for sufficiently small support. Its global
spectral limit is conjectural; no unconditional identification of a generic
self-adjoint spectrum with all zeta zeros is imported. The exact Fourier form
below is [S22], equation (5.15), also explicit in [S26], Section 1.1.
Connes--Consani [CC20], Lemma 3.3, explains the pole-annihilating differential
operator. Their positivity comparison has a fixed support restriction and a
retained rank-one correction; it is not a global positive supplier for our
growing compact tests. Finite-codimension positivity has classical ancestry in
Yoshida and Bombieri, reviewed in [S26]. We make sufficient constants explicit,
not a historical-priority claim.

Use hat f(t)=integral_R f(u)exp(itu)du, tilde f(u)=conj(f(-u)),
Q_W(f)=W(f*tilde f), supp f subset [-A,A], A>0. Initially f is smooth compactly
supported. Put N=||f||_2^2 and C_f(x)=integral f(u+x)conj(f(u))du. Set

 w(t)=Re psi(1/4+it/2)-log pi,
 P_A=2 sum_(2<=n<=exp(2A)) Lambda(n)/sqrt(n).

Every prime power is retained, including repeated prime bases. Then

 Q_W(f)=(1/(2pi))integral w(t)|hat f(t)|^2 dt
        +2 Re(hat f(i/2)conj(hat f(-i/2)))
        -2 sum_(n<=exp(2A)) Lambda(n)/sqrt(n) Re C_f(log n).       (1)

The polar term is zero ONLY with actual pole vanishing. In general it is
>=-4sinh(A)N, by Cauchy--Schwarz and integral_[-A,A] exp(+-u)du=2sinh(A).
Each autocorrelation has modulus <=N. Consequently, writing C_A=P_A+4sinh(A),

 Q_W(f)>=[(1/(2pi))integral w(t)|hat f(t)|^2 dt]-C_A N.           (2)

For pole-null f, C_A=P_A is permitted. This hypothesis must also hold for any
projected component to which that better cost is applied. Absolute values are
used only for these displayed lower bounds; equation (1) remains the source.
No Matsubara, Hodge, zeta-zero sample, or unsigned prime replacement is used.

2. Uniform archimedean lower bounds and an explicit positive seed
---------------------------------------------------------------
The classical digamma series [D57] gives for y=t/2 and a_k=k+1/4

 w(t)=w(0)+sum_(k>=0) y^2/[a_k(a_k^2+y^2)],
 w(0)=-gamma-pi/2-3log2-log pi > -11/2.                          (3)

Here gamma<3/5 follows from gamma<H_32-log32 and log2>693/1000.
The latter is certified by four positive terms of the atanh series. Further,
pi<22/7, log2<7/10 and log pi<6/5 give a total <11/2. The tests check the
rational comparisons, using Machin's arctangent brackets for pi and finite
positive exponential sums for the logarithm upper bounds. These analytic
series identities remain written/library mathematics, not Python deductions.

All summands in (3) are nonnegative and increasing with |t|. Their dependence
on a is decreasing. Retain the k=0 term and integrate the rest from 5/4 to
infinity. For every tau>0 and |t|>=tau,

 w(t)>=L(tau):=-11/2+16tau^2/(1+4tau^2)
                        +(1/2)log(1+4tau^2/25).                 (4)

Indeed integral_(5/4)^infinity y^2/[a(a^2+y^2)]da
=(1/2)log(1+4tau^2/25). L(tau) tends to infinity. The calculator encloses the
logarithm with a rational lower endpoint; no asymptotic constant is guessed.

For the small-support seed use the equivalent exact translation formula

 Q_W(f)=w(0)N+integral_0^infinity k(x)||f-T_x f||_2^2 dx
        +polar-prime, k(x)=exp(-x/2)/(1-exp(-2x)).                 (5)

It follows from (3), Plancherel and nonnegative-integral interchange. For
x>=2A the two translated supports are disjoint and ||f-T_x f||^2=2N.
Direct integration, with z=exp(-A), gives

 2 integral_(2A)^infinity k(x)dx=2atanh(z)+2atan(z).

For 0<A<1, z>=1-A, atan z>=z/(1+z^2), hence

 Q_W(f)>=q(A)N,
 q(A)=-11/2+log((2-A)/A)+2(1-A)/(1+(1-A)^2)-4sinh(A)-P_A.        (6)

The exact calculator finds q(1/64)>1/4 (approximately 0.281559>1/4).
Since 2A<log2, the prime sum is empty at A=1/64. Thus EVERY smooth compact
f supported in [-1/64,1/64] satisfies Q_W(f)>=||f||^2/4, with no pole-zero
or source-zero hypothesis. Smaller support follows by inclusion. This is an
explicit conservative instance of classical small-support positivity, not a
new optimal support theorem or a zeta-zero height certificate.

3. Positive complement to finitely many moments, with exact cost
---------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose integral u^j f(u)du=0 for j=0,...,m-1, m>=1. Taylor's integral
remainder on the REAL Fourier variable has |exp(itu)-Taylor_(m-1)|<=|tu|^m/m!
(no extraneous exp(|tu|) factor). Cauchy--Schwarz then gives

 (1/(2pi))integral_(-tau)^tau |hat f(t)|^2 dt <= mu N,
 mu<=min(1, 2(A tau)^(2m+1)/[pi(2m+1)(m!)^2]).                  (7)

Retaining all m moments is essential. A function with only one or two moments
cancelled cannot use a larger m. Split (2) at |t|=tau and combine (3)--(4):

 Q_W(f)>=[L-C_A-(L+11/2)mu]N.                                 (8)

Replace pi by its proved lower bound 3 for a rational allowance. A simple
all-m schedule avoids enormous factorials. From
log(m!)>=integral_1^m log x dx and e<3 follows m! >=(m/3)^m. If m>=6A tau,

 mu <= (1/18)4^(-m).                                         (9)

Choose tau with L(tau)-C_A>=1 and then m>=6A tau sufficiently large that
(L+11/2)4^(-m)/18<=1/2. This proves Q_W(f)>=N/2 on the moment-null subspace.
Such choices exist for EVERY fixed finite A. The executable uses a bounded
prime enumeration and bounded search; exhaustion returns failure. It does not
relabel that finite profile as an all-input termination proof. Equation (4),
finite P_A and (9) are the written existence argument.

Exact profiles: general tests at A=1/64,1/8,1/4,1/2 use respectively
(tau,m)=(64,6),(64,48),(128,192),(1024,3072). The pole-null profile A=1 uses
(16384,98304). Each certified lower constant is at least 1/2. These are
scalar sufficient costs; no matrix of these dimensions is constructed.
Ambiguous prime-cutoff comparisons INCLUDE the term. The program fails before
omitting an unenumerated arithmetic tail. Some counts are deliberately loose.

The dimension can be extremely large. P_A<=8A exp(A), so crude absolute prime
bounds can force tau and m to grow doubly exponentially with A. This method
proves finiteness of the dangerous sector; it does not make its sign easy.
Restricted-support positivity cannot be used for the previous tests with
half-support A=8L by rescaling without changing their Fourier evaluations.

4. Retain the finite block AND its coupling to the positive complement
--------------------------------------------------------------------
For the general (non-pole-restricted) profile, let V span the first m monomials
on [-A,A], extended by zero, and let H=V^perp in L2(-A,A). The source form
has Q_W>=c||.||^2 on H, c>=1/2 by Section 3. Polynomials on the interval belong
to the form and operator domain: their Fourier transforms are O(1/|t|), the
archimedean multiplier is O(log(2+|t|)), and the prime and polar parts are
bounded on this fixed support. Density extends the estimates from smooth tests.

In an orthonormal basis of V, let A00 be the ACTUAL low matrix, and let
B p=Pi_H A_source p. Its norm is finite. For f=p+h,

 Q_W(f)=Q_W(p)+2Re<Bp,h>+Q_W(h)
        >= p^*(A00-B^*B/c)p + c||h+Bp/c||^2.                  (10)

The conservative Schur matrix A00-B^*B/c is a sufficient source certificate.
Neither it nor its entries are computed in this pass. If it is >=-kappa I,
then Q_W(f)>=-kappa||f||^2. The exact Schur matrix instead uses the positive
high-block inverse. Its existence and norm bound follow from coercivity of
the closed high form, not a guessed inverse of a finite truncation.

The finite low matrix by itself is insufficient: [[1,2],[2,1]] has positive
diagonal blocks but value -2 on (1,-1). The cross term in (10) must survive.
The negative index of the full form is at most m: a negative-definite subspace
cannot contain a nonzero vector in the kernel of its first m moments. This
does NOT say a negative eigenvector lies literally in the polynomial subspace.
The pole-null profile requires a subspace retaining the two exponential
constraints as well; it is not silently substituted into the general split.

5. Fixed polynomial, joint Gaussian/support schedule, and its true L2 norm
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Now suppose a nonreal SOURCE frequency r=t+ib, 0<|b|<1/2, exists. Use the
previous finite source-window interpolation, retaining every analytic weight,
to obtain a fixed real P with values i,-i at r,conj(r), zero at all other
source frequencies |Re z-t|<=1, and zero at +-i/2. These last nodes are TEST
constraints, not source zeros. P=(z^2+1/4)Q, Q real; hold P and Q fixed.

Set G_L(z)=P(z)exp(-L((z-t)^2+b^2)), L integral>=1. The selected signed trace
is -M, M>0 its full pair multiplicity. HSW [H21] gives N_all(T)<=30T^2 for
T>=1. Dyadic summation gives sum m_z/(1+|z|^2)^2<=190, including the central
region and both signs. The typeset counting convention and Corollary 1.2 were
checked; its paper computations were not rerun or admitted in Lean.

Use the pole-preserving compact test

 phi_(L,t)(u)=(4pi L)^(-1/2)exp(-u^2/(4L))exp(-itu),
 g_L=exp(-Lb^2)Q(iD)phi_(L,t),
 f_L=(-D^2+1/4)(chi_(4L)g_L), supp f_L subset [-8L,8L].          (11)

Here chi_R is the retained smooth even cutoff, equal to one on [-R,R], zero
outside [-2R,2R], with derivative norms <=(16/R)^j, j<=4. It can be constructed
as the convolution of the indicator of [-3/2,3/2], four uniform probabilities
on [-1/16,1/16], and an even smooth probability on [-1/4,1/4], then rescaled.
The base derivative total variation is 16; the support and four bounds follow.
The operator in (11) is applied AFTER cutoff. Both pole values remain exactly
zero. The commutator -chi''g-2chi'g' is not removed.

The predecessor established existence of R for fixed L. For the present result
we need one JOINT schedule, not two unrelated limits. Put R=4L. Write Q's
coefficient absolute-value majorant and iterate

 U(A)=A'+(|t|+u/2)A.

It dominates all derivative polynomials uniformly for L>=1. In the Gaussian
tail recursion use c0=1,c1=4,cj=4^j+4(j-1)c_(j-2). At R=4L the corresponding
moment factor T_j is <=c_j L^j. Thus for d=deg Q there is an explicit constant
D, computed from these finite polynomials, such that the full compactification
error has weighted strip bound

 |hat f_L(z)-G_L(z)| <=delta_L/(1+|z|^2),
 delta_L <=D L^(d+4)4^(-L), |Im z|<=1/2.                       (12)

A coefficient majorant gives (1+|z|^2)|G_L(z)|<=B 2^ceil(L/2), B fixed.
The omitted far-source signed sum is <=190(2B)^2 exp(-3(L-1)/2).
The COMPLETE cutoff perturbation is

 190[2 B 2^ceil(L/2) delta_L+delta_L^2].                       (13)

Both tend to zero along R=4L. Once L>=4(d+4),
(1+1/L)^(d+4)<=4/3 (binomial coefficients bounded by powers, followed by a
geometric sum), hence delta_(L+1)/delta_L<=1/3. The cross budget decreases by
at most 2/3 each step, its square by at most 1/9, and the rational majorant
of the far tail by at most 1/2. The exact calculator returns one sufficient L0
in that range, which therefore also validates EVERY subsequent integer L.
For all L>=L0, Q_W(f_L)<-M/2, through the actual complete Weil formula [S22].
This statement is under a hypothetical off-line zero, not an observed example.

The NEW normalization estimate is critical. Put
R_j(v)=(v+t)^j Q(v+t)=sum_k r_(j,k)v^k, j=0,1,2,
C_j=(d+j+1)sum_k k! |r_(j,k)|^2.
Plancherel and the exact Gaussian moments give, for L>=1,

 ||D^j g_L||^2 <= C_j exp(-2Lb^2)/sqrt(L).

Indeed integral v^(2k)exp(-2Lv^2)dv=(2L)^(-k-1/2)Gamma(k+1/2), and
Gamma(k+1/2)<=k!sqrt(pi). The harmless 1/(2sqrt(2pi)) prefactor is bounded by 1.
For R=4L, 2||chi'||<=8 and ||chi''||+1/4<=65/4. Hence

 ||f_L||^2 <= C exp(-2Lb^2)/sqrt(L),
 C=3[C_2+64C_1+(4225/16)C_0].                                (14)

This is not the coefficient norm of P, not a source-observable norm, and not
assumed uniform across pairs. C is a fixed finite constant after the selected
source pair and interpolation polynomial are fixed. All coefficient costs stay.

6. A quantitative negative-growth obstruction and a new finishing target
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Define directly from the ACTUAL form

 lambda(A)=inf_(0!=f in C_c^infinity(R), supp f subset [-A,A]) Q_W(f)/||f||^2,
 kappa(A)=max(0,-lambda(A)).

The source lower bound (2)--(3) makes lambda(A) finite below. The infimum
matches the source operator bottom by [S26], but no eigenfunction or
self-adjoint spectral model is needed for the following argument.
Equations (11)--(14) imply for every integer L>=L0

 lambda(8L) <= -M/(2C) sqrt(L) exp(2b^2 L).                    (15)

The support is 8L, not 4L (the latter is the cutoff radius). The convolution
has half-support 16L. Therefore

 limsup_(A->infinity) log(1+kappa(A))/A >= b^2/4 >0             (16)

under any hypothetical off-line source pair. Every multiplicity and the
normalization cost C survives. No inference is made that such a pair exists.

A SUFFICIENT global source estimate is accordingly

 for every epsilon>0 there is finite C_epsilon>=0 such that
 Q_W(f)>=-C_epsilon exp(epsilon A)||f||^2
 for every A>=1 and every smooth f supported in [-A,A].       (SG)

To prove the return take epsilon=b^2/8 and (14). The allowed negative amount
is <=C_epsilon C exp(-b^2 L)/sqrt(L), tending to zero, contradicting -M/2.
This permits negative finite-support lower bounds; it does not require a
positive margin at every intermediate support. All constants may depend on
epsilon and on the fixed alleged pair in the contradiction construction.
Conversely RH gives Q_W>=0 by Weil's criterion, hence SG. SG is thus another
explicit equivalent global obstruction, NOT a proved arithmetic inequality.
No parameter or assumption is silently removed from a Lean RH theorem.
More quantitatively, if log(1+kappa(A)) has upper asymptotic rate alpha>=0,
then (16) forces |b|<=2sqrt(alpha) for every nontrivial zero. This is a
conditional collar; no improved alpha is established here. The elementary
absolute prime allowance has rate at most one, which gives no improvement
on the already known critical strip.

The crude source lower bound already proved here is
Q_W(f)>=-[11/2+P_A+4sinh(A)]||f||^2. It is exponentially costly in A under
absolute prime estimates, and does NOT imply SG. Combining the positive
moment-null complement with (10) gives a narrower place to seek SG: the complete
source low-block Schur correction, whose actual prime correlations remain.
Continuity of lambda(A) and the seed lambda(1/64)>0 do not prove SG or prevent
crossings; the continuous scalar family 1-A is a simple counter-control.

7. Execution, reusable formal leaves, and limits
-----------------------------------------------
The program computes the five sufficient complement profiles, the universal
small-support seed margin, and three synthetic selector-cost profiles. The
latter use only the selected pair and pole nodes; they are not source-window
lists for actual zeta. Extra near nodes in a real contradiction change Q,C,B,D
and must be included. No Weil integral, source eigenvalue, negative arithmetic
value, high-dimensional matrix or unknown off-line zero is numerically computed.

The two inactive Lean candidates formalize finite real norm-budget and signed
Schur inequalities only. The full Fourier/Weil/closed-form/zero-counting/
interpolation/limiting chain remains written, with explicit external inputs.
No active source, expected target, root definition, compiler pin or historical
receipt is edited. Native execution is blocked by the absent pinned runtime.
The exact tests include moment nullity, pole costs, prime powers, factorial
bounds, support scaling, cutoff commutators, all signed cross terms, and the
failure of finite-block diagonal positivity and positive-seed continuity.
