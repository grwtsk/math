1. Exact source and classical input
----------------------------------
Let D be the open unit disk, s(w)=(3+w)/(2(1-w)), and define

 r(w) = [psi(1+s(w)/2)-log(pi)]/2 = sum_(k>=0) r_k w^k.

Here psi is the digamma function, not the arithmetic Chebyshev function.
The gamma argument is (7-3w)/(4(1-w))=3/4+1/(1-w), with real part >5/4
on D. Thus r is holomorphic on D, without any zeta nonvanishing premise.
This is the archimedean term after the exact digamma recurrence in
  evidence/number-theory/zeta/hankel-quotient-20261015/derivation.txt, Section 6,
  grwtsk/math at 8a20ef34ed57ab38e74152e4f068f061db7ef5fb.
The rational 1/(s-1) term remains with the prime discrepancy, not in r.

The only special-function input needed here is the classical partial fraction
identity (NIST DLMF 5.7.6):

 psi(z)=-gamma+sum_(m>=0)[1/(m+1)-1/(m+z)].

This normally convergent identity on compact sets away from its poles is an
external standard identity, not a newly proved assertion about zeta. All
subsequent inequalities are derived below. No historical novelty is claimed.

2. A discrete positive moment measure
-------------------------------------
Put h_m=m+7/4, theta_m=(m+3/4)/(m+7/4), and omega_m=1/(2 h_m^2).
For EVERY integer k>=1,

 r_k = sum_(m>=0) omega_m theta_m^(k-1).                       (2.1)

Indeed 1/(m+1+s(w)/2)=(1-w)/(h_m(1-theta_m w)). Its coefficient at
w^k, k>=1, is -theta_m^(k-1)/h_m^2. Extract coefficients from the
normally convergent digamma series. The constant r_0 is retained separately;
it is not a nonnegative moment mass claimed by (2.1).

For H_N(r)_(i,j)=r_(i+j+1), 0<=i,j<N, equation (2.1) gives

 H_N(r)=sum_(m>=0) omega_m v_m v_m^T,
 v_m=(1,theta_m,...,theta_m^(N-1)).                            (2.2)

This is an ACTUAL archimedean positive Gram, with all N finite and no prime
or zero data. It does not assign positivity to H_N(a), K_N, or the prime part.
The vector formula is also positive for complex readers using the adjoint.

3. Coefficient and operator bounds independent of N
---------------------------------------------------
Let C=11/14. Since theta_0=3/7, theta_m increases to one, and

 theta_(m+1)-theta_m = 1/[h_m(h_m+1)],
 omega_m/(theta_(m+1)-theta_m)=(h_m+1)/(2h_m)<=C,

monotonicity of x^(k-1) on [0,1] proves

 0<r_k <= C integral_(3/7)^1 x^(k-1) dx
        = C[1-(3/7)^k]/k <= C/k.                            (3.1)

In particular, for EVERY N>=1,

 0 <= H_N(r),          ||H_N(r)|| <= 11*pi/14.                (3.2)

Here and throughout, the norm is the Euclidean operator norm. For completeness,
the required Hilbert-matrix bound has a direct proof. Put A_(i,j)=1/(i+j+1)
and weights w_j=(j+1/2)^(-1/2). For a=i+1/2, f(x)=1/[(a+x)sqrt(x)]
is convex and integrable on (0,infinity). Its second derivative is

 2/[sqrt(x)(a+x)^3] + 1/[x^(3/2)(a+x)^2]
                         +3/[4 x^(5/2)(a+x)] > 0.

Midpoint convexity, including the integrable improper first interval, gives

 sum_(j>=0) A_(i,j) w_j
 <= integral_0^infinity dx/[(a+x)sqrt(x)] = pi/sqrt(a)=pi w_i.

The weighted symmetric Schur inequality follows by expanding a quadratic
form and using 2|x_i x_j|<=|x_i|^2 w_j/w_i+|x_j|^2 w_i/w_j.
It proves ||A_N||<=pi. Equation (3.1) bounds H_N(r) entrywise by C A_N;
applying this to absolute values of reader coordinates proves (3.2).
No absolute value has been applied to an unassembled arithmetic prime sum.

A separate all-size coefficient-energy bound is

 sum_(k>=0)|r_k|^2 <= r_0^2+2(11/14)^2 =: C_gamma^2.          (3.3)

The elementary integral comparison sum_(k>=1)k^(-2)<=2 suffices. In particular
C_gamma is a finite, fixed source constant, not a dimension-dependent loss.
Also ||T_N(r)||<=|r_0|+C H_(N-1), where H_0=0 is the harmonic number.
The operator bound in (3.2), the coefficient bound in (3.3), and this logarithmic
Toeplitz bound are distinct statements.

4. An explicit finite positive approximation and its dimension price
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Retain m=0,...,M-1 in (2.2), calling the resulting rational matrix G_(N,M).
All theta_m and omega_m are rational. For M>=0 and N>=1,

 0 <= H_N(r)-G_(N,M),
 ||H_N(r)-G_(N,M)|| <= N/[2(M+3/4)].                          (4.1)

Each ||v_m||^2<=N. Moreover 1/h_m^2<=1/[h_m(h_m-1)], and the latter
sum telescopes from m=M to infinity to 1/(M+3/4). This proves the bound.
It is a proved universal tail price, NOT a claim that a selected finite sum was
executed this turn. The factor N must not be omitted for a growing section.

5. The archimedean denominator is uniformly invertible on the disk
-----------------------------------------------------------------
For z=x+iy with x>5/4, the digamma series implies

 Re psi(z)>=psi(x)> -1+(1-4/5)+(1/2-4/9)=-67/90.

Here gamma<1 follows from the decreasing harmonic-minus-log sequence; all
omitted terms in psi(x)+gamma are positive. Also log(pi)<6/5: the elementary
identity integral_0^1 x^4(1-x)^4/(1+x^2) dx=22/7-pi proves pi<22/7,
and the first five exponential terms give exp(6/5)>2059/625>22/7.
Consequently, on the ENTIRE open disk,

 Re(1+r(w)) > 1/36,
 b_gamma(w)=1/(1+r(w)) is holomorphic, |b_gamma(w)|<36.        (5.1)

For every finite N, Hardy multiplication and compression give
||T_N(b_gamma)||<=36. One can see this directly by integrating the squared
modulus of a finite polynomial times b_gamma on circles and passing to the
boundary in the coefficient norm. This asserts invertibility only of the
KNOWN archimedean denominator. It makes no such assertion about 1+a(w).

The arithmetic discrepancy note uses (3.3) to remove the archimedean jet at a
uniform additive coefficient cost. Multiplication of H_N(r) by the UNKNOWN
full-source filter B_N still costs ||B_N||. Equation (3.2) alone does not bound
H_N(r)B_N^T uniformly, and no such inference is made here.
