## 1. Corpus interfaces and the exact meaning of the two sides

Use the manuscripts' common normalization

    X(z)=xi(1/2-iz),  X_tau(z)=8 H_(4 tau)(-2z),
    E_tau=X_tau+i X_tau',  E_tau^#=X_tau-i X_tau'.

Primes on X differentiate z, not s. For rho=beta+i gamma,

    z_rho=i(rho-1/2)=-gamma+i(beta-1/2).

Thus the right side Re(s)>1/2 is the upper z half-plane; the left side is the
lower z half-plane. Critical reflection r(s)=1-conj(s) becomes z -> conj(z).
The full zero divisor and multiplicities are retained. A claim for the entire
left half-plane of zeta would be false because of its trivial zeros. Our zero
location target is the completed xi function, or equivalently the nontrivial
critical strip of zeta.

For delta in (0,1/2] and finite H>=0, define

    K^+_(delta,H)=[1/2+delta,1]+i[-H,H],
    K^-_(delta,H)=[0,1/2-delta]+i[-H,H].

These are reflected pairs. Taking delta=1/j and H=j, j>=3, exhausts both
noncritical parts of the closed strip. This is a geometrical exhaustion, NOT a
certificate that its members are zero-free. Without a source margin the program
returns UNRESOLVED_NO_NONVANISHING_MARGIN for both members.

If Omega_+ is Schur on C+, define Omega_-(z)=conj(Omega_+(conj(z))) on C-. The
correct residuals are

    R_+=E_tau Omega_+ + E_tau^#,
    R_-=E_tau^# Omega_- + E_tau.

Then R_-(conj(z))=conj(R_+(z)) exactly. The lower target is -E/E^#, not -E^#/E.
For a real-spectral resolvent m, Omega_-=(m+i)/(m-i); using (m-i)/(m+i) in C-
reverses the contractivity direction. At a reflected source zero of multiplicity
r, the respective removable quotient derivatives are +2i/r and -2i/r.

In the original coordinate, D(s)=4 Re(conj(xi(s))*xi'(s)) changes sign under
critical reflection. The desired modulus order is D>=0 on the right and D<=0
on the left. Equalizing the two sides by averaging D to zero would erase the
oriented inequality, not prove it.

## 2. Tao heat is transverse Hodge heat, in its actual function space

The updated sofic paper, Theorem 7.1 (PDF p.11), proves and attributes to
Polymath15 the Gaussian identity

    H_t(z)=pi^(-1/2) integral_R exp(-v^2) H_0(z-2i sqrt(t) v) dv.

In the normalized X coordinate, evenness and v -> -v give

    X_tau(z)=pi^(-1/2) integral_R exp(-v^2)
                         X_0(z-2i sqrt(tau) v) dv.

Thus for z=x+iy, partial_tau X_tau=partial_y^2 X_tau, and the transverse
Gaussian variance is 2 tau. Rodgers--Tao time remains t=4 tau. Reflection in y
commutes with this Gaussian averaging, and the same representation holds for
X_tau' after justified differentiation.

The scope is the Gaussian-integrable growing-profile class in Proposition 7.2,
not ordinary L2(R) contractivity. Positive averaging of complex function values
does not preserve zero-freeness. The paper's explicit example 2+exp(t)*cos(z)
has transition time log(2), not zero. Visual inspection of PDF p.12 confirms its
Hodge vector is sqrt(2)h+a; text extraction can lose that square-root sign.

For a compact K and 0<=tau<=tau_*, let

    B_j(K)=sup_{z in K,0<=theta<=tau_*}|X_theta^(j)(z)|.

The theta decay in the source gives finite B_j. Integrating the heat equation
proves

    ||X_tau-X_0||_K <= tau B_2(K),
    ||X_tau'-X_0'||_K <= tau B_3(K).

Consequently for every Schur Omega,

    ||R_tau-R_0||_K <= 2 tau (B_2(K)+B_3(K)).

This is the legitimate way to transfer a positive-time residual to zero. For a
polynomial local approximant P, if ||X_tau-P||_Q<=e and inf_Q|P|>e+tau B_2(Q),
then X_0 is nonvanishing on Q. Reflection supplies the same margin on its paired
cell. The inequality is a consumer; no new all-domain lower margin is assumed.

On a quartic P=z^4+a z^2+b the exact heat is

    exp(-tau D_z^2)P=z^4+(a-12 tau)z^2+b-2a tau+12 tau^2.

The finite tests verify this sign, the semigroup law, and imaginary-Gaussian
moments. They do not substitute polynomials for the actual theta integral.

## 3. A finite covariance-to-Hodge adapter with the correct mass

The original sofic manuscript, Sections 4--5, fixes q and a finite cycle of
length M=q n!. Its covariance is

    g_M(k/q)=Re <w,U^k w>-||w||^2.

Choose a cyclic eigenbasis and a REAL LIFT lambda_l of each phase, satisfying
exp(i lambda_l/q)=exp(2 pi i l/M). Put a_l=|w_l|^2. Then

    g_M(t)=sum_l a_l(cos(lambda_l t)-1)

agrees with the lattice covariance. This continuous interpolation is extra
specified data: lambda_l+2 pi q k has the same lattice values but generally a
different derivative, mass, and resolvent. The finite checker does not infer a
cyclic phase from an arbitrary rational squared frequency.

More generally retain an axial term alpha>=0 and define

    g(t)=-alpha t^2/2 + sum_l a_l(cos(lambda_l t)-1),  a_l>=0.

For a pure covariance of a finite cyclic vector alpha=0. A nonzero axial term
is an additional integrated-cocycle component, NOT the covariance of a fixed
vector under the identity representation.

### Theorem 3.1. Exact finite Hodge realization

For each frequency take a two-term cochain block d_l:C^0->C^1 with d_l=lambda_l.
On total cochains Q_l=[[0,lambda_l],[lambda_l,0]], Delta_l=Q_l^2. Select v_l in
degree zero with norm sqrt(a_l)*|lambda_l|. Add a harmonic vector of squared
norm alpha. Put Q=direct_sum Q_l plus that harmonic block and v=direct_sum v_l.
Then

    -1/2 ||integral_0^t exp(iuQ)v du||^2 = g(t),
    m(z)=<v,(Q-zI)^(-1)v>
        =-alpha/z + sum_l a_l lambda_l^2 z/(lambda_l^2-z^2),
    ||v||^2=alpha+sum_l a_l lambda_l^2 = -g''(0).

**Proof.** Each block has eigenvalues +/-lambda_l and seen mass
(a_l lambda_l^2)/2 at each sign. Integrate its two exponentials. Their squared
norm sum is 2a_l(1-cos(lambda_l t)); the harmonic integral is t v_0. This proves
the screw equality. Adding the two scalar resolvents gives the displayed
rational term. The mass and curvature identities follow directly. Zero frequency
with finite a_l contributes zero to the covariance and zero to this resolvent;
it is not the independent harmonic mass alpha. No arithmetic positivity was
used in this finite construction. Q is self-adjoint, so its Cayley transform is
genuinely Schur on C+.

This supplies an exact adapter between TWO PREVIOUSLY SEPARATE FINITE MODEL
INTERFACES, not an identification with the zeta source. It also explains why
bounded covariance mass sum a_l can coexist with growing resolvent mass
sum a_l lambda_l^2. The necessary divergence in the Grover--Hodge and
Gross--Zagier papers concerns the latter mass.

For Hodge time r>=0, v_r=exp(-rQ^2/2)v gives

    g_r(t)=-alpha t^2/2+sum a_l exp(-r lambda_l^2)(cos(lambda_l t)-1),
    m_r(z)=-alpha/z+sum a_l exp(-r lambda_l^2)
                                   lambda_l^2 z/(lambda_l^2-z^2).

The harmonic term remains unchanged. The signed heat channel j vanishes for this
specific degree-zero block vector by grading. This does NOT license dropping j
for arbitrary source readers in Theorem 11.3 of the updated sofic manuscript.

## 4. Both reflection channels in the sofic boundary

For nodes +/-t_i, A=[[B,C],[C,B]] as in Section 10.1 of the updated paper.
For the finite model above its exact channels are

    (B+C)_ij=2 sum_l a_l(cos(lambda_l t_i)-1)(cos(lambda_l t_j)-1),
    (B-C)_ij=2 alpha t_i t_j+2 sum_l a_l sin(lambda_l t_i)sin(lambda_l t_j).

Both are positive Grams. The first is fourth order at the common origin; the
second is second order, and

    (1/2) partial_s partial_t (B-C)(0,0)
         =alpha+sum_l a_l lambda_l^2=||v||^2.

Thus the odd channel carries the resolvent mass. Keeping only the symmetric
average loses precisely this second-order information. For the ACTUAL arithmetic
g_zeta, neither channel is declared positive without its own source certificate.
Reflection of real kernel labels and reflection across the complex critical line
are different actions; the explicit arithmetic transform in Section 5 connects
their analytic roles.

### Theorem 4.1. Paid Hodge compression in each fixed boundary corner

Let J exchange the two node halves, P_+/-=(I+/-J)/2, and
H_r=P_+ + r P_- with 0<r<=1. Suppose a Hermitian Btilde satisfies

    Btilde >= -eta I,  ||Btilde-H_r A H_r||<=epsilon.

Then

    A >= -(eta+epsilon)/r^2 I.

**Proof.** H_r A H_r>=-(eta+epsilon)I. Congruence by H_r^(-1) and
||H_r^(-1)||=1/r give the assertion. At r=0 this inverse argument is invalid;
projection requires the paper's radical/range condition or exact reconstruction
of the lost channel.

For r_n=exp(-t_n), restoring a source after growing heat requires
exp(2t_n)(eta_n+epsilon_n)->0. A smoothed negative block -r_n^2 is not a
vanishing source error: restoring it yields -1. If the two parity blocks are
verified separately, use their individual errors; do not multiply the already
retained even-channel error unnecessarily.

Because the sofic fixed-corner embedding is isometric and reflects positivity,
this argument holds in its norm-corona representation. The mesh q and node set
S remain fixed before cycle size grows. Only after that limit may an outer
sequence of rational meshes refine. A normalized trace is not used.

## 5. Actual arithmetic recognition on a safe half-plane

Let g_zeta be the EXACT real even arithmetic screw formula in the papers,
including its polar, prime-power, digamma, and Lerch terms. Suzuki's published
Theorem 1.1(1) states, with g=-Psi,

    m_xi(z)=-X'(z)/X(z)=i z^2 integral_0^infty g_zeta(t)exp(izt) dt,
    Im(z)>1/2.                                                   (5.1)

This is an external source identity, not a newly discovered transform.
The same transform of the finite g in Section 3 equals its m. Indeed

    integral (cos(lambda t)-1)exp(izt)dt
       =i lambda^2/[z(z^2-lambda^2)],
    integral t^2 exp(izt)dt=-2i/z^3.

Multiplication by i z^2 gives exactly Theorem 3.1, including the axial pole.
The exact tests verify both rational identities and the signs.

### Theorem 5.1. Weighted covariance error gives a full two-sided Schur return

Fix y0>1/2. Construct finite positive models g_n of Section 3 from independently
specified data, and PROVE

    delta_n=integral_0^infty exp(-y0 t)|g_n(t)-g_zeta(t)|dt -> 0.     (5.2)

Then their Schur functions Omega_n satisfy R_n->0 on every compact subset of
Im(z)>y0. In fact

    |m_n(z)-m_xi(z)|<=|z|^2 delta_n,
    |R_0,n(z)|<=2|X(z)| |z|^2 delta_n.                             (5.3)

Consequently the manuscripts' uniqueness-set Schur theorem excludes every zero
of X in C+, and reflection excludes C- as well: RH follows conditionally on
(5.2).

**Proof.** Bound the transform difference by the weighted L1 error. The equality
X m_xi+X'=0 on this safe domain and |m_n+i|>=1 imply the residual estimate.
Choose an infinite sequence iy_j with y_j>y0 accumulating at iy_*>iy0. Schur
normal-family compactness gives a locally uniform subsequential limit on ALL of
C+. Equation (5.3) forces the source identity on the uniqueness set, and hence
throughout C+. At a zero of multiplicity r the removable quotient has value one
and derivative 2i/r, impossible for a Schur function. Section 1 gives the lower
half-plane by exact reflected residual equality. This proof uses no prior
zero-free assertion for E inside C+ and no simplicity hypothesis.

This reduces the needed comparison to a concrete weighted scalar error in an
unconditional initial half-plane, but DOES NOT prove that positive g_n with
(5.2) exist. Fitting finitely many samples or using positive-part matrices is
not that theorem. The frequency lift and the full continuous interpolation
must be controlled, not just mesh values.

### Proposition 5.2. A weaker, anchor-based Hodge regularization cost

At z=iy, y>=y0, let M_n=sum_l a_l lambda_l^2 (nonharmonic spectral mass). Then

    |m_(n,r)(iy)-m_n(iy)|
      = y sum_l a_l(1-exp(-r lambda_l^2))lambda_l^2/(lambda_l^2+y^2)
      <= r y M_n.                                                (5.4)

Hence

    |R_(tau,n,r)(iy)| <= 2|X(iy)|(y^2 delta_n+r y M_n)
                           +2 tau(B_2+B_3).                      (5.5)

**Proof.** All summands in the imaginary-axis difference have the same sign.
Use 1-exp(-r lambda^2)<=r lambda^2 and lambda^2/(lambda^2+y^2)<=1.
For the source heat change use Section 2. Therefore delta_n->0, r_n M_n->0,
and tau_n->0 suffice on the fixed uniqueness set. For example the conditional
schedule r_n=1/[n(1+M_n)], tau_n=1/n pays these two KNOWN perturbation costs,
while leaving delta_n as the arithmetic input. No fourth spectral moment is
needed for this anchor-based estimate. It is a different proof from the
all-compact direct r||Qv||^2/eta bound in the supplied paper.

### Explicit source-tail supplier for (5.2)

An elementary absolute bound on the exact formula is

    |g_zeta(t)| <= (2t^2+4t+9) exp(t/2),  t>=0.                   (5.6)

To verify it, bound the prime sum by
 t sum_{n<=exp t}(log n)/sqrt(n) <=2t^2 exp(t/2).
The polar absolute value is at most 4exp(t/2). The digamma formula
psi(1/4)=-gamma-pi/2-3log2 gives |psi(1/4)-log pi|<8, so its linear cost is
at most 4t. Finally sum_{j>=0}(2j+1/2)^(-2)<=5 by its initial term plus the
integral test. Absorb 4t+5 into exp(t/2)(4t+5).

Put a=y0-1/2>0 and

    J_0(L,a)=1/a,
    J_1(L,a)=L/a+1/a^2,
    J_2(L,a)=L^2/a+2L/a^2+2/a^3.

The source tail is at most exp(-aL)(2J_2+4J_1+9J_0). For the finite model of

    delta_n <= e_n/y0 + both displayed tails.                    (5.7)

These are available tail schedules, not a construction of the finite-interval

## 6. A new full-strip analytic continuation for the centered pickup residual

This section concerns the different, precisely identified Möbius pickup W(t)
from the repository and Grover--Hodge Section 2, not g_zeta from Section 5.
Let |a_k|<=B, M_n=sum_(k<=n) a_k, A_n=sum_(k<=n) a_k/sqrt(k). On
I_n=[log n,log(n+1)], W(t)=2(A_n-M_n exp(-t/2)). Define

    h_n=log(1+1/n), S_n=2(n^(-1/2)-(n+1)^(-1/2)),
    mu_n=2(A_n-M_n S_n/h_n), r(t)=W(t)-mu_n on I_n.

The two endpoint definitions agree up to measure zero. Crucially

    r(t)=-2 M_n(exp(-t/2)-S_n/h_n), integral_(I_n) r(t)dt=0.

For complex w define the ENTIRE cell function

    L_n(w)=integral_(I_n) exp(-wt)dt,
    R_n(w)=-2 M_n[L_n(w+1/2)-(S_n/h_n)L_n(w)].                    (6.1)

The divided-difference form L_n(w)=[n^(-w)-(n+1)^(-w)]/w has its removable
value h_n at w=0. The integral definition retains this value automatically.

### Theorem 6.1. The centered-cell transform extends one half-plane farther

The series R(w)=sum_(n>=1)R_n(w) converges locally normally on Re(w)>-3/2.
Equivalently E_c(s)=R(s-1/2) is holomorphic on Re(s)>-1. It agrees with the
ordinary absolutely convergent Laplace integral of r for Re(s)>0.

**Proof.** With f(t)=exp(-t/2), v(t)=exp(-wt), the covariance identity gives

    R_n(w)=-(M_n/h_n) integral_(I_n x I_n)
                                (f(t)-f(u))(v(t)-v(u)) dt du.

On a compact where Re(w)>=sigma>-3/2 and |w|<=Z,

    |f(t)-f(u)|<=(2sqrt(n))^(-1)|t-u|,
    |v(t)-v(u)|<=Z n^(-sigma) 2^max(0,-sigma)|t-u|.

Moreover integral integral (t-u)^2=h_n^4/6. Since |M_n|<=Bn and h_n<=1/n,

    |R_n(w)| <= B Z 2^max(0,-sigma)/(12 n^(sigma+5/2)).           (6.2)

The exponent is greater than one; normal convergence proves holomorphy.
Without exploiting cancellation, |r(t)|<=B/sqrt(n) on I_n, so the original
absolute Laplace integral converges for Re(w)>-1/2. The two definitions agree
there. No rearrangement of a divergent ungrouped source integral is asserted.

In original s coordinates, on Re(s)>=a>-1 and |s-1/2|<=Z, the omitted tail is

    |sum_(n>N) R_n(s-1/2)|
      <= B Z 2^max(0,1/2-a) /[12(a+1) N^(a+1)].                 (6.3)

For the Möbius source B=1, on the WHOLE closed strip 0<=Re(s)<=1,
|Im(s)|<=H, sqrt(2)<3/2 gives the simple rational bound

    omitted centered-transform tail <= (2H+1)/(16N).            (6.4)

This covers both sides of the critical line with an ACTUAL analytic error
estimate. It is not a nonvanishing margin for xi or zeta.

### Corollary 6.2. The retained mean contains every nontrivial source pole

For Re(s)>1, the genuine pickup transform in the papers gives

    integral_0^infty exp(-(s-1/2)t) W(t)dt
       =1/[s(s-1/2) zeta(s)].

Therefore the transform of the retained cell means has meromorphic continuation

    M_c(s)=1/[s(s-1/2) zeta(s)]-E_c(s),  Re(s)>-1.              (6.5)

At every nontrivial zeta zero rho, the factors rho and rho-1/2 are nonzero;
E_c is holomorphic. Thus the full principal part, not just pole order, of the
reciprocal-source transform survives in M_c. No pole is hidden in the bounded
fine-scale residual. The elementary poles at s=0 and s=1/2 are separate from
nontrivial zeta zeros. Nothing in (6.5) proves M_c holomorphic.

## 7. Higher-moment projections give further leftward analytic charts

For an integer d>=0 let P_d be the cellwise L2-orthogonal projection onto
polynomials in t of degree at most d, retaining the original measure dt. Put
r_d=W-P_dW. Each cell has d+1 zero moments. The projection is a reconstruction
with a retained residual, not an assertion that its discarded part is absent.

On I_n, the derivative bound is

    |W^(d+1)(t)|<=B 2^(-d) sqrt(n).

A degree-d Taylor polynomial and the least-error property of orthogonal
projection give

    ||r_d||_(L2(I_n)) <= B 2^(-d) sqrt(n) h_n^(d+3/2)/(d+1)!.

Subtract a degree-d Taylor polynomial from exp(-wt) in its pairing with r_d.
Its L2 remainder is at most

    |w|^(d+1) n^(-sigma) 2^max(0,-sigma)
                                 h_n^(d+3/2)/(d+1)!.

Cauchy--Schwarz proves locally normal convergence of the resulting cell series
for Re(s)>-2d-1, with tail

    B 2^(-d) Z^(d+1) 2^max(0,1/2-a)
    /[(d+1)!^2 (a+2d+1) N^(a+2d+1)],  a>-2d-1.                (7.1)

The improved 1/12 constant in (6.3) is preferable at d=0. Taking d=1,2 gives
error charts Re(s)>-3 and Re(s)>-5. Increasing d covers any prescribed finite
leftward extent for this reconstruction hierarchy. Different d give different
retained projections; on overlaps their differences are the exact transforms of
P_eW-P_dW. One must retain those transition terms rather than identify all
error functions as one entire function. Actual source poles remain in the
corresponding retained parts.

## 8. The weaker remaining mean-growth target

The preceding log-cell work proves at T_N=log(N+1)

    E(T_N)=G_N+V_N,  G_N=sum_(n<=N) h_n mu_n^2,
    0<=V_N<=B^2/6,

and a sharper written/computational bound for the actual Mobius source. Therefore

    for every epsilon>0, G_N<=C_epsilon (N+1)^epsilon for every N

is equivalent to subexponential E(T) in the time variable. For the nontrivial
direction take N=floor(exp(T)), use monotonicity of E and
T_N<=T+log2, and add the uniformly bounded V_N. The converse follows by G_N<=E(T_N).
Grover--Hodge Theorem 16.1 or Gross--Zagier Theorem 10.1 then supplies the conditional
RH return. Polynomial growth in log(N) is sufficient but unnecessarily stronger.

Neither Theorem 6.1 nor the positive finite boundary/Hodge construction proves
this remaining mean-growth bound. The all-positive coefficient control satisfies
the same residual estimates while its retained mean energy grows exponentially
in T. This remains a regression against confusing a paid residual with a paid
source.

## 9. What has actually advanced

1. A specified finite cyclic covariance lift now has an exact Hodge generator,
   screw, resolvent, parity decomposition, and the correct frequency-squared mass.
2. Heat-compressed sofic corners have a priced inverse-return condition retaining
   the odd channel, whose low-order coefficient is the resolvent mass.
3. A weighted scalar arithmetic error on one safe half-plane feeds the existing
   source-specific Schur uniqueness theorem; an anchor-based smoothing bound
   pays only r times the second spectral moment of the covariance weights.
4. The actual centered Mobius pickup residual has a holomorphic reconstruction
   across both sides of the complete nontrivial strip, with an explicit all-height
   truncation error and fresh exact endpoint calculations.
5. Higher moments extend these error charts leftward, with explicit overlap debt;
   the remaining mean-energy target needs only subpower growth in arithmetic N.

Still open: a family satisfying (5.2), or the full fixed-corner source comparison,
or the equivalent sufficient mean-growth estimate. A fresh complete zero-free
cover is not claimed. The ten source papers are mined, not relabelled as an
already completed RH proof.
