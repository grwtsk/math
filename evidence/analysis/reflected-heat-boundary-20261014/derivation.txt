B. THE TWO ANALYTIC SIDES AND THE HEAT NORMALIZATION

Use X(z)=xi(1/2-iz), X_tau(z)=8 H_(4 tau)(-2z),
E_tau=X_tau+i X_tau', E_tau^#=X_tau-i X_tau'. A source zero beta+i gamma
maps to z=-gamma+i(beta-1/2). Thus right/left of the critical line in s map to
upper/lower half-planes in z. Critical reflection 1-conj(s) maps to conj(z).
For an upper Schur function Omega_+, put Omega_-(z)=conj(Omega_+(conj(z))).
The exact reflected residuals are

 R_+=E_tau Omega_+ + E_tau^#,
 R_-=E_tau^# Omega_- + E_tau,
 R_-(conj(z))=conj(R_+(z)).

The lower target is -E/E^#, not -E^#/E. A real-spectral resolvent uses
Omega_+=(m-i)/(m+i) above and Omega_-=(m+i)/(m-i) below. At a source zero of
multiplicity k the respective removable quotient derivatives are +2i/k,-2i/k.
For xi, D(s)=4 Re(conj(xi(s))*xi'(s)) changes sign under critical reflection;
right nonnegative and left nonpositive are the corresponding oriented orders.

The source's Gaussian identity becomes

 X_tau(z)=pi^(-1/2) integral_R exp(-v^2) X_0(z-2i sqrt(tau) v) dv.

It is forward heat in transverse y, variance 2 tau; Rodgers-Tao time is 4 tau.
Its domain is a Gaussian-integrable growing-profile class, not ordinary L2
contractivity. For B_j bounding |X_theta^(j)| on a compact and 0<=theta<=tau_*,
integrating partial_tau X_tau=-X_tau'' gives

 ||R_tau-R_0|| <= 2 tau(B_2+B_3)

for any Schur Omega. A local polynomial P with ||X_tau-P||_Q<=e excludes zeros
of X_0 on Q only if inf_Q|P|>e+tau B_2(Q). Reflection then gives the other cell.
This strict lower margin is not supplied by positive averaging. On a quartic,
exp(-tau D^2)(z^4+a z^2+b)=z^4+(a-12tau)z^2+b-2a tau+12tau^2.

C. EXACT FINITE COVARIANCE-TO-HODGE ADAPTER

For a fixed mesh q and cycle length M=q n!, write its covariance as
Re<w,U^k w>-||w||^2. A cyclic eigenbasis gives masses a_l>=0 and a chosen real
frequency lift lambda_l, exp(i lambda_l/q)=exp(2pi i l/M). Its continuous
interpolation is sum a_l(cos(lambda_l t)-1). The choice of lift is additional
data: adding 2pi q times an integer does not change mesh values but does change
the resolvent and its mass.

More generally retain an axial term and define
 g(t)=-alpha t^2/2+sum a_l(cos(lambda_l t)-1), alpha>=0.
For each l take a two-term cochain differential d_l=lambda_l, with
 Q_l=[[0,lambda_l],[lambda_l,0]],
and a degree-zero vector of norm sqrt(a_l)|lambda_l|. Add a harmonic component
of squared norm alpha. Direct diagonalization proves

 -1/2 ||integral_0^t exp(iuQ)v du||^2=g(t),
 m(z)=<v,(Q-zI)^(-1)v>=-alpha/z+sum a_l lambda_l^2 z/(lambda_l^2-z^2),
 ||v||^2=alpha+sum a_l lambda_l^2=-g''(0).

Indeed the two eigenvalues carry half the mass a_l lambda_l^2 each; integrating
their exponentials gives 2a_l(1-cos(lambda_l t)). The harmonic integral is tv_0.
A zero-frequency covariance weight contributes zero, not the separate axial
mass. Pure cyclic covariance has alpha=0. This finite adapter is not an
identification with the arithmetic source. It explains why bounded covariance
mass sum a_l can coexist with the diverging resolvent mass required in the
Grover-Hodge and Gross-Zagier papers.

For paired real nodes +/-t_i, let A=[[B,C],[C,B]]. Direct trigonometry gives
 B+C=2 sum_l a_l(cos(lambda_l t_i)-1)(cos(lambda_l t_j)-1),
 B-C=2alpha t_i t_j+2 sum_l a_l sin(lambda_l t_i)sin(lambda_l t_j).
The even block starts at fourth order; the odd block at second order, with
one half its mixed derivative at the origin equal to ||v||^2. Averaging away
the odd block loses this mass channel. Neither block of the ACTUAL arithmetic
kernel is declared positive without its own comparison.

For H=P_+ + r P_-, 0<r<=1, if Btilde>=-eta I and
||Btilde-H A H||<=e, congruence by H^(-1) gives
 A>=-(eta+e)/r^2 I.
Thus r=exp(-t) requires exp(2t)(eta+e)->0 for restoration. At r=0 no inverse
exists. A smoothed negative eigenvalue -r^2 restores to -1, not zero. The
sofic embedding retains this exact finite-corner norm and order; fix q and the
nodes before taking cycle size to infinity. No normalized trace is used.

D. A CONCRETE SOURCE COMPARISON AND A CHEAPER ANCHOR HEAT COST

Suzuki's published Theorem 1.1(1), equation (1.2), arXiv:2206.03682v4, gives
for the papers' complete arithmetic g_zeta=-Psi

 m_xi(z)=-X'(z)/X(z)=i z^2 integral_0^infty g_zeta(t)exp(izt)dt,
 Im(z)>1/2.

This external identity was checked in the full primary text. It is not a new
transform. Integrating one cosine atom and the axial t^2 term gives exactly
the finite m above, including -alpha/z.

Fix y0>1/2. The MISSING arithmetic task is to construct positive finite models
and prove delta_n=integral exp(-y0 t)|g_n-g_zeta|dt ->0. Then on Im(z)>=y0,
 |m_n-m_xi|<=|z|^2 delta_n,
 |E Omega_n+E^#|<=2|X| |z|^2 delta_n.
The proof is the transform bound and |m_n+i|>=1. A Schur normal-family
subsequence on all C+ has the exact source identity on any fixed accumulating
set iy_j above y0, hence on C+ by the identity theorem. The nonzero quotient
jet at any off-line zero contradicts the maximum principle. Reflection gives
the left side. This is a conditional return, not a proof that delta_n->0.

Let v_r=exp(-rQ^2/2)v and M_n=sum a_l lambda_l^2. At iy, y>0,
 |m_(n,r)(iy)-m_n(iy)|
 =y sum a_l(1-exp(-r lambda_l^2))lambda_l^2/(lambda_l^2+y^2)
 <=r y M_n.
Use 1-exp(-r lambda^2)<=r lambda^2 and lambda^2/(lambda^2+y^2)<=1.
Consequently the joint anchor residual is bounded by
 2|X(iy)|(y^2 delta_n+r y M_n)+2tau(B_2+B_3).
Thus delta_n->0, r_n M_n->0 and tau_n->0 suffice. The conditional choice
r_n=1/[n(1+M_n)] pays smoothing without a fourth spectral moment, while
leaving delta_n unproved. This is distinct from arbitrary large-time collapse.

A source tail is available without positivity: the exact arithmetic formula
implies |g_zeta(t)|<=(2t^2+4t+9)exp(t/2). Bound the prime sum by 2t^2exp(t/2),
the polar term by 4exp(t/2), the digamma linear coefficient by 4t, and the
Lerch difference by sum_(j>=0)(2j+1/2)^(-2)<=5. For a=y0-1/2, define
 J0=1/a, J1=L/a+1/a^2, J2=L^2/a+2L/a^2+2/a^3.
The source tail beyond L is <=exp(-aL)(2J2+4J1+9J0). The finite-model tail
is <=exp(-y0 L)(2 sum a_l/y0+(alpha/2)J2(L,y0)). A PROVED sup approximation
error e on [0,L] then gives delta<=e/y0+these tails. Samples or phase aliases
alone do not prove that sup error, especially at the source's origin.

E. UNCONDITIONAL ANALYTIC CONTINUATION OF THE CENTERED PICKUP ERROR

This is the different source W from Grover-Hodge Section 2 and the repository's
logarithmic-cell calculation. For |a_k|<=B, let M_n=sum_(k<=n)a_k and
A_n=sum_(k<=n)a_k/sqrt(k). On I_n=[log n,log(n+1)],
 W(t)=2(A_n-M_n exp(-t/2)), h_n=log(1+1/n),
 S_n=2(n^(-1/2)-(n+1)^(-1/2)), mu_n=2(A_n-M_n S_n/h_n).
Then r=W-mu_n=-2M_n(exp(-t/2)-S_n/h_n) has cell mean zero. Set the entire
cell transform
 L_n(w)=integral_(I_n)exp(-wt)dt,
 R_n(w)=-2M_n[L_n(w+1/2)-(S_n/h_n)L_n(w)].
The formula [n^(-w)-(n+1)^(-w)]/w for L_n retains the removable value h_n.

For Re(w)>=sigma and |w|<=Z, the covariance identity gives
 R_n(w)=-(M_n/h_n) integral integral
  (exp(-t/2)-exp(-u/2))(exp(-wt)-exp(-wu))dtdu.
The two Lipschitz constants are 1/(2sqrt(n)) and
Z n^(-sigma)2^max(0,-sigma). Since the double integral of (t-u)^2 is h_n^4/6,
|M_n|<=Bn and h_n<=1/n prove
 |R_n(w)|<=BZ2^max(0,-sigma)/(12n^(sigma+5/2)).
Hence sum R_n(w) converges locally normally on Re(w)>-3/2. In original
coordinates E_c(s)=sum R_n(s-1/2) is holomorphic on Re(s)>-1. It agrees with
the ordinary absolute Laplace integral of r on Re(s)>0. Beyond that domain
this is grouped analytic continuation, not an unproved rearrangement of the
original integral.

On Re(s)>=a>-1, |s-1/2|<=Z, the tail after N is at most
 BZ2^max(0,1/2-a)/[12(a+1)N^(a+1)].
For actual Mobius B=1, throughout 0<=Re(s)<=1, |Im(s)|<=H, this is at most
 (2H+1)/(16N), using sqrt(2)<3/2 and |s-1/2|<=H+1/2.
This covers BOTH sides of the nontrivial strip with a holomorphic error and
explicit finite-height approximation cost. It does NOT exclude zeta zeros.

On Re(s)>1 the actual source identity gives
 integral exp(-(s-1/2)t)W(t)dt=1/[s(s-1/2)zeta(s)].
Thus the RETAINED MEAN transform continues meromorphically as
 M_c(s)=1/[s(s-1/2)zeta(s)]-E_c(s), Re(s)>-1.
Every nontrivial zero has nonzero factors s and s-1/2; its full principal part
stays in M_c. The analytic residual cannot cancel it. Elementary poles at
0 and 1/2 are distinct. No holomorphy of M_c is asserted.

F. HIGHER MOMENTS AND THE REMAINING MEAN GROWTH

Let P_d be the cellwise L2(dt) projection onto polynomials of degree at most d.
For r_d=W-P_dW, the first d+1 moments vanish. A Taylor approximation and the
least-error property give
 ||r_d||_(L2(I_n))<=B2^(-d)sqrt(n)h_n^(d+3/2)/(d+1)!.
Subtract the degree-d Taylor polynomial of exp(-wt) in the dual pairing. Its
L2 error is <=Z^(d+1)n^(-sigma)2^max(0,-sigma)h_n^(d+3/2)/(d+1)!.
Cauchy-Schwarz gives a normally convergent error chart on Re(s)>-2d-1 and
tail, for a>-2d-1,
 B2^(-d)Z^(d+1)2^max(0,1/2-a)
 /[(d+1)!^2(a+2d+1)N^(a+2d+1)].
Different d have different retained sources. On overlaps retain the exact
transform of P_eW-P_dW; do not identify the errors as one entire function.

At T_N=log(N+1), the earlier result is E(T_N)=G_N+V_N, 0<=V_N<=B^2/6.
Thus subexponential E in T is equivalent to
 for every epsilon>0, G_N<=C_epsilon(N+1)^epsilon for ALL N.
Indeed G_N<=E(T_N); conversely use N=floor(exp(T)), monotonicity of E,
T_N<=T+log2, and the bounded variance. The supplied subexponential-energy
return would then give RH. This mean estimate remains unproved. The all-positive
coefficient control satisfies the same residual bounds while its mean energy
