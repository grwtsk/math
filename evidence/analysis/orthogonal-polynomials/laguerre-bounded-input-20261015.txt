A dimension-priced bounded-input estimate for finite Laguerre synthesis
====================================================================
Direction: R.A. Jacob Martone. Derivation: OpenAI ChatGPT. MIT.
Written proof, with finite polynomial regressions; no Lean admission.

Let L_k be the ordinary Laguerre polynomial, and phi_k(t)=sqrt(2)e^-t L_k(2t)
on t>=0. For every measurable bounded complex function f, define
 alpha_k(f)=integral_0^infinity f(t)e^-t L_k(2t)dt.
Every individual integral is absolutely convergent. For every integer N>=1,

 sum_(k<N)|alpha_k(f)|^2 <= 4N ||f||_infinity^2.             (1)

No global L2 hypothesis is imposed on f, and the right side is NOT independent
of N. The same estimate applies to f times an arbitrary measurable indicator.
For f in L2 the separate Bessel inequality gives

 sum_(k>=0)|alpha_k(f)|^2 <= ||f||_2^2/2.                    (2)

Proof. The normalization integral integral e^-x L_i(x)L_j(x)dx=delta_ij
follows from Rodrigues' formula and repeated integration by parts: all boundary
terms vanish, and the leading coefficient gives norm one. The substitution
x=2t proves orthonormality of phi_k. Classical ancestry: NIST DLMF 18.3.
For g=sum_(k<N)v_k phi_k, the Laguerre recurrence gives

 t phi_k = (k+1/2)phi_k -(k+1)phi_(k+1)/2-k phi_(k-1)/2.

The last outgoing term at k=N-1 is -N phi_N/2; it must be retained. The
three rectangular maps on the coefficient vector have norms at most
N-1/2, N/2, (N-1)/2. Consequently ||t g||_2 <=(2N-1)||v||_2 <=2N||v||_2.
The recurrence is DLMF 18.9, ordinary Laguerre row; it also follows directly
from the finite coefficients L_k(x)=sum_j binom(k,j)(-x)^j/j!.

For any b>0, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 ||g||_1^2 <= [integral_0^infinity (b+t)^(-2)dt]||(b+t)g||_2^2
           <= (b+2N)^2/b * ||v||_2^2.

Choose b=2N, giving ||g||_1<=sqrt(8N)||v||_2. Since
sum conjugate(v_k)alpha_k(f)=integral f conjugate(g)/sqrt(2), finite-dimensional
duality proves (1). Applying orthonormal Bessel directly proves (2).

The example f=1 has alpha_k=(-1)^k, hence exact prefix energy N. Thus linear
N growth is necessary for a bounded-input theorem of this type; replacing
(1) by a dimension-independent bound for all bounded f would be false.
The example does not identify f=1 with a full arithmetic discrepancy. The
application to prime squares uses an independently established bounded input.

No claim of historical novelty is made for this standard recurrence/duality
argument. It is supplied explicitly to price the retained critical component.
