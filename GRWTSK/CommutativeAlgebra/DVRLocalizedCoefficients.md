## Mathematical consumer and exact scope

The normalized slice in the submitted [SliceHolds.lean][slice] uses

\[
J=I+(f-1),\qquad B=A/J,\qquad
s=\overline\pi\,\overline g\in B,\qquad C=B[s^{-1}],
\]

where `O` is a discrete valuation ring, `π∈O` is irreducible, and
`A` is a commutative `O`-algebra. The ideal `(f-1)` means the ideal
generated by the single element `f-1`. The required coefficient supplier is

\[
\forall c\in O\setminus\{0\},\qquad
\operatorname{algebraMap}_{O,C}(c)\in C^\times.
\tag{units}
\]

This module proves (units) on those literal quotient and localization
objects. It supplies the remaining coefficient premise of
`FiniteTypePointRigidity.slice_finite_points_and_separation` and its
characteristic-zero specialization, whose complete argument is recorded
in [FiniteTypePointRigidity.md](FiniteTypePointRigidity.md).
Finite type and vanishing point derivations are separate suppliers;
this coefficient argument assumes neither conclusion.

The intermediate statements expose two useful levels of generality.
First, an arbitrary commutative `O`-algebra in which `π` becomes a unit
inverts every nonzero coefficient. Second, localizing any commutative
`O`-algebra `B` away from `algebraMap O B π * b` supplies that premise.
The exact quotient-slice theorem then preserves the objects expected by
the original consumer.

The only coefficient assumptions are `[CommRing O]`, `[IsDomain O]`,
`[IsDiscreteValuationRing O]`, and `Irreducible π`. The target needs
only a commutative ring and its `O`-algebra structure. No target domain,
nontriviality, faithful coefficient action, finite type, grading,
homogeneity, completeness, or characteristic-zero assumption occurs.
There is no field-valued point parameter in this supplier. Faithfulness
of the field-valued point target is needed elsewhere when extending
points across the localization; it is not a premise of (units).

## Complete primary analytic argument

### 1. Why every nonzero coefficient is a unit times a uniformizer power

Mathlib defines a DVR as a local principal ideal domain that is not a
field. Let `m` denote its unique maximal ideal and fix an irreducible
element `π`. The principal ideal `(π)` is maximal in a principal ideal
domain, so locality gives `(π)=m`. For any other irreducible element
`p`, the same reasoning gives `(p)=m=(π)`. Thus `p` and `π` divide
each other. Writing `p=aπ` and `π=bp` and cancelling the nonzero
element `π` gives `ba=1`; consequently `a` is a unit and `p` is
associated to `π`.

It remains to justify that a nonzero `c` has a **finite** factorization
into irreducibles. A principal ideal domain is Noetherian. If a nonzero
nonunit failed to factor into finitely many irreducibles, it would not
be irreducible, so it could be written as a product of two nonunits.
At least one factor would again fail to factor into irreducibles.
Repeating this choice would yield nonzero elements

\[
c=c_0=c_1d_1,\quad c_1=c_2d_2,\quad\ldots
\]

with each `d_i` a nonunit. Their principal ideals form a strictly
increasing chain

\[
(c_0)\subsetneq(c_1)\subsetneq(c_2)\subsetneq\cdots.
\]

Indeed, if `(c_i)=(c_{i+1})`, write `c_{i+1}=r c_i` and substitute
`c_i=c_{i+1}d_{i+1}`. Cancellation of the nonzero `c_{i+1}` gives
`1=r d_{i+1}`, contradicting that `d_{i+1}` is a nonunit. The
ascending chain contradicts the Noetherian property, establishing
finite factorization. Units themselves have a factorization with no
irreducible factors.

Write such a factorization as `c=u₀p₁⋯pₙ`. Every `p_i` is a unit
multiple of `π`, as proved above. Multiplying those units gives

\[
c=u\pi^n\qquad(u\in O^\times,\ n\in\mathbb N).
\tag{factorization}
\]

This argument needs no uniqueness of the chosen exponent or unit.
It does not use completeness or a power-series expansion. The inspected
Mathlib proof of `IsDiscreteValuationRing.associated_pow_irreducible`
obtains finite factors using `IsNoetherianRing.wfDvdMonoid` and
`WfDvdMonoid.exists_factors`, identifies each factor with `π` up to
association, and counts the finite multiset of factors. The following
theorem, `IsDiscreteValuationRing.eq_unit_mul_pow_irreducible`, gives
exactly (factorization). Our maintained proof uses that theorem from
the root dependency pin. See [DiscreteValuationRing/Basic.lean][dvr].

### 2. Transfer the factorization to any target algebra

Let `α:O→D` be the structure map of a commutative `O`-algebra and
suppose `α(π)` is a unit. For nonzero `c`, choose (factorization).
Multiplicativity gives

\[
\alpha(c)=\alpha(u)\alpha(\pi)^n.
\]

The element `α(u)` is a unit with inverse `α(u⁻¹)`, and a power
of a unit is a unit. Their product is a unit. More explicitly, if `v`
is the inverse of `α(π)`, then

\[
\alpha(u^{-1})v^n
\]

is an inverse of `α(c)`. This proves
`coefficients_are_units_of_uniformizer_isUnit`.
No cancellation in `D` is used, so this proof permits zero divisors
and the zero ring in `D`.

### 3. Inverting a product makes the uniformizer a unit

For a commutative `O`-algebra `B`, let

\[
s=\alpha_B(\pi)b,\qquad C=\operatorname{Localization.Away}(s),
\qquad\beta:B\to C.
\]

By the definition of localization, `β(s)` is a unit. Let `t` be its
inverse. Compatibility of the coefficient maps and multiplicativity give

\[
\beta(s)=\alpha_C(\pi)\beta(b).
\]

The element `β(b)t` is an inverse of `α_C(π)`:

\[
\alpha_C(\pi)\bigl(\beta(b)t\bigr)=\beta(s)t=1.
\]

Commutativity supplies the other inverse identity. Similarly,
`α_C(π)t` is an inverse of `β(b)`. Therefore a unit product supplies
units for both factors without any domain assumption. The Lean proof
uses `IsLocalization.Away.algebraMap_isUnit`, the actual scalar-tower
identity for `O→B→C`, and `isUnit_of_mul_isUnit_left`.
Applying the previous step proves `coefficients_are_units_away_mul`.
The localization facts come from [Localization/Away/Basic.lean][away].

### 4. Return to the literal normalized quotient

Take `B=A/J`, where `J=I ⊔ Ideal.span {f-1}`, and take
`b=Ideal.Quotient.mk J g`. The previous statement applies to

\[
\operatorname{Localization.Away}\left(
\operatorname{algebraMap}_{O,A/J}(\pi)
\operatorname{Ideal.Quotient.mk}_J(g)\right).
\]

This is exactly the type in `slice_coefficients_are_units`. Both
coefficient maps are the canonical ones already supplied by the quotient
and localization algebra instances. No replacement ring, unspecified
isomorphism, or assumption that the quotient embeds in a field is
introduced. The final conclusion is the actual `∀ c, c ≠ 0 →
IsUnit (algebraMap O C c)` required by the point-rigidity consumer.

The source block being reconstructed consists of `hs_unit`, `hs_eq`,
`hπu`, and `hunits` in `UpperTheory.V.Alg.M.slice_holds`, before
its finite-type and point-derivation arguments. The source's separate
`hgu` assertion is the other factor of the same unit product; the
imported audit checks both factors. This module supplies coefficient
inversion only. It does not itself prove finite type, finite points,
point separation, or the final scalar-multiple ideal membership.

## Hostile checks and exact hypothesis boundaries

All five controls below are proved in the imported audit, not merely
asserted as possible tests.

| Control | Checked statement and significance |
|---|---|
| A nonzero chosen element alone is insufficient | In any DVR, choose the element `1` and localize away from `1`. Its image is a unit, but a genuine irreducible `π` remains a nonunit. If its image were a unit, the localization criterion would give `π ∣ 1ⁿ=1`, contradicting irreducibility. |
| The nonzero coefficient premise matters | In `O[π⁻¹]`, the image of `0` is not a unit. Otherwise the localization criterion gives `0 ∣ πⁿ`, hence `πⁿ=0`, contradicting the domain hypothesis and `π≠0`. |
| The DVR condition carries mathematical content | In `ℤ[1/2]`, the irreducible element `2` becomes a unit but the nonzero coefficient `3` does not. If `3` were a unit there, `3 ∣ 2ⁿ`; primality would imply `3 ∣ 2`, a contradiction. |
| Unit products work without a domain target | For arbitrary commutative coefficient algebra `B`, the audit proves that both `π` and `b` become units in `B[(πb)⁻¹]`, with no DVR, irreducibility, nontriviality, or domain assumption. |
| The zero ring is admitted intentionally | The generic theorem applies to the actual target `O/⊤`. Its uniformizer image is a unit because all elements equal `1`. This checks that no hidden target faithfulness or nontriviality premise entered the statement. |

The first control does not say that irreducibility is the weakest possible
condition on the element being inverted. For example, inverting a
positive power of a uniformizer also inverts the uniformizer. It proves
the narrower and relevant fact that replacing irreducibility by mere
nonzeroness is invalid. Irreducibility is the source's chosen sufficient
condition.

A further analytical example explains the permitted zero divisors:
`D=Frac(O)×Frac(O)` receives `O` diagonally, every nonzero coefficient
is a unit, and `(1,0)(0,1)=0` with both factors nonzero. This example
is written analysis, not an additional compiled product-field fixture.
The maintained generic proof already has no target-domain premise.

These controls challenge the hypotheses and representation of the primary
proof. They are not claimed as a distinct proof of the coefficient
theorem. The factorization argument and its formal use share the DVR
structure and the pinned Mathlib algebraic facts. No novelty claim or
independently derived second proof is made.
