Under `GRWTSK.ComputerScience.AlgorithmsInComputerScience.AffineFiberTensorNetworkContraction`, `angularMatrix` makes each mode and color pair a matrix column. `renderViaMatrix` multiplies the pixel-by-atom visibility matrix by this atom-by-mode/color matrix. It takes the harmonic dot product of the resulting row for each pixel and color. The return is pointwise equality at every pixel and color, expressed by `render_eq_renderViaMatrix_of_commute` and its commutative-semiring specialization `render_eq_renderViaMatrix`.

At fixed pixel `p` and color `c`, applying the source list fold gives the sum of `(K[p,i] * Y[p,m]) * A[i,m,c]` over all atoms `i` and modes `m`. The inherited docstring gives the coefficient order as `K A Y`; the actual source body evaluates `K Y A`. Convert each complete `List.finRange` sum to a sum over the corresponding finite type. Swap the finite sums, commute `K[p,i]` with `Y[p,m]`, reassociate, and distribute `Y[p,m]` over the inner atom sum. That inner sum is the matrix product at row `p` and column `(m,c)`. This argument includes zero atoms and zero modes by the empty-sum laws. With one pixel, one atom, one mode and coefficients in the ring of 2-by-2 matrices over the integers, choosing `K=E₁₂`, `Y=E₂₁`, and `A=I` in a color gives `E₁₁` in the source and `E₂₂` in the factored expression. This witnesses the need for an extra assumption in the noncommutative setting.

The equality provides a finite semantic return for rectangular multiplication plans that compute the indicated matrix product exactly. Choice of algorithm, compiled layout, arithmetic count, peak storage and floating-point error require separate comparison theorems. This locally derived identity does not depend on an OpenAI exponent claim.
