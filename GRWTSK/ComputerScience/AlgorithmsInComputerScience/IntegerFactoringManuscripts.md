### Las Vegas complete factorization

Theorem 1.1 quantifies over one fixed multitape machine, every binary $N\ge2$, and its read-once independent fair bits. Every halting path has exact prime-power output. Halting has probability one. For $H(N)=(\ln N)^{1/3}(\ln\ln N)^{2/3}$ the claims are
$$
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln\max(1,\mathbb E_\omega T(N,\omega))}{H(N)}\le105,\qquad
\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln\max(1,\sup_\omega S(N,\omega))}{H(N)}\le40.
$$
The second supremum is over all bit streams and includes input, output and working tapes, with the random-source convention in §2.1. Neither limsup constant is a finite-input multiplicative bound at its endpoint.

For the restart calculation, fix the input. Write $R_j$ for reaching attempt $j$, starting at zero. Suppose the probability of failure conditional on each reached history is at most $1-\delta$, $0<\delta\le1$, and the conditional mean cost of an attempted phase is at most $C$. By conditioning, $\Pr(R_{j+1})\le(1-\delta)\Pr(R_j)$, so $\Pr(R_j)\le(1-\delta)^j$. Integrating the nonnegative sum of attempt costs, including the successful attempt, gives
$$
 \mathbb E T\le T_{\rm setup}+\sum_{j\ge0} C\Pr(R_j)
 \le T_{\rm setup}+C/\delta.
$$
Correlation between the cost and success of the same attempt is permitted. The [expected-cost and workspace lemmas](FactoringRestartCost.md) give separate composition rules.

### Deterministic proper divisor, order splitting and nonresidues

Put $u=\ln N$. Theorem 1.1 claims $C\exp(3u^{3/4})$ worst-case bit operations for a **verified proper divisor** of any binary composite $N\ge4$. In §11.3, the algorithm removes the even and perfect-power cases before calling the odd composite routines. Given fixed-machine, monotone splitter, exact division and polynomial primality/stack/sorting suppliers, each internal node costs at most the root splitting bound plus polynomial overhead. Prime leaves have product $N\ge2^{\#\mathrm{leaves}}$, so at most $\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor-1$ splitter calls are needed. This yields the conditional full-output bound $O(b\exp(3u^{3/4})+\mathrm{poly}(b))$, $b=1+\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor$. For large $b$, $b^{3/4}=o(b/\sqrt{\log b})$, since $\sqrt{\log b}/b^{1/4}\to0$. Polynomial overhead adds only $O(\log b)$ to the exponent. This compares upper envelopes after model and output alignment.

## Abundance decisions

The direct arithmetic reduction and its counts are in [IntegerFactorizationAbundance.md](../../NumberTheory/IntegerFactorizationAbundance.md). For the reverse direction Shunia's Theorem 1.2 reduces the comparison $\sigma(x)\ge h$, for $x\ge2,h\ge0$, to **strict** abundance $\sigma(z)>2z$. It charges polynomial work in $\log x+\log(h+2)$ and gives $O((\log x)^2)$-bit queries. Coprime multiplier construction and a half-integer threshold preserve equality in the source comparison. The algorithm replaces direct true/false branches by $z=12,1$, respectively. A proof of the uniform multiplier construction on the binary representation is still needed. [Shunia, arXiv:2611.34242v1, Theorem 1.2 and Algorithm 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34242v1)

Theorem 3.1 uses $m=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, $J=48(1+m)^2$, and $2m$ exact divisor sums at powers $n^{J+s}$, $0\le s<2m$. Trying all recurrence orders includes one whose rationally solved coefficients are within $1/2$ of the desired integers. Filtering integer roots by $d>1,d\mid w,\gcd(d,w/d)=1$ on the residual $w$ makes the smallest retained root a full prime power. Exact power testing recovers its prime base, and coprimality permits updating all sums by division. The results may emerge in increasing prime-power order, so sort by **prime base** for the canonical consumer (for $12$, $3<4$ while $2<3$). The local code now proves coverage at a successful order, performs the rational solves and updates the stored sums. A polynomial-time root finder and bit-costed rational solver remain open. The paper bounds queried powers and divisor sums by $O(b^3)$ bits and candidate rational coefficients by $O(b^4)$ bits. [Theorem 3.1 and Algorithms 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34242v1)

Binary search in $[1,x^2]$ consumes $O(\log x)$ comparisons at each power. For $O(b)$ powers the composition gives $O(b^4)$ adaptive queries, each of $O(b^6)$ bits. With monotone oracle time $A$, the claimed bound is $\operatorname{poly}(b)+O(b^4)A(O(b^6))$, including query construction/serialization in non-oracle work. The deterministic abundance-in-P and classical factoring-in-FP endpoints remain open here. The strict-abundance reduction still needs its uniform comparison-multiplier construction and polynomial-time machine implementation. Exact stored power sums already suffice for the local executable factorization theorem.

The threshold error calculation, exact adaptive sampling, signed Euler expansion, separated block of components and traversal of residual factors now have local consumers. The all-orders program constructs its candidates from the stored values, handles singular trials, and proves that one enumerated root is a least full prime-power component. Thus `allOrder_factorization` returns the canonical prime powers on every input at least two. The present implementation uses Laplace expansion and a scan of possible integer roots through the current residual. A separately proved floor formula obtains the normalizing totient from one exact divisor sum. On semiprimes a shorter decoder recovers the canonical output directly from the divisor sum of the input, including equal factors. ## Polynomial factorization over prime fields

The algebraic source takes a binary prime $p$ and a **nonzero dense polynomial** $f\in\mathbb F_p[X]$ of degree $n$. Put $L=\lceil\log_2p\rceil$, $B=20+(n+1)(L+1)$. It returns the leading coefficient and distinct monic irreducibles with positive multiplicities, including an empty list when $n=0$. Only when $p>B^{200000}$ and $n\ge2$ must each prime $q\le n$ be supplied with a prime $\ell_q\notin\{2,3,p,q\}$, $\ell_q\equiv1\bmod12q$, $p^{(\ell_q-1)/q}\not\equiv1\bmod\ell_q$. With $E=\max(2,\max_q\ell_q)$, the sequential cost claimed is $O(B^{500000}+B^{100}E^{20})$. Given witnesses, trial division and modular exponentiation check them; given guaranteed existence up to $E$, upward search finds them within the same kind of numerical bound. One **uniform** $E\le c B^C$ suffices. Supplying only $\log E\le\operatorname{poly}(B)$ does not. ## Batch number-field-sieve comparison

Dvijotham et al., *Fan Factories* (November 5, 2026), Lemma 2.2(i), starts with a base-$m$ digit polynomial $f_N$ and uses $f_j(X)=f_N(X)+j(X-m)$. Substitution gives $f_j(m)=N$ for every $j$, so the fields for a target retain a common root modulo that target. This exact algebraic identity does not estimate the number of relations. For a batch of $K$ targets in the same size range, charge a shared rational table once and all $K$ target-specific stages. The total is $P+\sum_{i=1}^K T_i$ and the amortized total includes $P/K$. Peak space retains the shared table while charging working space for the chosen concurrent or sequential schedule.

The matching comparison is Theorem 3.4, with the same $K=L[\mu]$, no prior table, common target range and an agreed memory cap. H1 models smoothness and independence between sides. H2 supplies the unit-cost RAM sieve and sparse linear-algebra costs, including memory. H3 supplies irreducibility, square reconstruction and independent fruitful dependencies. Converting to a multitape classical bound would require a bit-cost simulation and verified prime-power completion. The theorem's batch timing has no such consumer here.
