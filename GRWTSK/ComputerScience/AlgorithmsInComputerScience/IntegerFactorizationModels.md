## Output and open target

For every natural number $N\ge2$, the answer is a sequence of strictly increasing prime bases, each paired with a positive exponent, whose expanded product is $N$. Each exponent is the exact valuation. Put $b=1+\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor$. There are at most $b-1$ prime leaves with multiplicity because their product is at least two to the number of leaves. Every prime and exponent fits in $b$ bits. The count-prefixed, fixed-width binary certificate therefore uses at most $b(2b-1)$ bits. This bound includes the count word. For $N=1$ the internal arithmetic convention is the empty product. Zero is outside the public domain.

The open proposition [`ClassicalFactoringFP`](IntegerFactorizationTarget.lean) fixes a deterministic machine, finite alphabets and constants before quantifying over inputs. It requires the canonical wire bits within the time bound and uses Mathlib's full final configuration, including blank work stacks. The [ledger](ConjectureLedger.lean) records a conjectural residue. The implication between this TM2 cost and a fixed multitape bit cost uses the simulation below. Scott Aaronson’s November 24 clarification describes reports of a polynomial-time classical result as rumors. The open ledger requires a proof for the specified machine and output. [Comment #23](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10362).

The [wire checker](../../NumberTheory/IntegerFactorizationCertificate.lean) reads the count word before allocating rows and checks for a trailing symbol after the declared payload. At most $b-1$ rows reach the next stage. Ordering, base bounds, positive exponents and a total-exponent cap precede primality and multiplication. On an incorrect product that passes those guards, intermediate products can exceed $N$ but are less than $2^{b^2+1}$. A conservative bit implementation charges the actual input read, $O(b^4)$ ancillary work for checks and movement, at most $b-1$ primality tests at width $b$, and at most $b-1$ products of polynomial width. Any caller requiring the whole incoming stream to be consumed must charge its full length. The executable Lean `decide` primality branch has no proven AKS cost. [Tape multiplication call contract](FactorVerificationTapeCalls.lean).

For a replacement on a fixed execution, match the verifier's Boolean answer for every bit string and the same time/space coordinates. The tape-call histogram supplies one upper bound. Applying [`cost_le_of_bounds`](../../Combinatorics/ExactResourceReplacement.lean) also requires a lower bound for the chosen incumbent and an upper-to-lower comparison. Neither a histogram alone nor a comparison of two upper bounds establishes a strict resource improvement. In the Las Vegas case, form a justified expectation in the probability model first; the pathwise space cap remains a separate coordinate.

The [memo bridge](../../Combinatorics/GraphTheory/IntegerFactorizationMemo.lean) installs a complete answer and replays its certified prime leaves. A warm hit returns the previous certificate; on a cold miss, charge lookup, the factoring supplier, validation/replay and installation according to the actual implementation. The precomputed smallest-prime-factor table in [#1845](https://github.com/grwtsk/math/issues/1845) covers inputs through 65,536. Its preparation and lookup representation have independent costs. The earlier [factor-state completion](../../Combinatorics/FactorCompletion.lean) conserves the same prime valuations along an existential path, bounded by the input value. The binary resource proof uses the number of prime leaves directly.

## Two encodings and a machine comparison

Formal Conjectures asks whether `Nat.primeFactorsList` is polynomial-time computable with respect to its canonical encoders. At the specified revision this is the right side of an open conjecture containing placeholders. Its separate complexity definition is an inhabited Mathlib polynomial-time TM2 witness. The natural encoder is little-endian with zero encoded by the empty word and one by the single true bit. The list encoder emits one block per element. Each payload bit becomes a true marker and its value, followed by a false terminator per block. The pair encoder delimits only its first component. Both zero and one have empty repeated-prime output. The strict local input language may reject noncanonical words. Formal Conjectures specifies behavior on encoded values and its natural decoder accepts additional words. One cannot infer identical off-image behavior. [Statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/56534c04092446f2fd549d2865f2496924812da8/FormalConjectures/Wikipedia/PolyTimeFunctions.lean) · [Encodings](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/56534c04092446f2fd549d2865f2496924812da8/FormalConjecturesForMathlib/Computability/BitstringEncoding.lean) · [Time predicate](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/56534c04092446f2fd549d2865f2496924812da8/FormalConjecturesForMathlib/Computability/Complexity.lean).

For valid inputs, expand the prime-power answer into at most $b-1$ words, trimming width padding, and delimit each word. Conversely, parse each block, count adjacent equal primes and pad the prime and counter to width $b$. The sorted repeated list supplies the run boundaries. Each conversion needs at most $O(b^2)$ data bits and polynomial bit time on a fixed multitape machine. For example, repeated whole-buffer scans for each of at most $b$ leaves cost $O(b^3)$. Handle zero and one by two fixed branches when totalizing the public function. For decision queries the local implementation uses a third delimiter symbol. A fixed two-bit code per symbol works over Bool; reject the unused pair and odd-length words. The decision predicate includes both strict inequalities and the threshold. With $N=15$, thresholds 2 and 3 give opposite answers although the primality of $N$ is unchanged.

The pinned `Turing.FinTM2` fields provide finite stack indices, labels, controls and input alphabet. The typed output alphabet is also finite through the output equivalence. For each syntactic push to stack $k$, collect the image of its payload function on all finite control states. Take the union over all nodes of all finitely many labeled statements and the tagged input alphabet. [`reachable_holds`](../../MathematicalLogicAndFoundations/ComputabilityAndRecursionTheory/FiniteReachableStackAlphabet.lean) proves that every reachable item is in this finite set. Pop only removes an item, peek/load leave the stacks fixed, and a branch chooses one of the two recursively covered subprograms. Restrict the alphabet at stack $k$ to the fiber of this set; add a blank/end marker for an empty fiber. Encode reachable symbols in fixed-width blocks. Tabulate each control function on its restricted finite domain. The tables may depend on the fixed machine, and can be arbitrarily large constants.

A multitape simulator uses one tape per stack and keeps its head at the top block. Push and pop write or clear one block and move over it. Peek reads one block and restores the head. Goto and branch are finite-control lookups. The longest syntactic branch of the finite program bounds the number of primitives per TM2 macro-step. Thus $t$ macro-steps take at most $c_M(t+1)+O(b+u)$ bit steps with input staging and output serialization of $u$ bits. In the opposite direction a tape is represented by two stacks for the cells on either side of its head and a finite control register for its current symbol. Each fixed machine transition takes boundedly many stack operations. Initial loading, final clearing and output orientation must be charged on either translation. These are constructive machine descriptions. A Lean compiler, table realization and timed trace correspondence for the two simulators are outstanding. [Pinned Mathlib interface](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/Computability/TuringMachine/Computable.lean).

## Fixed-machine search reductions

Given a splitter, a primality tester and an exact divider, keep a stack of active factors. Test each factor. If prime, append it to the output tape. Otherwise obtain a proper divisor, divide exactly, and push both factors. Properness makes both children smaller; multiplication conserves the parent. Induction on this tree gives all prime leaves with product $N$. A full binary tree with $r$ leaves has $r-1$ internal nodes. There are exactly $2r-1$ primality calls, $r-1$ splitter calls and $r-1$ quotient calls. Every operand is bounded by $N$.

Fix callable deterministic multitape routines whose stated monotone costs $S,A,D$ include initializing and cleaning their private work tapes or a faithful charged return convention. Give each routine separate tapes; avoid copying its entire work space as an uncharged call. Write pending operands as delimited $b$-bit words. At most $b$ pending and emitted words occupy $O(b^2)$ bits. Whole-buffer passes for each operation cost $O(b^3)$. An insertion sort with even a full traversal of the $O(b^2)$-bit buffer per key comparison costs $O(b^4)$. Group equal primes, serialize the row count and output, and clear temporary tapes in this same polynomial. One fixed machine assembled from these routines therefore takes
$$
 (r-1)(S(b)+D(b))+(2r-1)A(b)+O((1+b)^4).
$$
This is a comparison for the specified attainable execution. The [Lean theorem `SplitMachine.completeOutput`](IntegerFactorizationReductions.lean) composes timed configuration segments on the same deterministic machine, including loop frames and returns, for supplied input, output encoder, per-frame cost, setup and finish. Its conclusion is meaningful for the open target only after fixing the real wire encodings and realizing these premises with polynomial overhead. The compiler and primitive timed traces for a concrete supplier remain open. Substituting an unconditional polynomial-time primality tester (AKS) and schoolbook exact division gives the Google deterministic completion envelope **conditional on its splitter claim**, $O(b\exp(3(\ln N)^{3/4})+\operatorname{poly}(b))$. The repository's Lucas condition characterizes prime inputs but has no such executable bit-cost bound. [AKS](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2004/160-2/p12) · [Lucas characterization](../../NumberTheory/LucasPrimality.lean).

For a complete answer, a composite has a least prime base strictly below $N$; compare that base to $k$. Conversely first ask whether a proper divisor exists at threshold $N-1$. A negative answer terminates this node as prime. For a positive answer, binary-search the monotone predicate. Its first true threshold is the least divisor, hence prime; the general tree reduction can use it just like any proper divisor. Each search asks at most $b$ thresholds, in addition to the existence test. There are $O(b^2)$ queries over the entire tree, each with $O(b)$ symbols. Charging $O(b^3)$ total query writing, erasure and serialization, plus the oracle cost at each query and the preceding polynomial, gives a polynomial-time Turing reduction. An arbitrary input threshold may have more bits than $N$; scanning it costs its actual length. Only the thresholds constructed by the search have the input-dependent $b$-bit bound. [Executable search and decision encoding](IntegerFactorizationReductions.lean).

## Which comparison functions grow between polynomial and exponential?

Write $t=\ln N$ and $u=t^a(\ln t)^g$. For $0<a<1$ and $g\ge0$, $u/t=(\ln t)^g/t^{1-a}\longrightarrow0$. Also $u/\ln t\to\infty$ since $t^a/\ln t\to\infty$ and eventually $(\ln t)^g\ge1$. The bit-length inequalities $(b-1)\ln2\le t<b\ln2$ imply $t/b\to\ln2$ and $\ln b/\ln t\to1$. Hence $u/b\to0$ and $u/\ln b\to\infty$. For every fixed $c>0$, $\exp(cu)$ eventually exceeds every fixed polynomial in $b$ and is $2^{o(b)}$. The deterministic expression uses $a=3/4,g=0,c=3$. The randomized comparison has $a=1/3,g=2/3$ with independent limsup constants on worst-input expectation and pathwise space. Exceeding these polynomials is a property of the displayed envelopes; no algorithmic lower bound follows. [Lean binary-input limits, `exponent_bit_limits`](IntegerFactorizationRates.lean).

For fixed $0<\eta<1$, the ratio of $v\log\log v/\sqrt{\log v}$ to $v^{1-\eta}$ diverges as $v^\eta\log\log v/\sqrt{\log v}$. The displayed Anthropic upper bound therefore does not refute the particular stretched-exponential exclusion in #379. A stronger algorithm or a different size-preserving reduction would need its own theorem. The same calculation gives $b^{3/4}=o(b/\sqrt{\ln b})$, so the proposed Google deterministic rate, after completion, has a smaller comparison exponent than the sharper counting claim. This comparison assumes a shared classical bit model and complete output; it compares upper envelopes only. [Anthropic ETH](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/eth/README.md) · [OpenAI #379](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/379.md).
