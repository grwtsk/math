# Integer factorization from exact divisor-prefix counts

Local attribution: grwtsk contributors. Coordination: [#1857](https://github.com/grwtsk/math/issues/1857).

## Shared arithmetic and costs

For a positive cofactor $m<2^w$, keep the divisor $m$ itself and exclude 1. For a prefix of length $w-r$ and value $x$, count divisors $d\mid m$ with $d\ge2$ and $\lfloor d/2^r\rfloor=x$. Leading zeroes are significant. The zero-child and one-child intervals partition the parent. At each level choose the zero child if it has positive count. The root contains $m$. Induction keeps the least eligible divisor in the current interval, so the final singleton is $m.\mathrm{minFac}$, also when $m$ is prime. A multiplication-network count that includes 1 must subtract exactly one whenever the fixed-width word for 1 has the requested prefix. On $m=2$, omitting that subtraction gives 1 and division fails to decrease the cofactor. [Shared count and cofactor loop](IntegerFactoringCounting.lean) · [Pinned upstream prefix proof](https://github.com/IsaacRudich/SubexponentialDeterministicFactoring/blob/c91cc3f4947caca8977e81a5d14244f73b5ae2b1/FactoringAux/Search.lean)

The loop emits the least factor and recurses on the exact quotient. Its equations agree with Mathlib's sorted repeated-prime output. The product of $t$ factors is at least $2^t$, so logarithmic fuel suffices. Grouping repeated primes returns the canonical prime powers. On a $b$-bit input, at most $b-1$ calls to the least-factor supplier require at most $b(b-1)$ prefix queries. Each query includes the cofactor, width and current prefix, using $O(b)$ bits with a length-delimited serialization. Explicitly copying these payloads costs $O(b^3)$ bits written. Network construction and counting are charged separately per query. A finite Python arithmetic check on all 1,023 inputs $2\ldots1024$ compared 21,708 queried counts against the multiplication-pair count corrected for 1. Every result had the expected product, sorted prime leaves, and stated call bounds. Eight direct Lean executable checks also passed, covering least-factor peeling on 2, 97, 4096, 180 and 194 and the exact shared prefix count on 2 and 180. [Canonical output](../../NumberTheory/CompleteIntegerFactorization.lean)

For a fixed complete-output implementation, suppose its actual time is bounded by $a(1+b)^d(1+R)$ whenever every supplier call on widths up to $b$ costs at most $R$. This accounts for polynomial loop, division, serialization, memory and output work once the caller has been implemented on the specified machine. The source's time function need not be monotone. For a requested integer $k\ge1$, use the source at $2k$ on every width up to $b$. For the polynomial factor choose blocks of length $L=2k(d+1)$. Writing $q=\lfloor b/L\rfloor$ gives $1+b\le L(q+1)\le L2^q$ and $dq\le\lfloor b/(2k)\rfloor$. The two exponents sum to at most $\lfloor b/k\rfloor$, giving the bound at **all** lengths with a fixed prefactor. The time of the composed TM2 remains a premise until its control flow, division, encoding and stack movement are realized and charged. [Local rate proof](IntegerFactoringCounting.lean)

The signed-network analysis treats field-valued tables, linear combinations and arithmetic costs. The divisor reduction needs a nonnegative integer count read without cancellation or modular ambiguity. A transfer must construct and evaluate the input in the compared representation, preserve its scalar, restore the integer and bound intermediate bit sizes. Likewise the Anthropic $v\log\log v/\sqrt{\log v}$ exponent divided by $v^{1-\eta}$ tends to infinity for fixed $0<\eta<1$. This upper bound supplies no power saving of the kind excluded by the tape paper's Weak ETH. If the Google deterministic splitter and the composition costs qualify, its displayed exponent $3(\ln N)^{3/4}=O(b^{3/4})$ is asymptotically smaller than the comparison function $b/\sqrt{\log b}$, even after adding $O(\log b)$ for polynomially many calls.
