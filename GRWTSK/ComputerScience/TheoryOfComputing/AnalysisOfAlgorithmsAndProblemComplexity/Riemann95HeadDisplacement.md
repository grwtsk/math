# Signed head displacement of the prime-addressed Riemann95 interpreter

The [Lean module](Riemann95HeadDisplacement.lean) extends the [prime interpreter](Riemann95PrimeMachine.lean), checked local Git blob `d8f0b66cff2e7e5983250b370cce0617b13b5732`. The original transition table comes from Google LLC's Cryptids revision `3647718b5df22e1a61462dec0376bd204526285a`. The published native projection and its exact blank-tape halting equivalence remain the semantic return. [Published interpreter and verification](https://github.com/grwtsk/math/issues/1821) · [Pinned transition source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/cryptids/blob/3647718b5df22e1a61462dec0376bd204526285a/Cryptids/Riemann95.lean)

Changing the absolute origin adds a constant offset to the stored position and preserves the prime, scanned symbol and both list halves. This change commutes with the original interpreter step in each direction and with its finite runs by induction. The native projection erases the offset. The blank configurations at offsets zero and one therefore give a concrete collision, and a successful run preserves a nonzero offset even at its endpoint. This isolates a different obligation from the peer's moving-window transport, which changes the prime label and keeps the position.

Given n successful instructions, the head displacement is 2r−n for some natural r at most n. The induction adds a signed unit and increments r on a right move. Consequently the absolute distance is at most n and the displacement has parity n, including zero-length runs. These are necessary checks on an absolute position reported at a physical handoff. An implementation must anchor the starting origin and justify the actual updates or measure the absolute position independently. The full tape simulation and progress of the physical controller still require their own premises.
