For F contained in G and nonreal z, let d be the difference between resolving a zero-extended vector in G and extending the resolved vector from F. Apply the larger shift zI − D_G to the existing defect identity. The inverse law gives exactly the cut residual r at the resolved F vector. The imaginary-part bound for the shift and the triangle inequality give |Im z| ‖d‖ ≤ ‖r‖ ≤ (‖z‖ + ‖D_G‖) ‖d‖. The exact zero test detects failure of intertwining on any input for which r is nonzero. In particular, by choosing the input to be the smaller shift of a coordinate vector, an individual nonzero cut entry supplies an analytic witness.

As an analytic control on the reverse estimate, consider scalar self-adjoint shifts with D_n = n, z = i and d_n = 1/n for n ≥ 1. Then d_n tends to zero while (i − n)d_n tends to −1. This tests the inference for general shifts with growing norm. To use the sufficient direction on an exhaustion, one needs vanishing cut residuals for the resolved source vectors in a common ambient space. A limiting domain and an exact identification with every complex zero and its order, including exclusion of extraneous spectrum and the normalization of any determinant, are separate obligations. The route to RH would then use real spectrum and the full shift −i(s − 1/2). [Existing source identity and normalization work](https://github.com/grwtsk/math/issues/1805)
