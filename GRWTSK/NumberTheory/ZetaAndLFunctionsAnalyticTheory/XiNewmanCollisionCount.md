# Counting off-line zeta zeros by the winding of a real theta heat flow

Let \(H(x,t)=H_t(x)\) be the de Bruijn–Newman flow in the normalization \(H_0(x)=\xi(1/2+ix/2)/8\). It is given by the cosine integral of \(e^{tu^2}\Phi(u)\) on the positive half-axis, with the explicit positive theta density in the [Jacobi construction](XiNewmanJacobiLimit.md). In particular \(H_t(iy)>0\) for real \(y\), and \(\partial_tH=-\partial_x^2H\). Define \(F(x,t)=H(x,t)+i\partial_x H(x,t)\) entirely from the real arithmetic theta integral.

Let \(K_0\) be the number of off-critical-line zero quadruples of zeta, weighted by the common analytic multiplicity in each quadruple. The following identities allow infinity:
\[
 K_0=\sum_{\substack{x>0,\ t>0\\F(x,t)=0}}\left\lfloor\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{x}H_t}{2}\right\rfloor
     =\sup_R\operatorname{wind}(F|_{\partial R},0).
\]
Here \(R\) runs over rational rectangles compactly contained in \((0,\infty)^2\), oriented counterclockwise in the ordered coordinates \((x,t)\), on whose boundaries \(F\) never vanishes. In particular RH is equivalent to the absence of simultaneous real zeros of \(H,H_x\) at positive space and positive time. The finite computation below covers a bounded range of this last test. The question at arbitrarily large space and arbitrarily small positive time remains open.

## The local integer

At a spatial zero of order \(m\ge2\) at time \(s\), the backward heat equation splits its zeros on the scale \(\sqrt{2|t-s|}\). After division by the leading constant and the corresponding scale to the power \(m\), the limiting polynomial is the probabilists' Hermite polynomial on the forward side, and its imaginary rotation on the backward side. Its roots are real and simple. Disjoint small root neighborhoods of the rescaled polynomial therefore give exactly \(m\) simple real zeros just after the event and \(m\bmod 2\) simple real zeros just before it. The realness statements follow from uniqueness in each conjugation-invariant neighborhood. This also proves isolation of each event. The cited primary result gives the expansion with its error term. [D. H. J. Polymath, arXiv:1904.12438v2, Proposition 3.1(ii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12438v2)

Take a sufficiently small rectangle about the event, with \(H\) nonzero on both vertical sides and simple spatial zeros on each horizontal side. Along increasing \(x\), the positive imaginary ray is crossed precisely where \(H=0,H_x>0\). At such a point the real component has positive derivative, so the ray is crossed clockwise. The top edge is traversed with decreasing \(x\); its upward-slope spatial zeros contribute positively. On a real slice simple zeros alternate their slope signs. The signs at the left and right ends are fixed as time varies. The number of upward-slope zeros thus increases by \((m-(m\bmod2))/2\). This gives the claimed local winding, even when the ordinary Jacobian is zero. For a double root the Jacobian is \(H_{xx}^2-H_x H_{xxx}=H_{xx}^2>0\). Additivity of planar degree gives the sum over the interior of any admissible rectangle. Any finite selection of events fits in one such rational rectangle: in a compact neighborhood there are finitely many events, and its four boundaries can be moved slightly to rational coordinates. This proves the supremum formula for the event sum.

In integral form, on each such rectangle the same integer is
\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0}\frac1\pi\int_R
 \frac{\epsilon^2(H_{xx}^2-H_xH_{xxx})}{(\epsilon^2+H^2+H_x^2)^2}\,dx\,dt .
\]
Pull back the differential of \((u\,dv-v\,du)/(2\pi(\epsilon^2+u^2+v^2))\) under \((u,v)=(H,H_x)\) and use Stokes' theorem. On the compact boundary the resulting one-form converges uniformly to the angular form. This provides a real-variable computation of the boundary integer without tracking the complex zero paths.

## Global counting and the endpoint at zero time

For \(s>0\) let \(K(s)\) count the zeros with positive real and positive imaginary parts, with multiplicities. On each compact positive time interval in \((0,1/2]\), the uniform high-ordinate theorem puts all sufficiently distant zeros on the real line and makes them simple. The zero strip bounds the imaginary coordinate uniformly, and the integral of the positive kernel against \(\cosh(yu)\) excludes the imaginary axis. The remaining zeros therefore move in a bounded region. At a nonreal repeated zero the local argument principle conserves multiplicity in its quadrant. At a real zero of order \(m\) with positive real coordinate there are \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) zeros in the upper half-plane just before the event and none there at or just after it. The Hermite splitting also isolates nonreal multiple zeros. Uniformity bounds all possible exceptional events in a compact region for times bounded away from zero. Compactness and isolation make their number finite there. At time \(1/2\) every zero is real and simple, since the published bound places the all-real threshold strictly below that time and an interior multiple would split backwards into nonreal zeros. Thus \(K(s)\) counts the weighted events at times strictly greater than \(s\). The known all-real ray excludes all later events. [Polymath, Theorems 1.1, 1.5(i)–(ii), 3.2 and Proposition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12438v2)

It remains to control zeros arriving from infinity as \(s\downarrow0\). Local uniform convergence and disjoint disks about any finite selection of nonreal zeros imply \(\liminf_{s\downarrow0}K(s)\ge K_0\). This suffices when \(K_0=\infty\). For finite \(K_0\), use the paired Hadamard product of \(H_0/H_0(0)\). Evenness, order one and nonvanishing at zero remove the linear exponential, and \(\sum |z_j|^{-2}<\infty\). Truncate in symmetric pairs, retaining the finite exceptional factors together in every truncation. The resulting real polynomials \(P_N\) have at most \(4K_0\) nonreal roots. Uniformly in \(N\), for every \(\eta>0\) their modulus is bounded by \(C_\eta e^{\eta|z|^2}\): absorb a finite head polynomial into half the exponent and use \(\log(1+r)\le r\) on the summable tail.

For any real polynomial \(P\) of positive degree, \(P+cP'\) for real \(c\ne0\) retains at least as many real zeros counted with multiplicity. Existing roots of order \(k\) retain \(k-1\) copies. Rolle's theorem applied to \(e^{x/c}P(x)\) supplies one zero between consecutive distinct roots. If there is a real root, one extra critical point occurs before the first root for \(c>0\), or after the last for \(c<0\), since the exponential times the polynomial tends to zero at that outer endpoint. The degree is unchanged. Consequently \((1-s\partial_x^2/j)^jP_N\), a product of real first-order factors, has at most \(4K_0\) nonreal roots. Letting \(j\to\infty\) gives the same bound for \(e^{-s\partial_x^2}P_N\) by polynomial convergence and the argument principle. Constants are immediate.

For fixed \(s>0\) the latter polynomial equals the Gaussian average of \(P_N(z+iv)\) with density \(e^{-v^2/(4s)}/\sqrt{4\pi s}\). The uniform sub-Gaussian bound just established gives locally uniform convergence after integration. The limit is \(H_s(z)/H_0(0)\), since the Gaussian average of each cosine multiplies its Fourier weight by \(e^{su^2}\); the double-exponential theta decay justifies the exchange. Applying the disk argument once more proves \(K(s)\le K_0\). Together the two inequalities identify the limit and complete the counting argument. This endpoint step avoids any uniform high-ordinate estimate at zero time.

## A weighted check on the collision count

For a regular positive time let \(y_j=\Im z_j\) and \(E=\sum_{y_j>0}y_j^2\). This sum has finite support. Taking imaginary parts in the zero velocity from Proposition 3.1(i) and symmetrizing gives
\[
 E'(t)=-2\sum_{j<k}\frac{(y_j-y_k)^2}{|z_j-z_k|^2}.
\]
Here all signed zeros are included. The remote real zeros contribute an absolutely convergent inverse-square tail. Pairs of real zeros contribute zero. Each conjugate pair contributes one to the unordered sum. On a compact positive time interval there are finitely many multiple events and the local analytic zero branches admit Puiseux expansions, so the piecewise derivative integrates to the endpoint difference. Equivalently integrate on the regular intervals and take one-sided endpoint limits using continuity and nonnegativity of the dissipation. For \(s>0\) one obtains the bound
\[
 E(s)\ \ge\ 4\sum_{\substack{x>0,\ t>s\\F(x,t)=0}}
 (t-s)\left\lfloor\operatorname{ord}_x H_t/2\right\rfloor .
\]
Indeed there are \(2K(t)\) conjugate pairs off the real line when counted at simple times. This gives a second comparison of the number of events, their times and the initial imaginary displacements.
