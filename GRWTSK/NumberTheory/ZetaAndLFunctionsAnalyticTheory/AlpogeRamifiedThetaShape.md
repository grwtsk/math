## The elliptic scalar and the normalized translates

The residue characteristic \(p=d\) is prime with \(d\equiv7\bmod8\), so \(p\ge7\). Write \(R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}\), \(\pi=\iota(\sqrt{-d})\), and normalize \(v(p)=1\). The discriminant of the period lattice is one. Its short equation \(y^2=x^3-g_2x/4-g_3/4\) has good reduction for this differential. Indeed the integral CM \(j\) and \(\Delta=1\) imply integral \(g_2^3\) and \(g_3^2\) by \(j=1728g_2^3/\Delta\) and \(\Delta=g_2^3-27g_3^2\); \(2,3\) are units. Adjoin the invariants and torsion coordinates under \(\iota\) to a local number field as needed, using algebraicity of the normalized invariants from these identities.

Put \(\omega=(1+\sqrt{-d})/2\), a CM endomorphism with \(\omega\bar\omega=(1+d)/4\) prime to \(p\). Its kernel is finite étale on the good model, so every nonzero kernel point has integral \(x=\wp\). The complex isogeny sum
\[
 \omega(\omega-\bar\omega)s_2(\Lambda)
       =\sum_{0\ne P\in\ker\omega}\wp(P)
\]
follows by differentiating the reduced-sigma isogeny formula, or by the elliptic trace identity and its constant from the quasiperiods in §14. As \(\omega\) is a unit at \(p\), this proves \(\pi S\in R\), equivalently \(|S|\le\sqrt p\), and \(s_2(\Lambda)\) is algebraic. The original scalar \(\mathrm{jet}(\log,\mathrm{phC},2)\) is exactly \(S\). In fact \(\mathrm{phC}=-\mathrm{sigS}(\wp_{\rm reg})(l)+S l^2/2-\log(l(t)/t)\). In the short model \(x(z)=\wp(z)=z^{-2}+O(z^2)\), \(y(z)=\wp'(z)/2=-z^{-3}+O(z)\); hence \(t=-x/y=z+O(z^5)\) and \(l(t)/t=1+O(t^4)\). Twice integrating \(\wp_{\rm reg}=O(z^2)\) starts at degree four. Differentiating twice in additive coordinate therefore gives \(S\). ## The integral Hermitian form and the two sheets

Write \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\), \(Z=\{0,1\}\times\mathrm{HH}\), and \(z^\dagger=(1-b,h)\) for \(z=(b,h)\). The embeddings \(\rho_z=\lambda\circ\mathrm{emb}_{\tau(z)}\) are precisely the embeddings of \(E\) extending the fixed embedding of \(K\). The host's relative different of \(E/K\) has support above \(Q_S q\), coprime to \(d\). Thus at \(p=d\) the inverse half-different ideal \(\mathfrak A_0\) equals the unit ideal locally and \(\mathcal O_E\) is étale over \(\mathcal O_K\) locally. With these embeddings
\[
 \mathcal O_E\otimes_{\mathcal O_K}R=\prod_{z\in Z}R.
\]
Indeed the finite étale algebra splits over the strictly henselian \(R\); its factors are labeled by the distinct embeddings on the generic fibre. Equivalently, distinct integral residue characters can be separated by interpolation using unit differences. Trace commutes with base change and is the sum in these coordinates. In particular each site idempotent is an integral finite combination of embedded elements of \(\mathfrak A_0\). [Relative different and inertia exclusions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CyclotomicBounds/RamifiedDiscriminants/OffSmall.lean#L25-L91) · [Strictly henselian finite algebras](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GE) · [Trace and base change](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BVH)

For each integral \(a\) locally at the relevant primes,
\[
              \rho_z(\bar a)\equiv \rho_{z^\dagger}(a)\pmod{\pi R}.        \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\bar{\ }\) is the host CM involution. To check the precision, in the given Galois labeling \(s=((1,-1),1)\) generates inertia at \(d\), changes the sign of \(\sqrt{-d}\), commutes with complex conjugation, and \(s\bar{\ }\) exchanges the two specified sites. These follow by multiplying the host group elements labeling the embeddings; the inertia condition also shows that the fixed local subfield is unramified at \(d\). In the finite local extension each \(a\) integral at the place has \(a-s(a)\) in its maximal ideal since inertia acts trivially on the residue. Its ramification index is exactly 2, as \(E/K\) is unramified there and \(K/\mathbb Q\) is ramified quadratic. Its uniformizer may therefore be taken to be \(\sqrt{-d}\). This proves (1), and exchanging sheets gives its reverse. This valuation argument takes place in the finite local field before scalar extension to \(R\). Reduction only modulo the maximal ideal of \(R\) would lose the required precision. ## Transport of the elliptic quadratic through the fourfold transform

Take the selected perturbed kernel and the \(\mathrm{LocNorm}\) witness \(g,\epsilon\) for it at \(p=d\) from the all-place normalization argument. Its fourfold tensor, after the same Gaussian translation in four independent copies, is an integral finite combination of pullbacks of products of elliptic translates. Indeed for \(\Gamma=g(\Lambda_1^4)\) and the standard elliptic product lattice \(S_0\), the forward relative index \(m=[\Gamma:\Gamma\cap S_0]\) is prime to \(p\), and \(m\Gamma\subset S_0\). Perturbation multiplies a torsion order by at most the two prime-to-\(p\) clearing factors for the original lattices. Multiplication by that order and \(m\) therefore takes every elliptic translate in the tensor into \(S_0\). The original finite transform has \(\iota(\epsilon)\in R\), normalization \(m^{-1/2}\in R^\times\), and unit phases by the signed translation law on rational periods. This argument retains the original isometry, representatives and scalar for each perturbed kernel. Express each adapted tangent column at the perturbed lattice by a finite integral linear combination of embedded tested vectors \(v_k\), and do so independently in the four copies. Let \(C_{\ell k}\) be the elliptic output coordinates of \(g(v_k)\), writing a vector in its specified copy. For each elliptic period \(\omega_0\), \(\omega_0 v_k\) belongs to the corresponding input lattice. It follows that \(m C_{\ell k}\Lambda\subset\Lambda\). Endomorphism scalars of this CM elliptic curve belong to its quadratic order; thus \(C_{\ell k}\in K\) in the chosen complex embedding and is integral at \(p\). In particular \(C_{\ell k}-\bar C_{\ell k}\in\sqrt{-d}\,\mathcal O_{K,p}\). This statement uses only the scalar multipliers of tested directions; it imposes no integrality on the entries of \(g\) in an arbitrary ambient basis. Write \(M_{\ell x}=\sum_k\iota(C_{\ell k})A_{kx}\) for the actual column in elliptic coordinates, where \(A_{kx}\in R\), and \(x\) labels a copy, a site and a rectangular matrix unit.

For every \(k,j\), the sum \(\sum_\ell C_{\ell k}\bar C_{\ell j}\) is \(H_0(v_k,v_j)\) within one copy and zero across different copies. The area factor converting the elliptic standard metric to the ordinary scalar product cancels the reciprocal area in the original metric. Consequently the Gram matrix of the *squares*, with entries \(\sum_\ell M_{\ell x}M_{\ell y}\), is congruent modulo \(\pi R\) to the bilinear extension by \(A_{kx},A_{jy}\) of the preceding Hermitian Gram. This replacement conjugates only the integral endomorphisms \(C_{\ell j}\). For the composed elliptic quadratic \(Q_{\rm ell}=\sum_\ell(\sum_x M_{\ell x}X_x)^2\), the opposite-sheet calculation gives the coefficientwise polynomial congruence
\[
   Q_{\rm ell}=2\sum_{j=1}^{4}\sum_{h,a,b}
          X_{j,0,h,a,b}X_{j,1,h,a,b}+\pi Q_1,\qquad Q_1\in R[X].

Both orders contribute the same cross coefficient. In particular the polynomial \(Q_1\) has zero constant term. ## The actual ramified entry threshold

For the same tied kernel the shape also gives the source's \(\mathrm{EntriesAtDiscriminant}\) with threshold \(-\tfrac12\sum_h\mathrm{ctD}(u,i+1,h)\). We give the counting argument since discarding alternation in a column can weaken this bound. Take any source choice \(I\) of strictly increasing target indices in every Young column. Regard each minor in \(G_{xz}\) as a determinant of linear differential operators in its sheet; its auxiliary matrix entries \(X\) are polynomial indeterminates. Evaluate the product of all minors on \(e^{-S\beta}\mathrm{sg'}\) at zero. Expand the determinants, keeping each derivative occurrence labeled by its factor and row. The mixed derivatives of the centered exponential are the sum over matchings pairing a positive-sheet occurrence with a negative-sheet occurrence of the same site and the same input and target indices. Each pair contributes \(-S\); all unmatched occurrences differentiate \(\mathrm{sg'}\). This follows from differentiating the exponential once and using the Leibniz rule, or by the Taylor expansion in the independent paired variables. Labeled matchings account for repeated indices with integer multiplicities. The remaining raw derivatives are integral. [Exact minor operator, raw evaluation and claimed bound](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/EntAtD.lean#L24-L76)

Fix a paired site \(h\). Write \(\lambda^+,\lambda^-\) for the two diagrams, with \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) the first two row lengths and \(|\lambda|\) the number of boxes. Each matching uses at most \(|\lambda^+|\) pairs at \(h\). To sharpen this on the marked set, group the positive height-one columns separately.
