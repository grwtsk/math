# Small-prime torsion and simultaneous Selmer windows

grwtsk contributors

This note gives an analytic derivation of the uniform cohomological estimate called LemmaK and the simultaneous dyadic and fixed-odd-prime estimate called ThmBab in the Alpöge source at commit 40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005. The latter estimate covers the full positive twist range on selected square windows, including the moving quadratic fields. A sharper Bernoulli estimate also gives the source's ThmBc at all odd primes. The opposite-sign odd-prime sum and the final analytic inequality require further work. The companion Lean file proves the finite-group bounds used in the first argument. The arithmetic arguments in this note have not been formalized here or independently reviewed. ## A cohomology bound retaining small-prime powers

Let \(p\) be prime, \(L\) totally imaginary of degree \(D\) and absolute discriminant \(\Delta\), with \(\mu_p\subset L\) and \(\mu_4\subset L\) if \(p=2\). Write \(S_p\) for the places over \(p\), \(e\ge1\), \(k=2e\), \(N=2k+4\). There is an absolute \(C_K\ge1\) such that
\[
 \log |H^2(G_{L,S_p},\mathbb Z_p(e+1))|
 \ \le\ \exp((2eD)^{C_K}\log(2\Delta)).                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here and below global cohomology is continuous. The set of places, local restrictions and twist character in the source are uniquely specified up to the usual isomorphism and conjugation of decomposition groups. [Cohomological definitions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean#L45-L84)

Put \(K=K_k(\mathcal O_L)[p^\infty]\), finite by Quillen's finite generation and Borel's rank calculation. Quillen–Lichtenbaum identifies \(K\) with this \(H^2\). At 2 the assumption on real places removes all real-place corrections. Localization from integers to \(S_p\)-integers is an isomorphism on this primary part because positive even \(K\)-groups of finite fields vanish and \(K_{k-1}\) of a residue field of characteristic \(p\) has order prime to \(p\). [Meng Fai Lim, *On the structure of even K-groups of rings of algebraic integers*, v2 (2023), §§3.2–3.3, Corollary 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00168v2#page=6) · [Bruno Kahn, *The Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture at the prime 2*, Theorem 1](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bruno.kahn/preprints/QL2.pdf#page=2)

Set \(\Gamma=\mathrm{GL}_N(\mathcal O_L)\). The base component of the infinite loop space of algebraic \(K\)-theory is \(B\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal O_L)^+\). Apply Mathew's Theorem 6.3 to this connected space with \(m=1\). The \(p\)-exponent of its degree-\(k\) Hurewicz kernel is at most \(k+4\). Integral stable homology in that degree is already \(H_k(\Gamma,\mathbb Z)\) by van der Kallen–Maazen stability, valid for \(N\ge2k+1\). Plus construction preserves integral homology. Tensoring the resulting map on \(K\) with the flat \(\mathbb Z_p\) gives exactly the source's KBridge, since \(k+4\le k(k+5)/2=\sum_{j=1}^k(j+2)\) for \(k\ge2\). [Mathew, Theorem 6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.07561#page=10) · [Bayer-Fluckiger, Emery and Houriet, v3, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5953v3#page=4) · [Target KBridge](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/GeneratorCountTuples/Bridge.lean#L10-L23)

Use the kernel \(\Gamma'\) of reduction modulo 3. Its index \(I\) is at most \(3^{DN^2}\). Its regular representation on \(\mathcal O_L^N\) embeds it in the integral principal congruence subgroup of level 3, which is torsion-free. Indeed, a matrix \(1+3^aB\) of prime order \(r\), with \(a\ge1\) and \(B\) nonzero modulo 3, cannot have \(r\)th power 1. For \(r\ne3\) the leading term has exact 3-adic order \(a\), and for \(r=3\) it has order \(a+1\), strictly below the other terms. Transfer from \(\Gamma\) to \(\Gamma'\) followed by pushforward is \(I\) on integral homology. The composite from \(K\) to \(H_k(\Gamma',\mathbb Z)\) thus has kernel killed by \(p^b\), \(b=k+4+v_p(I)\), by discarding the factor of \(I\) invertible on the \(p\)-group.

The reduction map for the multiplication-by-\(p\) coefficient sequence embeds \(K/pK\) into \(H^2(G_{L,S_p},\mu_p^{\otimes(e+1)})\). This coefficient module is isomorphic to \(\mu_p\). The Kummer sequence and Brauer reciprocity bound the order by \(h_Lp^D\), since there are at most \(D\) finite places in \(S_p\) and no real ones. Minkowski gives an ideal of norm at most \(2^D\sqrt\Delta\) in each class. The number of ideals of norm at most \(T\) is at most \(T^2\zeta_L(2)\le T^2\zeta(2)^D\), hence \(h_L\le8^D\Delta\). Also \(p\le D+1\). Since \(|K[p]|=|K/pK|\), successive multiplication by \(p\) bounds the order of the composite kernel by \((h_Lp^D)^b\). [Kummer and dyadic rank comparison in Kahn's proof of Corollary 2](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~bruno.kahn/preprints/QL2.pdf#page=16)

For the image, use Ash's contractible well-rounded retract \(W\). Write \(h(v)\) for the real trace of the Hermitian value on \(v\). Its cells, normalized to minimum 1, are specified by their minimal vectors. For the free lattice \(\mathcal O_L^N\) there is a finite set \(\Phi\) such that all minimal vectors of any one well-rounded form can simultaneously be moved into \(\Phi\) by an element of \(\Gamma\), with
\[
 |\Phi|\le N^{3N^2D^2}D^{5N^3D^4}\Delta^{9N^3D^3}.
\]
The hypotheses \(N\ge5\) and \(L\ne\mathbb Q\) hold. Put \(H=DN^2+1\), an upper bound on the dimension of the vector space of affine functions on the space of Hermitian forms. For a flag of cells, move the minima of a point in the relative interior of its smallest face into \(\Phi\). These include the minima for each cell of the flag. A closed cell with those minima is obtained by imposing their equalities \(h(v)=1\) on the common region \(h(w)\ge1\) for all nonzero lattice vectors in the positive cone. Indeed, a convex combination of a point satisfying these equalities with an interior point of that cell has exactly the prescribed minima whenever the interior point has positive coefficient. Each system of equalities can be spanned by at most \(H\) affine functions \(h(v)-1\). Recording these generators for each of at most \(H+1\) faces gives at most \((|\Phi|+2)^{(H+2)^2}\) flag orbits, allowing separators and padding. Each splits into at most \(I\) orbits for \(\Gamma'\). The latter acts freely because cell stabilizers are finite. Its quotient of the barycentric subdivision is a classifying space. Each boundary column in degree \(k+1\) has Euclidean norm at most \(k+2\), even if quotient faces coincide. The lattice determinant bound for the torsion of its cokernel therefore bounds \(\log|\mathrm{Tor}\,H_k(\Gamma',\mathbb Z)|\) by \(I(|\Phi|+2)^{(H+2)^2}\log(k+2)\). Adding the kernel cost \(b\log(h_Lp^D)\) proves (1), since \(N=4e+4\) and all exponents in \(\log|\Phi|\) are fixed polynomials in \(e,D\). This use of a congruence cover accounts for the primes excluded by a direct prime-to-small-torsion estimate. [Ash's retract and Gabber's lemma in Bayer-Fluckiger–Emery–Houriet, §§2.2–3.3 and Corollary 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5953v3)

## Split class modules in the actual fields

Let \(M\) be the fixed abelian number field represented by the source's pair consisting of a conductor multiple \(m\) and a subgroup of its group of units. Let \(q,d\) be primes prime to \(m\), with \(q\equiv3\bmod8\), \(d\equiv7\bmod16\). The field identified by the stabilizer in IsCompField is
\[
 E_s=M(\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d},\zeta_{2^{s+2}}+\zeta_{2^{s+2}}^{-1}).
\]
This follows by the fixed-field/compositum correspondence and the two primitive quadratic characters of discriminants \(-q,-d\). Put \(L_0=M(i,\sqrt{-q},\sqrt{-d})\) and \(L_\infty=L_0(\mu_{2^\infty})\). Then \(E_s(i)=L_0(\mu_{2^{s+2}})\), with \([E_s(i):E_s]\le2\). For \(A_e=\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p(-e)\) write \(\mathcal S(F,e)\) for the kernel of localization in \(H^1(G_{F,S_p},A_e)\) at every \(v\mid p\). Restriction from \(E_s\) to \(E_s(i)\) costs at most one unit of 2-length. Its kernel is contained in an inflation of \(H^1(C_2,A_e^{G_{E_s(i)}})\) if the extension is nontrivial. The invariants are cyclic of finite order since a finite field extension of \(\mathbb Q\) has open cyclotomic image and \(e>0\). This cohomology group is a cyclic subquotient killed by 2. [Field and layer definitions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/StmtFields.lean#L29-L56)

There is a bound \(R_0(M)\) on the minimal number of \(\mathbb Z_2\)-generators of the Galois group \(X'\) of the maximal unramified abelian pro-2 extension of \(L_\infty\) split at the primes over 2, uniform in \(q,d\). To see this, start at \(F_\infty=M(\mu_{2^\infty})\), the cyclotomic \(\mathbb Z_2\)-extension of \(M(i)\). Ferrero–Washington's vanishing of \(\mu\), the Iwasawa structure theorem and unramified class field theory give finite \(\mathbb Z_2\)-generation of the unramified abelian module over \(F_\infty\). The maximal unramified pro-2 group \(G_0\) therefore has finite generator rank by its mod-2 Frattini quotient. Let \(G\) allow ramification also at the primes above \(q,d\), retaining the unramified condition above 2. The number of the newly allowed primes is at most \(6[M:\mathbb Q]\). Indeed, in \(\mathbb Q(\mu_{2^\infty})\) the indices of the closed cyclic groups generated by \(q,d\) are respectively 2 and 4, by \(v_2(q^2-1)=3\), \(v_2(d^2-1)=4\) and lifting the exponent. There are at most \([M:\mathbb Q]\) times as many primes after compositum. Tame pro-2 inertia at each new prime is procyclic. Killing its normal closure recovers \(G_0\), hence the generator count for \(G\) increases by at most \(6[M:\mathbb Q]\). The extension \(L_\infty/F_\infty\) has degree at most four and is unramified outside these primes, including at 2 since the quadratic discriminants are odd. The index-at-most-four subgroup for \(L_\infty\) surjects onto \(X'\). Indeed the conjugates of any finite abelian unramified 2-extension over \(L_\infty\) have compositum a 2-extension Galois over \(F_\infty\) satisfying the indicated ramification conditions. The pro-2 Schreier bound proves the assertion. For an odd prime in the given finite set \(B'\), start instead with its fixed abelian field \(L_0=L(p)\supset\mu_p\). The same \(\mu=0\) result directly gives a finite generator bound \(R_p\) for the split module of \(L_\infty=L_0(\mu_{p^\infty})\). Choose these bounds positive. [Iwasawa, *On $\mathbf{Z}_l$-extensions of algebraic number fields*, Annals 98 (1973), 246–326](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970784) · [Ferrero–Washington, *The Iwasawa invariant μ vanishes for abelian number fields*, Annals 109 (1979), 377–395](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971116)

In either tower write \(\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(L_\infty/L_0)\cong\mathbb Z_p\), \(\gamma\) a topological generator, \(u=\chi_{\rm cyc}(\gamma)\), \(a=v_p(u-1)\ge1\) and \(a\ge2\) at 2. The integer \(a\) stays bounded on the moving dyadic family because \([L_0:\mathbb Q]\le8[M:\mathbb Q]\). At level \(L_h\) of index \(p^h\), inflation–restriction identifies
\[
 \mathcal S(L_h,e)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\Gamma^{p^h}}(X',A_e).
\]
Indeed, the cohomological dimension of \(\Gamma^{p^h}\) is 1 and \(H^1(\Gamma^{p^h},A_e)=0\), since multiplication by \(u^{-ep^h}-1\ne0\) on the divisible module is surjective. The local cyclotomic image at each \(v\mid p\) is also open and satisfies the same vanishing. Thus the strict restrictions descend exactly to characters killing the decomposition groups above \(p\); outside \(p\) unramifiedness is retained. Conjugating the chosen places leaves this kernel unchanged. Poitou–Tate identifies the Pontryagin dual of this strict group with the strict kernel in degree two. It therefore embeds in \(H^2(G_{L_h,S_p},\mathbb Z_p(e+1))\). By (1) it is finite. Write its length as \(\ell_h(e)\). [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, I, Theorem 4.10, applied to finite coefficients and passed to limits](https://mail.jmilne.org/math/Books/adt.html) · [Source strict group and duality convention](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GaloisCohomology/Statement.lean#L65-L110)

## One common window for all positive twists

Choose \(r\) minimal generators for \(X'\), \(r\le R_p\) with \(R_2=R_0(M)\). Lift the action of \(\gamma\) to a matrix \(U\) on \(\mathbb Z_p^r\). Its reduction mod \(p\) is conjugate to the action on \(X'/pX'\), hence unipotent because a pro-\(p\) group acts. In particular the lift is invertible. The dual description above gives the order of the quotient \(X'/((u^eU)^{p^h}-1)X'\), with matrices understood through the presentation. Let \(h_0=\lceil\log_p R_p\rceil+1\). For every \(e\), \(V=(u^eU)^{p^{h_0}}\equiv1\bmod p^2\), by the nilpotence index \(\le r\) mod \(p\) and one further \(p\)th power. The binomial theorem gives \(V^p-1=p(V-1)B\) with \(B\) an integral matrix congruent to 1 mod \(p\). Iteration gives equality of images \((V^{p^l}-1)\mathbb Z_p^r=p^l(V-1)\mathbb Z_p^r\), hence the same equality after projection to \(X'\). Thus
\[
 \ell_h(e)\le\ell_{h_0}(e)+r\max(0,h-h_0).
\]
For \(h<h_0\), the smaller-level relations contain the larger-level relations. For \(h>h_0\), the image of \(V-1\) has at most \(r\) generators. At the moving dyadic field \(E_s(i)\) one has \(h\le s+2\). At odd \(p\) the source quantifies over the layer of degree \(p^{j_0}\), \(j_0=\lfloor\log_p R_p\rfloor+3\), precisely the unique \(L_{j_0}\) up to \(L_0\)-isomorphism.

Fix \(C_t\ge1\), \(0<\epsilon<1/52\), \(A_1\) and large \(X\), and put \(Z=\lfloor X^{C_t}\rfloor\). For any eigenvalue \(\alpha\) of \(U^{p^{h_0}}\), at most one positive \(e\le Z\) satisfies
\[
 v_p(\alpha-u^{-ep^{h_0}})> a+h_0+\log_p Z .
\]
Indeed lifting the exponent gives valuation \(a+h_0+v_p(e-f)\) for the difference of two distinct comparison points with indices \(e,f\). The ultrametric inequality rules out two such exceptions. The valuation extends to a splitting field normalized by \(v_p(p)=1\). Thus there are at most \(r\) exceptional integers for this prime. Off them, the integral matrix \((u^eU)^{p^{h_0}}-1\) has nonzero determinant of valuation at most \(r(a+h_0+\log_p Z)\); its cokernel surjects onto the required quotient of \(X'\). This reasoning also handles repeated eigenvalues and \(p\)-torsion in \(X'\).

For the exceptions at the bounded layer, (1) bounds \(\ell_{h_0}(e)\) by \(\exp((2eD_*)^{C_K}\log(2\Delta_*))/\log p\). The dyadic field at that layer has bounded degree \(D_*=O_M(1)\) and \(\log(2\Delta_*)=O_M(\log(2qd))\), since it lies in the cyclotomic field of conductor dividing \(m\,2^{a+h_0}qd\) and the conductor–discriminant formula applies. The odd fields and their bounded layers are fixed.

Let \(R=R_0(M)+\sum_{p\in B'}R_p\), \(\eta=\epsilon/(2C_K)\) and \(J=2^{R+2}(2C_KC_t/\epsilon)^R\), as in the source. The union of all exceptional sets has at most \(R\) members. Put \(y_0=X^{1/J}\) and \(y_{i+1}=y_i^{2C_t/\eta}\). The \(R+1\) intervals \((y_i^\eta,y_i^{2C_t}]\), \(0\le i\le R\), are disjoint and \(y_R^2\le X\). One interval misses the exceptional set. With its \(Y=y_i\), every \(Y\le x\le Y^2\) and every exception \(1\le e<t(x)\le x^{C_t}\) satisfy \(e\le x^\eta\).

Assume \(\log(qd)\le X^{\epsilon/(4J)}\). Then \(\log(qd)\le x^{\epsilon/4}\) throughout this window. The bounded-layer logarithmic order for each relevant exception is at most \(\exp(Cx^{3\epsilon/4})\). The other \(O(x^{C_t})\) indices each have length \(O(\log X+s(x)+1)\), with an additional 1 allowed on descending to \(E_s\). Since \(2^{s(x)}\le x^{A_1}\) and \(\log X\le J\log x\), all contributions for each of the specified primes sum to at most \(\exp(x^{4\epsilon/5})\) for large \(x\). The lower threshold on \(X\) depends only on the fixed fields, \(A_1,C_t,\epsilon\); it is uniform in admissible functions \(s,t\) and the moving pair \(q,d\). These are the simultaneous finiteness and sum conclusions of ThmBab. The unweighted sum here equals the source sum over \(1\le e<t(x)\) of \(\mathbb Z_p\)-lengths. ## The odd minus contribution with the source's constant

Let \(E\) be any abelian CM field, \(n=[E:\mathbb Q]\) and \(N=\mathrm{condF}(E)\). Then \(3\le N\), \(n\le N\), and \(E\) embeds into \(\mathbb Q(\mu_N)\). For all \(t\ge2\), every finite set \(P\) of odd primes and every choice of local data,
\[
 \sum_{p\in P}\sum_{1\le e<t}\log|\mathcal S(E,e)^{c=(-1)^{e+1}}|
 \le 3nt^2\log(tN).                                                     \tag{2}
\]
Here \(\mathcal S\) at a summand carries coefficients \(A_e\) at its prime \(p\). This gives ThmBcFree, and hence ThmBc, with zero coefficient on the secondary sum in minusBound. [Definition of the two signs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/LengthEqAddEigen.lean#L65-L75) · [Target minusBound](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/StmtFields.lean#L69-L72)

Take \(j=e+1\), \(p>2\), \(S\) on \(E^+\) consisting of the archimedean places and every place over primes dividing \(N\). All places ramified in \(E/E^+\) are included. In the relative group ring the critical projector is \(\pi_j=(1+(-1)^jc)/2\), since every real-place Frobenius is \(c\). Gambheera–Popescu Theorem 7.14 applies for every odd prime, including primes dividing \(n\) and \(N\). Its relative projected Fitting ideal equality over \(\mathbb Z_p\) involves the torsion subgroup of \(K_{2j-1}(\mathcal O_{E,S})\), the finite \(K_{2j-2}(\mathcal O_{E,S})\), and the nonzero scalar
\(Z_j=\prod_{\chi(c)=(-1)^j}L^{(N)}(1-j,\chi)\) by Artin induction. The product omits Euler factors at all primes dividing \(N\). Matching parity makes the primitive values nonzero and the omitted factors are nonzero for \(j\ge2\). In particular the formula uses no regulator or derivative. Equivariance of Poitou–Tate, localization and Quillen–Lichtenbaum gives
\[
 \log|\pi_j\mathcal S(E,e)|\le \log w_{p,j}+v_p(Z_j)\log p,\qquad
 w_{p,j}=|H^0(E,\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p(j))|.
\]
Indeed Poitou–Tate has target \(\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p\) with trivial action after the invariant map, so duality preserves this sign. The inclusion from integral even \(K\)-groups into the localized ones follows from vanishing of the positive even residue-field groups. Degree-one torsion at the \(S\)-integer level equals the cyclotomic invariants by the coefficient exact sequence. These invariants are already in the stated eigenspace. [Local comparison with individual steps and Bernoulli denominator](AlpogeSelmerGrowth.md) · [Gambheera and Popescu, 2025, §7.3, Theorems 7.14 and 7.16](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40687-025-00578-8)

The rational number \(Z_j\) is Galois invariant. Set \(D_j=jN(j+1)!\). The Hurwitz formula and the integer property \((i+1)!B_i\in\mathbb Z\) show that \(D_j L^{(N)}(1-j,\chi)\) is an algebraic integer. To see the latter denominator explicitly, expand \(B_j(a/N)\) by the binomial formula. Multiplication by \((j+1)!N^j\) clears each term; the formula for \(L\) contributes \(N^{j-1}/j\). Consequently \(U_j=D_j^{n/2} Z_j\) is a nonzero rational integer. For every finite \(P\), one has \(\sum_{p\in P}v_p(Z_j)\log p\le\log|U_j|\), since each valuation is bounded by the nonnegative corresponding valuation of \(U_j\). The absolutely convergent Fourier series gives \(\sup_{a\in[0,1]}|B_j(a)|\le 2j!\zeta(j)/(2\pi)^j\le j!\). Thus the absolute value of each denominator-cleared factor is \(\le N^{j+1}(j+1)!\,j!\le(jN)^{2j}\), using \(j+1\le N^{j-1}\) and \(j!\le j^j\). It follows that \(\log |U_j|\le nj\log(jN)\). [Bernoulli Fourier expansion, equation 24.8.3](https://dlmf.nist.gov/24.8.E3)

If \(w_{p,j}=p^a>1\), the cyclotomic image modulo \(p^a\) is a subgroup of the cyclic units of index at most \(n\), and its exponent divides \(j\). Thus \(\varphi(p^a)\le jn\), \(p^a\le2jn\). There are at most \(jn\) odd primes at most \(2jn\). Hence \(\sum_{p\in P}\log w_{p,j}\le jn\log(2jn)\le 2jn\log(jN)\). Summing both bounds for \(2\le j\le t\), using \(\sum_{j=2}^t j\le t^2\), proves (2). In the opposite sign the relative equivariant special value vanishes, so the same numerator argument supplies no estimate there.

The next arithmetic obligation is simultaneous control of the varying odd primes in the opposite sign (ThmHV). For a growing set of primes, \(R\) in this argument can grow, which changes \(J\) and invalidates the fixed-window conclusion. Nor does (1) on its own cover growing twists at the full degree of \(E_s\). The source subsequently combines the three estimates for the concrete growing rows.
