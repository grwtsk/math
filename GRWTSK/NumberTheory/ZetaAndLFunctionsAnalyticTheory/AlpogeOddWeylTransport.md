# Integral transport of the odd Weyl blocks on a decorated class

Authors: grwtsk contributors.

Fix Levent Alpöge's source at 40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005. On a decorated class, the odd embedding uses the inverse transpose followed by reversal of the **columns**. The integral module is generated by divided row operators. We prove its transformation for every rank, dominant integral weight, and invertible frame. We then identify the determinant correction for the class character and the image of the local polynomial lattice. For the standard framed charts we also identify the class stabilizer equations of the given global space and reduce the integral-image comparison to the explicit action of the chosen local model. ## An integral identity on the full general linear group

Write \(n=m+1\), \(J_{i,n+1-i}=1\) for the reversal matrix and \(\Delta_i\) for the top-left \(i\)-row principal minor, with \(\Delta_0=1\). For \(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n\) set \(c=\lambda_n\), \(d_i=\lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1}\), \(\nu=\lambda-c\mathbf1\) and \(\lambda^*_i=-\lambda_{n+1-i}\). On invertible matrices put
\[
 \Phi_\lambda(Y)=(\det Y)^c\prod_{1\le i<n}\Delta_i(Y)^{d_i},
 \qquad \theta(Y)=JY^{-\mathsf t}J,\qquad R(Y)=Y^{-\mathsf t}J .
\]
The \(\Delta_i\) can vanish. Over any commutative ring, split a matrix \(U=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\ C&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) into blocks and let \((E,F)\) be the lower rows of a left inverse. Then
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\E&F\end{pmatrix}U
     =\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det U\,\det F=\det A .
\]
Reversing the row and column orders within the complementary minor contributes the permutation sign twice. Transposition preserves the determinant. Thus
\(\det Y\,\Delta_i(\theta(Y))=\Delta_{n-i}(Y)\). The successive differences for \(\lambda^*\) are \(d_{n-i}\), its final weight is \(-\lambda_1\), and \(\sum d_i=\lambda_1-c\). Multiplying these identities gives
\[
                        \Phi_{\lambda^*}(\theta(Y))=\Phi_\lambda(Y).
\]
This includes \(n=1\), pure determinant characters and negative \(c\). Only the full determinant needs to be invertible. [Block and weight identities in Lean](AlpogeOddWeylTransport.lean)

Write \(W_\nu\) for the exact integral polynomial module in the source and \(\mathcal W_\lambda=(\det Y)^c W_\nu\) for its determinant-weighted functions. We can recover \(\mathcal W_\lambda\) as the integer span of the coefficients in the independent entries of \(T\) of \(\Phi_\lambda((I+T)Y)\). Indeed, without the determinant twist the coefficient lattice is that of \(\Delta_\nu(XY)=\prod\Delta_i(XY)^{d_i}\). Every divided-row word is a coefficient of this generic expression after substituting a product of elementary one-parameter matrices. Conversely translate \(X=I+T\) and eliminate below and above the diagonal over \(\mathbb Z[[T]]\). Every pivot has constant term one, so the elimination factors and their inverses give integral elementary parameters with zero constant term. The diagonal factors \(1+t\) are included among the source's row operations. At each specified total degree only finitely many divided words contribute. Finally \(\det(I+T)^c\) and its inverse are integral formal series with constant term one, so multiplication by this factor preserves the coefficient span. This works without dividing by any factorial. The map \(\theta\) is a multiplicative involution. Put \(U(T)=J(I+T)^{-\mathsf t}J-I\). Its entries have zero constant term and integer coefficients by the geometric series for the inverse matrix. Applying the same construction twice gives \(T\). In the highest-vector identity substitute \((I+T)Y\); on the dual side we get \(\Phi_{\lambda^*}((I+U(T))\theta(Y))\). Each coefficient after this substitution is an integer linear combination of coefficients before substitution: in degree \(d\) one can truncate all series to degree \(d\) and each contributing monomial in \(U\) has degree at most \(d\) in the outer variables. The inverse substitution gives the reverse inclusion. Hence pullback by \(\theta\) identifies \(\mathcal W_{\lambda^*}\) and \(\mathcal W_\lambda\) **over the integers**. The companion coefficient lemma gives the finite substitution step for any scalar submodule. [Verified integral substitution lemma](AlpogeOddWeylTransport.lean)

For the source's actual \(R\), one still needs the left multiplication \(R(Y)=J\theta(Y)\). The coefficient model for \(\Delta_\nu(XY)\) proves invariance of \(W_\nu\) under left multiplication of \(Y\) by any member of \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)\), by substituting \(X\mapsto Xg\) and \(X\mapsto Xg^{-1}\). The character factor at \(J\) is \(\det(J)^c\in\{1,-1\}\), so the weighted module is invariant as well. Consequently pullback by \(R\) is an integral bijection of the two weighted modules. For example, in rank two \(\lambda=(1,0)\) has \(\Phi_\lambda(Y)=Y_{11}\); evaluating \(\Phi_{\lambda^*}\) directly on \(R(Y)\) gives \(Y_{21}\), whereas the formula with \(\theta(Y)\) gives \(Y_{11}\). The row reversal matters even though the two *modules* agree after transport. A different failure occurs at weight \((p,0)\). The coefficient of \(y_{11}^{p-1}y_{21}\) in the divided lattice is a multiple of \(p\); replacing that lattice by the integral coefficients of all homogeneous polynomials loses this constraint. The frame test is exactly \(H(gY)\in\mathcal W_\lambda\), since the scalar in \(\mathrm{deltaLTw}\) is \((\det g)^{-c}\). Any two bases of this column lattice differ by an integral invertible matrix on the right. This follows by expressing each set of columns in the other, with the two change matrices inverse by linear independence. Hence testing one frame gives the entire intersection in the source. Since \(R(gY)=g^{-\mathsf t}R(Y)\), the frame on the other side is \(g^{-\mathsf t}\). ## Descent at a fixed class and its coefficient place

Let \(e:L\hookrightarrow K_f\) be the specified coefficient-field embedding, \(O\subset K_f\) its local valuation ring, and \(R_0=e^{-1}(O)\). Keep the chart's finite index bijection \((v,s)\mapsto\tau\), its weight equality, and the two rational change matrices \(M_\tau,N_\tau\). The lattice tuple is required to satisfy the original basis hypothesis: each Frobenius-extended component equals the column span over \(O\) of the image of \(N_\tau\) at an even site or \(N_\tau^{-\mathsf t}\) at an odd site. This is a restriction on the common tuple and its frames. [Exact hypothesis on the tuple](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IntegralWeylLatticeSpan/WeylLatticeDescent.lean#L35-L44) On even blocks the new argument is \(Z_\tau=N_\tau^{-1} M_\tau Y_\tau\). On odd blocks the argument after the target basis change is \(R(Z_\tau)\). This follows directly by taking inverse transpose in the product, then reversing columns at the right; the matrix multiplying the old odd argument on the left is \(N_\tau^{\mathsf t}\). The equalities hold after applying the indicated coefficient embedding. The preceding bijection sends each local integral generator to a global one giving the same weighted evaluation on every invertible tuple of frames, and conversely. On the local side every divided word in the integral highest vector is obtained by applying the coefficient-ring map to the same word over \(\mathbb Z\), so the Weyl module over \(O\) is the \(O\)-span of the integral words. The generic-matrix proof of stability also works over \(O\): substitution by \(g\in\mathrm{GL}_n(O)\) and by its integral inverse gives both inclusions on the coefficient span, and \(\det g\) is a unit. Hence each specified column basis computes the full intersection over all bases in the local definition. Take products over sites, then \(R_0\)-linear combinations. A rational polynomial evaluated on all such tuples is determined uniquely: each even or odd block map is a bijection on \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb C)\), the multiplying determinants never vanish there, and the product of these groups is Zariski dense in the product of matrix spaces. Scalar extension introduces no extra rational polynomials. For a finite list of rational generators, write their monomial coefficients as columns of a finite matrix over \(L\), including all monomials in a proposed rational polynomial. Multiply the matrix and the proposed column by a common nonzero scalar to clear denominators in \(R_0\). In the nonzero part of the generator matrix choose an entry of least valuation. Swap it to the first pivot, use integral elementary operations to clear its row and column and repeat on the smaller rectangle. Kernels and membership in the span transform identically over \(R_0\) and \(O\). In diagonal form the only tests are vanishing at zero rows and membership of a rational coordinate divided by a nonzero pivot in \(O\); for a rational quotient this is exactly membership in \(R_0\). Every proposed membership uses finitely many generators, so this argument also applies to an initially infinite family. Thus a rational polynomial whose image belongs to the integral local block lattice has an \(R_0\)-linear expression in the source generators. This proves the polynomial comparison called Weyl lattice descent for the compatible input frames. ## The class character and the comparison of determinant powers

For a standard served chart at stage \(i\), let \(B\) be the chart's rational orthonormal base frame, let \(y\) represent a decorated class, and let \(B'\) be **any** of the source's rational \(p\)-local bases of the Hermitian dual of its class lattice. Use the source's conditions \(p=\operatorname{reschar}(w)\), \(p\nmid T\), hyperspecial level above \(p\), and the simultaneous splitting at all places over \(p\). The class lattice at \(p\) is \(y\) applied to the base lattice and both are self-dual there: the underlying standard unimodular lattices have this property and the word congruence of index supported on \(T\) disappears upon localization. Thus at each selected first place \(P\mid p\), \(\det(B')/\det(B)\) and \(\det(y_P)\) have the same valuation since the two frames differ by an integral unimodular change after transport. The conjugate component requires care: knowledge of a basis at the first place alone does not identify \(B\) as a basis at its conjugate. We address it through the Hermitian Gram determinant below. [The actual dual lattice, place and base-frame hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/ChartRank/HypVin.lean#L39-L82) · [Standard split and dual class lattices](AlpogeDecoratedClassFibres.md)

The double class set is finite. Two powers \(y^b,y^{b+k}\), \(k>0\), belong to the same double class. Write \(y^{b+k}=\gamma y^b\kappa\) with rational unitary \(\gamma\) and \(\kappa\) in the level. No group law on double classes was used. At each selected first place \(\det\kappa_P\) is a unit. Taking determinants in the relation shows that
\[
  \varepsilon=\bigl(\det B'/\det B\bigr)^k/\det\gamma
\]
is a unit there. If \(J_h\) is the Hermitian matrix in the rational basis and \(\sigma\) denotes CM conjugation, then
\[
       \sigma(\varepsilon)\varepsilon
           =\det({}^{\sigma\mathsf t} B' J_h B')^k .
\]
Indeed \(\gamma\) has determinant of norm one and \(B\) is orthonormal. The Gram matrix on the right is invertible integral at the selected place: the two components of \(B'\) are bases of the two split factors of a perfect Hermitian local lattice. Its Gram matrix is the matrix of the perfect pairing in these two bases. This proves the conjugate unit as well, including both inverses. Every coefficient embedding \(\tau\) above \(w\) is one of the designated Frobenius transports of a selected first embedding or its conjugate, and these transports preserve the coefficient valuation rings and their units. Hence all \(\tau(\varepsilon)\) are \(w\)-units. Its class value genuinely lies in the designated coefficient field when the chart uses the true determinant tie and the prescribed unramified Hecke-character values belong to that field. ## The actual global space is a product of fixed spaces

For the true step the stored global space is explicit. The variable set consists of the decorated classes \(c\). The ambient value space is the rational polynomial representation \(V\) of weight \(\mathrm{lamT}\), including its determinant shift. For each stabilizer element \(s\) the source imposes
\[
 \chi_s\,\rho_{\rm Vr}(\gamma_s) f_c=f_c .
\]
Here \(\gamma_s\) is the rational matrix of \(s\), and \(\chi_s\) is the coefficient-field character of the two frame changes. Its lattice further imposes \(f_c\in\mathrm{delAtr}(L_c^\sharp)\) at each \(c\). These assertions follow by substituting the original definitions of \(\mathrm{forms}\), \(\mathrm{hlat}\), the class-sum representation and \(\mathrm{latOf}\). There is no equation coupling the values at different class representatives in this space. A particular theta vector in the space can of course have correlated values. Over \(\mathbb C\) the actual \(\mathrm{Fi}\) uses the corresponding weighted polynomial at every class and the same stabilizer test on frames. The chart reads the inverse type character and the inverse tuple character on these coordinates. ## Scope and verification

For the true step, the global fixed-space description above removes the possibility of an additional interclass linear constraint; a rank computation is an alternative route. To apply the calculation to an independently selected entire chain still requires the simultaneous realization of the source's local action, away character and all rational frame embeddings on that chain, along with its identification with the chosen automorphic lift. In particular the determinant character's prescribed uniformizer values must belong to the **same** coefficient field. The result here concerns the full polynomial class spaces on the specified standard charts; it supplies no spectral nonvanishing or global analytic estimate for a theta vector.
