## A frame for the first rows

The source differentiates in directions \(\sum_{a'}X_{s,i,a'}\partial_{s,a',b}\) and takes determinants over \(i,b\) in each diagram column. Evaluate the finitely many frame indeterminates in the rational function field over \(\mathbb C_p\) in independent variables \(Y_{s,i,a'}\), with its Gauss valuation. Write \(L=\lfloor\log_p a\rfloor\). The evaluation row \(y_{s,i}\) has entries \(Y_{s,i,a'}\), except at each marked top row \(i=0\), where its entries are \(Y_{s,0,a'}^{p^L}\). For a nonzero polynomial its evaluated valuation is the minimum of its coefficient valuations. Indeed distinct monomials stay distinct under these coordinatewise substitutions, and the Gauss valuation is the minimum on polynomials. Multiplicativity follows by dividing each of two factors by a coefficient of minimal valuation and reducing their product in the polynomial ring over the residue field. Thus the valuation also exists on fractions. All frame manipulations take place in its valuation ring \(R\). [Source diagram evaluation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/StatementP.lean#L19-L46)

At site \(t\) take all signed odd integers \(j\) with \(|j|\le L\) and \(t-j\in T\), with associated linear forms
\[
                 \lambda_{t,j}(v)=\sum_{a'} Y_{t-j,0,a'}^{p^{L+j}}v_{a'} .       \tag{2}
\]
There are at most \(2L+1\) labels. By the rank inequality choose that many distinct pivot rows from the non-top rows at \(t\), excluding row zero only when \(t\in T\). The matrix of (2) on these rows has unit determinant. In the reduction its determinant is a polynomial. Specialize the pivot rows to distinct coordinate basis vectors. This leaves the variables of every form (2) indeterminate since \(t-j\) has parity opposite to \(t\). At a given pivot coordinate, the variable identifies the source site and the positive exponent identifies \(j\) whenever the source repeats. Thus two different permutations give different monomials in the specialized determinant, and the diagonal monomial has coefficient one. This includes an empty pivot matrix.

Taking the inverse pairing matrix over \(R\) gives dual pivot rows \(x_{t,e_j}\) with \(\lambda_{t,i}(x_{t,e_j})=\delta_{ij}\); the original pivots are integral linear combinations of these rows. Replace each remaining non-top row \(y\) by \(x=y-\sum_j\lambda_{t,j}(y)x_{t,e_j}\). Leave the marked top row fixed. Each old non-top row is an integral linear combination of new non-top rows, and \(\lambda_{t,j}\) vanishes on every new non-top row except its matching pivot. These are exact identities over the characteristic-zero valuation ring. [Upstream signed labels](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/Labels.lean#L57-L93) · [Upstream pivot-row comparison](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GaussJetBounds/GaussJetProofs/FrameA.lean#L48-L110)

Expand each column determinant by multilinearity after this replacement. A term with a repeated new row has zero determinant, since constant-coefficient derivatives commute. At a marked site the old top row contributes the fixed new top row exactly once per column, and all remaining rows exclude top. The resulting mixed derivatives have exponents \(m_{s,i,b}\) in the new directions. Their site totals are \(w_s\), of which the non-top total is \(D_s\). Each fixed non-top row has degree at most \(C_s\) across *all* \(b\). It can occur at most once per column, and at marked sites it can occur only in columns of height at least two. Evaluating \(X=y\) commutes with differentiating in \(Z\); repeating each new direction gives exactly \((\prod m_{s,i,b}!)[(Z')^m]\) after the linear substitution \(Z_{s,a',b}=\sum_i x_{s,i,a'} Z'_{s,i,b}\). ## Which coefficients can lose a factor of the prime

Write \(\beta\) for the admitted B0 witness. As in the unmarked calculation, composing with the inverse orbit logarithm gives an integral residual composed with that inverse, multiplied by \(\exp(\pm\sum_{a',b}\beta(Z_{\bullet,a',b}))\), in coordinates rescaled by the place unit \(ep\). At total degree \(n\) the residual has valuation at least \(-\rho n\), also after the integral linear frame substitution. Each logarithm coefficient has valuation at least \(\eta-\rho n\), with a possible loss of one for a pure pair with powers \(p^k,p^h\) at opposite-parity sites \(s,t\), \(k>h\), \(t=s+k-h\) modulo \(d\). This follows from the two split coboundary bounds, splitting off a nonzero base-\(p\) digit and applying the original torus invariance. The orientation uses the *actual* positive odd difference \(e=k-h\) below. The uniform sign and unit rescaling multiply each degree by a common unit. A proper redistribution of a \(p\)-power among multiple new rows gains at least one \(p\) by the multinomial theorem in characteristic \(p\). Collect across the original row index \(a'\) **before exponentiating**. A term still losing valuation must have a single high row \(i\) and single low row \(r\), at the same matrix column \(b\). Its coefficient contains the factor
\[
         \sum_{a'}x_{s,i,a'}^{p^k}x_{t,r,a'}^{p^h}.                    \tag{3}
\]
Here the same \(\beta\) occurs for every original \(a'\) by B0. Only \(p^k\le w_s\) matters for a target derivative, hence \(e\le L\). Work modulo \(pR\). If the high row is marked top, (3) equals \(\lambda_{t,+e}(x_{t,r})^{p^h}\) in this quotient. If the low row is marked top, (3) equals \(\lambda_{s,-e}(x_{s,i})^{p^k}\), since the low top variable was raised to \(p^L\). Thus when the opposite row is non-top, only its matching signed pivot can incur the loss. These congruences use a full factor of \(p\). The argument for a *unit determinant* used the residue field, whereas the ring \(R/pR\) used for (3) may have nilpotents. Its characteristic suffices for the binomial identities. ## Counting the exceptional occurrences

Expand the exponential of the collected logarithm by monomials. At each target multi-index only finitely many terms contribute since every logarithm monomial has positive degree. For multiplicity \(n\), \(v_p(n!)\le n\eta\), so the baseline \(\eta\) of each occurrence pays its exponential denominator. The remaining loss is at most the total number of exceptional occurrences surviving (3). There are three disjoint classes.

1. **Both rows non-top.** Count one unit of flow from \(s\) to \(t\) at lag \(e\bmod d\), odd and between 1 and \(d-1\). It uses at least \(p^{e\bmod d}\) of the high-site degree and one at the low site. All these counts together fit within the non-top degree \(D_s\) at every site, so their sum is at most the source supremum \(M(D)\).
2. **The high row is marked top at \(s\).** Write \(E=\mathrm{dTop}(s)\), the first positive odd lag reaching a marked site, with \(E=\infty\) allowed. For each odd \(e<E\) the low site is unmarked, and all surviving occurrences use its one \(+e\) pivot. Their number is at most \(\min(A_s/p^e,C_{s+e})\). If \(E\) is finite, the total number at *all* \(e\ge E\) is at most \(A_s/p^E\), since every such occurrence uses at least \(p^E\) of the common top degree \(A_s\). For infinite \(E\) the tail is empty. The sum of these bounds is exactly \(\gamma A_s-\mathrm{topTerm}(s)\), by the source definition and \(\sum_{e\ge1,\ e\ \mathrm{odd}}p^{-e}=\gamma\). An occurrence with both rows marked top lies solely in this class.
3. **Only the low row is marked top at \(t\).** For each odd \(e\), its high \(-e\) pivot has degree at most \(C_{t-e}\), and each occurrence consumes at least \(p^e\) there. A contributing lag requires \(p^e\le w_{t-e}\). Thus \(C_{t-e}\le W_c(t)\) from the source supremum. It is a supremum of bounded natural numbers since there are finitely many sites. Summing over odd lags bounds the number by \(\gamma W_c(t)\).

Multiply each ordinary coefficient by its raw derivative factorials and apply Legendre's formula. If \(s_p(m)\) denotes the base-\(p\) digit sum, the rounding loss is \(\eta\sum_{s,i,b}s_p(m_{s,i,b})\le dN_0N_1(1+\log_p a)\), since every entry is at most \(a\). Each coefficient of the evaluated minor pairing therefore has valuation at least
\[
   \gamma\sum_s w_s-M(D)-\sum_{s\in T}(\gamma A_s-\mathrm{topTerm}(s)+\gamma W_c(s))
                        -dN_0N_1(1+\log_p a).
\]
The original \(\mathrm{lpF}(p,d,D)=\gamma\sum_sD_s-M(D)\) and \(\sum_sD_s=\sum_s w_s-\sum_{s\in T}A_s\) give (1). Applying the Gauss valuation comparison returns every coefficient before evaluation. The upstream proof constructs signed Frobenius frames and proves a monomial weight estimate using dual weights. The argument here counts exceptional occurrences of the expanded logarithm as a feasible flow and charges marked endpoints directly to their original row degrees. It shares the source's B0–B4 assumptions, torus action and geometric inputs. Two tests distinguish hypotheses needed in the argument. First, replacing the independent top indeterminates by elements of \(\mathbb F_p\) makes all their Frobenius powers coincide. Labels at the same site can then give zero pivot determinant; Gauss evaluation preserves a polynomial coefficient that a small-field specialization can lose. In an exact finite control take \(p=5,d=2,L=3,N_0=9\) and the signed labels \(-3,-1,1,3\) at a single receiving site. In \((\mathbb F_5[t]/(t^7+t+1))[u]/(u^2)\), four integral row changes gave all 432 predicted pairings for the nine new rows and lower exponents \(5^0,5^1,5^2\). The finite field check uses \(t^{5^7}=t\) modulo the polynomial and the absence of roots in \(\mathbb F_5\), sufficient since 7 is prime. The nilpotent coefficients test the use of a nonreduced characteristic-\(p\) ring in (3).

Second, aggregate site sizes alone lose a linear term. Take \(p=5,d=4,T=\{0\}\), one row of length \(25h\) at site zero and five rows of length \(h\) at site one, the other diagrams empty. The aggregate flow has maximum \(5h\), achieved with lag one. Indeed the two surviving directed edges carry \(x,y\) with \(x+125y\le5h\); thus \(x+y\le5h\). Its unmarked objective is \(5h/4\). For \(h\ge25\), \(D=(0,5h,0,0)\) has zero flow, \(W_c(0)=h\), and \(\mathrm{dTop}(0)=\infty\). Only lags \(e\equiv1\bmod4\) subtract a cap in \(\mathrm{topTerm}(0)\). The subtraction is \(h\) at \(e=1\) and the full \(25h/5^e\) at every such \(e\ge5\). Thus the marked objective before the common logarithmic loss is \(1047h/208\), exceeding the unmarked objective by \(787h/208\). In (1) a single pivot direction at site one has degree at most \(h\), even though all five rows there have total degree \(5h\).
