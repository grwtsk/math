# The exceptional split factors and the definite mass in the theta norm

Fix the archive's actual lift datum of index \(i\). Write \(r=\mathrm{rank}(W_c(i)),\ n=r+t=\mathrm{rank}(V_c(i))\). At each marked split place the finite theta coefficient is a compact subgroup character times its exact squared norm. This evaluates the exceptional part of the Petersson convolution for every second test vector. The target level index times the squared norm restores the missing inverse normalizers. ## Exceptional test and both group actions

Let \(k\) be the completion of \(F\) underneath the selected prime of \(E\), \(O\) its integers, \(\pi\) a uniformizer and \(\kappa\) its residue field of size \(q\). Use the selected prime to identify the split unitary target and source groups with general linear groups over \(k\). Let \(K_a=\mathrm{GL}_a(O)\), \(K_a(1)=\ker(K_a\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\kappa))\). A subgroup \(K_r(1)\subset P\subset K_r\) is the inverse image of \(H=\bar P\). Let \(\lambda:P\to\mathbb C^\times\) factor through \(H\); let \(\mu:k^\times\to\mathbb C^\times\) be tame, \(\eta\) unramified and unitary. Normalize the additive measure so \(M_{n,r}(O)\) has volume one. In the split Schrödinger model put
\[
 \Phi_P\binom{A}{B}={\bf1}_{A\in P,\ B\in\pi M_{t,r}(O)}
                       \lambda(A)\mu(\det A)^{-1}.                      \tag{1}
\]
The characters in (1) have modulus one on the support since they factor there through finite groups. The function is locally constant with compact support. Its squared norm is
\[
 M_P=|H|q^{-nr}.
\]
Indeed precisely \(|H|\) of the \(q^{nr}\) residue matrices are allowed. The normalized measures on the upper and lower matrix spaces multiply to the original Haar measure by uniqueness. Their separate support volumes are \(|H|q^{-r^2}\) and \(q^{-tr}\). [Exact local function](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/LocalNewformFunction.lean#L267-L280) · [Single-block action, norm and integral hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TorsionBlocks/LocalIntegralGL.lean#L15-L35)

For the \(r\)-side group the specified action sends \(\Phi(X)\) to \((\mu\eta)(\det g)|\det g|^{n/2}\Phi(Xg)\). In a nonzero overlap the upper block satisfies \(A,Ag\in P\), hence \(g\in P\). Conversely for \(g\in P\) right multiplication preserves the bottom condition since \(g,g^{-1}\) are integral. The determinant is a unit, \(\eta(\det g)=1\), and the \(\mu\)-factor cancels the factor from (1). Thus on all \(X\) the transformed vector is \(\lambda(g)\Phi_P\) in that case. With the first argument linear, its coefficient against \(\Phi_P\) is exactly \({\bf1}_P(g)\lambda(g) M_P\), where the product is understood by zero extension. For any unitary representation and a vector \(x_1\) of this type,
\[
 \int \langle\omega(g)\Phi_P,\Phi_P\rangle_{\rm lin\,1}
          \overline{\langle x_2,\sigma(g)x_1\rangle_{\rm lin\,2}}\,dg
   =\operatorname{vol}(P) M_P\,\overline{\langle x_2,x_1\rangle_{\rm lin\,2}} .
\]
The integrand is bounded on compact support. More generally this convolution inserts the orthogonal projection onto the \(\lambda\)-isotypic subspace, since \(\operatorname{vol}(P)^{-1}\int_P\overline{\lambda(g)}\sigma(g)dg\) is self-adjoint idempotent by Haar invariance. This gives the same coefficient for an entangled adelic vector of the stated type; it requires no decomposition of that vector as a pure tensor.

On the \(n\)-side let \(J\subset K_n\) be the inverse image of the block group \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}H&*\\0&\mathrm{GL}_t(\kappa)\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). This is precisely the source definition by closure: its stated generating set is already this subgroup. For \(h\in J\) let \(a=h_{11}\). It is integral with invertible residue in \(H\), so \(a\in P\). On the support of (1), the new top block of \(h^{-1}X\) reduces to \(\bar a^{-1}\bar A\), and the new bottom block vanishes modulo \(\pi\). Applying the same calculation to \(h\) proves equality of supports. The \(n\)-action contributes \(\mu(\det h)^{-1}\) and a unit modulus factor \(|\det h|^{-r/2}=1\). The net scalar is
\[
 [\,\lambda(a)\mu(\det a)^{-1}\mu(\det h)\,]^{-1},
\]
as required on the opposite group. The exact upper-left block \((h^{-1})_{11}\) can differ from \(a^{-1}\); the reduction suffices precisely because the characters used here factor through the residue group. ## The index and the actual finite set

Reduction \(K_n\to\mathrm{GL}_n(\kappa)\) is onto, since any lift of the entries of an invertible residue matrix has unit determinant. Let \(I=[K_n:J]\). The lower-right group, the \(r\times t\) upper off-diagonal entries and \(H\) give
\[
 I=\frac{|\mathrm{GL}_n(\kappa)|}{|H|\,q^{rt}|\mathrm{GL}_t(\kappa)|},
 \qquad I M_P=\prod_{j=t+1}^{n}(1-q^{-j}).                             \tag{2}
\]
Another count acts on ordered independent \(r\)-frames. \(J\) fixes the \(H\)-orbit of the standard frame and every such orbit contains \(|H|\) frames. The probability of a full-rank \(n\times r\) matrix is \(q^{-nr}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q^n-q^j)\) by choosing successive independent columns, again giving (2). This also handles \(r=0\) or \(t=0\) with the usual trivial zero-rank group. [Finite field frame density in Lean](AlpogeSplitDoublingFactor.lean)

In \(\mathrm{IsLiftData}\), \(\mathrm{IsLocalLevel}\) writes the finite type as the product of its subgroups \(H_{PP}\) and characters. At \(PP\in R.P_g\) take \(H=H_{PP}\), \(\lambda\) its character. At \(PP\in R.Q(t)\) take \(H=\mathrm{GL}_r(\kappa)\) and \(\lambda=1\). The two finite sets are disjoint in the core data. The split idempotent supported on their union satisfies \(e\bar e=0\), excluding any inert, ramified or opposite conjugate prime there. Thus the underlying \(F\)-places are distinct. The source uses the **conjugate** half, followed by its adjoint-frame map to the selected coordinate. Its conditions **LowZero** and invertible top block give exactly the support of (1); the zero extra blocks of the \(W\) datum remove the auxiliary block product. **IsRes** identifies the unit values of the splitting character with \(m_{PP}\), giving (1)'s inverse determinant factor at both kinds of prime. For the transfer to the adelic theta vector, normalize local additive measures to give integral matrices mass one. In the Schrödinger realization of the theta section space, translations and opposite modulations act on this marked matrix variable, with multiplicity space for all complementary coordinates. The vacuum at every marked place is the indicator of the integral lattice tensored with a single vector in that multiplicity space. Indeed every opposite modulation trivial on the integral position lattice fixes its indicator, hence fixes \(f_0\) by the place-model equation. The double annihilator of the position lattice is itself, so the local Schrödinger function is supported there. Invariance under its integral translations forces constancy there. This uses additive local duality for the actual nontrivial character pairing. It requires no choice of a conductor-one coordinate for that pairing. The same two intertwining laws transport every finite linear combination of translated or modulated indicators, including (1). In the actual splitting law the right \(W\)-action on the chosen adjoint variable is right multiplication by its selected component, and the inverse \(V\)-action is left multiplication. Covariance with the two Heisenberg actions forces this change of variables up to a scalar; evaluation at zero against an arbitrary complementary functional is invariant under the opposite modulations and determines the phase by **om_split**. Its positive modulus is the square root of the Jacobian by unitarity. For unit determinants the \(W\)-phase is \(c_\mu(\det g)\) and the \(V\)-phase is \(c_\mu(\det h)^{-1}\), since \(c_\eta\) is unramified here. The coefficient is consequently the one computed above, including tests with zero automorphic pairing. ## A class contributing to the definite mass

The archive obtains **MassLower** under a separate assumption **StdV** that the specified frame is globally orthonormal and spans \(L_0\) integrally. The following argument proves the same inequality for the original totally positive definite \(V\) with its given unimodular lattice, without requiring this of the specified frame. [Archive's additional frame hypothesis](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/FormalDegreeUpperC.lean#L96-L98) · [CM, unimodularity, positive signatures and actual rank](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataSpaces.lean#L14-L49)

The field extension \(E/F\) is unramified at finite places. In the archive **IsCMUnram** supplies, at each prime of \(E\), an integral element differing from its conjugate in the residue field; a ramified quadratic place would have trivial residue action. At a split place choose a basis of the first lattice and its dual basis of the second. At an inert place, the residue Hermitian form on the unimodular lattice is nonsingular and has a vector of nonzero norm. Indeed, if all norms vanish, polarization against \(a v\) for every residue scalar \(a\) and nondegeneracy of the finite-field trace pairing force all pairings to vanish. Lift such a vector. Its unit norm can be scaled to one by surjectivity of the unramified quadratic norm on units: the residue norm is surjective and successive principal-unit digits are corrected with the surjective residue trace, including in characteristic two. The orthogonal complement splits integrally and remains unimodular; induction gives a standard lattice isometry. At every real place the recorded signature is the actual signature by the space datum. Hence the rational Hermitian space is locally isometric at all places to the standard positive space. Landherr's local-global theorem gives a rational isometry. Alternatively apply Hasse–Minkowski to represent one by the underlying rational quadratic form over \(F\) and continue on the Hermitian orthogonal complement, using local Witt cancellation at each step (positive definiteness excludes an isotropic vector with homogenizing coordinate zero). [Archive unramified condition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/HermitianLattices/StatementMass.lean#L44-L48) · [Landherr, *Äquivalenz Hermitescher Formen über einem beliebigen algebraischen Zahlkörper*, Hamburg 11 (1935)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/landherr.pdf)

Pull back \(O_E^n\) by this rational isometry. Its local lattices and the original \(L_0\) agree almost everywhere by commensurability and are isometric everywhere by the preceding classification. A finite adele therefore takes one to the other. In the mass sum for the *original* compact open there is a double coset with stabilizer the rational automorphisms of this pulled-back lattice. A rational vector integral at every finite place belongs to the lattice itself, so this group is exactly the standard integral unitary group after conjugation. For a nonzero \(a\in O_E\), the arithmetic mean of \(|\tau(a)|^2\) over the embeddings \(\tau:E\to\mathbb C\) is at least \(|N_{E/\mathbb Q}a|^{2/[E:\mathbb Q]}\ge1\). A unitary column has sum of these means one. Thus it has exactly one nonzero entry, of absolute value one at every embedding. Such an algebraic integer is a root of unity: all powers have bounded monic characteristic coefficients and bounded degree, hence lie in a finite set. Distinct columns must occupy distinct rows by orthogonality. Conversely every permutation matrix decorated by \(n\) roots of unity is an integral isometry. This class contributes exactly
\[
 \frac1{|\mu(E)|^n n!}
\]
to the positive genus mass. Other classes can have different stabilizers. This proves the original lower bound for every definite \(V_c(i)\), independent of the class represented by the given frame. The global Haar volume and the completed theta pairing still require their stated measure comparisons. [Identity-class comparison in the original proof](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/UnitaryMassLowerBoundOfOrthonormalLatticeFrames.lean)


In fact many classes have computable stabilizers. Let \(A\) be the group of fractional ideals \(J\) with \(J\bar J=O_E\), modulo \((u)\) for \(u\bar u=1\), and \(h_U=|A|\). Every such ideal is \(I/\bar I\): only split primes have nonzero exponents and each conjugate pair has opposite exponents. The induced map \(\mathrm{Cl}(E)\to A\) is surjective. Its kernel consists of classes represented by conjugation-invariant ideals. Indeed if \(I/\bar I=(u)\) with \(u\bar u=1\), write \(u=b/\bar b\) by Hilbert 90 (take \(b=1+u\) unless \(u=-1\), then take a nonzero purely imaginary \(b\)). Now \(I/(b)\) is invariant. Conversely an invariant ideal maps to the identity. Every invariant fractional ideal here extends an ideal of \(F\), by the split and inert prime exponent descriptions and absence of finite ramification. Hence
\[
 h_U=|\mathrm{Cl}(E)/\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{Cl}(F)\to\mathrm{Cl}(E))|.
\]

Consider all orthogonal coordinate sums \(J_1\oplus\cdots\oplus J_n\) in the globally standard Hermitian space, with each \(J_i\) as above. They are unimodular, so the local classification puts them all in the given genus. A rational unitary isomorphism to \(I_1\oplus\cdots\oplus I_n\) must satisfy \(x_{ji}J_i\subset I_j\) entrywise. The absolute ideal norms of all \(I_j,J_i\) are one. Thus \(|N_{E/\mathbb Q}(x_{ji})|=[I_j:x_{ji}J_i]\ge1\) for nonzero entries, including fractional ones. The same embedding-average argument gives just one nonzero entry per column and row. Unitarity then gives \(x_{ji}\bar x_{ji}=1\) and the index is one. Conversely any matching of coordinate ideal classes can be implemented by such a monomial unitary matrix. These genus classes are therefore indexed exactly by multisets from \(A\). If a multiset has multiplicities \(m_a\), its stabilizer order is \( |\mu(E)|^n\prod_a m_a!\): the admissible permutations preserve each class, and for a matched coordinate the possible unitary isomorphisms form a torsor under the finite norm-one unit group, equal to \(\mu(E)\). Summing the reciprocal orders by the multinomial theorem strengthens the archived result to
\[
             \operatorname{mass}(V,L_0)\ \ge\
                    \frac{h_U^n}{|\mu(E)|^n n!}.
\]
For rank one the sum exhausts the genus, since every lattice is an ideal, yielding equality. For a simple check of the removed hypothesis, even the free standard lattice can be described in the rational basis \(e_1,e_1+e_2\) with a nonidentity Gram matrix. The mass bound is invariant under that choice. This argument establishes a source-specific positive contribution from a described set of classes without computing the mass of the remaining classes.

## Pairing two different types

There is a stricter support check if the first and second tests (1) use subgroups above \(H_1,H_2\) and unit characters \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\), with the same \(\mu\). A nonzero overlap forces \(g\in K_r\). Write \(s=\bar g\). If no \(a\in H_2\) has \(as\in H_1\), the coefficient is zero. Otherwise all such residues are \(Ca\) for \(C=H_1\cap H_2\), since \((bs)(as)^{-1}=ba^{-1}\). Every allowed matrix residue has mass \(q^{-nr}\). The common determinant twist cancels the action phase; summing the remaining unit characters on \(Ca\) gives
\[
  q^{-nr}|C|\,\lambda_1(as)\overline{\lambda_2(a)}
  \quad\text{if }\lambda_1|_C=\lambda_2|_C,
\]
and zero if their restrictions differ. For the latter case translate the sum by an element on which the ratio is nontrivial. This includes complete cancellation even when the supports intersect. It supplies an independent check on the orientation of the two characters and specializes to the marked factor at equal types.

The [Lean module](AlpogeExceptionalRallis.lean) proves the diagonal coefficient, disjoint-support case, vanishing for incompatible restrictions and the exact mixed coefficient for agreeing restrictions on arbitrary finite second level. The analytic local Haar formulas, return to the adelic test, and the genus bound use the arguments above. Their full Lean formalizations and external independent review are unrecorded.
