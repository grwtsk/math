# Varying the prime in the actual Frobenius orbit gain

Assume the archived \(\mathrm{SizesAt}(L)\) hypotheses and \(k\ge1\) when specializing to the archive. Fix one admissible lift \(j=i+1,\ i<m\), its place weights and one Frobenius action. For each cycle take nonnegative demands \(d_s=(k-1)\mathrm{wPl}(D,j,z_s)\), \(W=\sum_s d_s\). Let \(F(x)\) be the archive's \(\mathrm{lpF}\) with real variable \(x>1\) in place of the prime, keeping that cycle and those demands. The following estimates for \(q\ge p\ge3\) are uniform in the period and the full demand distribution:
\[
 0\le F(p)\le \frac{pW}{p^2-1},\qquad
 |F(q)-F(p)|\le4W(1/p-1/q).                                          \tag{1}
\]
They continue to hold after summing whole cycles, with \(W\) their total demand. They apply separately to the full place sum in \(\mathrm{FInert}\) and the invariant subset \(\mathrm{kindBC}\) in \(\mathrm{lowCs}\). At an actual prime, summing one representative per cycle equals the specified starting-place sum divided by the period, by cyclic invariance. More generally summing starting-place estimates divided by the specified period gives the same total demand accounting even if cycles are repeated. The special residue-class-zero, \(h_P=0\) branch of \(\mathrm{FInert}\) is the displayed baseline \(W/(p^2-1)\) using its own sum over \(\tau\); it must be evaluated by that branch. ## Resolvent comparison across different primes

Write \(S\) for cyclic backward shift, \(t_p=p(pI+S)^{-1}d\), and \(A_p=\sum_s(-t_{p,s})_+\). In the \(\ell^1\) norm \(S\) is an isometry, so \(\|(pI+S)v\|\ge(p-1)\|v\|\). The equations imply
\[
 (qI+S)(t_q-t_p)=(q-p)(d-t_p),\quad p(d-t_p)=St_p .
\]
The two inverse norm estimates give
\[
 \|t_q-t_p\|_1\le\frac{(q-p)W}{(p-1)(q-1)}.
\]
The [Lean lemma](AlpogeOrbitPrimeVariation.lean) proves this for every real linear isometry. The positive-part functional is 1-Lipschitz in \(\ell^1\). Also the Neumann expansion \(t_p=\sum_{k\ge0}(-S/p)^k d\) bounds \(A_p\le pW/(p^2-1)\) by retaining the odd powers. Consequently the common even-cover contribution \(G_p=(1-1/p)A_p\) has almost everywhere
\[
 |G'_p|\le W/[p(p^2-1)]+W/[p(p-1)].
\]
Absolute continuity on compact subintervals follows from the finite difference estimate. This avoids summing separate error bounds at every sign change of every orbit coordinate.

By the [even-period formula](AlpogeOrbitFlowClosedForm.md) and [odd-period formula](AlpogeOddOrbitCapacity.md), \(F(p)=W/(p^2-1)+G_p+H_p\), where \(H=0\) for even period. For odd \(r\ge3\) the numerator of \(H\) is \(\sum_s(d_s-L_s)_+\), divided by \(p^r+1\); \(L_s\) is a nonnegative sum of monomials \(p^a d_t\) with \(|a|\le r-1\). On each active site \(L_s<d_s\) one has \(|L'_s|\le(r-1)d_s/p\). At almost every other point the derivative of the hinge is zero. Including the derivative of the denominator gives \(|H'|\le (2r-1)W/p^{r+1}\). For \(r=1\) the original LP has no admissible edge and \(F=pW/(p^2-1)\). For \(p\ge3,\ r\ge3\), the odd correction times \(p^2/W\) is bounded by \(2r/3^{r-1}\le2/3\); the same normalization bounds the baseline derivative by \(27/32\) and the \(G\) derivative by \(15/8\). Thus \(|F'|\le4W/p^2\) in all cases. The upper amplitude bound in (1) also follows from feasibility of the zero flow. At a single occupied site in period \(r\ge2\) no positive edge is feasible, giving \(F=pW/(p^2-1)\) and showing that the uniform derivative order is sharp.

For fixed action, the coordinates of \(t_p\) are alternating polynomials times \(p/(p^r-(-1)^r)\), by multiplying the finite geometric progression for the inverse. The incident surpluses are Laurent polynomials. Thus \(F\) is continuous and piecewise rational, with explicitly computable breakpoints from the positive real roots of these finitely many polynomials (identically zero expressions carry no breakpoint). Moreover if \(r\ge2\) and \(p d_s\ge d_{s-1}\) at every site, then \((I+S/p)^{-1}d=\sum_{j\ge0}(S/p)^{2j}(I-S/p)d\ge0\). The \(j=1\) summand in every odd incident load \(L_s\) is \(p d_{s+1}\ge d_s\). The gain is then precisely the baseline. A common sufficient threshold is \(p\ge \max d/\min d\) for strictly positive demands; both vanishing demands and a changing Frobenius action require attention.

## A character-sum error on the original window

For each fixed class \(c\), group only primes with the same action, period, special branch and invariant subset for the chosen consumer. Include the specified lift weights and their factor \(k-1\) in \(W_c\). Write \(f_c(x)\) for the resulting gain and \(\theta_c(t)=\sum_{p\le t,\,p\in c}\log p\), with any exceptional primes excluded precisely as in the consumer. For any assigned density \(\delta_c\), put \(E_c(t)=\theta_c(t)-\delta_c t\). On \((P,Q]\), \(3\le P<Q\), Stieltjes integration by parts gives the exact error and the unconditional bound
\[
 \left|\sum_{P<p\le Q,\ p\in c}f_c(p)\log p-\delta_c\int_P^Q f_c(t)\,dt\right|
 \le W_c\left[\frac{P|E_c(P)|}{P^2-1}+\frac{Q|E_c(Q)|}{Q^2-1}
       +4\int_P^Q\frac{|E_c(t)|}{t^2}\,dt\right].                      \tag{2}
\]
Each term is evaluated on the original prime window; the continuous integral can be split into the explicit rational chambers. For example an independently available \(|E_c(t)|\le A_c t^\sigma,\ 0\le\sigma<1\), on this same window gives a bound \(W_c A_c(9/4+4/(1-\sigma))P^{\sigma-1}\). On a wholly flat interval with baseline gain, the endpoint factors improve to \(1/(x^2-1)\) and the integral kernel to \(2t/(t^2-1)^2\), saving a further power of \(P\). Endpoint conventions include a prime equal to \(Q\) and exclude a prime equal to \(P\); the missing endpoint is inserted explicitly if needed.

For \(\mathrm{IsCs}\) one takes \(p\ge5,\ p\le X''(n)\), \(z\in\mathrm{kindBC}(D,p)\), and the actual \(i+1\). The three losses in \(\mathrm{lowCs}\), namely \((k-1)\sum_U w/p^3,\ 8\sum_U w/p,\ 3fN_m^2\log_p a\), still have their specified signs and must be summed as well. Multiplication by \(\log p\) cancels the base-\(p\) logarithm in the third loss exactly. Equation (2) pays for the class discrepancy against an explicitly varying real weight. It supplies neither an estimate for those discrepancies uniform in growing field data nor the integral scaled generators or the remaining terms of (M). No RH-equivalent prime error bound has been used. The analytic transfer and application are manuscript arguments; external independent review is unrecorded. The archive's **PrimesRel** is a count for a single reduced residue class, with an exceptional-character term in the main term and a lower cutoff \(X\ge M^{c_2}\). One can use that type of count directly on any part of the window with a proved matching congruence description. If \(\pi_a(t)=m_a(t)+R_a(t)\) on \([P,Q]\), apply summation by parts to \(f_c(t)\log t\). The exact main term is \(\int_{(P,Q]} f_c(t)\log t\,dm_a(t)\), including the exceptional component. In the remainder, the endpoint terms have modulus at most \(|R_a(x)|\,xW_c\log x/(x^2-1)\) at \(x=P,Q\); the integral is bounded by \(W_c\int_P^Q |R_a(t)|(4\log t/t^2+1/(t^2-1))dt\). This derivation only requires \(m_a\) of bounded variation on the window. It avoids requiring the count estimate down to \(2\). Identifying the Artin fibers from \(h_P\), verifying the conductor threshold for the original growing data, and accounting for any excluded prime remain substantive steps.
