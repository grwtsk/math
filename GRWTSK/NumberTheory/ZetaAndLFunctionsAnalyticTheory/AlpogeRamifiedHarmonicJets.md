# Ramified harmonic jets and translated toric theta series

grwtsk contributors

Let \(E/\mathbb Q\) be the Galois CM field in Levent Alpöge's host. For every prime \(p\) in the conductor, complex conjugation moves every prime of \(E\) above \(p\). Put \(h_p=e(E_{\mathfrak p}/\mathbb Q_p)\), and use \(v_p(p)=1\) on extensions. Consider any of the source's complete DVRs \(R=T.V\), coefficient embedding, integral framed point and integral chart \(C\). Write \(k\) for the integer power of the theta bundle, \(n\) for the number of copies and \(D=\mathrm{DF}(Dw,w)\) for the weight degree. The input \(P\) lies in the source's integral-coefficient, row-weighted joint harmonic polynomial space. In particular all its monomials have degree at most \(D\). The bound below is uniform over all integral formal series in the \(n\) chart variables, including series which are not products. It quantifies over existing integral charts. At a conductor prime define the distance between two *distinct* characters \(i,j\) of \(\mathcal O_E\) in the coefficient field by
\[
     c(i,j)=\min_{a\in\mathcal O_E}v_p(i(a)-j(a)),\qquad c_p=\max_{i\ne j}c(i,j).
\]
Take an empty maximum to be zero. For \(k>0\) put \(u_p(k)=\max(0,c_p-v_p(k))\), and set \(u_p(0)=0\). In the source valuation \(v_T/e_0\), for **every** positive normalization integer \(e_0\), one may use the ramified chart loss
\[
 \rho_p=\frac{v_T(p)}{e_0}\left\lfloor\frac D2\right\rfloor u_p(k),\qquad
 c_p\le\min\!\left\{\frac{v_p(|\mathrm{disc}(E)|)}{[E:\mathbb Q]},
                   \frac1{h_p}+\frac1{p-1}\right\}.                    \tag{1}
\]
For tame ramification with \(h_p>1\), \(c_p=1/h_p\); in the unramified case \(c_p=0\). Dropping the \(v_p(k)\) saving gives a bound uniform in all powers, as required by \(\mathrm{ChBound}\). When \(R\) has residue characteristic zero all rational integers are units and the chart has integral harmonic values. The same conclusion holds away from the discriminant, including primes fixed by complex conjugation, by the integral embedding projectors described below.

## The pairing that can have a denominator

Write \(F=\mathrm{Frac}(R)\). Let \(u_i\) be the actual integral tangent eigenvectors, indexed by an embedding and a row, and write \(\tau_i\) for its embedding. With the ordinary cotangent dot product, set
\[
 \beta((x,y),(z,t))=t\cdot x-y\cdot z,\qquad
 L=\{(x,\phi-Sx):x,\phi\in R^d\}.
\]
Here \(S\) is the matrix of the linear term of \(-\partial_2 h(X,0)\) for the chart's canonical biextension logarithm \(h\). In this formula the logarithm can have nonintegral coefficients. Projection of \(L\) gives the integral tangent lattice and its vertical kernel is the integral cotangent lattice. The \(\mathcal O_E\) action has blocks \(A(a)\), \(A(\bar a)^t\), with \(A(a)=d[a]\) at the origin. It preserves \(L\): the matrix \(SA(a)-A(\bar a)^t S\) is integral. Indeed the mixed derivative at the origin of the logarithm of the adjunction unit comparing the biextension at \(([a]X,Y)\) and \((X,[\bar a]Y)\) is this defect up to sign. The unit is integral, equals one on the two axes, and its mixed logarithmic coefficient equals its mixed ordinary coefficient. This argument uses the integral adjunction frame, part of the chart. The formal group and biextension identities over \(R\) follow by injectivity of the generic-fibre map coefficient by coefficient. The tangent eigen equations follow by functoriality of the tangent trivialization under the given \(\mathcal O_E\)-action. Decompose \(\mathcal O_E\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R\) by the primes above \(p\): the projections are integral by the product decomposition of \(\mathcal O_E\otimes\mathbb Z_p\), since \(R\) is complete of mixed characteristic. Acting by the prime projector of \(\tau_i\) on \((u_i,-Su_i)\) gives \(v_i=(u_i,y_i)\in L\). Project further to the single embedding \(\tau_i\) over \(F\), giving \(v_i^*=(u_i,y_i+\psi_i)\). The second projector exists by separability and the presence of all coefficient embeddings. Neither its denominator nor that of the full vector \(\psi_i\) will be needed. Adjointness gives \(\beta(av,w)=\beta(v,\bar a w)\). Thus \(\beta(v_i^*,(u_j,0))=0\) whenever \(\tau_i\ne\bar\tau_j\). The same orthogonality holds for the coarse vectors whenever the two primes are not conjugate. Coarse prime projectors exchanged by conjugation give the two commuting sides of the ordered calculation.

For every \(a\in\mathcal O_E\), the vector \((a-\tau_i(a))v_i\) is vertical and integral. Its dot product with \(u_j\) is integral. Pairing by adjointness consequently gives the simultaneous clearing identities
\[
  (\tau_j(\bar a)-\tau_i(a))\,\beta(v_i,(u_j,0))\in R,\qquad
   v_p(\psi_i\cdot u_j)\ \ge -c(\tau_i,\bar\tau_j)
                   \quad(\tau_i\ne\bar\tau_j).                         \tag{2}
\]
In the last expression \(v_p\) divides \(v_T\) by \(v_T(p)\). Indeed for such a pair \((y_i+\psi_i)\cdot u_j=0\). One difference clears precisely the *paired scalar*. Bounding the entire fine projector by the different before this contraction loses the distinction. For a residue-characteristic-zero chart the integral algebra already splits into embedding factors, since its discriminant is a unit and the generic embeddings land in \(R\). This splitting also holds at an unramified \(p\): the inverse of the embedding matrix of an integral basis is integral, since its determinant squared is the discriminant. Taking all lifts directly in their fine factors makes \(\psi_i=0\). In this case the original horizontal and projected connections agree on harmonic inputs by the same ordering argument using an orientation of conjugate embeddings. ## Removing the conjugate terms before taking valuations

Order the two sides separately within each pair of conjugate primes. The formal covariant derivatives with parameters \((u,y)\) have central commutator \(k\beta\), by differentiation of the explicit first-order operator. For a word only in integral lifts \(v_i\), its constant reading on \(e^{k h_L}f\) is integral if \(f\) is integral. Here \(h_L(X)=h(X,[w_0]X)\), with \(w_0+\bar w_0=1\). To see the lattice assertion in chart coordinates, conjugate the differential operator by \(e^{k h_L}\). Its horizontal derivative has the integral invariant-vector-field coefficients of the group law. Its vertical coefficient is \(\phi_i+(D_u s_i-(Su)_i)\) for \(s_i=-\partial_{2,i}h(X,0)\). The zero-order term is \(k(D_u h_L+s\cdot u-Y\cdot u)\), where \(Y\) are the auxiliary cotangent variables. The difference \(D_u s_i-(Su)_i\) is an integral series: differentiate the additivity equation for \(h\) once in the added direction at zero and once in the second point at zero. Its residual terms are logarithmic derivatives of the integral, axis-normalized biextension unit. Likewise differentiating \(h(X,[w_0]X)\) by the group law and using symmetry and adjunction changes the terms linear in \(s\) to \(-s\cdot (d[w_0]+d[\bar w_0])u=-s\cdot u\). The remaining terms are logarithmic derivatives of the integral addition, symmetry and adjunction units. An integral unit has integral logarithmic derivative, including at 2. This proves preservation under any number of iterations. The constant of the conjugating exponential is one. For the horizontal directions the generic harmonic value equals the ordered reading in the fine lifts. One can see this by moving the added vertical derivatives through the horizontal operators. A vertical derivative kills the original lifted section, which has no auxiliary variables. A transposition produces the central factor \(k\beta\). With fine lifts this factor is supported only on conjugate embedding rows. Upon summing in the copies \(b\) it acts on \(P\) through a scalar multiple of \(\sum_b\partial_{i,b}\partial_{j,b}\), zero for every such pair by the defining harmonic equations. Reading the zero-vertical horizontal word at \(Y=s(X)\) gives the invariant directional derivative of \(e^{k h_L}f\), since the vector field is tangent to this graph and its multiplication term vanishes there. The two evaluations at the origin agree because \(s(0)=0\). These checks identify the covariant evaluation with the actual chart reading. Now expand the ordered fine lifts as \(v_i+(0,\psi_i)\). Commuting a vertical letter toward the original lifted section gives a contraction only from the true side \(i\) towards the false side \(j\), with coefficient \(\kappa_{ij}=-k\,\psi_i\cdot u_j\). Direct normal ordering puts every chosen vertical derivative on the right before evaluation, where it annihilates the lifted section. The vertical derivatives on the right side of the order commute past the remaining same-side letters and annihilate the initial series; the left-side verticals cross the opposite-side letters. Same-side commutators vanish by prime orthogonality. Derivatives from different copies commute. Thus the correction to the coarse ordered words is the divided exponential of \(\sum_{ij}\kappa_{ij}\sum_b\partial_{i,b}\partial_{j,b}\) acting on \(P\). In each degree this is a finite sum. **Delete every conjugate pair from this second correction as well**: the operators are constant coefficient, so the sum of the deleted terms commutes with the other terms and kills \(P\) at the first application. This global deletion occurs *before* estimating individual columns.    \sum_{bij} t_{bij}\max\{0,c(\tau_i,\bar\tau_j)-v_p(k)\}.             \tag{3}

There are no denominator contributions from the divided powers. Label the occurrences of each letter and choose disjoint left-right pairs of prescribed types. ## Local ramification distance

Distinct primes can be separated by the Chinese remainder theorem. Two embeddings at one prime with different residue maps are separated by a residue unit difference. Otherwise they differ, after identification with the local Galois completion \(L\), by a nonidentity inertia element \(\sigma\). Let \(\pi\) be a uniformizer and \(L_0\) the maximal unramified subfield. The integer ring is \(\mathcal O_{L_0}[\pi]\): the powers below \(h_p\) are a field basis, and the valuations of their scalar multiples are distinct modulo \(h_p\), forcing every coefficient of an integral element to be integral. Thus
\[
                 c(\tau,\tau')=v_L(\sigma\pi-\pi)/h_p .
\]
Local minima are attained by global integers by approximation modulo a sufficiently high prime power. The derivative of the Eisenstein polynomial of \(\pi\) is \(\prod_{\sigma\ne1,\ \sigma\in I}(\pi-\sigma\pi)\). The trace-dual basis for a power basis is given by the coefficients of \(f(X)/(X-\pi)\) divided by \(f'(\pi)\), so the different is generated by that derivative. Using transitivity through the unramified part and summing norms over the equally ramified primes in the Galois field yields the first bound in (1).

For the second, put \(b=v_L(\sigma\pi-\pi)-1\) and \(\Delta=\sigma-1\). For \(x\ne0\), \(v_L(\Delta x)\ge v_L(x)+b\), by expanding powers of \(\pi\) with coefficients in \(\mathcal O_{L_0}\), or by their convergent uniformizer expansions for arbitrary integral valuation. When \((p-1)b>h_p\), the term \(p\Delta\pi\) is the unique term of lowest valuation in \(((1+\Delta)^p-1)\pi\). The intermediate binomial coefficients are divisible by \(p\), and \(1+pb>1+h_p+b\). Hence the break of \(\sigma^p\) equals \(h_p+b\); iteration makes \(\sigma^{p^m}\) distinct with unbounded finite breaks, impossible in a finite group. For tame inertia, \(b=0\) for nonidentity \(\sigma\): if \(b>0\) and the order \(t\) is prime to \(p\), then \(t\Delta\pi\) is the unique leading term in \((\sigma^t-1)\pi\). For unramified places no positive distance arises. For a control at wild 2, \(\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt2)\) has \(h_2=2\) and distance \(3/2\), attaining the uniform bound. In \(\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^r})\), the maximum is \(1/(p-1)\) when there is an automorphism of relative order \(p\) on the cyclotomic unit, whereas the full normalized different is \(r-1/(p-1)\). A CM example with both ramification and moved primes is \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7},\zeta_{2^r}+\zeta_{2^r}^{-1})\), \(r\ge3\). Two splits in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})\), and the real factor has a single totally ramified prime of index \(h=2^{r-2}\). With \(z=\zeta_{2^r}\), \(2-z-z^{-1}=(1-z)(1-z^{-1})\) is a uniformizer there. The automorphism \(z\mapsto-z\) changes it by \(2(z+z^{-1})\), of normalized valuation \(1+1/h\) since \(-z^2\) has order \(2^{r-1}\). Thus the depth-independent bound is *attained* by the character distance for every \(r\). The different exponent of the full cyclotomic field is \((r-1)2^{r-1}\), by differentiating \(X^{2^{r-1}}+1\). Its quadratic extension over the real subfield has different generated by \(z-z^{-1}\), of full-field valuation two; \(1,z\) are an integral relative basis since they span every integral power of \(z\). The real factor has normalized different \(r-1-1/h\). This family illustrates the two local quantities under the moved-prime CM hypothesis; no full preliminary host or sharp geometric lower bound is inferred. ## A semiabelian chart with a nonidentity torsion component

In the split totally degenerate symmetric period presentation take a lattice \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z^s\), symmetric multiplicative periods \(q_{ij}\), and the principal period map \(q^\lambda\). Suppose the matrix \(B=(v(q_{ij}))\) is positive definite. After the finite extension needed to choose compatible roots, write \(q^{Q(x)}=\prod_iq_{ii}^{x_i^2/2}\prod_{i<j}q_{ij}^{x_ix_j}\). The section in the symmetric frame is \(\Theta(z)=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}q^{Q(x)}z^x\). Its convergence and that of each formal jet follow from coercivity. A point of order dividing \(k>0\) has a representative \(t=u q^a\) with \(ka\in\Lambda\), \(u^k=1\). On degree \(k\) sections the normalized lift has the form
\[
                 (U_tF)(z)=\eta\,q^{kQ(a)}z^{ka}F(tz)
\]
with \(\eta\) a unit root. Indeed quasi-periodicity gives \(F(q^\lambda z)=q^{-kQ(\lambda)}z^{-k\lambda}F(z)\); multiplication of the \(k\) iterates gives a scalar \(u^{ka\,k(k-1)/2}\) of finite order, all period factors cancelling. Choosing its inverse \(k\)-th root fixes the order, and all order-normalized lifts differ by a root of unity. For the symmetric lift the sign condition may select such a root, which has identical valuation. This accounts explicitly for torsion components even when \(a\notin\Lambda\). For \(F=\Theta^k\) every summand with indices \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) then has valuation
\[
                  \tfrac12\sum_{\nu=1}^k B(x_\nu+a,x_\nu+a)\ge0.
\]
Its exponent in \(z\) is \(\sum x_\nu+ka\in\Lambda\). Around the torus identity \(z_j=1+X_j\), its Taylor coefficients are integral by the integral binomial expansion for all integer powers. The quadratic growth in the tuple guarantees convergence coefficient by coefficient over the complete DVR. Thus **every** such term is regular to all orders even for components outside the identity section. Omitting the scalar can fail: at rank one, \(k=3,a=2/3,v(q)=1\) and all \(x_\nu=-1\), the unnormalized summand has valuation \(-1/2\), and the normalized one \(1/6\). The same calculation handles every succession of normalized torsion moves and polarized torus similitude pullbacks in this presentation. Define \(Q(\lambda)=B(\lambda,\lambda)/2\). Say a degree \(k>0\) series \(\sum_\lambda c_\lambda z^\lambda\) satisfies \(v(c_\lambda)\ge Q(\lambda)/k\). This holds with equality for the displayed degree-one section. Translation replaces index \(\lambda\) by \(\lambda+ka\) and adds \(B(\lambda,a)+kQ(a)\) to its valuation, exactly preserving the inequality. If the pullback on character indices is \(C\) with \(Q(C\lambda)=gQ(\lambda)\), the new degree is \(kg\), and \(Q(C\lambda)/(kg)=Q(\lambda)/k\). In a torus group homomorphism the characters pull back without constants. For these statements on normalized sections use the symmetric frames with compatible multiplicative periods and the rigidified identification of the two frames at the identity. Under this compatibility the pullback frame is the standard degree-\(kg\) frame: the ratio of the automorphy factors is one by the period similitude, and their rigidifications agree. A constant root change is harmless. Products also preserve the bound because
\[
 \frac{Q(x)}k+\frac{Q(y)}l-\frac{Q(x+y)}{k+l}
   =\frac{Q(lx-ky)}{kl(k+l)}\ge0.
\]
Consequently the full family generated by these operations has integral germs on the torus identity chart after any of its allowed torsion moves, including moves to a different component before pullback. This resolves marked-translate regularity throughout this split symmetric presentation with its specified frame compatibility. The source's general Raynaud extension also has Fourier coefficients in line bundles over an abelian part, projection isogenies and a fixed primitive generator. This split calculation proves the comparison for the displayed series and frames. Extending it to these coefficient sections and proving regularity of every reachable translate in the specific host remain separate geometric obligations.
