# Time averages of an arithmetic resolvent and the loss of trace

Start with the [critical-line boundary reconstruction](XiBoundaryResolventMeasure.md). It gives a Herglotz function \(M\) defined by an integral of \(\log |(s-1)\zeta(s)/(s+2)^8|\) on the critical line and the logarithmic derivative of the gamma factor. Stieltjes inversion gives a locally finite spectral measure \(\mu\); multiplication by the coordinate in \(L^2(\mu)\) on its maximal domain is the self-adjoint operator \(H_0\). For real \(a\), put \(v_a(t)=1/(t-a-i)\) and
\[
 G_{a,T}=\frac1{2T}\int_{-T}^T
   (e^{iuH_0}v_a)\otimes(e^{iuH_0}v_a)\,du .
\]
Here \(v\otimes v\) is the positive rank-one operator with trace \(\|v\|^2\). The integral exists in trace norm by strong continuity of the unitary orbit. These definitions use only the boundary input and the spectral theorem for a self-adjoint multiplication operator.

For each \(a\) there is an unconditional positive trace-class limit \(G_a\) in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. The support projection of \(G_a\), independent of \(a\), extracts exactly the real modes. On the resulting space, arithmetic spectral projections recover their full multiplicities and the corresponding self-adjoint operator has Hilbert–Schmidt inverse unconditionally. The possible missing nonreal divisor is detected by a different limit:
\[
 \ell(a)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\|G_{a,T}-G_a\|_1
        =\Im M(a+i)-\mathrm{Tr}\,G_a.
\]
Either \(\ell(a)=0\) for all real \(a\), or \(\ell(n)>6/5\) for some **integer** \(n\). Uniform trace-norm control at this fixed tolerance would therefore give RH. Small displacement of a zero from the line does not weaken this threshold.

## The two operator limits

Use the unitarily equivalent cyclic representation \(L^2(\nu_a)\), \(d\nu_a=|v_a|^2d\mu\), sending the constant one to \(v_a\). Its total mass is \(\Im M(a+i)\) by the Poisson formula with height one. In these coordinates the integral kernel of the time average is \(\mathrm{sinc}(T(x-y))\), with value one on the diagonal. It is bounded by one and converges pointwise to the indicator of \(x=y\). Dominated convergence for the finite product measure gives Hilbert–Schmidt convergence; the limit acts as multiplication by \(\nu_a(\{x\})\). In particular its trace is the sum of all atomic masses, whereas every finite average has trace \(\nu_a(\mathbb R)\). The kernel calculation allows any finite Borel measure, including a singular continuous part. The arithmetic identification below is what excludes a singular continuous part in this application. Related qualitative separation of pure point and continuous spectra by time averages occurs in the RAGE theorem. [Teschl, *Mathematical Methods in Quantum Mechanics*, second edition, §5.2, Theorem 5.8 and the preceding time-average argument](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~gerald/ftp/book-schroe/schroe2.pdf)

The trace discrepancy gives a lower bound for the trace-norm distance. For the reverse bound take the finite-rank projection \(P\) onto any finite set of atomic lines. Terms of \(G_{a,T}-G_a\) meeting \(P\) tend to zero in trace norm, since their ranks are bounded and their Hilbert–Schmidt norms tend to zero. On the complement \(Q=I-P\), positivity gives an upper bound \(\mathrm{Tr}(QG_{a,T}Q)+\mathrm{Tr}(QG_aQ)\). The first trace is the total mass minus the sum of the selected atomic masses; this is exact at each \(T\) by compression of the defining rank-one integral. The second is the omitted atomic mass. Exhaust the countably many atoms to obtain precisely the mass of the nonatomic part in the trace-norm limit.

For verification, the boundary formula for \(\mu\) assigns mass \(m_t\) to a real xi zero \(t\) and density \(2\sum_p m_p P_\sigma(t-\gamma)\), one term per distinct upper zero \(p=\gamma+i\sigma\). Here \(P_\sigma(t)=\sigma/[\pi(t^2+\sigma^2)]\). There is no other measure. Poisson convolution gives the two exact trace formulas
\[
 \mathrm{Tr}\,G_a=\sum_{t\ \mathrm{real}}\frac{m_t}{1+(t-a)^2},\qquad
 \ell(a)=2\sum_p\frac{m_p(1+\sigma)}{(a-\gamma)^2+(1+\sigma)^2}.
\]
At an integer within half a unit of any upper zero the single contribution exceeds \(6/5\). Indeed it is bounded below by \(2(1+\sigma)/(1/4+(1+\sigma)^2)\), a decreasing function of \(1+\sigma\in(1,3/2)\) with endpoint value \(6/5\). At \(a=0\) the classical strip and the published verified height give \(\ell(0)<1.5(2.960\cdot10^{-12})\) by comparing all off-line terms with the two-sided ordinate-square tail. Thus the small central discrepancy and the height-independent translated test have different uses. The [exterior prime-side test](XiPoissonCellCertificate.md) obtains a sharper threshold by probing the original unreflected logarithmic derivative. [Platt–Trudgian, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09765) · [Explicit two-sided tail](XiSechProjectionTransport.md)

## The compact part and its spectral determinant

Take the support projection of \(G_0\). The closed multiplication operator \(N=(1+H_0^2)G_0\) is meant in the maximal joint functional calculus of these commuting multipliers. On the support its values are the positive integer masses of \(\mu\); elsewhere they are zero. For \(j\ge1\) restrict \(H_0\) to the spectral projection \(\mathbf 1_{[j,\infty)}(N)\) and take their Hilbert direct sum \(H_{\mathrm{pp}}\). This procedure uses the time-average operator constructed from \(M\) and its functional calculus. In particular it requires neither a table of real zeros nor a simplicity assumption. The comparison with (2) of the boundary reconstruction gives one independent eigendirection in each of the first \(m_t\) summands. It identifies this realization with the real-root component obtained earlier by local theta-jet measures. Their common reliance on the analytic function xi is explicit. [Local theta-jet construction](XiJetDivisorMeasures.md)

Each restriction is self-adjoint on its maximal domain, as is the orthogonal sum. The real ordinates form a discrete set disjoint from zero; their squared reciprocals are summable with the stated multiplicities. The inverse is everywhere defined, self-adjoint and Hilbert–Schmidt. The determinant of this arithmetic inverse has exactly the real xi zeros with their analytic multiplicities on the **entire** line. Its missing factor in the all-plane xi identity is the canonical product over nonreal zeros. This factor has no real zeros and is positive on the real axis by reflection, continuity and its value one at zero. It is identically one exactly when the missing divisor is empty. Consequently the requested all-plane identity for this self-adjoint operator is equivalent to trace-norm convergence at all integer translates, or to the stated uniform tolerance bound. Establishing this bound at arbitrarily high translates remains open. A compact operator obtained by passing to the Hilbert–Schmidt limit alone does not imply conservation of trace. For a purely continuous nonzero test measure the same time averages have Hilbert–Schmidt limit zero and their trace norms remain equal to the original positive mass.

The [Lean module](XiTimeAveragedSpectrum.lean) proves convergence of the actual squared sinc-kernel error integral to zero for every finite Borel measure on the frequency plane, with no atomlessness hypothesis. Applying it to the product measure gives the analytic kernel-norm convergence at integer times. The kernel representation, trace-norm recovery and application to the arithmetic operator are manuscript results.
