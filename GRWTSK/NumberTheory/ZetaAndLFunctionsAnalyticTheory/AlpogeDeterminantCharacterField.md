# The field and fractional ideals of the determinant character

Authors: grwtsk contributors.

We determine the field contribution of the genuine everywhere-unramified character of infinity type two in Levent Alpöge's determinant construction. The result applies to every permitted choice of that character, including class-character twists. Its local value at an arbitrary prime belongs to the source's prescribed Kummer field. We also compute its principal fractional ideal at every coefficient prime. This supplies the field-of-values input to the normalized twist on the standard decorated charts. ## From the genuine character to fractional ideals

Let \(E/F\) be the CM extension on the given host, \(c\) its conjugation, \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\subset E\) its specified imaginary quadratic subfield and \(\phi_0:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\) the given embedding. Here \(E/\mathbb Q\) is Galois. The embeddings indexed by \(\Psi\) are exactly \(\phi_0\sigma\) for \(\sigma\in G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\). Indeed the source indices are precisely the automorphisms fixing \(\sqrt{-d}\), which generates \(K\). Write \(N(x)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma x\), viewed either in \(K\) or its specified image in \(E\). Write \(N_I(A)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma A\) for the corresponding product of fractional ideals of \(E\). This convention keeps the ideals in \(E\). [The host embedding index and its norm](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/WeightCongruences/ProdCmTypeEqNormAndWeightCongruenceOne.lean#L8-L62)

Fix the source's \(\chi=\chi_\Psi\). Let \(\lambda\) be its finite character on nonzero fractional ideals of \(E\), using **positive** local uniformizer valuations. It sends a prime \(P\) to \(\chi.\mathrm{val}(P)\), the source's \(\mathrm{valTw}(0,P)\). Unramifiedness makes the choice of uniformizer immaterial. A continuous character trivial on each local unit group is trivial on their product because the finite-support subgroup is dense. Triviality on the diagonally embedded \(E^\times\) and the source's positive archimedean weight two then give
\[
 \lambda((a))=\prod_{\tau\in\Psi}\left(\tau(a)/|\tau(a)|\right)^{-2}
             =\phi_0 N(ca/a),\qquad a\in E^\times .
\]
For every prime \(v\) of \(F\), take an element of \(F^\times\) of valuation one at \(v\) and apply the finite-place part of \(\chi.\mathrm{TrivOn}(F)\) at that single place. It gives \(\prod_{P\mid v}\lambda(P)^{e(P/v)}=1\). Therefore \(\lambda(jB)=1\) for every fractional ideal \(B\) of \(F\), with \(jB=B\mathcal O_E\). This also explains the ramification multiplicity if one works over a general CM extension. ## The exact Kummer field and the valuation at every prime

Let \(C=\mathrm{Cl}(E)/j\mathrm{Cl}(F)\), and let \(h>0\) annihilate the class of a particular fractional ideal \(A\) in \(C\). Write \(A^h=(a)jB\). Set \(u=N(ca/a)\in K^\times\) and \(J(A)=N_I(cA/A)\). Applying the preceding two identities gives
\[
                   \lambda(A)^h=\phi_0 u,\qquad (u)\mathcal O_E=J(A)^h .
\]
For the second identity, conjugate the class relation and divide by the relation itself. The extended real ideal cancels before taking the product of conjugates. Thus \(u\) belongs to the source's exact set of elements whose ideals are \(h\)-th powers. Its Kummer field adjoins **every** root of \(X^h-\phi_0 u\). This proves membership for the preselected complex number \(\lambda(A)\), without selecting a new root. [Original power ideals, Kummer field and root membership](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/AutomorphicMembership/FixedCoefficientFieldInComplex.lean#L247-L270) · [Two simultaneous power witnesses in Lean](AlpogeDeterminantCharacterField.lean)

In particular take \(h=h^*=[D.\mathrm{ray}:E]\). The ray class group maps onto the ordinary class group: every ideal class has a representative coprime to the modulus by approximation, and ray-principal relations are principal. The source's ray-field predicate identifies the character kernel of the Artin map on coprime ideals with the ray-principal ideals, and its Frobenius elements generate the abelian Galois group. Characters separate elements of a finite abelian group. Positive products of the stated Frobenius generators already contain their inverses, so the Artin image of coprime ideals is the whole group. If two coprime integral ideals have the same Artin symbol, multiply both by a third coprime integral ideal with the inverse symbol. The kernel test now shows both products principal, giving the same ordinary class for both original ideals. Hence \(h_E\mid h^*\) also under the precise source predicate. Every \(P\) therefore has \(\chi.\mathrm{valTw}(0,P)\in D.V\). The field generated by all these values lies in \(D.V\le D.\mathrm{Om}\), recovering the weight-two, zero-twist case of the archived general ray argument by relative ideal classes. For the source fields chosen with \(B.\mathrm{KsC}=D.\mathrm{Om}\), the designated fixed coefficient field contains this field by \(B.\mathrm{KsC}\le B.\mathrm{fixedCoefficientFieldInComplex}\). For an arbitrary independently chosen field the finite test below applies. Identify \(E\) with its image under \(\phi_0\). In any number field \(L\) containing \(E\) and \(\lambda(A)\), the fractional ideals \((\lambda(A))\) and \(J(A)\mathcal O_L\) have the same positive \(h\)-th power. Unique ideal factorization gives
\[
  (\lambda(A))=J(A)\mathcal O_L,\qquad
  \operatorname{ord}_{W}(\lambda(A))
   =e(W/P)\sum_{\sigma\in G}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{\sigma^{-1}P}(cA)
                                  -\operatorname{ord}_{\sigma^{-1}P}(A)\bigr)
\]
for every \(W\mid P\) of \(L/E\). This works when the residue characteristic divides \(h\). In particular if \(A\) is prime to all places over a rational prime \(p\), every \(W\mid p\) sees a unit. The formula also allows negative valuations for other ideals. For a norm-one rational determinant \(z=k/ck\), finite evaluation on \((k)\) gives \(\phi_0 N(z)^{-1}\). Choice of lift does not matter since two such lifts differ by \(F^\times\). A lift exists by taking \(k=1+z\) when \(z\ne-1\) and any nonzero trace-zero element when \(z=-1\). On each tied determinant chart the surjection from idèles and the triviality on the finite units now recover the actual coefficient-field class values by multiplication, including all integer chart powers. ## Finite generators, existence and a sharp test

Take invariant-factor generators \([A_i]\) of \(C\), of orders \(d_i\), with \(A_i^{d_i}=(a_i)jB_i\). Above the embedded quadratic field \(K\), the field of all character values on ideals is exactly \(K(\lambda(A_i):i)\). Every prime ideal value occurs on the left, and every ideal value is a finite product of these values and their inverses. To see the displayed generators suffice, write an arbitrary ideal as a product of their powers times a principal and an extended real ideal. The last two factors contribute an element of \(K^\times\). Conversely each generator value occurs among the ideal values. Each successive adjunction has degree at most \(d_i\) by the monic equation \(X^{d_i}=\phi_0 N(ca_i/a_i)\). The tower law gives a total relative degree at most \(\prod_i d_i=|C|\), including the trivial group. The argument proves finiteness of this particular field without an estimate on the entire Kummer field.

We can also construct such characters. Put \(S=\{(a)jB\}\) and send \((a)jB\) to \(\phi_0 N(ca/a)\). If \((a)\) is an extended real ideal, \(ca/a\) is a unit, all of whose conjugates have modulus one. It is a root of unity by Kronecker's argument: all powers have bounded integral minimal-polynomial coefficients and bounded degree, so only finitely many can occur. On the selected host the norm to \(K\) of every root of unity is one. Indeed \(d>3\) is prime, hence the only roots of unity in \(K\) are \(\pm1\), which disposes of the odd-order part. For exact two-power order at least four, \(K\) is disjoint from the two-power cyclotomic field because \(d\) ramifies in \(K\) while only 2 ramifies in that cyclotomic field. The norm over \(K\) in their compositum is the product of all primitive conjugates, equal to one by inverse pairing. Transitivity gives the same answer in \(E\). Order two contributes \((-1)^{[E:K]}=1\) since \(4\mid [E:\mathbb Q]\). Thus the map on \(S\) is well defined. For each generator choose any root of \(X^{d_i}=\phi_0 N(ca_i/a_i)\), all of modulus one. Unique normal form modulo \(S\) with powers in \([0,d_i)\) extends the map to all fractional ideals; carries use exactly the specified power relations. Every permitted finite character arises this way. Composing with the finite ideal map and multiplying by the product of the positive archimedean phases squared gives a continuous unitary idèle character trivial on diagonals. It has trivial finite restriction to \(F\) by construction; the phase squared on a real component is one. Its finite character is unramified at every prime. This recovers the source existence input as well as field control for the original choice. There is a second construction specific to this induced CM type. Choose a weight-two unramified ideal character \(\lambda_K\) on the quadratic field alone. The principal formula \((x)\mapsto \phi_0(\bar x/x)\) is well defined there because \(\mathcal O_K^\times=\{\pm1\}\). Extend it by the same invariant-factor root construction on \(\mathrm{Cl}(K)\); rational ideals have value one. Pull back by the ideal norm \(N_{E/K}\). On principal ideals this has exactly the required complex type. For every real ideal \(B\), its extended ideal has norm \(N_{F/\mathbb Q}(B)\mathcal O_K\): after extending to \(E\), the left side is the product of the \(G\)-conjugates of \(B\mathcal O_E\), including splitting and ramification multiplicities. Restriction identifies \(G\) with \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\), since \(F\) is the fixed field of the central CM conjugation and \(E=FK\). The product is the claimed right side extended to \(E\), and extension of fractional ideals from \(K\) is injective by prime factorization. Thus the pullback is trivial on extended real ideals. For the actual preselected character the ratio
\[
       A\longmapsto \lambda(A)/\lambda_K(N_{E/K}A)
\]
is trivial on principal ideals and extended real ideals. It factors through the finite group \(C\). In particular, if \(t=\exp C\), its values are \(t\)-th roots of unity. Every permitted determinant character therefore has values in the same field \(K(\lambda_K(\text{all ideals}),\mu_t)\) after fixing a single quadratic-field character. This gives the additional degree bound \(h_K\varphi(t)\) by the quadratic-field generator argument and the cyclotomic degree. This common field also reconciles all admissible class-character twists; it need not be the minimal field for one choice.

For a sharp check on the general relative degree bound, take \(E=K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})\), \(F=\mathbb Q\), \(\omega=(1+\sqrt{-23})/2\), \(P=(2,\omega-1)\). This tests the fractional-ideal assertion for a general CM character, outside the host's degree restriction. The three reduced forms of discriminant \(-23\) give class number three. The lattice products give \(P^3=(1+\omega)\), \(P\bar P=(2)\); there is no element of norm 2 since \(4N(a+b\omega)=(2a+b)^2+23b^2\). Thus \([P]\) has order three. The ratio \(u=(1+\bar\omega)/(1+\omega)\) cannot be a cube in \(E\): a cube root would generate the ideal \(\bar P/P\), whose class is \([P]^{-2}\ne1\). Hence \(X^3-u\) is irreducible and the degree bound is attained. Here the character on principal ideals is well defined since every unit is \(\pm1\); extended rational ideals are principal with value one. The character extension therefore exists in this example too. Independent integer lattice controls distinguish the prime from its conjugate and the two possible signs in the valuation formula.
