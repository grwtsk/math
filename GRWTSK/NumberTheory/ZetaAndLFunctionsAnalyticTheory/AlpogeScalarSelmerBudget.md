# Residual character orders in the scalar even-weight Selmer bound

grwtsk contributors

Write \(G_m=K_{4m}(\mathbb Z)\) for \(m\ge1\). The trivial-character restriction of Alpöge's `CorG` requires \(\log|(G_m)_{\mathrm{odd}}|\ll m^7\) with an absolute constant. For each odd prime \(p\), put \(r_p=(p-1)/\gcd(p-1,2m)\), the order of the residual character \(\omega^{-2m}\). We obtain the following results for the actual groups.

* The entire \(p\)-primary group vanishes if \(p-1\mid 8m\) or \(p-1\mid12m\).
* If \(r_p=5\), the entire primary group is cyclic. If also \(p\mid m\), its order is at most \(p\).
* Over all \(p\) with \(r_p\ge2\) and \(v_p(2m)\ge r_p-2\), the product of the full primary orders divides \(2m\).
* For arbitrary integers \(A,R\ge1\), all \(p\) with \(r_p\le R\) together contribute at most \(A\tau(2m)R(R+1)\log(2mR+1)/2\) to \(\sum_p\log|G_m[p^A]|\). This gives a uniform polynomial range in both depth and residual degree.

The first statement recovers the small-order vanishing obtained by a separate analysis of the ordinary host. The last two statements apply also at larger residual orders. [Alpöge, CorG, revision 40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeightAndVanishing/Counts.lean#L18-L47) · [Scalar strict-group identification](AlpogeSelmerExceptionalWeights.md) · [Prior small-order results, Cohomological windows for the ordinary host, §4](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/198)

## Prime-conductor subfields

Let \(E_p\) be the subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\mu_p)\) cut out by \(\omega^{-2m}\). It is totally real, cyclic of degree \(r_p\), and has conductor \(p\) if \(r_p>1\). Lim's Corollary 4.3 identifies the rank of \(G_m[p]\) with the \(\omega^{-2m}\)-rank of the cyclotomic class group for \(r_p>1\). The unique prime above \(p\) is principal in the full cyclotomic field, and the decomposition group there is its entire Galois group. Restriction and norm identify the \(p\)-classes invariant under the kernel of the character with the \(p\)-class group of \(E_p\), because the kernel order is prime to \(p\). In particular the rank times \(\log p\) is at most \(\log h(E_p)\). At \(r_p=1\) the rank is zero by the same corollary with base field \(\mathbb Q\). Zero rank implies full primary vanishing by finiteness. [Meng Fai Lim, On the structure of even K-groups of rings of algebraic integers, v2, Corollary 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00168v2#page=9)

For \(r_p=2\), the real quadratic Minkowski bound gives \(h(E_p)<p\) as in the preceding manuscript. For \(r_p=3\), Moser proved \(h(E_p)<p/3\). For \(r_p=4\), with real quadratic subfield \(F\), Balady and Washington give \(h(E_p)/h(F)<p\) at prime conductors larger than 268.01 from a regulator bound and an explicit estimate at one for an even primitive Dirichlet character. This ratio is an integer. Indeed \(E_p/F\) is totally ramified at the prime over \(p\) and hence disjoint over \(F\) from its Hilbert class field. Base extension of that Hilbert class field is an unramified abelian extension of \(E_p\) of degree \(h(F)\). Thus neither integer factor of \(h(E_p)\) is divisible by \(p\). The finite computational result cited below covers the remaining conductors. [Claude Moser, Sur le nombre de classes d'un corps réel cyclique de conducteur premier, degré au plus quatre, Grenoble 1979–1980](https://www.numdam.org/article/STNG_1979-1980__8__A3_0.pdf) · [Balady and Washington, A family of cyclic quartic fields, §6, p. 55](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/112758)

For \(r_p=6\), write \(E=E_p\) and \(F,C\) for its quadratic and cubic subfields. Write \(H=h(E)/(h(F)h(C))\). This is a positive integer by the class-number divisibility in Louboutin §3.1. For a generating relative unit \(u\), its norms to both subfields are signs. With a generator \(s\) of the cyclic Galois group, put \(a=\log|u|, b=\log|s(u)|, Q=a^2+b^2-ab\). The relative units are generated modulo signs by \(u,s(u)\). Louboutin's Lemma 3 gives \(12 R_FR_C Q=I R_E\), \(I\in\{1,3,4,12\}\), where the \(R\)'s are the ordinary regulators. For a generating sextic Dirichlet character \(\chi\), divide the class number formula for \(E\) by the formulas for \(F,C\). The two faithful characters give \(|L(1,\chi)|^2\), the discriminant factor is \(p\), and the real-place and root-of-unity factors give \(1/4\). Thus
\[
         H=\frac{I p |L(1,\chi)|^2}{48Q}\le\frac{p|L(1,\chi)|^2}{4Q}.
\]
[Louboutin, Class numbers of real cyclotomic fields, §3.1, Lemma 3 and adjoining divisibility result](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1169/download/)

There is an elementary lower bound for \(Q\) using the norm of one unit difference. Since \(p\) is totally ramified, the conjugates of \(u\) have the same residue \(x\in\mathbb F_p^\times\). The two subfield norms give \(x^2=\pm1\) and \(x^3=\pm1\), so \(x^2=1\). Choose \(\delta\in\{1,-1\}\) with \(x=\delta\). The nonzero integer \(N_{E/\mathbb Q}(1-\delta u)\) is divisible by \(p\). The six logarithms of the absolute conjugates of \(u\) are \(a,b,b-a,-a,-b,a-b\). Indeed the quadratic norm to \(C\) pairs indices three apart with opposite logs, while the cubic norm to \(F\) sets the sum at three even indices to zero. Each pair of absolute values \(e^t,e^{-t}\) contributes at most \((1+e^t)(1+e^{-t})\le4e^{|t|}\) to the absolute norm. Consequently
\[
 \log(p/64)\le |a|+|b|+|a-b|,\qquad
 3(|a|+|b|+|a-b|)^2\le16Q .
\]
The second inequality follows by the six orderings of \(0,a,b\), or by bounding each of \(a^2,b^2,(a-b)^2\) by \(4Q/3\). For \(p\ge2^{16}\) we obtain \(Q\ge(3/16)\log^2(p/64)\). The even primitive character bound \(|L(1,\chi)|\le (\log p+c)/2\), \(c=2+\gamma-\log(4\pi)<\log2\), now gives \(H<p\). Indeed \((L+c)^2<3(L-6\log2)^2\) for \(L\ge16\log2\). The cubic and quadratic factors are already prime to \(p\). Below this finite cutoff we use Buhler and Harvey's published verification of Vandiver through their stated range, which includes every prime below \(2^{16}\). We have not rerun that published computation. The class-number bound for large conductors uses none of its data. The order divides 4 or 6 exactly when \(p-1\mid8m\) or \(p-1\mid12m\), respectively. [Even-character bound in Balady–Washington, p. 55](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/112758) · [Buhler and Harvey, Irregular primes to 163 million, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.2121v2)

The prior ordinary-host manuscript reaches the same sextic vanishing using Gras's resolvent and a stronger conductor bound. The norm of \(1-\delta u\) above gives a different integral estimate and a larger finite cutoff. As checks on the factors themselves, PARI/GP 2.17.4 and `bnfcertify=1` gave \((h(F),h(C),h(E))=(1,4,16)\) at sextic conductor 349, and quadratic and quartic class numbers \((1,5)\) at conductor 457. These are nontrivial relative class numbers. At \(m=29,p=349\) and \(m=57,p=457\) the corresponding residual orders are exactly six and four. [Prior sextic calculation, §4](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/198)

## Full primary orders controlled by the weight

For \(r=r_p\ge2\), the conductor–discriminant formula gives \(D_{E_p}=p^{r-1}\). All the nontrivial characters have conductor \(p\). Put \(M_r=r!/r^r\). Minkowski gives an integral representative of norm at most \(M_r\sqrt{D_{E_p}}\) in every class. Counting these ideals by the Dedekind zeta series at two gives
\[
 h(E_p)\le M_r^2 D_{E_p}\zeta_{E_p}(2)\le M_r^2 D_{E_p}\zeta(2)^r < D_{E_p}.
\]
Indeed \(M_1=1\) and \(M_{r+1}/M_r=(1+1/r)^{-r}\le1/2\) by the binomial theorem. Thus \(M_r^2 2^r\le2^{2-r}\le1\), and \(\zeta(2)<2\). In particular \(j=v_p(h(E_p))\le r-2\).

Write \(v=v_p(2m)\). Up through depth \(v+1\), the cyclotomic character to the power \(-2m\) has values equal to its Teichmüller lift modulo \(p^{v+1}\). Indeed the principal-unit part belongs to \(1+p\mathbb Z_p\) and raising it to \(2m\) puts it in \(1+p^{v+1}\mathbb Z_p\). For \(1\le b\le v+1\), the \(p^b\)-torsion of the strict group is therefore the group of equivariant maps from \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_{E_p}[1/p])\) to \(\mathbb Z/p^b\) with that fixed character. To see this, lift a strict class along multiplication by \(p^b\) on the divisible coefficient. Residual inertia at \(p\) is nontrivial since \(r>1\), giving zero local and global invariants and hence a unique lift which remains strict at \(p\). At primes away from \(p\) the coefficient is unramified and its inertia invariants are divisible, so the lift is unramified. In the other direction every such unramified finite-coefficient class is locally zero on divisible coefficients away from \(p\), since Frobenius minus one is surjective. Prime-to-\(p\) descent to \(E_p\) and class field theory give the asserted Hom group. The cardinality is bounded by \(p^j\) by elementary divisors. [Finite-layer strict descent and stabilization](AlpogeSelmerReflection.md)

If \(v\ge j\), the sizes at depths \(j,j+1\) coincide because the class group has \(p\)-primary exponent at most \(p^j\). This includes \(j=0\), when there is no residual class. Equal successive torsion subgroups of a finite \(p\)-group force stabilization forever, since any element of greater order would have a multiple of order \(p^{j+1}\). Thus \(|(G_m)_{p^\infty}|\le p^j\le p^v\). In particular the explicit condition \(v\ge r-2\) suffices, and multiplying over those primes yields **a divisor of \(2m\)**. This is a full-order estimate at unbounded residual degree. The class-number criterion \(v\ge j\) can also apply outside the explicit range.

## A quintic bound for all weights

Suppose \(r_p=5\) and write \(E=E_p\). We show \(v_p(h(E))\le1\). For a unit \(u\ne\pm1\) put \(x_i=\log|u_i|\) at the five real embeddings and \(T=\sum_i\max(0,x_i)\). Every such unit generates the field, since the degree is prime. The discriminant of its minimal polynomial is divisible by \(p^4\). Bounding each of its ten root differences by \(2\max(1,|u_i|)\max(1,|u_j|)\) gives \(p^4\le 2^{20}e^{8T}\). Thus \(T\ge\tfrac12\log(p/32)\). The positive and negative coordinates each have absolute sum \(T\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\sum_i x_i^2\ge(1/k+1/(5-k))T^2\ge5T^2/6\) with \(k\) positive coordinates, placing any zeros on the other side. The shortest log-unit vector consequently has squared norm at least \((5/24)\log^2(p/32)\) for \(p>32\). The rank-four unit lattice has Euclidean covolume \(\sqrt5 R_E\), since orthogonal projection deleting one coordinate has Jacobian \(1/\sqrt5\) on the zero-sum hyperplane. The exact Hermite constant \(\gamma_4=\sqrt2\) now gives
\[
 R_E\ge \frac{25}{1152\sqrt5}\log^4(p/32).
\]
The class-number formula is \(16h(E)R_E=p^2\prod_{\chi\ne1} L(1,\chi)\), with four primitive even characters of conductor \(p\). Thus
\[
 \frac{h(E)}{p^2}\le\frac{9\sqrt5}{50}
 \left(\frac{\log p+c}{\log(p/32)}\right)^4<1
 \qquad(p\ge2^{27}).
\]
Indeed \(c<\tfrac12\log2\) and the logarithmic ratio is at most \(5/4\). The coefficient is less than \(81/200\), and \((81/200)(5/4)^4<1\). For \(p<2^{27}\), the published Buhler–Harvey cutoff of 163,577,356 gives zero \(p\)-part by class-group descent. This proves the claimed valuation bound at every such prime. [Ryshkov and Baranovskii, Classical methods in the theory of lattice packings, §6, four-dimensional bound](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/2c27056c794a85f3b79d4848a361520d/rm4080_eng.pdf) · [Even-character bound](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/112758) · [Buhler–Harvey, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.2121v2)

By residual descent the rank of the full primary group is at most one, so it is cyclic. If \(p\mid m\), the class-number valuation is at most \(v_p(2m)\); the full-order argument above then bounds its order by \(p\). The product over all such order-five primes dividing \(m\) divides the radical of \(m\). The rank bound alone leaves the exponent undetermined when \(p\nmid m\). The earlier ordinary-host small-order calculation does not supply this quintic estimate. The geometric quintic argument is proved here in prose.

At prime conductors 191 and 941, PARI/GP certified quintic class groups \(\mathbb Z/11\) and \((\mathbb Z/2)^4\), respectively. Neither has a conductor-primary factor. The fundamental-unit regulator is strictly between 185 and 186 at 191 and strictly between 580 and 581 at 941. The replay isolates every real root with rational sign-changing endpoints and encloses the logarithmic determinant using rational bounds on its entries. Both intervals verify the lower bound above on these fields. The cases \(m=(p-1)/10\) and \(m=p(p-1)/10\) check the two divisibility regimes with the same residual order. The script also certifies the sextic and quartic controls at 349 and 457. [PARI/GP verification script](../../../scripts/verify_alpoge_scalar_selmer.gp)

## Counting small residual degrees at arbitrary finite depth

For any depth \(A\ge1\), elementary divisors give \(\log|G_m[p^A]|\le A\log p\cdot\dim_{\mathbb F_p}G_m[p]\). At \(r=r_p\ge2\) the latter bound is at most \(A(r-1)\log p\) by the preceding class-number estimate. For fixed \(r\), every candidate prime satisfies \(p=1+rg\) with \(g=\gcd(2m,p-1)\mid2m\). These at most \(\tau(2m)\) choices include all such primes. Therefore, for every \(A,R\ge1\),
\[
 \sum_{p\ {\mathrm{odd}},\ r_p\le R}\log|G_m[p^A]|
 \le \frac{A\tau(2m)R(R+1)}{2}\log(2mR+1).
\]
In particular use \(R=m^2\) and \(A=\lfloor m^3/[\tau(2m)\log(2m^3+1)]\rfloor\), allowing \(A=0\) and the trivial group at depth zero. The sum is at most \(m^7\). This estimates a growing number of layers without passing to fields of conductor \(p^A\). Contributions at larger residual degree or deeper torsion outside the full-order cases above remain unbounded by this argument.

## Comparison with general K-theory estimates

Soulé's Theorem 2 gives \(\mathrm{card}_{2n+2}K_n(\mathbb Z)_{\mathrm{tors}}\le k(n)\), where the subscript discards entire primary parts at primes at most \(2n+2\). Lemma 2 gives \(\log k(n)\le n^{20n^4}\) for \(n\ge6\). His Theorem 3 uses the odd twist \(2m+1\) and bounds the product at primes \(p\ge8m+3\). Thus \(n=4m\) yields a uniform upper bound for the joint large-prime order with a right-hand side much larger after taking logarithms than the required \(Cm^7\). Gabber's elementary determinant bound in Soulé's Proposition 3 acts on a complex whose number of cells enters that bound. Bayer-Fluckiger, Emery and Houriet's Theorem 1.1 excludes \(F=\mathbb Q\) and likewise removes small primes. Passing by transfer to \(\mathbb Q(i)\) gives an upper bound of their fixed-degree form and retains the degree-dependent powers of \(2n+1\). Emery's imaginary-field result for \(K_2\) covers the preceding weight-one argument. It supplies no estimate as the K-degree grows. [Soulé, Perfect forms and the Vandiver conjecture, Theorems 2–3 and Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9812171v1#page=9) · [Bayer-Fluckiger, Emery and Houriet, Hermitian lattices and bounds in K-theory of algebraic integers, v3, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5953v3#page=2) · [Emery, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.6132v2#page=2)

De la Torre Durán's v2 Theorem 4.5 gives, on substituting \(F=\mathbb Q\), rank parameter \(8m+1\) and \(D_F=1\), a large-prime log-order bound \(\exp(O(m^2\log(2m)))\). It still removes all primes at most \(8m+2\). The stronger target allows contributions at every odd prime and asks for a polynomial log-order bound even on the retained large primes. The Bernoulli numerator formula for the orders in degree \(4m-2\) has the opposite parity of Tate twist. Substituting it for \(G_m\) would already presuppose control of the even cyclotomic class components. [De la Torre Durán, Perfect Forms and Torsion Bounds in Algebraic K-Theory over Totally Real and CM Fields, v2, Theorem 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.06300v2#S4.SS2) · [Soulé, §3.1, parity of the Tate twist](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9812171v1#page=10)
