# An integral dyadic coordinate on the CM period lattice

grwtsk contributors

Let \(d>4\) be prime, \(d\equiv3\pmod4\), \(K=\mathbb Q(w)\), \(w=i\sqrt d\), and let the period pair \(L\) span the embedded ring of integers \(\mathcal O_K\). For any nonzero odd-order point \(P\in\mathbb C/L\) and any \(\Omega^{12}=\Delta(L)=g_2(L)^3-27g_3(L)^2\), the number
\[
                  X(P)=\frac{s_2(L)+\wp_L(P)}{\Omega^2}
\]
is algebraic and integral at every place above 2. Here \(s_2\) is the complex-linear coefficient of the real-linear Weierstrass quasi-period map. The conclusion holds for any invertible ideal lattice of the same maximal order. The original general bound allows \(v_2(X(P))\ge-1\) in this case. Its universal hypothesis of stability under \(w\) alone permits an order of conductor two. We use the full order specifically in the inert case. The period pair prescribed in **ClassTermBoundBeta** is exactly \(1,(1+w)/2\) for these \(d\). ## The integral trace and its factor of two

Write \(Y(P)=\wp_L(P)/\Omega^2\). The short equation with coordinates \(Y(P),\wp'_L(P)/(2\Omega^3)\) has discriminant 1. Its coefficients are algebraic because \(j(L)\) is a singular modulus and \(g_2^3/\Omega^{12}=j(L)/1728\), \(27g_3^2/\Omega^{12}=j(L)/1728-1\). Torsion coordinates are therefore algebraic. Fix a dyadic embedding. The CM \(j\)-invariant is integral. After a finite extension take a good integral Weierstrass equation, with coefficients \(a_i\), \(b_2=a_1^2+4a_2\), coordinate \(x\), and unit discriminant. The standard change to short form gives \(Y=u^2(x+b_2/12)\), with \(u\) a unit since the discriminants differ by its twelfth power. A nonzero odd-order point retains nonzero reduction by the formal-group reduction theorem, so its affine coordinate \(x\) is integral. In particular \(4Y(P)\) and every \(Y(T)-Y(P)\) at nonzero odd-order \(T,P\) are integral. This proves the required bounds for the actual normalized differential, even at residue characteristic two. A different twelfth root multiplies \(X,Y\) by a root of unity. For a nonreal multiplier \(a\in\mathcal O_K\) of odd norm \(n\), the trace
\[
 F_a=(a^2-n)X(P)-(a^2-1)Y(P)
      =\sum_{0\ne T\in\ker[a]}(Y(T)-Y(P))
\]
belongs to \(2\mathcal O_{\overline{\mathbb Q}_2}\). To see the equality, the elliptic functions \(\sum_T\wp_L(z+T)\) and \(a^2\wp_L(az)\) differ by the constant \(c=\sum_{T\ne0}\wp_L(T)\), by their principal parts and their expansions at zero. Integrating with \(\zeta'=-\wp\) and taking the quasi-period at \(\lambda\in L\) gives \(n\eta(\lambda)=a\eta(a\lambda)-c\lambda\). Substituting \(\eta(z)=s_2 z+h\bar z\) gives \(c=(a^2-n)s_2\). In particular the trace also proves algebraicity of \(s_2/\Omega^2\). In the finite sum, \(T\mapsto-T\) pairs equal summands because the kernel has odd order and \(\wp\) is even. Each pair is twice an integral difference. The primary argument in the archive bounds each difference and sums them, which leaves this factor unused. If \(d\equiv7\pmod8\), take \(a=w\), for which \(F_w=-2dX+(d+1)Y\). Both \(F_w\) and \((d+1)Y\) are divisible by two in the valuation ring. The second assertion uses \(8\mid d+1\) and the bound on \(4Y\). Thus \(dX\), and hence \(X\), are integral.

If \(d\equiv3\pmod8\), take \(a=m+(1+w)/2\), \(b=\bar a\), with \(m=0\) or \(1\). The possible norms \(n=m^2+m+(d+1)/4\) are odd and differ by two, so one is \(3\bmod4\). The two integral traces have even right sides. Eliminating \(Y\) gives
\[
       (b^2-1) F_a-(a^2-1)F_b=(n-1)(a^2-b^2)X.
\]
Now \(a^2-b^2=(2m+1)w\) is a dyadic unit and \((n-1)/2\) is odd. Division by two in the equality yields integrality of \(X\). Subtracting the traces also gives \((a^2-b^2)(X-Y)\in2\mathcal O\). Hence \(s_2/\Omega^2\) is itself even in the inert case and \(Y(P)\) is integral. Both multipliers preserve the actual lattice. If \(d\equiv1\pmod4\), \((1+w)/2\) is not integral, so this choice gives no such general bound there.

## Exact controls on the two dyadic branches

For \(d=7\) the curve \(y^2+xy=x^3-x^2-2x-1\) has \(b_2=-3\), discriminant \(-7^3\) and full CM. The three distinct \(x\)-coordinates in the nonzero \([w]\)-kernel are the roots of the unique cubic factor \(x^3+x^2-2x-1\) of its seventh division polynomial. Indeed that kernel is Galois stable, since \(w\) and \(-w\) have the same kernel. Factoring gives no lower-degree factors. The sum of these three roots is \(-1\). The trace formula at \(w\) gives \(X(P)/u^2=x(P)+b_2/21-(-1)/7=x(P)\). For \(d=11\), take \(y^2+y=x^3-x^2-7x+10\), with \(b_2=-4\), discriminant \(-11^3\) and full CM. Its third division polynomial factors as \((3x^2-13x+15)(x^2+3x-6)\). The two conjugate norm-three kernels are defined over \(K\), so their single \(x\)-coordinates are \((13\pm w)/6\). The other factor has discriminant 33. In the traces for \(a=(1+w)/2,b=\bar a\), equality of the two computed values of \(s_2/(u^2\Omega^2)\) forces the value \(-2/3\). Hence \(X(P)/u^2=x(P)-1\). For \(d=7\), \(Y(P)/u^2=x(P)-1/4\) has valuation exactly \(-2\) at each dyadic place for every nonzero odd-order point. The period correction cancels its denominator. For \(d=11\), the correction \(s_2/(u^2\Omega^2)=-2/3\) has valuation exactly one, attaining the inert divisibility just proved. ## Return to the full lattice hypotheses

Under the source change \(L=c(\mathbb Z+\tau\mathbb Z)\), its variable \(\omega=\Omega c/(2\pi i)\) satisfies \(\omega^{12}=DD(\tau)\). Its shifted \(G_2^*\)-and-\(\wp\) expression divided by \(\omega^2\) is exactly \(X(P)\). Thus for \(d\equiv3\bmod4\) at 2, the elliptic torsion input in the Hilbert lattice jet argument has exponent zero for every nonzero point of the specified odd order, independently of level. The consumer also requires a Hilbert Eisenstein expansion, a full lattice inclusion, a different bound, and a differentiated evaluation with the published normalizations. Its parameter \(\rho\) must still dominate the unrelated inequality \(b+v_2(\operatorname{disc}F)/[F:\mathbb Q]+\rho_0\le\rho\). Improving this elliptic input alone gives no automatic reduction in the final global loss.  12\big(c_E+kgc+h(2c+\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\ell=d})+Wa_0\big)
