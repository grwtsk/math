# Finite places and period descent of the normalized theta lift

grwtsk contributors

Fix a stage, twist, definite source and target of Levent Alpöge's theta construction. Write \(E\) for the CM field, \(m=\dim_E V\), \(r=\dim_E W\), and \(K'\) for the coefficient field of the marked geometric row. The class scalar multiplying the short theta sum admits an exact valuation at **each** finite place of \(K'\). In particular it is a unit wherever the class lattice is standard above the underlying rational prime. The index used to refine the definite classes cancels on every source input. These calculations concern two specific factors in the map whose analytic adjoint was computed [previously](AlpogeThetaAdjointPairing.md). The remaining integral content of the theta sections and the identification of their rational period are separate obligations.

## The class scalar, including the primes which split

Let \(L\) be the class lattice in the finite adelic source frame, and \(\Lambda=\overline L\) the lattice used for the short sum. Define
\[
 I=[L:L\cap\mathcal O_E^m]
   =[\Lambda:\Lambda\cap\mathcal O_E^m],\qquad
 s_L=\mathrm{ep}(L)\frac{\mathrm{zCard}(N,r)}{\mathrm{mIdx}(N,r)}.
\]
In a full term there is also a division by the cardinality of the finite marking tuples, a finite Fourier weight, a harmonic polynomial, and the common cover correction. They are outside \(s_L\). The field data specify \(\mathrm{ep}(L)=q/B(\mathfrak c)\) for the positive rational \(q\) satisfying \(q^2I^r=N\mathfrak c\). Here \(\det L=\mathfrak c/\bar{\mathfrak c}\), with \(\mathfrak c\) integral, coprime to its conjugate and supported away from the bad ideal of the tuple. In particular the prescribed finite character is unramified on its support. The determinant relation determines \(\mathfrak c\) by taking positive valuations. The index quotient above equals \(I^r\), by multiplying the indices along \(N\mathcal O_E^m\subseteq\Lambda\cap\mathcal O_E^m\subseteq\Lambda\) and taking \(r\) copies. All these lattices have finite index by admissibility of \(N\). Thus the factor is \(s_L=q I^r/B(\mathfrak c)\) for every admitted \(N\). Take the distinguished embedding of \(K'\) in \(\mathbb C\) from the data. Fix a prime \(\lambda\mid p\) of that field and extend it to a normal number field containing all the embedded fields used below. Normalize its valuation \(v\) by \(v(p)=1\). For each CM embedding let
\[
 b_\tau=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{\tau^{-1}\lambda}(\mathfrak c)}
               {e(\tau^{-1}\lambda/p)}.
\]
Here \(\tau^{-1}\lambda\) denotes the restriction of the *chosen extension* via \(\tau\). Put \(\chi=\mu_{0,i}\xi_t\eta^{-1}\). For one \(\tau\) from each CM pair, write \(n_\tau\) for its exponent on \(z/|z|\). In the archive's distinguished CM type the \(\mu_{0,i}\) exponent is \(-2e_{i,\tau}\), the finite-order twist has zero exponent on the connected complex archimedean groups, and the \(\eta\) exponent is one. Hence \(n_\tau=-2e_{i,\tau}-1\). The two formulas are
\[
\begin{split}
2v(\mathrm{ep}(L))&=-r v_p(I)+\sum_{\tau} n_\tau(b_\tau-b_{\bar\tau}),\\
2v(s_L)&=r v_p(I)+\sum_{\tau} n_\tau(b_\tau-b_{\bar\tau}).                 \tag{1}
\end{split}
\]
The ideal appearing in the \(b\)'s belongs to \(L\). Replacing it with the positive determinant ideal of \(\Lambda\) would reverse the signed sum. [Definition of the ideal character and infinity convention](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/UnitaryNorms/IsUnitaryNormOf.lean#L56-L99) · [Weights of the two splitting characters](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Columns/AbelianHostFieldConditions.lean#L308-L325) · [Finite order of the twist](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/LinksDefs.lean#L443-L455)

To prove (1), take a ray modulus containing the conductor of \(\chi\), coprime to \(\mathfrak c\). Finiteness of the ray class group gives \(h>0\) and \(\alpha\in E^\times\) with \(\mathfrak c^h=(\alpha)\) and \(\alpha\equiv1\) modulo that modulus. This finiteness follows from finiteness of the ordinary ideal class group and the finite group of residue units. At the conductor the principal finite idele is in the kernel. At unramified places its unit part drops out. Triviality of the global character on the principal idele therefore gives \(\chi(\mathfrak c)^h=\chi_\infty(\alpha)^{-1}\). By the prescribed value \(B(\mathfrak c)=\sqrt{N\mathfrak c}\,\chi(\mathfrak c)\) in the chosen complex realization,
\[
 B(\mathfrak c)^{2h}
 =(N\mathfrak c)^h\prod_{\tau}
       \left(\frac{\bar\tau(\alpha)}{\tau(\alpha)}\right)^{n_\tau}.
\]
Both sides are now algebraic. Taking the chosen valuation uses \(v(\tau(\alpha))/h=b_\tau\) and gives (1) after substituting \(q^2I^r=N\mathfrak c\). This calculation eliminates the ray exponent without division in a residue field. It is valid also when \(p\mid h\) or \(p\) divides the conductor. The value on the left makes the resulting expression independent of the chosen extension of \(\lambda\). At every complex embedding each algebraic ratio in the product has absolute value one, since conjugation on the CM field commutes with re-embedding. Consequently \(|\sigma(s_L)|^2=I^r\) at every embedding and \(|N_{K'/\mathbb Q}(s_L)|^2=I^{r[K':\mathbb Q]}\). This archimedean norm identity alone loses the signed local sum in (1). [Prescribed value of the character on the ideal](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/ThPinsR.lean#L96-L103)

If \(L\) is standard at every prime over \(p\), its local determinant ideal is the unit ideal there. Coprimality of \(\mathfrak c,\bar{\mathfrak c}\) forces all \(b_\tau=0\). Its intersection has full local index one, so (1) gives \(v(s_L)=v(\mathrm{ep}(L))=0\). More generally at a split pair the two valuations of \(\mathfrak c\) cannot both be positive. Knowing only their sum cannot recover (1). For example the rational Gaussian number \((2+i)/(2-i)=(3+4i)/5\) has absolute value one in both complex embeddings and opposite valuations at the primes above five. The class norm bound cannot turn an arbitrary such phase into a local unit.

## Refining the definite classes

Let \(J\) be the source level and \(J'\le J\) any admitted refinement of index \(d\). At the coarse class of \(y\) put \(\Gamma_y=U(V)(F)\cap yJy^{-1}\). Its action on \(J/J'\) by \(kJ'\mapsto y^{-1}\gamma ykJ'\) has orbit set exactly the refined classes over \(y\): two representatives \(yk,yk'\) give the same class precisely when \(k'J'=y^{-1}\gamma ykJ'\). The stabilizer has order \(g_o=|U(V)(F)\cap ykJ'k^{-1}y^{-1}|\). Orbit-stabilizer gives \(\sum_o|\Gamma_y|/g_o=d\). At a fixed target argument and for a genuine matched source input the full theta summand has the same value \(A_y\) on this fibre. Indeed the oscillator contributes \(\chi_J(k)^{-1}\) at the right source translate, and the source coefficient contributes \(\chi_J(k)\). The diagonal rational action is absorbed by coefficient covariance and automorphy. The unitary real component may be removed using the compact source equivariance. This proves
\[
 \sum_o\frac{A_y}{d g_o}=\frac{A_y}{|\Gamma_y|}.
\]
The argument applies to all inputs and all target evaluations, hence to every exterior power of the map. An arbitrary list of independently assigned refined coefficients need not satisfy the required covariance. The character identity here concerns the complete analytic summand. Transferring it termwise to an integral geometric model requires the comparison of its full marked readings. [Double class and source coefficient](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/Bridge.lean#L33-L49) · [Exact volume, stabilizer and matched inputs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/ThPinsR.lean#L73-L95) · [Source character calculation](AlpogeThetaAdjointPairing.md)

The coarse stabilizer acts faithfully on \(E^m\) and is finite because the definite rational group is discrete in its adelic group and the stabilizer is compact at every place. If \(p\mid|\Gamma_y|\), Cauchy's theorem gives a matrix of order \(p\), with a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity among its eigenvalues. Its characteristic polynomial over \(E\) implies \([E(\zeta_p):E]\le m\). In particular \(p>m[E:\mathbb Q]+1\) is excluded. For smaller primes the faithful representation over \(\mathbb Q\) of dimension \(D=m[E:\mathbb Q]\) gives \(v_p(|\Gamma_y|)\le\sum_{j\ge0}\lfloor D/((p-1)p^j)\rfloor\) by Minkowski's bound. Source coefficient invariants in a stable integral lattice are saturated, as they are the simultaneous kernel of differences with values in a torsion-free module. Taking a basis per invariant class over the local DVR, the stabilizer cost in a degree-\(k\) minor is at most the sum of the \(k\) largest \(v_p(|\Gamma_y|)\), with each class repeated only its invariant rank times. This follows by factoring the denominators from its selected columns. All these valuations use \(v(p)=1\). [Serre, *Bounds for the orders of the finite subgroups of G(k)*, Lecture I, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0346)

## The common period and harmonic jets

The distinguished row uses the **specified** image of \(K=\mathrm{LL}\) under \(\sigma_0\), with equality of coefficient fields. The general twist row only requires containment. Let \(a\) be the flattened vector of the actual comparison matrix in the fixed source and target rational bases, after applying the fixed component identifications. A single phase rationalizes all its entries exactly when, for any nonzero pivot \(a_p\), all \(a_i/a_p\) lie in \(\sigma_0 K\) and \(|a_p|=|\sigma_0 d|\) for some \(d\in K\). Indeed the required phase is \(\sigma_0(d)/a_p\). For the zero matrix take phase one. This includes the period modulus, which is lost in projective descent. Over \(\mathbb Q(i)\) the vector \(\sqrt3(1,2+i)\) has rational projective coordinates and a Hermitian Gram matrix over the field. Three is not a norm from \(\mathbb Q(i)\): clearing denominators in \(a^2+b^2=3c^2\) forces both \(a,b\) and then \(c\) divisible by three. Thus the analytic Hilbert inner product alone does not produce the required scalar. [Exact distinguished and twist fields](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/OneMapT.lean#L143-L166)

In the archival formula the degree \(d_w\) is the total homogeneous Lie degree of the weight. The coefficient in front of the marked complex sum is \(\kappa(-\delta)^{d_w}(\sqrt{2\pi\xi_R})^{-d_w}\), where \(|\kappa|=1\) and \(\delta=\pm1\). The final Lie factor is \((\sqrt{2\pi\xi_R})^{-d_w}\), by exponentiating the defining sum of the row weights times \(-\log(2\pi\xi_R)/2\). In a comparison with the geometric reading the surviving unit factor is therefore \(u=\kappa(-\delta)^{d_w}/c_0\), with \(c_0\) the unit cover-comparison constant. This division uses the comparison of actual marked vacuum and geometric theta trivializations. It determines a permissible overall rescaling of the component constants once that comparison holds. A fixed family's prescribed component constants still require the source's phase compatibility. [Precise comparison and congruence of Lie powers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ExistsAdelicLiftEqGeometricClassSumAllTuples.lean#L114-L206)

One part of the comparison can be checked on every characteristic-zero coefficient algebra, including algebras with nilpotents. Fix the framed exponential coordinates of the abelian scheme and two normalized exponential gauges of its line bundle. Their compatible frame ratios give \(R(z)\) with \(R(0)=1\). The rigidified generator of the polarization identity for \(a\in\mathcal O_E\) is unique: the ratio of two generators is a global unit on the abelian scheme, pulled back from the base since an abelian scheme has structure-sheaf direct image equal to the base structure sheaf, and its value on the identity is one. Dividing the two frame identities gives, with \([a]\) acting diagonally by the CM embeddings,
\[
 R([1+a\bar a]z)=R([a]z)^2R(z)R([a\bar a]z).
\]
Set \(q=\log R\). At \(a=1\), \(q(2z)=4q(z)\), which kills every homogeneous degree except two over a rational algebra. For a quadratic monomial of embedding types \(\tau,\rho\), the coefficient times
\[
 \tau(a)\bar\tau(a)+\rho(a)\bar\rho(a)-2\tau(a)\rho(a)
\]
must vanish for every \(a\). Unless \(\rho=\bar\tau\), this is a nonzero polynomial in the independent embedding coordinates. The integral basis identifies \(\mathcal O_E\) with an integer grid whose image is Zariski dense after the invertible splitting transformation. Some \(a\) gives a nonzero element of the coefficient **field**, hence a unit after base change to any nonzero coefficient algebra. Only conjugate-type quadratic terms remain. A section of power \(k\) changes its reading by \(\exp(-kq)\). [Gauge ratios and their actual functional equation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/GeneratorPowers/GaugeUniqPf.lean#L22-L99)

For \(n\) columns, set \(Q=\sum_b q(z_b)\). Under the jet pairing \(\langle P,f\rangle=P(\partial)f(0)\), multiplication by a variable on the series side is adjoint to differentiation on the polynomial side. Consequently multiplication by \(Q\) is adjoint to a linear combination of the contractions \(\sum_b\partial_{\tau i b}\partial_{\bar\tau j b}\). Every admitted harmonic polynomial is killed by each of them. For a polynomial of finite degree the exponential pairing is a finite sum. All positive powers of \(Q\) pair to zero. This proves invariance in every harmonic degree between normalized exponential gauges. The argument uses a common power \(k\) across the columns, exactly as in the source. For a nonharmonic test \(P=XY\), \(q=XY\), and constant section 1, the degree-two reading changes by \(-k\). Conversion from every allowed raw chart to a normalized exponential gauge, compatibility with the finite flat marked cover, and existence and integrality of the global cusp sections are additional steps. ## The geometric lattice scalar and exterior denominators

There is a second useful comparison at each finite place. In the common coefficient field of the actual twist and the short-rule field the ratio taking the normalized class reading to the geometric short-rule reading is
\[
 c_1=\frac{I^r}{\beta_H(\Lambda)s_L}
     =\frac{1}{\beta_H(\Lambda)\,\mathrm{ep}(L)}.                       \tag{2}
\]
The marking-tuple cardinal is common to both readings and cancels here. At every \(p\) where \(\Lambda\) is standard, the defining determinant ideal of \(\beta_H\) is locally trivial since \(\Lambda\) and \(\Lambda\cap\mathcal O_E^m\) agree there. In the alternative branch of the definition \(\beta_H=1\). Thus the actual ratio (2) is a **unit** at every such place in the common field. Rationality of this ratio in a smaller prescribed field requires descent if the tuple was built over an extension. [Definition including the alternative branch](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L55-L86) · [Twisted scalar and required local integrality](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/TwistedKernelClassScalarIntegralStandard.lean#L26-L50)

At the source's special twisted comparison the scalar already descends to the distinguished field. Use the same admissible lattice and determinant ideal in the untwisted pinned row (the support is required to avoid both bad sets). Its twist is trivial and its coefficient field equals \(\sigma_0 K\). The positive rational \(q\) and \(I\) are identical for both rows. Multiplicativity of the finite product over primes therefore gives \(s_{L,t}=s_{L,0}/\xi_t(\mathfrak c)\). For the special twist the source assumes all its character values lie in \(\sigma_0 K\); evaluating the character at the finitely many uniformizer ideles entering \(\mathfrak c\) gives the same inclusion for \(\xi_t(\mathfrak c)\). It is a root of unity by finite order. Also \(\beta_H\) lies in the geometric base field, embedded in \(\sigma_0 K\) at the pinned base realization. This proves the membership of (2) in the **prescribed** field and its local unit property there at every standard prime. The proof uses the special character-field assumption only at this descent step. For a general twist the identical argument yields membership in the field generated by \(\xi_t(\mathfrak c)\) over the base field. [Admissibility in both rows and the finite-character field assumption](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/TwistScalarMemRange.lean#L62-L141)

For the nontrivial determinant-ideal branch, there is an explicit form of (2) also at nonstandard places. For \(w\mid p\), let \(a_{w,j}\) be the elementary divisor exponents of \(L_w\) relative to the standard lattice, put \(t_w=\sum_j\max(-a_{w,j},0)\), and \(d_w=\sum_j a_{w,j}\). Unimodularity for the standard Hermitian pairing identifies the exponents at \(\bar w\) with the negatives at \(w\), up to reordering. Consequently \(t_{\bar w}-t_w=d_w\), and \(v_p(I)=\sum_{w\mid p} f_w t_w\). The determinant quotient for \(\Lambda=\bar L\) contributes \(t_{\bar w}\) at \(w\). Writing \(m_\tau=\mathrm{mult}(\tau)\), the determinant-ideal equation gives \(v(\beta_H)=\sum_{\tau\ \mathrm{all}}m_\tau t_{\bar w}/e_w\), with \(w=\tau^{-1}\lambda\) using the pinned embeddings of the geometric coefficient field. Where these embeddings agree with the CM labeling in (1), \(m_\tau+m_{\bar\tau}=r\) and \(b_\tau-b_{\bar\tau}=d_w/e_w\) give
\[
 2v(c_1)=\sum_{\tau\ \mathrm{one\ per\ pair}}
       (r-2m_\tau-n_\tau)\,d_w/e_w.                                     \tag{3}
\]
Indeed, the full sum of \(t_w/e_w\) over all embeddings equals \(\sum f_wt_w\), because \(e_w f_w\) embeddings induce \(w\). Subtract twice the displayed determinant-ideal contribution from \(r v_p(I)\) and group the two embeddings of each CM pair. Formula (3) applies with an honest generator of that determinant ideal. In the fallback branch (1) and (2) still apply with \(v(\beta_H)=0\). The unit conclusion at standard places holds in both branches.

Over a DVR let \(C\) be the matrix of integral geometric readings of a basis of the prescribed target lattice. These can include several components and jets. A reading defined over a finite local extension can first be expressed in an integral basis by restriction of scalars, which introduces no inverse residue degree. Suppose that \(C\) has full column rank \(R\). Recovering coordinates over the fraction field requires one nonzero maximal minor with readings over that field. Over a general base ring, if the maximal minors generate the unit ideal, a Bézout combination of their adjugate numerators recovers integral coordinates. Over a DVR this is equivalently injectivity of the reading matrix modulo the uniformizer. Separation by characteristic-zero points alone proves only the generic rank.

In integer-valued uniformizer units let \(b_1\le\cdots\le b_R\) be the Smith exponents of \(C\), and let \(T\) be a theta matrix in the specified rational bases, once defined. For \(1\le k\le\min(R,\dim\mathrm{source})\) define \(\nu_k(M)\) as the least valuation of a \(k\)-minor, with infinity for rank below \(k\). Then
\[
 \nu_k(T)\ \ge\ \nu_k(CT)-\sum_{a=R-k+1}^{R} b_a.                    \tag{4}
\]
Integral basis changes preserve each determinantal ideal by Cauchy–Binet in both directions. In Smith bases a nonzero output row minor for a fixed row set acquires precisely the sum of its selected exponents, bounded above by the displayed sum. Equality can occur by taking source columns along the last \(k\) Smith vectors. Formula (4) distinguishes determinant-line losses from \(k\) repetitions of the largest exponent. The analytic covolume for a rank-\(k\) target lattice changes by its index when replacing it by a sublattice in the same real span (by the squared Gram determinant). Thus using (4) for a covolume comparison requires the **actual** target period lattice and local morphism. Convert (1)–(3) to (4)'s integer units by multiplying by the target ramification index.

Concretely, the per-column readings still involve the finite Fourier weights, the marking denominator, the cover section, the Lie polynomial and the translated torsion sections. For the full type projection one applies the finite sum with coefficients \(e_g\chi(g)^{-1}\) to the translates by \(g z\), including the scalar \(\zeta\) prescribed by the level comparison. If the individual actions preserve an integral lattice, its denominator loss in degree \(k\) is at most \(k\max_g(0,-v(e_g))\) for a finite character \(\chi\), in addition to the loss of \(\zeta\). These are conditional costs for actual readings. The archive's \(\mathrm{PhRat}\) already quantifies a finite decomposition in cusp forms of the smaller field for every source input. Invoking it would assume a substantial part of the problem. An arithmetic completion still needs to construct the marked global readings with the pinned component constants, prove descent of the projected operator to that prescribed field, and establish sufficient local divisibility for the full theta expression and the actual period lattice at every place.
