# Finite Euler changes, positive theta kernels, and central Pick inertia

Fix a prime \(p\), an integer \(L\ge1\), \(q=p^L\), and any integer \(A\) with \(2\sqrt q<A<q+1\). Consider the Dirichlet series
\[
 F(s)=\zeta(s)(1+Aq^{-s}+q^{1-2s}).
\]
Its coefficients \(a(n)=1+A\,1_{q\mid n}+q\,1_{q^2\mid n}\) are positive multiplicative integers, bounded by \(1+A+q\) for each fixed example. Its Euler factors at every prime except \(p\) are exactly those of zeta. Its first altered Dirichlet coefficient is at \(q\). All first powers at every prime also agree if \(L>1\). The completion uses the same gamma factor with conductor \(q^2\). Specifically \(X_F(t)=q^{s-1/2}\xi(s)(1+Aq^{-s}+q^{1-2s})\) at \(s=1/2+it\) is entire, even and real on the real axis. Division by its value at zero gives the exact multiplier below for the normalized determinant target:
\[
 \frac{X_F(t)}{X_F(0)}=\frac{\xi(1/2+it)}{\xi(1/2)}
 \frac{d+\cos(ht)}{d+1},\qquad h=\log q,\quad d=A/(2\sqrt q)>1.        \tag{1}
\]
This follows by expanding the three-term polynomial before continuation. If \(\Phi\) denotes the even positive Riemann Fourier density of the normalized xi function, the density in (1) is \((d\Phi(u)+[\Phi(u-h)+\Phi(u+h)]/2)/(d+1)\). It is strictly positive for **every** such \(q\), including large \(q\) where bounding each atom of a rescaled Gaussian separately fails. All critical-line zeros and their multiplicities survive. Additional zeros are at frequencies
\[
 t=(2j+1)\pi/h\ \pm i\delta,\qquad \delta=\operatorname{arcosh}(d)/h\in(0,1/2).
\]
Each increases the multiplicity by one, since the cosine derivative is nonzero. [Riemann theta and reciprocal-lattice comparison](XiPoissonGap.md)

**Prescribed location and invisibility to a finite amount of prime data.** Given any horizontal displacement \(0<\delta_0<1/2\), any height \(T\), and any fixed prime \(p\), take \(L\to\infty\) and choose the integer nearest \(2\sqrt q\cosh(\delta_0\log q)\). Eventually the two constraints on \(A\) hold. Since the rounding error in \(d\) is \(O(q^{-1/2})\), the displayed \(\delta\) tends to \(\delta_0\). An odd multiple of \(\pi/h\) is within \(\pi/h\) of \(T\). Taking instead \(A=\lfloor2\sqrt q\rfloor+1\) gives \(0<\delta\ll q^{-1/4}/\log q\), by \(\cosh x\ge1+x^2/2\). Thus a fixed exceptional prime suffices, the alteration can begin beyond any specified integer cutoff, and the extra zeros can tend to any prescribed point of the open critical strip on either side of or on the line. For the off-line targets, along each added progression the number of coincidences with original zeros up to \(T'\) is \(o(T')\) by the fixed-abscissa zero-density theorem and reflection. Even when parameters vary, new locations can be guaranteed near a specified point by isolation: away from an original zero take a zero-free neighborhood; at an original zero use a punctured such neighborhood and, if necessary, the next odd multiple, displaced by \(2\pi/h\). These arguments never require knowledge of the zeros to specify a single member of the family. [Fixed-abscissa bound, explicit form of Ingham's estimate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15184)

## The first obstruction in the Euler logarithm

Write \(G(x)=(1+A x^L+q x^{2L})/(1-x)\) for the modified Euler factor at \(x=p^{-s}\), and \(b_k=[x^k]\,xG'(x)/G(x)\). The von Mangoldt coefficient at \(p^k\) is \(b_k\log p\). Define \(\alpha,\beta>0\) by \(\alpha+\beta=A,\ \alpha\beta=q,\ \alpha>\beta\). Logarithmic differentiation at \(x=0\) gives
\[
 b_k=1\quad(L\nmid k),\qquad
 b_{Lj}=1+L(-1)^{j-1}(\alpha^j+\beta^j).
\]
In particular the first negative value occurs exactly at \(k=2L\), with \(b_{2L}=1-L(A^2-2q)<0\). The classical Ramanujan bound on the Dirichlet coefficients holds here because they are bounded. The growth restriction on coefficients of the **logarithm** fails because \(\alpha=q^{1/2+\delta}\). The conductor and the logarithmic coefficients are consequential arithmetic differences from the genuine degree-one datum. [Kaczorowski and Perelli, Selberg class axioms](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/173-3/p04)

## Rigidity for any finite family of rational Euler changes

There is a converse limited to a precise class. Let \(D(s)=\prod_{p\in S}R_p(p^{-s})\) for finitely many distinct primes, each \(R_p\) a real rational function with \(R_p(0)=1\). Suppose \(Q^{(s-1/2)/2}\xi(s)D(s)\) is entire and invariant under \(s\mapsto1-s\), for a positive real \(Q\). For each modified prime suppose the coefficients of the ordinary local logarithmic derivative \(x\,d\log(R_p(x)/(1-x))/dx\) are eventually nonnegative. Then \(D=1\) and \(Q=1\). The same conclusion follows in place of the sign condition from the local growth bound \(O_p(k^C p^{k\theta})\) on those coefficients, for any fixed \(C\) and \(\theta<1/2\). The proof does not assume a classification of degree-one \(L\)-functions.

First reduce each rational function to coprime numerator and denominator. Any denominator root, necessarily nonzero, gives poles on a vertical arithmetic progression. A root of a numerator at a distinct prime can meet this progression at most once: two intersections would give a rational ratio of logarithms of distinct primes. Consequently all but finitely many of these poles must be canceled by zeros of \(\xi\). For abscissa \(1/2\) this contradicts the positive proportion of nonzero zeta values on every inhomogeneous vertical arithmetic progression proved by Li and Radziwiłł. For fixed abscissa greater than \(1/2\) in the critical strip the zero-density estimate gives \(o(T)\) zeros up to height \(T\), whereas the progression has positive linear density. Reflect abscissae to the left, and use the usual zero-free exterior of the strip for the remaining lines. This excludes every denominator. [Li and Radziwiłł, *The Riemann zeta-function on vertical arithmetic progressions*, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.2684) · [Explicit zero-density theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15184)

The resulting \(D(s)=\sum_{n\le N}c_n n^{-s}\) has \(c_1=1\), \(c_N\ne0\), and \(N=\prod p^{d_p}\), with \(d_p=\deg R_p\). Cancelling the ordinary xi functional equation gives \(D(s)=Q^{1/2-s}D(1-s)\). Uniqueness of finite sums of distinct real exponentials, or successively taking limits along the positive real axis, first yields \(Q=N\) by comparing the lowest frequencies \(1,Q/N\), and then \(c_m=(m/\sqrt N)c_{N/m}\) at divisors of \(N\). In particular \(c_N=\sqrt N\); taking \(m=p^{d_p}\) and factoring \(c_{N/m}=c_N/c_m\) gives \(|c_{p^{d_p}}|=p^{d_p/2}\). In particular exact preservation of the conductor alone already forces \(D=1\).

For a positive-degree local polynomial write \(R_p(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d_p}(1-\alpha_j x)\), counting multiplicities. The largest modulus \(R=\max|\alpha_j|\) is at least \(\sqrt p>1\) by the leading coefficient. The ordinary logarithmic coefficients are precisely \(1-\sum_j\alpha_j^k\). Infinitely many positive integers \(k\to\infty\) simultaneously take the phases \((\alpha_j/R)^k\) of all roots of modulus \(R\) to 1 in the limit. For example use simultaneous Dirichlet approximation on the arguments, taking multiples of an exact return if the approximating integers stay bounded. All smaller moduli give \(o(R^k)\), and conjugation makes the sum real. If \(m\) parameters have maximal modulus the liminf of the coefficients divided by \(R^k\) is exactly \(-m\). This contradicts either hypothesis for every altered prime. The earlier examples show why requiring these signs only through a given, arbitrarily large level cannot support the same conclusion.

## Consequences for the central arithmetic matrix

At \(s=n+1/2\), let \(\ell_n=\xi'(s)/\xi(s)\). The centered logarithmic derivative in (1) is \(\ell_n+h\sinh(nh)/(d+\cosh(nh))\). Subtracting the constant \(h\) accounts exactly for the added conductor. Put \(u=q^{-n}\); the remaining perturbation \(e_n\) satisfies
\[
 |e_n|=\frac{2h(du+u^2)}{1+2du+u^2}\le 2h(dq^{-n}+q^{-2n}).
\]
Here \(d<(q+1)/(2\sqrt q)\), so \(\sup_{n\ge1}|e_n|=O(h/\sqrt q)\). On any set of positive integers of divergent reciprocal sum the central matrix \(((\ell^\mathrm{new}_n+\ell^\mathrm{new}_m)/(nm(n+m)))\) for the completed function has infinite negative inertia on every tail by the same paired-product and density argument as for xi. All zeros remain in a bounded strip, the added cosine has a square-summable reciprocal divisor and there are infinitely many nonreal zeros. Removing the conductor term subtracts the positive operator \(2h B\) with \(B_{nm}=1/[nm(n+m)]\); every strictly negative subspace persists. Nevertheless the resulting self-adjoint trace-class matrix is within \(\zeta(3)\sup_n|e_n|\) in trace norm of the genuine xi matrix: its difference is \(D_eB+BD_e\), and \(B\) is a positive Gram matrix of trace at most \(\zeta(3)/2\). Thus even arbitrarily small perturbations originating at a single arbitrarily late prime power can have infinite negative index on every determining tail. The finite-Euler rigidity theorem pinpoints an all-power condition that excludes these examples. It leaves positivity of the genuine unaltered matrix open.
