# A simultaneous coefficient field for the CM theta lifts

grwtsk contributors

For every preliminary host \(P\) in Levent Alpöge's field-size construction we construct **one** finite Galois field \(B\subset\mathbb C\) satisfying the five arithmetic requirements of \(\mathrm{Rich}(P,B)\). The field works for every signature row and both auxiliary primes. For every \(L\) satisfying the source's \(\mathrm{HostSz}(P,L)\), it satisfies
\[
 \max\{\log[B:\mathbb Q],\log\operatorname{rd}(B)\}
 \le \tfrac{37}{2}fr^2 L+106fL
 \le 19fr^2L+198f^2L.                                             \tag{1}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{rd}(B)=|D_B|^{1/[B:\mathbb Q]}\), \([E:\mathbb Q]=2f\) for the host CM field, and \(r\) is the Hermitian rank. The controlled field is the coefficient field in the distinguished complex embedding. An arbitrary larger auxiliary field in which one presents the data need not satisfy (1). The inputs below use the principal ideal theorem, class field theory, and classical complex multiplication, all independent of zeta zero-freeness. ## One radical extension for every Hermitian signature

Use the designated embedding of \(E\); the source host is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\). Its distinguished embeddings are exactly those fixing the chosen embedding of \(k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\), where \(d\equiv7\bmod16\) is prime. Indeed the \(f\) distinct automorphisms in the specified group half fix \(\sqrt{-d}\), and the subgroup fixing \(k\) has order \(f\). The other half is obtained by complex conjugation. For an integral ideal \(\mathfrak a\ne0\) of \(E\), put \(J=N_{E/k}\mathfrak a\). In \(E\) one has
\[
  \prod_{\tau\mid\mathrm{id}_k}\tau\mathfrak a=J\mathcal O_E.
\]
For a prime \(w\mid v\), every conjugate prime appears on the left with exponent \(e(w/v)f(w/v)\), its decomposition-group order. This is exactly the exponent on extending \(v^{f(w/v)}\). Multiplicativity proves the formula without excluding ramified primes. Thus the source ideal for signature \((a,b)\) is the extension of \(J^a\bar J^b\). This use of the induced type is specific to the actual host. [Distinguished automorphisms on the quadratic generator](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DecoratedLatticeProofs/LabelAtRealPlaceEqEmbeddingOverReflexField.lean#L13-L32) · [Source two multiplicities and product](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ConjugateEmbApply.lean#L97-L119)

Choose integral ideals \(J_i\) representing invariant-factor generators of \(\mathrm{Cl}(k)\), of orders \(n_i\), with \(J_i^{n_i}=(\alpha_i)\). Put \(h_k=\prod n_i\). Fix complex roots \(t_i^{n_i}=\alpha_i\). The field \(T=k(t_i:i)\) has relative degree at most \(h_k\). For every embedding of \(T\), the absolute square of the image of \(t_i\) is \(N J_i\): its \(n_i\)th power is the absolute norm of \(\alpha_i\), namely \((N J_i)^{n_i}\). At every finite prime, equality of the \(n_i\)th powers of the fractional ideals gives \((t_i)=J_i\mathcal O_T\). In particular complex conjugation sends \(t_i\) to \(N J_i/t_i\).

Write \(J=(x)\prod J_i^{b_i}\), taking \(x\in k^\times\). Then \(t_J=x\prod t_i^{b_i}\) generates \(J\mathcal O_T\), and \(|\sigma t_J|^2=NJ\) at all complex embeddings. Consequently
\[
           \beta_{\mathfrak a,a,b}=t_J^a\bar t_J^b,qquad
           |\sigma\beta_{\mathfrak a,a,b}|^2
              =(N_{E/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak a)^{a+b}                       \tag{2}
\]
gives precisely the required generator of the type ideal after extension, also for zero signature entries. Choosing \(x\) and class representatives separately for each ideal is legitimate for the source's existence quantifier. The argument needs no assertion of a canonical multiplicative section of the class group. In particular the number of signature rows never multiplies the degree or ramification cost. For ordinary ideals of \(E\), take the Hilbert class field \(H_E\) of \(E\). The principal ideal theorem gives ordinary capitulation for every ideal, with zero handled by its zero generator. This contributes relative degree \(h_E\) and trivial relative different. [Source's distinct generator and embedding conditions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/HeightProofs/ExistsCapitulationFieldSize.lean#L64-L139) · [Furtwängler's principal ideal theorem, Milne, *Class Field Theory*, V §3](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf)

In particular this supplies the actual branch in the normalized theta scalar \(\beta_H\) for every admissible class lattice \(\Lambda\), in every degree. Write \(L=\Lambda\cap\mathcal O_E^n\) and \(A=\det L\,(\det\Lambda)^{-1}\), where determinant ideals are generated by all maximal minors. The source's self-dual lattice is full: otherwise its proper rational span has a nonzero vector in its Hermitian orthogonal complement; the entire rational line on that vector would lie in the self-dual module. Its pairings with itself cannot all be integral since the Hermitian form is positive definite and one can divide the vector by arbitrary integers. After choosing a rational basis in the lattice, self-duality also bounds the lattice inside the integral dual of that basis; it is consequently finitely generated. Locally Smith reduction of \(L\hookrightarrow\Lambda\) shows that \(A\) is integral and \(N_{E/\mathbb Q}A=[\Lambda:L]\). Taking \(\mathfrak a=A\) in (2) gives \((\beta)\prod_\tau\tau(\det\Lambda)^{\mathrm{mult}(\tau)}=\prod_\tau\tau(\det L)^{\mathrm{mult}(\tau)}\) and \(|\sigma\beta|^2=[\Lambda:L]^r\) at all embeddings. Thus the default branch of \(\beta_H\) is unnecessary on these inputs. The ratio of *any* generator satisfying both equations to this explicit generator is an algebraic unit all of whose archimedean absolute values are one, hence a root of unity by Kronecker's theorem. In particular the distinguished-embedding local valuation formula now holds for the chosen \(\beta_H\) itself. This removes the alternative branch left in the [class scalar calculation](AlpogeThetaPeriodDescent.md). All these assertions transport through a pinned coefficient-field isomorphism: generators and ideals map together, and composition permutes the embeddings in the absolute-value condition. [Definitions of the determinant lattices and the actual chosen scalar](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L43-L85) · [All-ideal and all-place consumer](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L277-L289)

The local different of each radical adjunction divides \((n_i)\), even after compositum with any preceding field. At a completion choose a generator \(j\) of \(J_i\); \(y=t_i/j\) is a unit satisfying \(y^{n_i}=u\) with \(u\) an integral unit. The monic minimal polynomial \(f\) of \(y\) divides \(X^{n_i}-u\) integrally over the DVR. Evaluating the derivative gives \(f'(y)g(y)=n_i y^{n_i-1}\). The field different divides \((f'(y))\): the trace dual of the monogenic order is \(f'(y)^{-1}V[y]\), and the trace dual of its normalization lies inside this module. Thus the different of the field divides \((n_i)\), with no monogenicity assumption on the maximal order. This proves an all-place local bound from the ideal powers; the valuations of the particular representatives \(\alpha_i\) do not enter. The example \(J^{n_i}=(\alpha_i)\) with \(J\) nonprincipal already explains why the base field \(k\) alone cannot generally supply these generators.

## Ray field, roots of unity and normalized discriminants

Write \(g_0,g_1\) for the two marking primes, \(p,p'\) for the auxiliary primes, \(M=g_0g_1\), \(N_p=Mp\) and \(m=\operatorname{lcm}(Mp,Mp')\). The ray class field \(W=k_{(m)}\) contains the two ray fields at the actual levels. The first and second main theorems of complex multiplication give all the corresponding Weber values: \(j/1728=g_2^3/(g_2^3-27g_3^2)\) and \(g_2g_3\wp(z)/(g_2^3-27g_3^2)\) are the scale-invariant functions of the specified CM lattice and its torsion. The latter is the Weber function up to a nonzero rational scalar under this normalization; \(d\equiv7\bmod16\) excludes the two exceptional unit groups. The marked-curve condition also follows directly from CM reciprocity: a Galois automorphism fixing the ray class field has idèle class represented by a unit equal to one modulo \(m\), after rescaling by its principal factor. Thus the CM elliptic curve is isomorphic to its conjugate compatibly with all the labeled \(m\)-torsion. This supplies the field premise of the [level-character descent](AlpogeLevelCharacterDescent.md) simultaneously for the two levels. [Archival comparison with two Weber fields](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/HeightProofs/ExistsIsRayIntermediateField.lean#L74-L113) · [Main theorem of CM, Milne §11](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/svi.pdf)

The ray group exact sequence gives \([W:k]=h_k\Phi_k(m)/2\), with \(\Phi_k(m)=|(\mathcal O_k/m)^\times|\); the global units are \(\{\pm1\}\), distinct modulo the odd \(m>1\). More precisely, at \(v^t\parallel(m)\) put \(q=Nv\). With at least two prime ideals dividing \((m)\), as on the split marking levels, the local units modulo \(1+v^t\) inject into the ray group: a diagonal global unit trivial at the other odd prime must be one. For uniform characters of the full ray group the fraction trivial on a local subgroup is its reciprocal order, by extension of characters of a finite abelian group. The subgroup orders are \((q-1)q^{t-1}\) at depth zero and \(q^{t-j}\) at depths \(1\le j<t\). The expected conductor exponent is the sum over those \(j\) of one minus this reciprocal, hence \(t-1/(q-1)\). The abelian conductor-discriminant formula now gives
\[
 \log\operatorname{rd}(W)=\tfrac12\log d + \log m
                   -\tfrac12\sum_{v\mid m}\frac{\log Nv}{Nv-1}.         \tag{3}
\]
The coarse bound with the final sum dropped holds without the multiple-prime condition since every conductor divides \((m)\). Keeping the unit correction is necessary: for a modulus supported at just one odd prime ideal the image of the full depth-zero unit group can instead have half the displayed order. [Ray field estimates compared in the archive](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/HeightProofs/FieldSizeWeberRay.lean#L15-L48) · [Ray groups and conductor-discriminant formula, Milne, *Class Field Theory*, V §2 and VI §3](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf)

Let \(C\) be the prescribed finite row group cardinality, \(u_s=|(\mathcal O_E/N_s)^\times|\) and \(X_s=2h_Eu_s\) for \(s=p,p'\). Take \(Z\) to be the least common multiple of
\[
 C,\ g_0(g_0-1),\ g_1(g_1-1),\ 3^4,\ 5^4,\ p,\ p',\ h_E,\ X_p,\ X_{p'},\ h_k.
\]
The first ten are exactly the root orders in the source. The last ensures that the field
\[
                    B=H_E W T\,\mathbb Q(\zeta_Z)                          \tag{4}
\]
is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\). The conjugates of each radical over \(k\) are among \(\zeta_{n_i}^j t_i\); over the other embedding of \(k\) they are among \(\zeta_{n_i}^j NJ_i/t_i\). The Hilbert and ray moduli are invariant under the relevant Galois groups, so their fields are normal as well. Equations (2) and (4) supply every clause of \(\mathrm{Rich}\). In particular the ambient field includes every specified embedding of the host, and the assertion in (2) persists upon restriction of every embedding of \(B\) to \(T\).

There is a prime-by-prime discriminant check. Use \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\) on an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_\ell\). The valuation of the absolute different of \(B\) is bounded by the sum of the corresponding contributions
\[
 v_\ell(\mathfrak D_{E/\mathbb Q}),\quad
 \frac{t-1/(q-1)}{e(k_v/\mathbb Q_\ell)}\ (v^t\parallel m),\quad
 v_\ell(h_k),\quad v_\ell(Z)-\frac{1}{\ell-1}\ (\ell\mid Z).            \tag{5}
\]
The ray and cyclotomic terms are zero off their indicated supports. At each chosen extension of \(v_\ell\) there is one restricted \(v\) of \(k\). The ray term is the relative different in this normalization, since the local normalized exponent equals the exponent of the relative discriminant divided by \([W:k]\) and then by the base ramification index. The cyclotomic expression is the elementary cyclotomic different formula, including the zero contribution for a factor two to exponent one. For a local base change the new relative different divides the old different extended: take a generator of the full local integer ring over the original local field, factor its minimal polynomial over the new base, and use the same derivative and trace-dual argument as above. Such generators exist for finite extensions of local fields with finite residue field, by lifting a residue generator and a uniformizer. Unramified base change and trivial compositum factors satisfy the same inequality. Applying transitivity down the tower proves (5); summing with local degree weights gives in particular
\[
  [B:\mathbb Q]\le 2f h_E\,[W:k] h_k\varphi(Z),\qquad
  \log\operatorname{rd}(B)\le\log\operatorname{rd}(E)+\log m+\log h_k+\log Z.     \tag{6}
\]
One may subtract the corrections of (3) and the cyclotomic discriminant in the second inequality. They remain upper bounds because of possible intersections among components of (4).

## Characters on the two auxiliary idèle groups

For each \(s\in\{p,p'\}\) let \(A^1\) be the finite norm-one idèles of \(E\), \(E^1\) its rational norm-one elements, and \(U(N_s)\) the full finite integral unit congruence subgroup at level \(N_s\). Form the abelian quotient \(Q_s=A^1/(E^1(U(N_s)\cap A^1))\). Its exponent divides \(X_s\). Indeed for \(x\in A^1\), the fractional ideal of \(x^{h_E}\) is principal, say \((a)\). Write \(x^{h_E}=a u\) with \(u\) an integral unit idèle. Then
\[
              x^{2h_E}=(a/\bar a)(u/\bar u).
\]
The first factor is rational norm-one; the second is integral norm-one and its \(u_s\)th power is one modulo \(N_s\). This proves the exponent assertion without assuming that a principal generator can itself be chosen norm-one.

The subgroup \((\mathcal O_E/I_s)^\times\) injects into \(Q_s\) by lifting to units on the chosen half over \(s\), using inverse conjugate on the opposite half and one elsewhere. Changing lifts does not affect the class. If it is trivial, the comparing rational norm-one element is a global algebraic unit; all its complex absolute values are one, hence it is a root of unity. It is one modulo \(M\), since \((M,s)=1\). Such a root must be one: for an odd \(\ell\mid M\), binomial expansion gives \(v_\ell(z^\ell-1)=v_\ell(z-1)+1\) when \(v_\ell(z-1)\ge1\) and \(z\ne1\); exponents prime to \(\ell\) preserve that valuation. These two facts preclude nontrivial torsion. The equality in the chosen half modulo \(I_s\) now forces the original residue unit to be one.

In particular the class of the unit minus one on the two halves at \(s\) is nontrivial. Choose the nontrivial character on the cyclic group it generates and extend it to \(Q_s\) using the divisibility of \(\mathbb C^\times\). The character on \(A^1\) has values in \(\mu_{X_s}\subset B\). It induces a character on \(A^1 U(N_s)/U(N_s)\), and a second application of the same extension property gives a character of the full finite idèle group modulo \(U(N_s)\). This is continuous because that subgroup is open; the full idèle quotient is discrete. Its norm-one restrictions satisfy every clause of \(\mathrm{XiOK}\): triviality on rational elements, the level-\(N_s\) congruence, a nontrivial value at level \(M\), bounded order and membership in the controlled coefficient field. The ramified witness is integral at all places, including \(s\). Values on idèles of arbitrary norm are not required to have that bounded order. The two auxiliary characters need not coincide. ## Numerical comparison with the original budget

The same \(B\) works for **every** permitted \(L\); its definition includes no \(L\). The source gives \(f\ge8\), \(r\ge62\), \(L\ge4\), \(\log f\le L\), \(\log\operatorname{rd}(E)\le2L\), \(\log g_i\le9L+1\), and \(\log p,\log p'\le L\). The row group is the product of the indicated copies of \(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb F_{g_i})\). Counting it inside all matrices gives \(\log C\le fr^2(\log g_0+\log g_1)\). Thus \(\log(g_0g_1)\le18.5L\), \(\log N_s\le20L\), \(\log m\le21L\), and \(\log u_s\le 2f\log N_s\) by counting the whole quotient ring. [Finite row group and character orders in the source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/HeightProofs/HostXiOrderBounds.lean#L14-L62)

For a number field \(A\) of degree \(D\), Minkowski supplies an ideal of norm at most \(2^D\sqrt{|D_A|}\) in each class. The number of ideals of norm at most \(Y\) is at most \(Y^2\zeta_A(2)\le Y^2\zeta(2)^D\le Y^2\cdot2^D\), by the positive Euler factors. Hence \(h_A\le 8^D|D_A|\). The discriminant tower gives \(\operatorname{rd}(k)\le\operatorname{rd}(E)\), so \(\log h_E\le6fL\), \(\log h_k\le6L\). No residue estimate at 1 or zero-free region is involved.

When bounding \(Z\), charge the common factor \(2h_E\) only once: \(\operatorname{lcm}(h_E,X_p,X_{p'})=2h_E\operatorname{lcm}(u_p,u_{p'})\). The two terms \(g_i(g_i-1)\) cost at most \(37L\), \(3^45^4\) costs at most \(3L\), \(p,p'\) cost at most \(2L\), \(\log 2\le L\), and the extra \(h_k\) costs at most \(6L\). Hence
\[
 \log Z\le18.5fr^2L+86fL+49L .
\]
Since \(\Phi_k(m)\le m^2\), \(\log[W:k]\le48L\). In (6) use \(\log(2f)\le1.25L\). The degree bound is at most \(18.5fr^2L+92fL+104.25L\) and the root-discriminant bound is at most \(18.5fr^2L+86fL+78L\). Since \(f\ge8\), both are bounded by (1). This proves the field-size inequality for the actual numerical target with slack, simultaneously paying for both levels and an optional Galois closure already contained in (4). The norm identities also justify every signature's determinant-ideal normalization in this field. No estimate here controls the size of arbitrary generators of ordinary capitulated ideals; the exact archimedean normalization in (2) is proved for the type ideals.

For the source's stage realization, take this chosen \(B\) as the controlled coefficient field; the auxiliary field for other finite algebraic choices may be enlarged separately. The split-prime and nonzero-level clauses of \(\mathrm{FieldOK}_0\) come from the original preliminary host. The full ring of integers is taken as the integer coefficient ring, and the required roots of unity and type generators are present by construction. This resolves the two CM marking conditions and the quantitative degree and discriminant inputs in that realization. A preassigned stage with a different coefficient field still requires identification of its specified image with the controlled field. The source distinguishes the Haar-volume tie from \(\mathrm{FieldOK}_0\). The all-place field comparison supplies no Petersson covolume, semiabelian integral jet estimate or bound for the unaccounted opposite-sign Selmer sum. Those are separate premises in returning to (M). The local radical cost in (5) is attained in a small actual CM example. In \(k=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-23})\), let \(\omega=(1+\sqrt{-23})/2\). The element \(1+\omega\) has norm eight. Exactly one prime above two divides it, so its principal ideal is the cube of that prime. There is no element of norm two, by the quadratic norm form \(x^2+xy+6y^2\). A cube root generates the degree-six field with polynomial \(X^6-3X^3+8\): its cube generates \(k\), and a root in \(k\) would principalize that prime already in \(k\). PARI/GP 2.17.4 computed its absolute discriminant as \(-3^6 23^3\), with certified class group computations for both base and radical field. The root-discriminant ratio is therefore exactly three.
