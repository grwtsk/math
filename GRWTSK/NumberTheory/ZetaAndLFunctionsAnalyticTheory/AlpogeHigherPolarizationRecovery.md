# Detecting a Honda twist at every polarization order

Let \(p\equiv3\pmod4\) be an odd prime, \(q=p^2\), \(O_0=\mathbb Z_p[i]\), and normalize \(v(p)=1\). Choose an aligned unit \(c_0\in O_0\) with \(\bar c_0^{(q-1)/4}=-1\). For each nonzero integral \(c\in\mathbb C_p\) choose \(u^4=c/c_0\). The four choices have identical distance to \(O_0\). On \(y^2=x^3-cx\) take \(t=-x/y\), the logarithm inverse \(t_c(z)\), the normalized elliptic sigma function and \(m_c(z)=\log(\sigma_c(z)/t_c(z))\). Let \(L(X)=\sum_{j\ge0}X^{q^j}/p^j\). For every \(r\ge3\) form the unprojected series
\[
 E_{r,c}=\exp\left(\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}}
 (-1)^{r-|S|} m_c\left(\sum_{j\in S}L(X_j)\right)\right).
\]
Its coefficients are integral **if and only if** \(u\in O_0\). For \(u\notin O_0\), put \(S+e=\max_{b\in O_0}v(u-b)\), \(S\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\), \(0\le e<1\). Its exact isotropic open convergence radius is
\[
 R_* = p^{-q^{-S}(1/(q-1)-e/q)} .
\]
Normal convergence on the closed polydisc of radius \(R_*\) fails at every order. These conclusions concern the indicated sigma ratio in Honda coordinates. The source's finite orbit projection changes the observable, so transfer requires its own proof. ## The integral direction and translation

For a rigidified section expressed by a unit \(f\) in a frame, write \(C_r f\) for the multiplicative subset difference, including the empty set. Two ordinary multiplicative differences in directions \(a,b\) equal the difference in direction \(a+_F b\) divided by the separate differences. This follows by canceling the four translates of the remaining difference at a common base point. It holds on a commutative formal group with values in units. From the integral unit \(C_3\) in the elliptic frame and integrality of the group law it gives \(C_r\) integral for every \(r\ge3\). The aligned coordinate \(B_0=t_{c_0}\circ L\) and its inverse are strict integral series. Homogeneity gives \(m_c(z)=m_{c_0}(uz)\), hence \(E_{r,c}=E_{r,c_0}([u](X_1),\ldots,[u](X_r))\), where \([u]=L^{-1}(uL)\). For \(u\in O_0\) the Honda scalar is integral. Below the stated boundary for misaligned \(u\) it converges with absolute value less than one, uniformly on each smaller closed disc. This proves the lower radius bound. The scalar boundary proof supplies coefficients of \([u]\) in degrees \(q^j\), \(j>S\), with valuations \(1/(q-1)-q^j \lambda\), where \(p^{-\lambda}=R_*\). In particular the scalar does not converge normally at its boundary. Finite products of integral units preserve integrality. For a unit with constant term one the normalized root of degree prime to \(p\) is integral, by the triangular coefficient recursion with unit linear multiplier. This accounts for taking tame norms and normalized tame roots on domains where the translated section is a unit. At good reduction a nonidentity prime-to-\(p\) torsion section cannot specialize to the identity. The formal completion at the identity has no nonzero such torsion, since multiplication by its order has unit derivative and integral inverse. A section of the identity divisor in an integral local frame at that specialization therefore has unit value, and division by its value at the torsion point preserves integrality. A change to another integral unit frame preserves the assertion.

For the nonzero two-torsion point \(T=(0,0)\) on the good unit model, the chord law gives \(x(P+T)=-c/x(P),\ y(P+T)=cy(P)/x(P)^2\). Set \(U=t^2x\). Then \(U^2-U-ct^4=0\), \(U(0)=1\), and

             (-y/c)(P+T)=t/U=t\sum_{n\ge0} C_n(-ct^4)^n .
\]
Indeed \(V=1/U\) satisfies \(V=1-ct^4V^2\), the Catalan equation. At a formal torsion point \(T\ne O\) reducing to the identity the distinction is exact. With \(a=t(-T)\ne0\), in an integral unit frame the translated section cutting out the identity has the form \((t-a)V(t)\) with \(V\) an integral unit. This follows by factoring the difference of the formal group law from its value at \(a\); its derivative there is a unit. Dividing by its value at zero gives \((1-t/a)V(t)/V(0)\). Its linear coefficient has valuation \(-v(a)\), all coefficients have valuation at least \(-v(a)\), and the reciprocal has radius precisely \(|a|\) because the numerator has a simple zero there. Thus tame specialization is a mathematical hypothesis with a concrete failure mode.

## Recovery of the elliptic curve from one mixed coefficient

Work over \(K=\mathbb C_p\) on the aligned elliptic curve \(E=E_{c_0}\), with invariant derivation \(D=d/dz\). Write \(g=D^{r-3}m_{c_0}''\). Every term in the logarithm of \(E_{r,c}\) uses all variables. Extracting the coefficient linear in each of the last \(r-1\) variables kills every exponential power above the first and gives
\[
      [X_2\cdots X_r] E_{r,c}
       =u^{r-1}\{g(P(X_1))-g(O)\},\qquad t(P(X))=B_0([u](X)).
\]
Indeed applying the \(r-1\) derivatives leaves the difference of \(m_{c_0}^{(r-1)}\) at \(uL(X_1)\) and zero. The value at zero is essential for orders congruent to one modulo four.

The Weierstrass and logarithmic sigma differential equations give
\[
 m_{c_0}''=-4c_0t^2-c_0/x=-4c_0x/(x^2-c_0)-c_0/x .
\]
Its poles are double at the three distinct nonidentity points of \(E[2]\), with no others. At each such point \(x-x(T)\) has order two, and the displayed numerators and the other summand cannot cancel its pole. In characteristic zero invariant differentiation raises every pole order by one and creates no new pole. Consequently \(g\) has those same three poles. A translation preserving \(g\) preserves their set. Its displacement lies in \(E[2]\) by taking the difference of two points in the set. It must then preserve the omitted singleton \(O\) in \(E[2]\), so it is zero. [Rational second derivative in the fixed elliptic frame](AlpogeCubicalSecant.md)

In fact \(K(E)=K(g,Dg)\). To see this take the smooth projective curve of the latter subfield and the associated finite morphism from \(E\). On the target the meromorphic differential \(dg/(Dg)\) pulls back to the invariant differential, which has order zero everywhere. Above a point where its order is \(m\), a local parameter and ramification index \(d\) give pullback order \(d(m+1)-1\). Characteristic zero ensures the leading derivative is nonzero. Thus \(d=1,m=0\) everywhere. The canonical degree shows that the target has genus one. With the image of the origin as its origin the map is an isogeny. Every kernel translation fixes \(g\), so the kernel is trivial. Separability gives degree one. In particular both elliptic affine coordinates are rational functions of the two observed functions. Suppose \(E_{r,c}\) converges normally on a closed polydisc of value-group radius \(0<R<1\). Its mixed slice gives \(g(P(X))\) in the one-variable disc algebra. Differentiation and division by \(uL'(X)\) recover \(Dg(P(X))\) there. Indeed \(L'=1+\sum_{j>0}p^j X^{q^j-1}\) has an analytic inverse. Rational reconstruction gives meromorphic projective coordinates of \(P\). The germ is nonconstant, so no nonzero rational denominator on the curve vanishes identically. Clear denominators and common factors in the one-variable Tate algebra, a principal ideal domain by Weierstrass division. The resulting homogeneous coordinates have no common zero and satisfy the projective cubic. They give an analytic map on the closed disc whose germ is \(P\). The identity \(P([p]_H X)=[p]_E P(X)\) extends throughout the disc, by the identity theorem on the projective cross products. The integral source formal group has \(|[p]_H X|\le\max(|p|\,|X|,|X|^2)\), so its iterates tend to zero there. For each \(X\), some iterate of \(P(X)\) therefore reduces to the identity. Supersingularity of the aligned special fiber gives trivial geometric \(p\)-power torsion, so \(P(X)\) itself reduces to the identity. In homogeneous coordinates this puts every value in the chart where the projective \(y\) is a unit after normalization. Thus \(t=-x/y\) is analytic and \(|t(P)|<1\) pointwise. The Gauss norm on a strict closed disc over \(K\) is attained at a geometric point, by avoiding the roots of the nonzero residue polynomial after rescaling. Hence the norm is strictly less than one. Composing with the strict integral inverse \(B_0^{-1}\) analytically continues \([u]\) to that entire closed disc, with its original Taylor series. At \(R=R_*\) this contradicts the scalar boundary coefficients. It also excludes every larger open radius. Since an integral series converges on every smaller closed polydisc, this proves the converse and the claimed boundary failure.
