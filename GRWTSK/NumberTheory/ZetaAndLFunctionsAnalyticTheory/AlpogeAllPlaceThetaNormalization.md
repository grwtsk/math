## Valuation of the geometric change of frame

Write \(J_{\Lambda,\Gamma}\) for the actual intertwiner from \(\Lambda\) to \(\Gamma\) with the positive inverse square root of \([\Gamma:\Lambda\cap\Gamma]\). Use the selected sign on each self-dual period lattice. Every triangle of these intertwiners has scalar of valuation zero at **every** finite place. To recall why this includes 2, §17 expresses the scalar as a positive rational square root times a nonzero finite quadratic sum \(\sum_G q\). The phase is normalized at zero, and its multiplicative polarization is a bicharacter. Distinct primary subgroups are orthogonal, so the sum factors. Translation by the radical and character orthogonality give \(S\bar S=|G||\operatorname{rad}|\) on every primary component (a nontrivial restriction to the radical would give \(S=0\)). On components of order prime to \(p\), this makes both algebraic integers \(S,\bar S\) units at \(p\). On a \(p\)-primary component, if \(x\) has order \(p^k\), the power identity \(1=q(x)^{p^k}b(x,x)^{\binom{p^k}{2}}\) shows that \(q(x)\) has \(p\)-power order, including a possible extra factor 2 when \(p=2\). Thus \(S,\bar S\) lie in a cyclotomic field totally ramified over \(p\) and have the same valuation. It follows that \(2v(S)=v(|G||\operatorname{rad}|)\) in that component. In the triangle scalar the square-root prefactor cancels these valuations because its positive complex value is \(1/|S|\), by the isometry on the normalized section line. This argument assumes commensurable principally polarized lattices with compatible signs and the exact normalized traces from §17.
