# The finite theta convolution on a simultaneous Hecke eigenspace

grwtsk contributors

Let \(E/F\) be the host CM extension, \(G=U(W)\), \(r=\dim_E W\), \(n=\dim_E V=r+t\), and \(d=(t-r+2)/2\). The original rank margin gives \(d\ge7/2\). The finite exceptional set \(S\) consists of the places below \(P_g\cup Q(t_0)\), where \(t_0\) denotes the twist index. For a full lift datum, retain its oscillator \(\omega\), normalized finite modification \(f\), splitting character \(c_\mu\), and field embedding. Let \(K=\mathrm{levW}\) be the reference level, \(J\) the actual target level and \(\chi\) its unitary character. Normalize finite Haar by \(\operatorname{vol}(K)=1\). The simultaneous eigenspace of the good Hecke operators need not be one dimensional.

For every vector \(F_1=U_1(v_1)\) from the given full character and Hecke eigenspace and every \(F_2=U_2(v_2)\) from the full allowed cusp space, the finite sum in the Rallis pairing is
\[
 \sum_{gK}(\omega(1,g)f,f)_X\,
       \overline{(R(g)F_1,F_2)_W}
 = |P.H|\,\|f\|_X^2\,
       \frac{L^{S}((1+t)/2,B\otimes c_\mu^{-1})}{d^{S}}\,
       \overline{(F_1,F_2)_W}.                                            \tag{1}
\]
Both pairings in parentheses are linear in the first entry. The construction below gives absolute summability over the individual cosets. The same normalized convolution is an invertible bounded operator on the entire cuspidal \((J,\chi)\)-space. In particular its scalar at the given eigensystem satisfies
\[
  \log\left|L^{S}((1+t)/2,B\otimes c_\mu^{-1})/d^{S}\right|
  \ \ge\ -14[F:\mathbb Q]\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}m^{-d}.                   \tag{2}
\]
The bound uses the exact local absolute kernel masses. Its right side is strictly greater than \(-2[F:\mathbb Q]\) throughout the original rank range. ## The oscillator coefficient and exceptional cosets

Let \(q_v\) be the cardinality of the residue field of \(F_v\). The local distributions entering (1) are normalized at the identity. At a marked split prime, in the specified conjugate half and adjoint frame, write the matrix as \(\binom{A}{B_0}\) with \(r\) top rows. Its support is integral with \(\bar B_0=0\), \(\bar A\in H_v\), and its weight on the support is \(\lambda_v(\bar A)m_v(\det\bar A)^{-1}\). At \(Q(t_0)\) use \(H_v=\mathrm{GL}_r(\kappa_v)\), \(\lambda_v=1\). If both a test and its translate are nonzero, \(A,Ag\) lie in the inverse image \(J_v\) of \(H_v\), so \(g\in J_v\). On \(J_v\) the splitting character cancels the tame determinant factor and the transform of the function is \(\lambda_v(g)f_v\). Thus the local squared norm is \(|H_v|q_v^{-nr}\) in additive lattice volume one and the normalized matrix coefficient is \(\mathbf1_{J_v}\lambda_v\). These support and phase calculations include all marked primes, including a trivial character on a nontrivial subgroup. In frames this uses unimodularity of both Hermitian lattices and the fractional-ideal identity \((\xi_0)\mathfrak A\bar{\mathfrak A}=\mathfrak d_{E/\mathbb Q}^{-1}\), which matches the conductor of the absolute trace character. At odd residue characteristic twice the lattice is the full lattice. At dyadic places the host splits, and the two split half-lattices separately fix the vacuum. In a Schrödinger polarization the translation and modulation laws force the vacuum line: modulation restricts support to the annihilator lattice, and translation makes the function constant there. The same assertion holds in a tensor product with an arbitrary complementary Hilbert space, by taking its matrix elements. At an inert place choose integral hyperbolic pairs of \(W\) and a polarization of the remaining symplectic coordinate if the rank is odd. Thus translations at other places do not affect the vacuum line at this place. At a selected place the Fourier expansion of the marked function in residue characters of the lattice gives the actual modified vector there. ## Uniform local mass

At a good place write \(q=q_v\), \(a=q^{-d}\) and \(A_v=\sum_{gK_v}|\phi_v(g)|\). The identity coset contributes one. Since twisting contributes a phase, the full sum is exactly the spherical transform of the absolute kernel on the trivial representation. Specializing the [split graph formula](AlpogeSplitDoublingFactor.md) with trivial Satake multiset \(q^{(r-1)/2-i}\), \(0\le i<r\), gives
\[
 A_{\rm split}=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-q^{-t-j})}
                    {\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(1-aq^{-i})^2}.
\]
The [inert radical integral](AlpogeInertRadicalIntegral.md) at the trivial representation gives
\[
 A_{\rm inert}=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-(-1)^j q^{-r-t+j})}
                    {\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(1-a^2 q^{-2i})}.
\]
Here the base-change parameters form \(q^{r-1-2i}\), including the central one in odd rank. They follow by canceling the inducing modulus in the normalized Borel model; equivalently the positive shell series follows from the lattice recursion.

For nonnegative \(u_i\le1\), \(\prod(1-u_i)\ge1-\sum u_i\) by induction. The split numerator is at most one, and the geometric sum of the denominator arguments in one copy is at most \(2a\). In the inert numerator majorize each \(1+x\) by \((1-x)^{-1}\). The sum of its absolute arguments is less than \(2q^{-t-1}\le2a^2\) for \(r\ge1\), and the denominator arguments sum to at most \(2a^2\). Thus \(A_v\le(1-2b)^{-2}\), with \(b=a\) at split places and \(b=a^2\) at inert places. Since \(a\le2^{-7/2}<1/11\), we have
\[
 0\le A_v-1\le (1-2b)^{-2}-1
 =\frac{4b(1-b)}{(1-2b)^2}\le 6b.
\]
This degree of decay makes the perturbation strictly smaller than one even at the smallest allowed residue field. The archive obtained \(27^r q^{-2}\) for summability. That majorant by itself cannot give the local inverse estimate at small primes. ## Operator product and the finite scalar

In the closed \((J,\chi)\)-subspace of cuspidal \(L^2\), sum \(\overline{\phi_v(g)}R(g)\) over \(gK_v\), with the good Haar above. Each individual translate maps the fixed-vector space isometrically into the ambient Hilbert space. Summability in that operator space follows from \(A_v<\infty\). The sum lands back in the fixed-vector space by left \(K_v\)-invariance, and commutes with the actions at other places. Denote the resulting endomorphism by \(T_v=I+E_v\). Then \(\|E_v\|\le A_v-1\), and its Neumann inverse satisfies \(\|T_v^{-1}-I\|\le\|E_v\|/(1-\|E_v\|)\). There are at most \([F:\mathbb Q]\) prime ideals of any given integer norm. The sums of the perturbation norms of both the operators and their inverses are therefore finite. For any finite ordered list of additional factors,
\[
 \left\|\prod (I+E_v)-I\right\|\le \prod(1+\|E_v\|)-1.
\]
Expansion or induction proves this even for noncommuting factors in the chosen order. Here factors at distinct places commute, so the net over all finite sets converges in operator norm, as does the product of inverses. Their limits are mutual inverses. Each factor is self-adjoint: the unitary oscillator coefficient takes conjugate values at inverse group elements, and inversion preserves Haar on this unimodular group. Its operator distance from the identity is at most \(6/11<1\), so it is positive definite. Finite products of commuting positive operators are positive; their invertible limit is strictly positive as well. More explicitly if \(T_{\le X}\) includes all good places of norm at most the positive integer \(X\), then
\[
 \|T-T_{\le X}\|
 \le e^{6[F:\mathbb Q](\zeta(d)-1)}\,
          \frac{6[F:\mathbb Q]}{d-1}X^{1-d}.
\]
Indeed the products of absolute majorants with at least one omitted nonidentity component are bounded by the full exponential times \(1-e^{-u}\le u\), using the tail sum \(u\le6[F:\mathbb Q]\sum_{m>X}m^{-d}\). Their total mass is finite by monotone convergence of the products \(\prod_v A_v\). This proves absolute convergence on the full restricted coset set against any bounded Hilbert matrix coefficient. Cauchy–Schwarz gives its bound \(\|F_1\|\|F_2\|\). No individual factorization of either cusp form is needed.

For the given nonzero joint eigenspace choose a nonzero vector and one nonzero evaluation of its multilinear map. Right-compact double-coset sums have the prescribed eigenvalues on every such evaluation. For each good place the normalized spherical Satake isomorphism gives the multiset \(B\) from these eigenvalues, with reciprocal parameters at the conjugate split place and the odd central parameter at an inert place. One can recover the character from the nonzero evaluation because the corresponding compactly averaged Hilbert coefficient at the identity is its squared norm. Each compact double-coset operator acts by that same character on every vector in the given simultaneous eigenspace. The inverse-size and size bounds \(|\beta|\le (\mathrm Nw)^{(r-1)/2}\) on the corresponding parameters follow from the bounded coefficient calculations for the split and inert local transforms: the transform is holomorphic in the absolute-convergence disc of the positive trivial kernel and its uncancelled standard poles must lie outside that disc. In the split case the two one-sided lattice series give the bounds separately. The global partial standard product converges absolutely since each root times its Euler argument has absolute value at most \((\mathrm Nw)^{-d}\). For clarity, on the split chart let \(m=c_{\mu,w}(\pi)\) and \(x=q^{-(1+t)/2}\). The conjugated oscillator kernel used in \(T_v\) has twist \(\bar m=m^{-1}\). Its standard denominator is \(\prod_{\beta\in B_w}(1-\bar m\beta x)(1-(\bar m\beta)^{-1}x)\). The adjoint in the spherical Hecke algebra sends a normalized torus monomial to its inverse and conjugates coefficients. On an eigenspace in a unitary representation this implies \(\bar B_w=B_w^{-1}\), by equality of the symmetric polynomials. The conjugate of the displayed denominator therefore equals itself. The conjugate split chart has \(B_{\bar w}=B_w^{-1}\) and reciprocal twist. ## The numerical bound required of the actual theta scalar

Write \(f_* = D.R.f\), \(h=[F:\mathbb Q]\), \(w_E=|\mu(E)|\) and \(d_S=\prod_{v\in S}d_v\). The source's normalized adjoint divides by the same \(|P.H|\) as in (1). For the scalar obtained from the global adjoint pairing and the index-times-vacuum-norm identity, the [archimedean cancellation](AlpogeArchimedeanPairing.md) and (2) imply
\[
 \log l\ge \mathrm{mainTerm}-n\log w_E-\log(n!)
       - f_*\binom r2\log n-\log d_S-2h .                              \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the definite genus contains a class of mass \(1/(w_E^n n!)\), and the normalized compact/real factor at each of the \(f_*\) infinite labels is at least \(n^{-\binom r2}\) times the two actual hook products. The source level volume supplies \([K_V:J_V]\,\mathrm{mass}(V)\) and its squared level norm supplies \(([K_V:J_V]d_S)^{-1}\). There is no covolume left to estimate. [Mass, lattice norm and original marked sets](AlpogeExceptionalRallis.md) · [Cardinality of the infinite label set](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Columns/TiedRec.lean#L16-L19)

All marked places split. There \(d_v=\prod_{j=1}^r(1-q_v^{-t-j})^{-1}\). Since \(-\log(1-x)\le2x\) for \(0\le x\le1/2\), summing the geometric progression gives \(\log d_v\le4q_v^{-t-1}\). We have \(t\ge r+5\), \(r\ge1\), so \(t+1\ge7\). Counting ideals of a given prime-power norm as above yields
\[
 0\le\log d_S\le4h\left(2^{-7}+\int_2^\infty x^{-7}\,dx\right)
    =h/24.                                                               \tag{4}
\]
Thus the entire contribution of the finite Euler quotient and marked norm to the lower logarithmic error is at most \(49h/24\), independently of the number and norm of the marked primes.

Fix any face and full lift datum, any stage \(i\), embedding, level and twist allowed there, any \(b\in\mathbb R\) satisfying \(\mathrm{Sizes}(D,i,b)\), and any good Hecke eigensystem with nonzero \(\mathrm{typePart}\). At that label the highest-weight gap \(e_i-b_i\ge(t+3)/2\) and the size bound \(|e_i-b_i|\le b\) give \(b\ge1\). The nonzero integral discriminant gives \(\mathrm{rd}_E\ge1\), and both positive primes are at least one. Hence \(L\ge\log n>1\), using \(n=r+t\ge7\). Also \(h\le2f_*\) and \(w_E\le8f_*^2\): if \(m=w_E\), the cyclotomic degree \(\varphi(m)\le[E:\mathbb Q]=2f_*\). In the product \(\varphi(m)^2/m=\prod_{p^k\parallel m}p^{k-2}(p-1)^2\), every odd-prime factor is at least one and the possible factor for two is at least \(1/2\). Thus \(m\le2\varphi(m)^2\). The torsion error in (3) is at most \(3nf_*\), by \(\log8<3\) and \(\log f_*\le f_*-1\).
