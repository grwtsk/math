# Resonances under a change in modular damping

Fix \(1\le Y<4\pi e^{-\gamma_E}\), \(L=\log Y\), and the self-adjoint, injective compact operator \(A\) and cyclic vector \(v=\sqrt{Y/2}\,S^{-1}e\) built from the modular truncation. In this note \(\kappa=\|v\|^2\). Vary only the coefficient of the rank-one dissipative part,
\[
 R_\tau=A-i\tau v\otimes v,\qquad H_\tau=R_\tau^{-1},\quad \tau>0 .
\]
The domain is \({\rm ran}(R_\tau)\). Both \(R_\tau\) and its adjoint are injective: taking imaginary parts of a kernel equation gives orthogonality to \(v\), and then injectivity of \(A\) applies. Hence the inverse is densely defined and closed. At \(\tau=1\) its eigenfrequencies are the original shifted xi zeros. [Geometric operator, characteristic function and exact measure](XiCuspClarkSpectrum.md)

For **every fixed \(\tau\ne1\)** the high eigenfrequencies are simple, approach the real axis, and their normalized eigenvectors form a Riesz basis of their closed span. Together with a basis of the finitely many remaining root spaces they form a Riesz basis of the full space at every permitted truncation height. There is a universal limit for the norms of the high simple spectral projections. Set
\[
 a=\frac{\tau-1}{\tau+1},\quad c=-\log|a|,\quad K(t)=\log(|t|/(2\pi))+L .
\]
Use the unique large positive or negative real solutions \(t_n\) of \(\phi_L(t_n)=\arg a+2\pi n\), for the unwrapped boundary phase of the geometric characteristic function. With the matching enumeration,
\[
 z_n=t_n+\frac{ic+o(1)}{K(t_n)},\qquad
 t_{n+1}-t_n=\frac{2\pi+o(1)}{K(t_n)},\qquad
 \|P_n\|\longrightarrow \frac{\sinh c}{c}.                                      \tag{1}
\]
The formula for the imaginary height is unconditional for each such fixed coefficient. The limit is singular as \(\tau\to1\). At \(\tau=1\), infinitely many eigenfrequencies have imaginary part \(1/2\) by Hardy's critical-line theorem. Uniform passage in (1) to the arithmetic coefficient would give a false conclusion. [Hardy, *Sur les zéros de la fonction ζ(s) de Riemann* (1914)](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AComptes_rendus_hebdomadaires_des_s%C3%A9ances_de_l%E2%80%99Acad%C3%A9mie_des_sciences%2C_tome_158%2C_1914.djvu/1014) · [Earlier unbounded critical spectral projections](XiCuspResonanceProjections.md)

## Actual determinant and the level equation

Put \(m=z\langle(I-zA)^{-1}v,v\rangle\) and \(\Theta=e^{iLz}\xi(1+iz)/\xi(1-iz)=(1+im)/(1-im)\). The determinant of \(I-zR_\tau\) relative to \(I-zA\) is \(1+i\tau m\). Correcting the trace of their rank-one difference gives the entire identity
\[
 \det\nolimits_2(I-zR_\tau)=\frac{e^{-i\tau\kappa z}}{2\xi(1)}
 \big[(1+\tau)e^{iLz/2}\xi(1+iz)+(1-\tau)e^{-iLz/2}\xi(1-iz)\big].              \tag{2}
\]
For example it follows first off the poles from the rank-one determinant and the two amplitudes for \(\det_2(I-zA)\), then everywhere by continuation. Each nonzero compact eigenvalue has its algebraic multiplicity in (2). All inverse eigenfrequencies have positive imaginary part: a real eigenvalue would make an eigenvector of \(A\) orthogonal to its cyclic vector; the sign of \(\Im R_\tau\) excludes the opposite half-plane. Thus throughout the frequency spectrum the denominator \(\xi(1-iz)\) is nonzero. The zero equation is exactly \(\Theta(z)=a\), or \(m(z)=i/\tau\). The characteristic inner function of \(R_\tau\) is \(B_\tau=(\Theta-a)/(1-a\Theta)\). ## The asymptotic near the real axis and exclusion of further roots

Let \(X=|x|\to\infty\). For every fixed \(M\), uniformly on \(|z-x|\le M/\log X\),
\[
 \Theta'(z)/\Theta(z)=iK(x)+o(\log X),\qquad
 \Theta(x+w/K(x))=e^{i\phi_L(x)}e^{iw}(1+o(1)),\quad |w|\le M.                   \tag{3}
\]
Here zero-freeness of \(\zeta\) near the **one-line** suffices. To see the strength needed, take \(r=\eta(\log X)^{-2/3}(\log\log X)^{-1/3}\) with a sufficiently small fixed \(\eta>0\). The disc of radius \(4r\) about \(1+r+ix\) is zero-free by the Vinogradov–Korobov region. On it \(\log|\zeta|=O(\log\log X)\) as an upper bound: on the left use the Ford–Richert estimate \(O(X^{C(1-\sigma)^{3/2}}(\log X)^{2/3})\), and on the right the truncated Dirichlet series through \(X^2\) with the pole integral and first-order summation remainder gives \(O(\log X)\) for \(|\zeta|\). At the center the Euler logarithm is bounded in absolute value by \(\log\zeta(1+r)=O(\log\log X)\). Borel–Carathéodory and Cauchy's estimate on nested discs therefore give \(|\zeta'/\zeta|\ll r^{-1}\log\log X=o(\log X)\) within distance \(3r/2\) of the center, covering both arguments in (3), with conjugation for the opposite sign of the height. Logarithmic differentiation of the elementary and gamma factors in \(\Theta\) by Stirling now gives (3); the two gamma logarithms contribute \(\log(X/(2\pi))+o(1)\). Integration on the short segment gives the second assertion, with local uniformity also for complex \(w\). This proof does not use a zero-free strip near the critical line. [Ford, zero-free regions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08205) · [Ford–Richert bounds near one](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2024.128124) · [Related estimates of Cully-Hugill and Leong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09412)

The boundary derivative is consequently \(K(x)(1+o(1))\), giving the real node spacing. At \(t_n\), the level equation on the rescaled disc converges to \(e^{i\arg a}e^{iw}=a\), which has a simple root at \(w=ic\). Rouché's theorem on every sufficiently small fixed circle about \(ic\) gives one simple frequency and its asymptotic in (1).

For completeness of the enumeration, fix \(C>c\). At \(y_0=C/\log X\), (3) bounds \(|\Theta(u+iy_0)|\le e^{-C}+o(1)\) uniformly for \(|u-x|\le X/2\). On the half-plane above this line, bounded analyticity gives the Poisson majorant of its boundary modulus. For \(y_0< y\le\sqrt X\) the kernel mass outside that interval is \(O(1/\sqrt X)\), so these points never have modulus \(|a|\) eventually. For \(y\ge\sqrt X\) use the horizontal boundary at height 2, where the functional equation, the gamma quotient of half-step difference, and absolutely convergent Euler products give \(\Theta(u+2i)=O_Y((1+|u|)^{-1/2})\) by Stirling. Its Poisson majorant tends to zero uniformly in the real part as \(y\to\infty\): bound the boundary tail by its supremum outside \([-D,D]\) and the compact part by \(2D/(\pi(y-2))\), then let \(D\to\infty\). This argument only needs decay to zero; the same argument covers bounded real parts. Any remaining unbounded sequence of roots thus has \(0<y\le C/\log X\). In (3) at its real part the modulus gives \(yK(x)=c+o(1)\), and the argument gives \(\phi_L(x)=\arg a+2\pi n+o(1)\). By the real phase derivative \(x-t_n=o(1/K(x))\), putting it in the circle already counted. [Classical differenced xi and its real phase, Lagarias, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601653)

## Projection norms, Gram matrices and completeness

At a simple root \(z=x+iy\) the right and adjoint eigenvectors are proportional to \((I-zA)^{-1}v\) and \((I-\bar z A)^{-1}v\). Their common squared norm is \(\Im m(z)/y\), and their pairing has modulus \(|m'(z)|\), by the spectral theorem for \(A\). Hence
\[
 \|P_z\|=\frac{1}{\tau y|m'(z)|}
        =\frac{1-a^2}{2y|\Theta'(z)|}.
\]
Equation (3) gives \(y|\Theta'(z)|\to c|a|\), proving the last assertion of (1). For any fixed number of consecutive normalized high eigenvectors the limiting Gram entries, for an inner product linear in the first argument, are
\[
 \lim_{|n|\to\infty}\frac{2i\sqrt{y_{n+j}y_{n+k}}}{z_{n+j}-\bar z_{n+k}}
       = \frac{c}{c-i\pi(j-k)}.
\]
The exact Gram law before taking the limit follows by polarizing the scalar resolvent and substituting the common response value \(i/\tau\). The limit is the matrix of Fourier coefficients \(\int_0^1 e^{2\pi i(j-k)s}\,2ce^{-2cs}/(1-e^{-2c})\,ds\). In particular for any window size its eigenvalues lie between \(2c/(e^{2c}-1)\) and \(2c/(1-e^{-2c})\). Both are sharp as the window size tends to infinity, by squared normalized Dirichlet kernels concentrating on interior points arbitrarily near either end. This local limit by itself would not control correlations between distant modes.

This gives a conditioning obstruction independent of how each high eigenvector is scaled. For the synthesis map from \(N\) orthonormal coordinates to \(N\) consecutive eigenvectors, allow any nonzero diagonal scaling \(d_j\). Test on the two input vectors with entries \(e^{2\pi ij\theta}/d_j\), at two different phases. Their norms are identical. Their output squared-norm ratio is therefore a lower bound for the squared condition number, for every scaling. First send the starting index to infinity at fixed \(N\), then let \(N\) grow. The two Fejér averages of the displayed symbol tend to its values at the two chosen interior points. Bringing these points to opposite ends gives the lower bound \(e^{2c}\) for the squared condition number. Unit scalings give the matching upper bound for the iterated local limit by the two symbol bounds. More precisely the iterated lower and upper limits, taking the infimum over scalings before the starting-index limit, both equal \(e^c\) for the condition number. Every global similarity making the high simple modes orthogonal consequently has condition number at least \(e^c=(1+\tau)/|1-\tau|\): restrict the similarity or its inverse to the corresponding finite spans and choose orthonormal coordinates in the orthogonal image. Even optimizing the lengths of all the vectors cannot give a uniformly conditioned route to the critical coupling.

For the global result, \(B_\tau\) is a meromorphic inner function continuing across every finite real point, with no real zeros or poles. Its only possible singular inner factor is \(e^{idz}\), \(d\ge0\), by half-plane inner factorization. Since \(\Theta(iy)\to0\) and \(a\ne0\), \(B_\tau(iy)\to-a\ne0\), which forces \(d=0\). Thus it is a pure Blaschke product up to a constant phase. For a simple zero \(z_n\) the product of pseudohyperbolic distances to all other zeros, counting multiplicities, is \(2y_n|B_\tau'(z_n)|=1/\|P_n\|\). Equations (1)–(3) give a positive limiting product \(c/\sinh c\). Throwing out finitely many low roots only increases the products. The remaining sequence therefore satisfies Carleson's uniform separation condition. Its normalized Hardy kernels are a Riesz sequence by the Hardy-space interpolation theorem of Shapiro and Shields. Their Gram matrices are exactly those of the high modular eigenvectors by the preceding polarized identity. [Carleson, interpolation with multiplicities](https://www.acadsci.fi/mathematica/Vol10/vol10pp235-237.pdf) · [Shapiro and Shields, interpolation in Hilbert spaces of analytic functions](https://doi.org/10.4064/sm-Special%20Series-1-109-110)

Finally log-differentiate this pure product at the real point zero. The boundary derivative gives \(2\tau\kappa=\sum_n 2\Im z_n/|z_n|^2\), with multiplicities. The formula follows also by normal convergence on a disc at zero; the Blaschke sum converges and the zeros have positive distance from zero. Let \(M\) be the closed span of all nonzero root spaces of \(R_\tau\). Compression to finite sums of them gives \({\rm Tr}(\Im R_\tau|_M)=-\sum_n\Im z_n/|z_n|^2=-\tau\kappa\), where the left side means the trace of the compressed imaginary part. Consequently the quotient has nonpositive imaginary part of trace zero and is self-adjoint. It is compact and quasinilpotent since the algebraic multiplicities are already exhausted on \(M\) (use the block triangular Riesz projection); it must be zero. Adjoint injectivity forces \(M^\perp=0\). The Riesz span of the high vectors is closed, and the finitely many remaining root spaces give a direct complement by their Riesz projections. Adjoining any bases of these finite spaces yields the asserted full Riesz basis. The corresponding operator can have finite Jordan blocks. The argument at \(a=0\) loses both the separation bound and the test that removes the exponential inner factor. [Critical-coupling quotient and density at height one](XiCuspTraceNormInfimum.md)

There is also a sharp change in the essential spectrum of the time evolution. For \(\tau\ne1\), \(e^{itH_\tau}\) extends to a bounded operator for every fixed positive **or negative** real \(t\). On the high Riesz basis it acts diagonally by \(e^{itz_n}\); the \(y_n\) are bounded and tend to zero. On the finite complement it is the finite Jordan exponential. These maps form a strongly continuous group by uniform boundedness on compact time intervals and density of the finite spans. Its generator is \(iH_\tau\): on the Riesz coordinates the domain requires \((z_n u_n)\in\ell^2\), exactly the range of the compact inverse on these coordinates. For every \(t\ne0\) its Fredholm essential spectrum is **the unit circle**. Indeed the high diagonal differs compactly from the diagonal \(e^{it\Re z_n}\). Every point of the circle is a limit point since the positive real parts increase eventually without bound with successive gaps tending to zero, by (1). All finite Jordan contributions disappear in the Fredholm essential spectrum. At \(\tau=1\) every positive time instead has the entire closed disk as Fredholm essential spectrum, including at height one. This difference persists regardless of the locations of the central xi ordinates. [Proof at the arithmetic coefficient](XiCuspHorizontalResolvent.md)
