## Full groups from positive Tate twists

Fix an odd prime \(p\) and a host character \(\psi\) of conductor dividing \(N\), of order prime to \(p\). Use its minimal unramified coefficient DVR \(\mathcal O\), with fraction field \(K\). Write \(S_w\) for the group of classes with coefficient \((K/\mathcal O)\psi^{-1}\chi_{\rm cyc}^{-w}\), unramified everywhere and zero at primes dividing \(pN\), for nonzero integral \(w\). The original weight \(e\) is positive and satisfies \(\psi(-1)=(-1)^e\). Suppose the residual coefficient action at \(p\) is nontrivial, as holds throughout Case I.

Take \(j\ge1\) and \(h\ge0\). If
\[
 (p-1)p^h\mid e+j,\qquad p^h S_{-j}=0,
\]
then **\(S_e\cong S_{-j}\)** over \(\mathcal O\). Indeed the two coefficient characters agree modulo \(p^{h+1}\) since the unit group modulo that power has exponent \((p-1)p^h\). For each depth \(1\le b\le h+1\), the common finite coefficient has the same totally real field of trivialization and the same action there. Global and \(p\)-local invariant corrections vanish by residual local nontriviality. Inflation–restriction gives the common finite-layer group as equivariant characters on the class group with primes above \(p\) inverted. At \(r\ne p\) unramified finite character lifts suffice: if inertia acts nontrivially its prime-to-\(p\) image kills invariants, and otherwise Frobenius on the divisible module is multiplication by a root of unity times \(r^{-w}\), with absolute value different from 1. The unramified cohomology at a required strict prime is therefore zero for either sign of \(w\). Thus the source finite-layer argument applies also to \(-j\). Equality of consecutive torsion layers at \(h,h+1\) for one weight now gives it for the other. A group in which every element is killed by some power of \(p\) cannot grow above two equal consecutive layers. For \(h=0\) use the zero residual group directly. [Finite coefficient proof and stabilization](AlpogeSelmerResidual.md)

For \(j\ge2\) there is a regulator-free upper bound on the requisite \(h\). Form an abelian CM field \(E=L_\psi(i)\) from the field of the character, or use \(L_\psi\) itself when already CM. Its degree divides \(2\operatorname{ord}(\psi)\). Write \(g\) for the \(\mathcal O\)-length of the \(\psi\)-component of \(H^2(G_{E,S_p},\mathbb Z_p(j))\otimes\mathcal O\), where \(S_p\) contains the places over \(p\) and infinity. Write \(b,c\) for the lengths of the local and global invariants over \(\mathbb Q_p,\mathbb Q\), respectively, of \((K/\mathcal O)\psi\chi_{\rm cyc}^{1-j}\). Then
\[
                     \operatorname{length} S_{-j}\le g-b+c.                    \tag{1}
\]
Restriction to \(E\) is injective by transfer. A strict class restricts to an everywhere unramified class over \(E\) which is zero over \(p\), so it lies in cohomology with ramification allowed only over \(p\). On the sign \(\psi(-1)=(-1)^j\), the rational group \(H^1(G_{E,S_p},\mathbb Q_p(j))\) vanishes. Indeed Quillen–Lichtenbaum identifies its dimension with Borel's rank of \(K_{2j-1}\). The plus part comes from the totally real fixed field by rational transfer, whose rank is zero for even \(j\) and equals the full CM rank for odd \(j\). Localization at \(p\) makes no change to rational ranks here. The connecting map from cohomology with divisible coefficients therefore injects into the indicated \(H^2\)-component. A strict class maps to zero locally. By the terminal Poitou–Tate sequence the cokernel of restriction to the sum of the local \(H^2\)'s at \(p\) has length at most \(c\) in this component, and the local target has length \(b\) by local duality. To identify these lengths one may restrict and corestrict over \(E\): its degree is prime to \(p\), taking character components is exact, and the sum over places above \(p\) is induced from a decomposition subgroup. Duality changes \(\psi\) to \(\psi^{-1}\) on the dual, giving precisely the invariant module displayed above. This proves (1). [Borel, *Stable real cohomology of arithmetic groups*, Prop. 12.2](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1974_4_7_2_235_0.pdf#page=37) · [Milne, *Arithmetic Duality Theorems*, I.4, Poitou–Tate sequence](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/adt.html) · [Gambheera–Popescu, Theorems 7.14 and 7.16, integral Fitting and étale comparison](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40687-025-00578-8)

The global integer length \(g\) can be bounded by inverting also the ramified primes of \(E/\mathbb Q\) and applying the Fitting equality at \(1-j\). The relevant eigenspace is critical. The localization map on even \(K\)-groups is injective, since the even positive \(K\)-groups of the finite residue fields vanish. At \(j=1\) there is a different bound. Here \(\psi\) is odd and \(S_{-1}\) injects after restriction into the corresponding component of \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_E)[p^\infty]\otimes\mathcal O\). In detail, Kummer theory identifies the group with divisible coefficients before imposing local conditions with \(E^\times\otimes\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p\). The strict classes have every valuation zero in \(\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p\), including at \(p\) by local splitting. The valuation kernel is an extension of \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_E)[p^\infty]\) by \(\mathcal O_E^\times\otimes\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p\), by the ideal sequence, or by sending \((\alpha)=\mathfrak a^{p^a}\) to \([\mathfrak a]\) at finite Kummer levels. The odd component of those tensored units is zero: \(u/\bar u\) is a root of unity and every finite group tensors trivially with this divisible group. Using ordinary units is justified by the valuation-zero condition at *every* prime. Omitting the condition at \(p\) would introduce additional \(S_p\)-units.

For a quantitative consequence choose \(J\ge2\). For any fixed weight \(e\), retain each pair \((p,\psi)\) admitting a critical twist \(1\le j\le J\) as above with \(h\le v_p(e+j)\), where \(h\) can be the exponent index of the actual critical strict group, the length bound (1), or a larger verified integer. Impose \(p-1\mid e+j\) and local residual nontriviality. The sum of **full** normalized lengths times \(\log p\) on all these pairs is
\[
           O\!\left(N^2J^2\big(J+\log(8N)\big)\right).                        \tag{2}
\]
Assign each retained pair one admissible \(j\). For each complex host character, use its fixed field \(E\) of degree at most \(2N\) and conductor dividing \(4N\). Changes of coefficient embedding are harmless after enlarging to the complex character list and bounding each component by the length of its whole sign over \(\mathbb Z_p\). For \(j\ge2\) sum the regulator-free odd-prime bound for \((1+(-1)^jc)/2\) on \(K_{2j-2}(\mathcal O_E)\) from the preceding manuscript, with \(c\) here denoting complex conjugation. For \(j=1\) the sum over all \(p\) is at most \(\log h(E)\le 2N\log(32N)\) by Minkowski and conductor–discriminant. Summing over at most \(N\) characters proves (2). For \(J\le (Nt)^B\), \(B\) fixed, this fits the polynomial budget in CountI and permits arbitrary character orders, including \(p\nmid e\). The congruence and exponent test remains a real restriction. [All-odd-prime critical projector estimate](AlpogeSelmerGrowth.md) · [Denominator and sign checks](AlpogeSelmerTail.md)

## A complete nonzero column at three

For \(\psi=\chi_{-107}\), \(p=3\), **\(S_e\cong\mathbb Z/3\) for every positive odd \(e\)**. Every such weight has nontrivial residual inertia at 3, and \(S_e[3]\cong\mathbb Z/3\) by the exact real quadratic class calculation in the preceding manuscript. It suffices to bound exponents at \(j=1,3,5\), as every odd \(e\) satisfies \(6\mid e+j\) for exactly one of these three twists. [Residual class of discriminant 321](AlpogeSelmerReflection.md)

At \(j=1\) the imaginary quadratic field \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-107})\) has class number three. Indeed its reduced primitive positive forms have leading coefficient \(a\le\sqrt{107/3}<6\) and are \((1,1,27),(3,1,9),(3,-1,9)\). At \(j=3,5\) take the extension \(E/\mathbb Q\) itself and allow the ramified prime 107 in the Fitting equality. Its character is primitive and ramifies there, hence its Euler factor is one. The odd eigenspace of torsion in the degree-one integral group is zero: the cyclotomic image of \(G_E\) modulo 3 has order two since \(E\ne\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), and odd \(j\) gives no fixed points in \(\mathbb Q_3/\mathbb Z_3(j)\). Thus (using localization for an upper bound) \(g\le v_3(L(1-j,\psi))\). The Euler factor at 3, if omitted, is a 3-adic unit for \(j>1\). The exact values are
\[
      L(0,\psi)=3,\qquad L(-2,\psi)=-1746,\qquad L(-4,\psi)=6468966 .
\]
Both latter integers have 3-adic valuation two. The finite generalized Bernoulli formula gives these values from \(M_n=\sum_{a=1}^{106}\psi(a)a^n\). For \(n=1,\dots,5\) the moments are \(-321,-34347,-3114663,-273299079,-24148197951\). Applying the degree-three and degree-five Bernoulli polynomials gives the displayed values, independently checked by the Lean integer identities.

The global dual invariants vanish because the quadratic character ramifies at 107 and the cyclotomic twist is unramified there. Locally 3 splits since \(-107\equiv1\bmod3\). The dual coefficient is locally \(\mathbb Q_3/\mathbb Z_3(1-j)\); its invariant length is \(1+v_3(j-1)=1\) for both \(j=3,5\), by the full local cyclotomic image and lifting the exponent at 4. Subtract this local length in (1). Each of the three critical strict groups has length at most one, giving an exponent of at most three. The congruence argument transfers the upper bound at every odd positive weight and the nonzero residual class gives equality. Using the raw global order nine at both higher twists would leave congruence classes untreated.

## Arbitrarily deep classes on genuine Case I rows

For **each prime \(p\ge11\) and each \(a\ge1\)** one can exhibit a primitive quadratic initial character, source hosts for it, and positive weights \(e\) prime to \(p\) such that **\(S_e\) contains an element of order \(p^a\)** in Case I. The conductor is allowed to vary. In fact the same construction works at \(p=5\).

Put \(m=p^a\) and choose an integer \(U\ge2\) divisible by 21 and congruent to 1 modulo \(p\). Let \(L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{1-4U^m})\), take \(\psi\) its primitive quadratic character and put \(\alpha=(1+\sqrt{1-4U^m})/2\). The numerator inside the square root need not be squarefree; all ideals and local conditions below are in the maximal order of the quadratic field. The conjugate ideals of \(\alpha\) are coprime since their generators sum to one. Their product is \((U)^m\), whence \((\alpha)=\mathfrak a^m\). The class of \(\mathfrak a\) has exact order \(m\). For if its order divided \(m/p\), the integral nonunit \(\alpha\) would be a unit times a \(p\)th power. Louboutin's trace-one lemma rules this out. For clarity, all units of an imaginary quadratic field have order prime to \(p\ge5\), so an associated power is a power. If \(\alpha=\beta^p\), then the trace of \(\beta\) divides \(\mathrm{Tr}(\beta^p)=1\) by the odd power-sum recursion and is congruent to 1 modulo \(p\) by the binomial theorem. Thus \(\mathrm{Tr}(\beta)=1\). Expanding \((1-\beta)^p+\beta^p=1\), dividing by \(p\beta\), and using \(p\mid\binom pk\) for \(1\le k<p\), gives \(1\in(\beta)\), a contradiction. [Louboutin, *On the divisibility of the class number of imaginary quadratic number fields*, Lemma 2, Proposition 3 and proof of Theorem 1, pp. 4026–4027](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-09-10021-7)

A class of this order alone would be insufficient. To check the strict local condition observe that \(U^m\equiv1\bmod p^{a+1}\), by the binomial theorem or lifting the exponent. At \(p\) the discriminant of the displayed defining polynomial is congruent to \(-3\), so all completions are unramified. Here \(\alpha^2-\alpha+1=1-U^m\) is zero modulo \(p^{a+1}\) and \(2\alpha-1\) is a unit. Hensel supplies a sixth root of unity \(\gamma\equiv\alpha\bmod p^{a+1}\) in each completion, satisfying \(\gamma^2-\gamma+1=0\). Since \(p\nmid6\), \(\gamma\) is an \(m\)th power. The quotient \(\alpha/\gamma\) is also an \(m\)th power: on an unramified extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) for odd \(p\), log and exp identify \(1+p\mathcal O\) with \(p\mathcal O\) and its \(p^a\)th powers with \(1+p^{a+1}\mathcal O\).

The finite Kummer class of \(\alpha\) maps to \([\mathfrak a]\), so it has exact order \(m\) and stays of that order on passing to divisible coefficients. Equivalently the global connecting kernel vanishes since \(L\) has no \(p\)-power roots of unity. It is zero at \(p\) by the preceding local computation, and is unramified at every other prime since its valuations are divisible by \(m\) and raising units to the \(m\)th power is surjective over the maximal unramified local extension away from \(p\). On divisible coefficients the unramified local group away from \(p\) vanishes for the nonzero Tate twist over \(L\). Conjugation sends the class to its negative because \(\alpha\bar\alpha=U^m\). Restriction and corestriction across \(L/\mathbb Q\), of degree prime to \(p\), now descend it into \(S_{-1}\) for \(\psi\), preserving all these local conditions. For \(e\equiv-1\bmod (p-1)p^{a-1}\), the two modules agree at depth \(a\) and the residual cyclotomic factor is nontrivial on inertia. Hence \(S_e[p^a]\) contains a class of order \(p^a\) by the finite comparison. For example \(e=(p-1)p^a-1\) is prime to \(p\). If \(p^H\) is the full \(p\)-part of \(h(L)\), then choosing \(e=(p-1)p^H-1\) also gives the upper bound and full group comparison with \(S_{-1}\). Thus (2) includes positive weights with individual summands of unbounded exponent.

For the source's exceptional-prime condition, put \(f=|\operatorname{disc}L|\). The numbers 3 and 7 do not divide \(f\), since \(21\mid U\); also \(p\nmid f\) since the defining discriminant is \(-3\bmod p\). The initial character has order two, so the source chooses \(p_K=7\), \(h_*=h(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7}))=1\) and \(p_1=3\). Thus \(p\notin B_\psi\). For every positive weight above, \(p-1\nmid2e\) since \(e\equiv-1\bmod p-1\) and \(p\ge5\). The parity is odd. Take the initial character as the host character, which belongs to the character family by the host kernel definition. The two moving primes can be chosen above the finitely many prescribed bounds and \(2t+1\) for any chosen \(t>e\), coprime to \(f\) and in the required progressions \(3\bmod 8\) and \(7\bmod16\), by Dirichlet. The definition of the exceptional set and these checks place the terms in actual Case I. This lower bound permits dependence of the budget on the full conductor and on the initial character; it refutes a fixed depth cutoff independent of both. [Exceptional set and auxiliary fields](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/StmtFields.lean#L54-L68) · [Host prime pair](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/PrimeSetBoundDataOfPrimitiveChar.lean#L24-L27)

One smaller exact Case I example is \(p=5, L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-611})\), \(\alpha=(19+\sqrt{-611})/2\). The discriminant \(-611=-13\cdot47\) is fundamental, the norm is \(3^5\), and the two conjugate ideals are coprime. A prime above 3 is nonprincipal because the quadratic norm \((x^2+611y^2)/4\) cannot equal 3. The ten primitive reduced positive forms have leading coefficients \(1,3,3,5,5,9,9,11,11,13\) and respective middle coefficients \(1,-1,1,-3,3,-1,1,-7,7,13\). These exhaust the reduced forms by \(a\le\sqrt{611/3}<15\) and the standard boundary convention. Hence the 5-class group has order five. Locally the polynomial \(X^2-19X+243\) splits with distinct roots at 5. Reducing powers in the quotient by this polynomial gives \(X^{24}=1\bmod25\); thus each of the two roots is a fifth power in \(\mathbb Q_5\) by the unit criterion. The Kummer, conjugation and local argument above gives \(S_{-1}\cong\mathbb Z/5\), and \(S_e\cong\mathbb Z/5\) for **all \(e\equiv19\bmod20\)**. Since \(3,5,7\nmid611\), the same auxiliary field check puts these rows in Case I, with a fixed initial character. This gives a nonzero check on the direction of transport and on the local splitting requirement.

There is a further uniform classification at this fixed prime and character. For \(j=3,5,9,13,17\), the 5-adic valuations of the primitive values \(L(1-j,\chi_{-611})\) are respectively \(0,3,2,2,2\). These are finite exact numbers given by the same generalized Bernoulli formula, using the product of Legendre symbols modulo 13 and 47. For example \(L(-2)=-138204\) and \(L(-4)=16446388500\). The critical torsion correction is zero: the global cyclotomic image over \(L\) modulo five is full, so an odd twist has no residual invariants. The character is ramified at 13 and 47, and the global dual invariants vanish. For \(j=9,13,17\) the split prime five contributes local length \(1+v_5(j-1)=1\). Thus \(S_{-3}=0\) and the groups at \(j=9,13,17\) each have length at most one by (1). Residual transport from \(j=1\) proves that \(S_e[5]\cong\mathbb Z/5\) throughout \(e\equiv3\bmod4\). It follows that **\(S_e=0\) for all \(e\equiv1\bmod4\)** and **\(S_e\cong\mathbb Z/5\) for all \(e\equiv3,7,11,19\bmod20\)**. This leaves one residue to check.

At \(e\equiv15\bmod20\), the residual field of \(\psi\omega^{-e}=\psi\omega\) is the cyclic real quartic field generated by \(\beta=\sqrt{-611}(\zeta_5-\zeta_5^{-1})\). In the compositum with \(\mathbb Q(\mu_5)\) the kernel fixes precisely this generator: an automorphism fixing it acts on \(\zeta_5\) by the identity or inversion, with the matching sign on the square root. Its polynomial is \(P=x^4-3055x^2+1866605\), by squaring the generator. PARI/GP 2.17.4 with `b=bnfinit(polredabs(P),1)` returned discriminant 46665125 and cyclic class group of order 10; the full unconditional test `bnfcertify(b)` returned **1**. The reduced polynomial is \(x^4-x^3-764x^2+764x+116281\). Thus the 5-primary class group, also after inverting 5, has exponent at most five. Since \(5\mid e\), the constant-action calculation at depths 1 and 2 applies, yielding \(S_e=S_e[5]\cong\mathbb Z/5\). Consequently the complete Case I column for this fixed initial character is **zero at \(e\equiv1\bmod4\) and cyclic of order five at \(e\equiv3\bmod4\)**. The quartic calculation is a certified PARI computation, separate from the Lean checks. ## Evidence and remaining obstruction

The formal companion proves the consecutive-layer exponent transfer for torsion groups, the congruence of inverse and positive powers, integer Bernoulli moment and valuation certificates at conductor 107, and the coverage of all odd weights by the three critical congruences. The number-field comparisons, class computations and full arithmetic estimates are manuscript results; their end-to-end formalization and independent external review remain open. In particular, when no sufficiently close bounded critical twist is certified, (2) gives no bound on the corresponding deep contribution. A varying prime can exceed \(e+J+1\), in which case no positive \(j\le J\) even gives the first weight congruence. Controlling the aggregate of these terms with the original parameter dependence remains necessary for CountI and (M). [Lean companion](AlpogeSelmerCriticalWeights.lean)
