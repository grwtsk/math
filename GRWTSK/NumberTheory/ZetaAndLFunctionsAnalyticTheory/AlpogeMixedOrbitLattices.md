# From separate Frobenius orbits to the mixed kernel and the rational lattice

grwtsk contributors

In Alpöge's `OrbitPlaces`, `OrbGe` bounds **every** series with a specified orbit expansion, with the integral residual as a variable. This quantifier allows an arbitrary mixed residual in the global expansion. Under the original hypotheses it proves `OrbitsAddLowerBound`, `OrbitsAddAtConjugate`, `OrbitRestriction` and `OrbitRestrictionAtConjugate`. The thresholds on the individual orbits may be arbitrary real numbers for which the respective `OrbGe` or `OneGe` holds. The mixed statement bounds the actual `KvalGe` for the original Young diagrams and column choices. ## Finite decomposition at the degree of the minors

Fix a source datum \(D\), entry record \(E\), prime \(p\), word, and stage \(i<D.R.m\). In the even-orbit case assume `OrbOK`, choose any `IsReps` representative set \(R\), and retain the common unit \(ep\), signs and individual series from `BeOK`. The orbit coordinates \((r,s)\) are bijective onto all the sites. Indeed the successor adds \(h_P\) in the group coordinate, also toggling the Boolean coordinate at a signal prime. Its period is the order of \(h_P\) without a toggle, and twice the order of \(2h_P\) with the toggle, since a returning step is then even. This is exactly \(d=n_P\). The orbit logarithm \(L_r\) takes its \(p^{2j}\)-power term from the site displaced by \(-2j\) within that orbit. Its linear term is the identity, so its compositional inverse \(g_r\) exists over the coefficient field; both maps have zero constant term. These statements also hold with independent copies for each matrix entry. [Original orbit length](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GrowthBounds/IsResidueClassZero.lean#L159-L166) · [Original site maps, logarithm and restrictions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/OrbitPlaces.lean#L122-L157)

Write \(Z_r\) for the variables on orbit \(r\), \(B_r(Z_r)=\mathrm{sg}_r\sum_{a,b}\mathrm{be}_r(Z_{\bullet,a,b})\), and \(U=\sum_k u_k\prod_r T_r^{k_r}\) for the integral residual of `ThetaExpansionAllPlaces`. The \(B_r\) have zero constant term by B3. Inverting the block logarithms in the given identity gives the formal equality
\[
 \mathrm{Th}(Z)=\sum_k u_k\prod_{r\in R} F_{r,k_r}(Z_r),\qquad
 F_{r,q}(Z_r)=g_r(Z_r/ep)^q\exp(B_r(Z_r/ep)).                    \tag{1}
\]
Multi-index powers are taken over all coordinates in a block. Substituting \(Z_r=ep\,L_r(T_r)\) in \(F_{r,q}\) gives exactly the original `OrbitThetaExpansion` with residual \(T_r^q\). Every factor therefore receives the threshold \(b_r\) from the stipulated `OrbGe`, including \(q=0\). Integrality of \(g_r\) is unnecessary: it is the coefficient of the residual monomial in the *original* coordinates that is integral.

For each global column choice the minor polynomial is the product of its orbit polynomials in disjoint \((X_r,Z_r)\). On a product of the \(F_r\), `applyAt0` gives the product of the corresponding paired polynomials. Indeed a joint exponent decomposes uniquely into its block exponents, its factorial is the product of the block factorials, and the coefficient of a tensor product is the product of the corresponding coefficients. The output \(X\)-variables are likewise disjoint. Every contributing derivative has total order \(W=\sum_z|\nu(z)|\), by the homogeneity of each column determinant. The \(k\) term in (1) starts in degree at least \(|k|\); all sums can consequently be truncated at \(|k|\le W\). This is a finite set because the number of variables is finite. The valuation of a tensor term in the pairing is at least \(\sum_r b_r\); multiplication by \(u_k\) and finite summation preserve the bound. This proves `OrbitsAddLowerBound`. Setting all variables outside a chosen orbit to zero in the original log-coordinate identity gives `OrbitRestriction`, since the other exponentials have constant one. Neither conclusion requires the residual to factor. When \(n_P=1\) and \(p\ge5\), each site is a separate block, and the original `ThetaExpansionRealPlaces` uses a common \(ep_0\), individual units \(ep_z\), and logarithms
\[
 B_z(Z_z)=(\mathrm{sg}_z/2)\sum_{a,b}\mathrm{be}_z(Z_{z,a,b},ep_z Z_{z,a,b}).
\]
Use \(ep_0\) in place of \(ep\) in (1). For every residual monomial this factor satisfies `Loc1.AdmB` with **the same** \(ep_0,ep_z,\mathrm{sg}_z,\mathrm{be}_z\) as in the mixed input. The preceding degree and factorial argument proves `OrbitsAddAtConjugate` for the supplied `OneGe` bounds. Setting all other sites to zero proves `OrbitRestrictionAtConjugate`. This assembly statement retains the real-place shape hypothesis; the fixed-site construction below supplies it for the selected family. [One-site expansion, including the sign and one-half factor](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/ProductZerothFirstParts.lean#L244-L264)

Single-orbit restrictions of the *given* series alone do not justify adding their bounds. At \(p=5\) take two length-two orbits, use one entry variable in each, choose \(ep=1\), all \(\mathrm{be}=0\) and \(U=1+T_1T_2\). Each selected logarithm is \(L(T)=T+T^{25}/5+\cdots\) because stepping back two sites fixes the starting site. Both restrictions are constant one. But the mixed series is \(1+g(Z_1)g(Z_2)\) with \(g=L^{-1}\). Its raw derivative of order \((1,1)\) is one and that of order \((25,25)\) is \((25!)^2/25\), of valuation 10. Quantifying over the monomial residuals in (1) captures these derivatives.

## The fixed-site expansion for the selected normalized kernel

For the first clause of `EvenConditionsAtC`, retain the tied kernel family, its `Hyp` and `Adapted` predicates, the original host requirements, and \(3\le\mathrm{splitBound}\) as in §19 of the cubical argument. Use §19's geometric \(q=\Phi\), Honda law \(F\) with logarithm \(L(X)=\sum_{j\ge0} X^{p^{2j}}/p^j\), and polarization \(P(X,Y)=q(X+_F Y)-q(X)-q(Y)\). Write \(f\simeq g\) when \(\exp(f-g)\) is an integral unit with constant one. In the auxiliary length-two torus the trace extension is \(O_2/O_2\) and both trace-dual bases are the single element 1. Thus the mean defining \(b(x,y)\) averages \(P(L^{-1}(ux),L^{-1}(u^{-p}y))\) over all Teichmüller units \(u^{p^2-1}=1\). The two independent orbit logarithms are exactly \(L(X),L(Y)\) as a step of two fixes each site. Section 19 proves B1–B4 for this two-variable mean before specialization. Its CM adjoint gives \(P([u]X,[\bar u^{-1}]Y)\simeq P(X,Y)\) since \(\bar u=u^p\). The normalized \((p^2-1)\)-st root of an integral unit with constant one is integral by the coefficient recurrence with unit leading multiplier. Taking the mean therefore preserves the congruence. Setting \(Y=X\) and using \(P(X,X)\simeq2q(X)\) from evenness and biadditivity modulo integral units yields
\[
                       q(X)\simeq \tfrac12 b(L(X),L(X)).                    \tag{2}
\]
The last division is valid because the normalized square root is integral at odd \(p\). This explains the precise half independently of the normalization of a trace basis in a larger field. ## Rational descent and stabilizer loss

The companion marked and one-letter estimates, with (1), now give mixed `EntGe` whenever the relevant all-place expansion and individual Hida hypotheses are met for the specified family of entries. Integral straightening converts this polynomial coefficient estimate to an expansion in the standard minors at the same real threshold \(B\). Standard monomial straightening applies to products over the entire finite site set, or by relabeling it as a finite cyclic index set for this purely polynomial statement. [Marked orbit estimate](AlpogeMarkedMinorFlow.md) · [One-letter theorem and integral straightening](AlpogeOrbitFlowCorollary.md)

For a return to a representation lattice, retain the source's `KerEnt`, matching \(\nu\) and \(\mathrm{Th}\), `KmapRat`, `KFi`, and unit normalization `UnUnit` at the used level-free place \(w\). Take a lattice input. By `lat_S` each class has a rational source vector whose functionals on the standard source minors are integral. By `KmapRat`, the image under each individual kernel map comes from a rational target vector. The specified `kform` identity expresses each target coordinate as a unit times the source functional applied to the paired polynomial. Its standard-minor expansion thus gives valuation at least \(B\) on every coordinate. [Rational spaces, coordinate functionals and exact kernel multiplier](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataKerI.lean#L74-L131)

Put \(e_w=\operatorname{ord}_w(p)\) for the discretely normalized valuation of the given number field. Choose a field element \(a\ne0\) with \(\operatorname{ord}_w(a)=\lceil e_wB\rceil\) by surjectivity. `prT_val` asserts that each target coordinate has the *absolute value* of a field element. Thus each nonzero one has integer order at least \(\lceil e_w B\rceil\), and division by \(a\) makes every coordinate integral, including for negative \(B\). Rounding an arbitrary coefficient in \(\mathbb C_p\) at this point would require a value-group hypothesis: for example a coordinate of valuation \(1/2\) over \(\mathbb Q_p\) meets \(B=1/2\), but division by an element of ground-field order one gives valuation \(-1/2\).

If each stabilizer order \(h_c\) divides the prescribed positive integer \(n\), put \(k=v_p(n)\). Then \(r_c=p^k/h_c\) is in the local valuation ring, and the actual averaging weight is \(p^{-k}r_c\). The class sum of the scaled rational outputs is coordinate-integral by ultrametricity. Its image lies in the target function space by `KFi` and linearity, so `levelFree_lattice_condition` puts it in the localized target lattice. The omitted common scalar is \(a/p^k\), of normalized valuation at least \(B-k\). Taking \(n=D\Gamma\) gives the usual loss \(v_p(D\Gamma)\); at stage zero with the specified trivial stabilizers take \(n=1\). The source's degree-zero identification transports the twisted result to the untwisted lattice. Thus the exact `KForm` return is conditional on these rational lattice suppliers, including the prescribed `DGamDef` and stage-zero convention.
