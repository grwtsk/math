## The field of each individual target entry

At each site \(s\) the Young diagram has column heights \(h_c\leq N\). Write \(m_J(X)\) for the product, over sites and columns, of the minors of \(X_s\) with first \(h_c\) rows and the \(h_c\) increasing columns \(J_{s,c}\). The source's formally infinite row index only enters below \(N\). All polynomials in this argument belong to the finite polynomial ring on square matrices. The empty product is 1. Write \(\Delta(Y)\) for the product of the leading principal minors of these heights. Cauchy–Binet on **each column** and multiplication give the polynomial identity over \(\mathbb Z\)
\[
   K(X,Y)=\Delta(XY)=\sum_J m_J(X)m_J(Y^{\mathsf t}).
\]
The \(J\)'s are ordered by their actual site and column labels. Distinct indices may yield identical polynomials. The bijection \(\mathrm{tauPl}\) identifies the sites with the original embeddings. Vanishing rows of the diagram after row \(N-1\) give exactly the zero-padded \(\mathrm{colsOf}\) convention. [Original minor, column indexing and squared-matrix kernel](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/UnipotentAdelicQuotients/WeylLatDual.lean#L38-L68)

Let \(B_{\mathbb Q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{m_J\}\). For a rational linear functional \(l\) which annihilates all \(K(X,Y)\) for rational \(Y\), the polynomial \(\sum_J l(m_J)m_J(Y^\mathsf t)\) vanishes by the polynomial identity principle over the infinite field \(\mathbb Q\). After relabeling the variables, applying \(l\) gives \(\sum_J l(m_J)^2=0\). Each term vanishes. Separation by linear functionals proves that every \(m_J\) lies in the rational span of the evaluated kernels. Consequently
\[
 \mathcal C:B_{\mathbb Q}^{\vee}\longrightarrow B_{\mathbb Q}^{\mathsf t},\qquad
 l\longmapsto \sum_J l(m_J)m_J(Y^{\mathsf t})
\]
is injective and then bijective by equality of finite dimensions. These conclusions extend to every characteristic-zero field by scalar extension. The rational span assertion also applies to an extension-valued functional with prescribed values in a ground-field subspace, as proved directly in the companion Lean module. The sum of squares is taken over \(\mathbb Q\), where positivity applies. [Verified rational span and descent](AlpogeKernelFieldEntries.lean)

For the source's \(\mathrm{WtPol}\), over an infinite characteristic-zero field, the right diagonal torus gives column multihomogeneity of each polynomial and elementary upper unipotents give the column-addition identities. The converse follows by factoring an upper triangular matrix. These identities pass to new coefficient fields by polynomial identity in the torus and root subgroup parameters. The flag-basis argument gives the resulting component spanned by the \(m_J(Y^\mathsf t)\). More precisely, the initial-column bideterminants have shapes \(\nu\), determined up to column reordering by the multiset of positive heights. Every basis element therefore occurs among the stated products. Independence at multiple sites follows by taking tensor products, since the variables and group actions at different sites are independent. Left substitution \(Y\mapsto MY\) commutes with all right actions. Thus any such substituted weight polynomial lies in the image of \(\mathcal C\), using the preceding bijection and extending an arbitrary functional from the finite-dimensional subspace to the ambient polynomial ring. This establishes the range property needed for the source's chosen \(\mathrm{canInv}\) on the normalized source and target. ## Integral Cauchy duality from generic row substitutions

Let \(B_{\mathbb Z}\) be the lattice spanned by the same \(m_J(X)\), and let \(W_{\mathbb Z}\) be the source Weyl lattice on \(Y\). First use one site. The semistandard basis over \(\mathbb Z\) consists of flag products with distinct leading monomials of coefficient \(1\). All the \(m_J\) lie in its component, by column homogeneity and the determinant column-addition identities after transposing. Conversely every basis bideterminant is among the \(m_J\) after transposition and reordering. Thus these polynomials are an integral basis for \(B_{\mathbb Z}\). Taking their leading coefficient positions in decreasing term order gives a unitriangular coefficient minor. It follows by integral elimination that \(B_{\mathbb Z}\) is a direct summand of the finite free module of ambient monomials of the specified total degree. Every integral functional on \(B_{\mathbb Z}\) hence extends to an integral combination of coefficient extractions in \(X\). This statement also follows by successively solving the triangular system of coefficients. [Basis specification over every integral domain](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/GradedWeylDivisibility/StatementD.lean#L9-L32) · [Kernel-checked integral pivot descent](AlpogeKernelFieldEntries.lean)

Write \(C\) for the integer span of the polynomials \([X^\alpha]\Delta(XY)\). The coefficient family is finite. The source defines \(W_{\mathbb Z}\) as the span of words of operators
\[
 D_{ab}^{(k)}f(Y)=[t^k]\,f((I+tE_{ba})Y)
\]
applied to \(\Delta(Y)\). The product formula for column heights gives exactly its highest vector \(\mathrm{hw}\). Every word is a coefficient after substituting a finite product of elementary matrices with independent parameters for \(X\). It belongs to \(C\). To prove the reverse inclusion, first translate \(X=I+T\). Integer polynomial translations in both directions give identical spans of the coefficients in \(X\) and \(T\). Over the formal power series ring \(\mathbb Z[[T_{ab}]]\), the matrix \(I+T\) factors into finitely many matrices \(I+uE_{ba}\), including diagonal factors \(a=b\), all with \(u\) in the augmentation ideal. Indeed eliminate entries below and then above the diagonal. At each step the pivot has constant term 1. Its inverse is an integral formal series, every off-diagonal elimination scalar has zero constant term, and a remaining diagonal pivot \(v\) gives \(u=v-1\). Reverse the elementary operations to recover \(I+T\). In the product expansion of \(\Delta((I+T)Y)\), the coefficient of \(T^\alpha\) receives contributions only from words with total index at most \(|\alpha|\). Its value is an integral linear combination of the divided-row words. This proves \(C=W_{\mathbb Z}\) at every rank and dominant diagram, including the empty diagram. [Source definition of the highest vector and divided-power word lattice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/UnipotentAdelicQuotients/WeylLatDual.lean#L9-L36)

Integral extension of functionals now identifies \(\mathcal C(B_{\mathbb Z}^{\vee})\) with \(C\). Rational injectivity proves an isomorphism onto \(W_{\mathbb Z}\). For several sites the Cauchy kernel is the product of the individual ones. The semistandard bases tensor to a split basis of their product, and \(\mathcal C\) becomes the tensor product of the site maps. Let \(R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C_p}\). An arbitrary \(\mathbb C_p\)-functional takes all \(m_J\) into \(R\) exactly when its restriction to the split basis lies in \(R\otimes B_{\mathbb Z}^\vee\). The image under the integral isomorphism is precisely the \(R\)-span of products from \(W_{\mathbb Z}\), evaluated on square matrices. This is the source's additive closure \(\mathrm{DelW}\). Evaluation identifies these polynomials with their functions since \(\mathbb C_p\) is infinite. This proves the full integral equivalence of \(\mathrm{WeylLatticeDuality.Stmt}\) in the manuscript, including every ambient functional, admissible reindexing, and rank. The standard monomial theorem is the input shared with the primary source. The generic-row argument does not use its duality theorem as a premise. [Exact integral equivalence](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/UnipotentAdelicQuotients/WeylLatDual.lean#L51-L71)

A one-row shape of length \(p\), in ambient rank two, gives a concrete obstruction to checking ordinary integral monomial coefficients instead. On the polynomial variables \(x_1,x_2\) in the first row, take the functional equal to \(1/p\) on \(x_1^{p-1}x_2\) and zero on all other monomials. Applied to \((x_1 y_1+x_2 y_2)^p\), this gives \(y_1^{p-1}y_2\). That polynomial has integral coefficients. Its corresponding divided-power coordinate must be a multiple of \(p\), since the coefficient lattice for this kernel has the factor \(\binom p1\) there. Thus the divided-power test detects precisely the missing denominator.
