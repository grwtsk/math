# Petersson determinants and the norm of the moved theta section

grwtsk contributors

For the definite unitary class-sum model, the Petersson covolume of an integral Hecke subspace has three finite contributions. They come from the Weyl coefficient index, failure of integral orthogonal splitting, and the image of the stabilizer transfer. We compute the formula over a CM coefficient field, evaluate the complete first contribution in arbitrary highest weight before taking invariants, and determine the Gaussian norm of the geometrically normalized theta section at every complex host. The automorphic image of that section and its global Petersson norm require a further comparison.

## The coefficient metric in a self-dual frame

Let \(F\) be a CM coefficient field containing the images of the coefficient embeddings, with the CM involution inducing ordinary conjugation at every infinite place. Work with realized positive definite Hermitian host data and an \(F\)-rational subspace below. Scalars can be extended first to such a field when the class model, local frames and subspace descend there. Let \(c\) be a lattice class of the prescribed level. Write \(\Delta_c,\nabla_c\) for the transported source Weyl and dual Weyl lattices. Use the Hermitian form with the source's determinant twist and Fischer denominator at the highest vector. The image of a basis of the input lattice in each local coefficient frame must be unimodular relative to its Hermitian dual at the conjugate prime. This is the self-dual coefficient case, including the identity frame. For a non-self-dual input the same calculation pairs the frame with its dual. On the square matrix model write \(H_\lambda\) for the product of the initial principal minors with exponents \(\lambda_i-\lambda_{i+1}\). The space \(\nabla_{\mathbb Q}\) consists of polynomials covariant under right upper triangular matrices, with the prescribed diagonal character. The integral model consists of those with integral polynomial coefficients. On this space the normalized Fischer pairing with \(H_\lambda\) equals evaluation at the identity. Indeed both functionals kill all row weights other than \(\lambda\): on the identity this follows by moving a left diagonal factor to the right. In weight \(\lambda\) the highest-weight line is spanned by \(H_\lambda\) (equivalently only the highest semistandard tableau has that content). Both functionals take value one there. Adjoint to a divided row operation is its transposed divided row operation, by the monomial identity \(x_i^\ast=\partial_i\) for the Fischer form. Thus pairing with words applied to \(H_\lambda\) reads off individual coefficients of \(f(g(t_1,\dots,t_k))\), with \(g\) a product of elementary row matrices at the identity. These coefficients are integral for integral \(f\). Conversely if \(f\) has a denominator at \(p\), multiply by the minimal positive power of \(p\) making it integral locally. Its nonzero polynomial reduction restricts nontrivially to the lower unitriangular group: otherwise right upper triangular covariance makes it vanish on the entire open \(LU\) cell, hence identically. This is a polynomial identity over the algebraic closure, so small residue fields pose no exception. Ordered elementary lower transvections parametrize the lower unitriangular group over \(\mathbb Z\), with polynomial inverse by elimination. Some coefficient tested by a word is therefore nonzero after reduction, contrary to integrality of every original pairing. This proves \(\Delta^\sharp=\nabla\); finite projectivity yields the reversed duality. ## Three finite indices for a Hecke subspace

Let \(\Gamma_c\) be the finite unitary stabilizer and \(h_c=|\Gamma_c|\). Include a level-character twist in the stabilizer action when it preserves the lattice and is unitary. With the original class-sum metric set
\[
 D=\bigoplus_c\Delta_c^{\Gamma_c},\quad N=\bigoplus_c\nabla_c^{\Gamma_c},
 \quad B=\bigoplus_c\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_c}\gamma\Big)\nabla_c,\qquad
 (x,y)_{\mathrm{Pet}}=\sum_c h_c^{-1}(x_c,y_c)_c .
\]
Then \(D^{\sharp_{\mathrm{Pet}}}=B\). Indeed \(\Delta_c^{\Gamma_c}\) is saturated: testing invariance after multiplying by a nonzero scalar tests it before multiplication. Its quotient in \(\Delta_c\) is finitely generated torsion-free over the Dedekind ring, hence projective. An integral functional on the invariant lattice therefore extends to the full lattice. By the coefficient duality it is represented there by an element of \(\nabla_c\). Orthogonal projection to invariants is \(h_c^{-1}\sum_\gamma\gamma\); compensating for the Petersson factor makes the representing vector the unnormalized transfer. Conversely the same pairing shows each transfer is integral. This proof applies even at primes dividing \(h_c\). [Exact class spaces, weights, form and lattices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Divisibility/ClassSum.lean#L50-L99)

For any \(F\)-rational subspace \(U\) with the induced positive Hermitian metric, let \(\pi\) denote orthogonal projection. It is \(F\)-rational by inversion of the Gram matrix over \(F\). Write \(D_U=D\cap U,\ N_U=N\cap U\). The following indices count elements of the finite underlying abelian quotients:
\[
 i=[N_U:D_U],\qquad g=[\pi N:N_U],\qquad t=[\pi N:\pi B].
\]
The normalized covolume of a rank \(r\) projective lattice \(P\) is its ordinary real covolume, with Euclidean volume at the complex places, divided by \((2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta_F|})^r\). Use the positive Hermitian form at each place to define real volume. Then
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle \mathcal C(D_U)^2=\frac{i\,g}{t}.}                         \tag{1}
\]
In fact saturated extension inside \(D\) gives \(D_U^\sharp=\pi B\). For any Hermitian lattice \(\mathcal C(P)\mathcal C(P^\sharp)=1\): in a pseudo-basis the normalized volume is the product of absolute norms of the coefficient ideals times the product of Hermitian Gram determinants over the complex places. The dual pseudo-basis has inverse Gram and conjugate-inverse ideals. Now \(\mathcal C(D_U)=i\,\mathcal C(N_U)=ig\,\mathcal C(\pi N)\), whereas \(\mathcal C(\pi B)=t\,\mathcal C(\pi N)\), proving (1).

The isomorphism \(\pi N/N_U\simeq N/(N_U+N\cap U^\perp)\) follows by projecting and taking the kernel. Thus \(g\) is the genuine congruence index, and \(t\) is the order of a quotient of \(\bigoplus_c\widehat H^0(\Gamma_c,\nabla_c)\) by functoriality of projection. In particular \(t\) is bounded by the order of that finite group and its quotient is annihilated by \(\mathrm{lcm}_c h_c\). For \(U\) the whole space \(g=1\). If every stabilizer is trivial, \(t=1\). The latter condition is specified for stage zero by the source's level condition; it must be checked at the selected level. [Stage-zero stabilizer condition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/FlipTwoThree.lean#L140-L149)

Suppose an integral self-adjoint Hecke operator \(T\) preserves \(N\) and monic coprime-over-\(F\) polynomials \(P,Q\in\mathcal O_F[X]\) annihilate its actions on \(U,U^\perp\), respectively. For \(R=\operatorname{Res}(P,Q)\ne0\), integral Bézout gives \(AP+B'Q=R\); then \(R\pi=B'(T)Q(T)\). This proves \(R\pi N\subset N_U\). The congruence quotient embeds into \(R^{-1}N_U/N_U\); applying the same proof to the orthogonal complement gives
\[
 g\le |N_{F/\mathbb Q}R|^{\min(\dim U,\dim U^\perp)} .
\]
The integer can really grow even for a rank-two unimodular class block with trivial stabilizer. In the standard plane let \(U=F(1,m)\), \(m\in\mathbb Z\). Its projection denominator is \(1+m^2\), and \(g=|N_{F/\mathbb Q}(1+m^2)|\). The integral symmetric matrix \((1,m)^t(1,m)\) separates its two eigenspaces by exactly \(1+m^2\). Thus replacing the congruence factor by one would already fail in rank two.

## Coefficient index at every highest weight

In one untwisted integral coefficient block write \(A_\lambda=[\nabla_{\mathbb Z}:\Delta_{\mathbb Z}]\). Its exact value can be computed without choosing any automorphic periods. For the standard monomial basis \(P_T=\sum_\alpha c_{T,\alpha}X^\alpha\) of semistandard products of initial-column minors let
\[
 G_{TS}=\frac{\sum_\alpha\alpha!\,c_{T,\alpha}c_{S,\alpha}}
                    {\sum_\alpha\alpha!\,c_{H,\alpha}^2}.
\]
Then \(A_\lambda=(\det G)^{-1}\), by integral duality with respect to this rational form. Here the standard monomials are an integral basis by bideterminant straightening and their distinct unit leading terms. Independently, writing \(u_i=\lambda_i+n-i\) for \(1\le i\le n\), set \(d(v)=\prod_{a<b}(v_a-v_b)/(b-a)\). One has
\[
 A_\lambda=\prod_{i<j}\prod_{1\le k<u_i-u_j}
         k^{-d(u-k(e_i-e_j))}.                                                  \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the canonical map from the Weyl module into its contragredient sends the primitive highest vector to the primitive highest functional, as in the preceding Fischer normalization. At a prime \(p\) the sum of the dimensions of its positive Jantzen filtration layers is the \(p\)-adic length of its cokernel, by elementary divisors. The Weyl-module sum formula expresses the sum of their characters as \(\sum_{i<j}\sum_{0<k<u_i-u_j}v_p(k)\,\chi(s_{ij,k}\cdot\lambda)\), where the affine reflection is at level \(k\); terms with \(p\nmid k\) vanish. Its shifted coordinate vector is obtained by swapping the \(i,j\) entries of \(u-k(e_i-e_j)\). Weyl's dimension formula evaluated on the resulting virtual character gives the negative signed Vandermonde dimension in (2), including zero on walls. Taking all prime valuations proves the identity. This application uses the ordinary integral highest-weight form and does not use a theorem about zeta zeros. [Andersen and Kulkarni, Weyl-module sum formula, §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0612768) · [Gruber, affine reflection convention and sum formula, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03310)

For \(n=2,\ \lambda=(m,0)\), the monomial \(x^{m-j}y^j\) has normalized squared Fischer norm \(\binom mj^{-1}\), so \(A_\lambda=\prod_{j=0}^m\binom mj\). The determinant twist removed at the start acts identically on the two integral lattices in each unimodular frame. For embedding weights \(\lambda_\tau\) of dimensions \(r_\tau\), putting \(r=\prod_\tau r_\tau\) gives
\[
 [\nabla_c:\Delta_c]=\prod_\tau A_{\lambda_\tau}^{[F:\mathbb Q]r/r_\tau}
\]
before stabilizer invariants. This follows by tensoring the local elementary divisors and transporting both lattices by the same local basis. In particular (1) computes the entire covolume of the full class space with trivial stabilizers by multiplying this explicit number over the classes and taking its square root. For nontrivial stabilizers, \(i\) is the index in the invariant subspace. It embeds into the original coefficient quotient, so the full product of the displayed indices bounds \(i\), also after intersection with \(U\).

## Norm on the moved theta line

The original lattice move \(J_{A,B}\) on sections is the sum of signed Heisenberg translates over \(B/(A\cap B)\), divided by the positive square root of this cardinality. Take the source's full-rank self-dual commensurable lattices \(A,B\) for the imaginary part of the positive Hermitian form \(H\). On the common quotient by \(C=A\cap B\), use its Haar-probability Gaussian norm \(\int |F(z)|^2e^{-\pi H(z,z)}\,dz\). Each translate has the same squared norm, since its Gaussian point norm translates exactly and mean measure is translation invariant. Under any \(a\in A\) the terms indexed by \(b,b'\) transform by eigencharacters whose ratio is \(\exp(\pm 2\pi i\,\Im H(a,b-b'))\), from the Heisenberg commutator. If the cosets differ, self-duality of \(A\) supplies an \(a\) on which this ratio is nontrivial. Unitary eigenvectors for these two eigencharacters are orthogonal. The sign law makes the summands independent of coset representatives and makes the result a \(B\)-section by translation of the finite sum. Summing their squared norms now cancels the square of the explicit denominator. Normalized integrals of \(A\)- or \(B\)-periodic point norms on the common cover equal the corresponding means downstairs. Hence this exact lattice move is an isometry, even when its finite index is divisible by a bad prime. The source takes a fourth tensor root of the kernel obtained by this move from \(4f\) copies of the marked elliptic section after a unitary coordinate change, with \(f=[E:K]\). The chosen correction \(\kappa\) has modulus one in every geometric realization: each scalar in its finite product has a positive power \(\pi_p/\overline{\pi_p}\) in the imaginary quadratic field. Therefore the squared norm of the fourth tensor is the \(4f\)-th power of the elliptic squared norm, and each solution of the fourth tensor equation has squared norm equal to its \(f\)-th power. In particular there is no residual positive factor from the choice of fourth root. After the \(NN'\) tensor power in the matrix entries the source makes two further lattice moves. Both preserve the norm just computed. The determinant-ideal character contributes modulus one as well. Indeed a norm-one ideal is \(J/\bar J\), by assigning its opposite exponents at each conjugate pair. Principalizing \(J\) shows that a positive power has generator \(a/\bar a\). The specified character on such principal generators is a product of their CM embeddings with integral exponents, hence has modulus one at every realization. This uses the original character normalization.
