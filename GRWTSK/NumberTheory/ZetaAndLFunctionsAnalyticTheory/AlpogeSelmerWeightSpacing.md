# Spacing of opposite-sign Selmer lengths in weight

grwtsk contributors

For every tame character branch in Case I, a window of weights contains at most one exceptional weight per generator of its cyclotomic split class module. Outside those weights the **full** Selmer lengths satisfy an explicit factorial estimate. Summing the retained terms in the original rational orbit at a single weight \(e\) costs at most \(C\varphi(d)e\log^2(2e)\), uniformly in the initial conductor and coefficient primes. The remaining exceptional terms enter CorG without a cutoff. We also determine a full Case I column in which orders 1, 5 and 25 all occur. ## A weight estimate valid in every rank

Let \(p\) be odd, \(\psi\) a finite prime-to-\(p\) character and \(\mathcal O\) its minimal unramified coefficient DVR. Fix one residue of the positive weight modulo \(p-1\), satisfying the source parity and residual local nontriviality at \(p\). Write \(F\) for the totally real field of the residual coefficient character \(\theta=\widetilde{\bar\psi^{-1}\omega^{-e}}\). Let \(M\) be the \(\theta\)-component over \(\mathcal O\) of the inverse limit of \(p\)-class groups with primes above \(p\) inverted, in the cyclotomic tower of \(F\). Normalize a generator \(\gamma\) of the tower group to have principal cyclotomic value \(u=1+p\). The finite-layer and strict character identifications give
\[
 C_n=M/(\gamma^{p^n}-1)M,\qquad
 S_e^\vee\simeq M/(\gamma-u^{-e})M.
\]
Here the dual uses \(\mathcal O\)-linear continuous characters into its fraction field modulo \(\mathcal O\). The local hypotheses kill the ambiguity and the local inflation corrections. They hold on every source Case I entry because the squared tame inertia character is trivial and \(p-1\nmid2e\). [Full control and the twist convention](AlpogeSelmerTowerControl.md) · [Minimal coefficient normalization](AlpogeSelmerReflection.md)

The structure theorem for cyclotomic class modules and Ferrero–Washington's \(\mu=0\) theorem imply that \(M\) is finitely generated over \(\mathcal O\). Indeed the ordinary module over the real abelian field \(F\) is a finitely generated torsion Iwasawa module. With \(\mu=0\), its structure decomposition is, up to finite groups, a sum of quotients by distinguished polynomials, each finite free over \(\mathbb Z_p\). Finite scalar extension, the split quotient and the tame direct summand preserve finite generation over the coefficient DVR. This allows finite torsion in \(M\). Write \(r=\dim_k M/pM\), including these generators. [Iwasawa, *On* \(\mathbf Z_l\)*-extensions of algebraic number fields*, Annals 98 (1973)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1973/98-2/p04) · [Ferrero–Washington, *The Iwasawa invariant* \(\mu_p\) *vanishes for abelian number fields*, Annals 109 (1979)](https://doi.org/10.2307/1971116)

Take all \(h\) weights of the specified residue in an interval of consecutive positive integers. There is a set of at most \(\min(r,h)\) of them outside which
\[
 \sum_{e\ \mathrm{retained}}\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O} S_e
 \ \le\ r\big(h-1+v_p((h-1)!)\big) \qquad(h\ge1).                  \tag{1}
\]
For \(h\ge2\), each retained term is at most \(r(1+\lfloor\log_p(h-1)\rfloor)\). If \(r=0\) all terms vanish. When \(r\ge h\) one may discard the entire interval on this branch.

To prove (1), choose a minimal surjection \(\pi:\mathcal O^r\to M\) and lift the action of \(\gamma\) on its generators to an integral matrix \(A\). For any scalar \(x\), the image of \((A-x)\mathcal O^r\) is \((\gamma-x)M\). With \(x_e=u^{-e}\) and \(P(X)=\det(XI-A)\), Smith normal form therefore bounds the full length by \(v_p(P(x_e))\) where the determinant is nonzero. Each root \(\alpha_j\) of the monic polynomial is integral in a finite splitting field. Use its valuation normalized by \(v_p(p)=1\). Select a weight \(e_j\) where \(v_p(x_{e_j}-\alpha_j)\) is maximal, allowing infinity. At every other weight the ultrametric inequality gives
\[
 v_p(x_e-\alpha_j)\le v_p(x_e-x_{e_j})
                   =1+v_p(e-e_j).
\]
The equality follows by factoring out a unit and applying odd-prime lifting of the exponent to \((1+p)^{|e-e_j|}-1\). Delete the selected weights. Every remaining determinant is nonzero. Number the weights \(0,\ldots,h-1\) in the progression. If \(e_j\) has index \(i\), the product of the nonzero index gaps from it is \(i!(h-1-i)!\), which divides \((h-1)!\). The progression step \(p-1\) is a \(p\)-unit. Summing the bounds root by root over the retained weights gives (1), since all omitted gap valuations are nonnegative. The pointwise estimate follows from the largest possible index gap. One may instead discard the \(\min(r,h)\) largest actual lengths, with ties broken by weight. The decreasing order statistics give the same pointwise and sum bounds. Finiteness of each individual original strict group also follows from the separate finite even \(K\)-group comparison. [Principal-unit valuation in Lean](AlpogeSelmerReflection.lean) · [Finite arithmetic at a fixed weight](AlpogeSelmerBudget.md)

Take the window \(1,\ldots,W\), \(W=2e\), at a target weight \(e\), and use the choice that discards the entire branch if \(r\ge h>0\). Any retained positive length has \(1\le r\le h-1\le (W-1)/(p-1)\), hence \(p\le W\). The pointwise bound after multiplication by \(\log p\) is at most \(2W\log(2W)/(p-1)\). Sum over any finite list of coefficient primes and its rational character orbit of size at most \(\varphi(d)\), \(d=H.\mathrm{index}\). The sum of \(1/(p-1)\) is at most \(1+\log W\) by the harmonic series bound. This proves the stated pointwise orbit estimate on all retained entries with an absolute constant. Thus no upper bound for \(r\) uniform in \(p\) or \(N\) is needed for these entries. The deletions depend on the prime and character. For \(p>W\) a single occupied weight in a nonzero branch can constitute its entire window. The original CorG requires the specified \(e\) for all primes, including these entries.

There is a finite method to bound the exceptional count of an individual branch. If \(s=\dim_k C_n/p C_n<p^n\), then \(r=s\). Put \(V=M/pM\) and \(T=\gamma-1\). In characteristic \(p\) one has \(C_n/p C_n=V/T^{p^n}V\). If every inclusion \(T^{j+1}V\subset T^j V\) for \(0\le j<p^n\) is strict, that quotient has dimension at least \(p^n\). Otherwise equality at one step gives \(T^jV=0\) by Nakayama over \(k[[T]]\). This proves the claim. In particular (1) applies without assuming that the tower orders stabilize.

For a check on the missing terms, fix a positive \(e_0\) prime to \(p\) and let \(M=\mathbb Z/p^B\) with \(\gamma\) acting by \(u^{-e_0}\). This defines a continuous pro-\(p\) action. The zeroth-layer order is \(p\); at layer \(n\) it is \(p^{\min(B,n+1)}\). The quotient at \(e_0\) has order \(p^B\), while at \(e\ne e_0\) it has order \(p^{\min(B,1+v_p(e-e_0))}\). Thus arbitrary growth at one chosen weight is consistent with the same low layers, one generator and the ultrametric spacing. This is a module counterexample to removing the exception using spacing alone. Its realization as a class module of the given arithmetic family is unproved.

## A complete nonconstant Case I column at five

Take the primitive initial character \(\chi_{-7723}\), \(p=5\). For all positive odd weights, the full strict group is
\[
 S_e\cong\begin{cases}
   0 & e\equiv3\pmod4,\\
   \mathbb Z/25 & e\equiv5\pmod{20},\\
   \mathbb Z/5 & e\equiv1,9,13,17\pmod{20}.
 \end{cases}                                                            \tag{2}
\]
Here \(d=7723\) is squarefree, congruent to 3 modulo 4, and coprime to 105. As with the conductor 611 realization, the source's auxiliary quadratic field has discriminant \(-7\), class number one, and next auxiliary prime three. For this initial character five is outside the exceptional set, \(4\nmid2e\), and the moving primes can be chosen above all the source lower bounds. The field cut out by the initial character and \(\mu_5\) is unramified at seven, so it does not contain the auxiliary quadratic field. [Source hypotheses and host prime choices](AlpogeSelmerCriticalWeights.md) · [Original exceptional set](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/StmtFields.lean#L54-L68)

Both odd residual branches have the same totally real cyclic quartic field \(F\). With \(d=7723\), \(x=\sqrt{-d}(\zeta_5-\zeta_5^{-1})\) satisfies \(x^4-5dx^2+5d^2=0\). The automorphism \(\sigma\) fixing \(\sqrt{-d}\) and squaring \(\zeta_5\) acts on \(x\) by \(x^3/d-3x\). Indeed \(x^2/d=3+\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}\). In PARI/GP 2.17.4 the commands \(b=\mathtt{bnfinit}(P,1)\), \(\mathtt{bnfcertify}(b)\) gave the cyclic class group of order 25 and full certificate 1. On its ideal generator \(I=b.\mathrm{gen}[1]\), the coordinate of \(\sigma I\) from \(\mathtt{bnfisprincipal}(b,\mathtt{nfgaloisapply}(b,x^3/d-3x,I),0)\) was 18 modulo 25. This is the Teichmüller lift of 3 modulo 5, since \(18^2=-1\bmod25\). In particular the base involution \(\tau=\sigma^2\) acts by inversion.

For the degree-20 first layer \(E_1\), use the relative circular-unit index of the \(\tau=-1\) part from the preceding manuscript. The rank is ten. The two split primes \(2075556251,2461706251\) both have primitive root 2. Use the signed, periodic logarithm rows \(L_{i+j}\) modulo 3125 described there. The respective initial vectors are
\[
 (617,2692,1612,724,1083,89,1985,1954,2079,2546),\qquad
 (2181,2988,2877,2522,2670,3089,711,808,634,907).
\]
Take rows \(i=0,\dots,8\) at the first prime and row zero at the second. The determinant is \(1425\bmod3125\), of 5-adic valuation two. This proves that the entire anti-invariant 5-part in the first layer has order at most 25. Taking a complete orbit of rows at either prime separately gave the weaker determinants 1500 and 1250. All these rows evaluate the same global unit lattice. [Relative regulator identity, local residue maps and reconstruction algorithm](AlpogeSelmerUnitIndex.md)

Norm from the first layer surjects onto the base class group since \(E_1/F\) is totally ramified at the unique prime above 5 and its intersection with the Hilbert class field of \(F\) is trivial. Chebotarev gives representatives in each base class splitting in \(E_1\). Taking the anti-invariant part preserves this surjectivity as 2 is invertible. The unique prime above 5 is fixed by \(\tau\) and contributes no anti-invariant part when localizing. Hence the first-layer and base localized anti-invariant groups are isomorphic by norm. At each tame character, adjacent-level control gives \(M=C_0\) with \(\gamma=1\). For \(e\equiv1\bmod4\) the residual character takes the value \(2^{-e}=3\bmod5\) on \(\sigma\), matching its actual action on \(C_0\). The resulting cyclic quotient has order \(5^{\min(2,1+v_5(e))}\). In the other branch the residual eigenvalue differs and the quotient is zero. This proves (2). [Stabilization proof and formula for the full groups](AlpogeSelmerTowerControl.md)

For conductor 611, mixing the first seven of ten rows at \(45825001\) with the first three at \(87831251\) gives determinant 95 modulo 3125 in layer one. In layer two, mixing the first 47 of 50 rows with the first three at the other prime gives 1770. The primitive roots are 21 and 7 respectively. Both valuations are one. The base has anti-invariant 5-class order five, so the first-layer certificate proves full tower stability on the two quartic branches. This also gives an independent derivation of the earlier full-column result there.

## Conductor 479 and the surviving weights

For \(\chi_{-479}\) the same quartic computation gave full certificate 1, cyclic class group of order five, and \(\sigma\) acting by 2. The prime above 5 is again unique. Consequently the residual strict groups have dimension one for \(e\equiv3\bmod4\) and zero for \(e\equiv1\bmod4\). The latter full group vanishes. For the former, a finite strict group is cyclic, since its \(5\)-torsion has dimension one. The first-layer relative determinant at \(8981251\), generator 3, is 2975 modulo 3125. The anti-invariant first-layer length is therefore at most two, giving \(r\le2\) by the finite generator test. Unlike at conductor 7723, this bound exceeds the base length.

To obtain full groups at actual weights use the primitive critical values \(L(1-j,\chi_{-479})\). For \(j=5,13,17,21\) their 5-adic valuations are \(2,2,2,3\). The dual local invariant correction is \(1+v_5(j-1)\), since five splits in the quadratic field and \(4\mid j-1\). The global invariant correction vanishes, as does the global torsion correction for the odd twist. Thus the critical upper lengths are all one. The comparison through depth two proves \(S_e=\mathbb Z/5\) for **\(e\equiv3,7,15,19\bmod20\)**. On the remaining branch \(e\equiv11\bmod20\), take \(j=9,29,49,69\). All four valuations equal three and the local subtraction is one. For \(e\equiv91,71,51,31\bmod100\) this gives cyclic order 5 or 25. At \(j=89\) the valuation is four, giving upper length three when \(e\equiv411\bmod500\). These critical calculations alone leave part of the surviving branch undecided. Each displayed critical value was evaluated with rational Bernoulli polynomials and independently by exact quadratic-character power sums. [Critical transport and local correction](AlpogeSelmerCriticalWeights.md) · [PARI class-group certification semantics](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html-stable/General_number_fields.html#bnfcertify)

These lower bounds can be sharpened to exact order 25 on all four progressions \(e\equiv31,51,71,91\bmod100\). Here is an independent certificate of the first-layer class and its action. Form \(P_1=\mathtt{polredbest}(\mathtt{polcompositum}(x^4-2395x^2+5\cdot479^2,\mathtt{polsubcyclo}(25,5))[1])\). In its degree-20 field use the ideal \(I\) and the 19 candidate units returned by \(b=\mathtt{bnfinit}(P_1,1)\), \(I=b.\mathrm{gen}[1]\). These data need no completeness claim from the class group computation. The following checks certify the needed conclusions.

1. From \(\mathtt{bnfisprincipal}(b,I^{25},1)\) take the proposed generator \(a\), convert it from integral-basis coordinates, and compare ideal Hermite normal forms. They give \((a)=I^{25}\) exactly. Every proposed unit has monic integral characteristic polynomial with constant coefficient \(\pm1\).
2. At the primes \(95801,574801\), use every one of the 20 distinct roots of \(P_1\bmod\ell\) to evaluate all 19 units and \(a\). Their coefficients have denominators invertible there and all evaluated elements are nonzero. Raise each residue to \((\ell-1)/5\) and take its logarithm in the order-five group generated by the same power of a primitive root. The two blocks of 20 rows give ranks 17 and then 20 cumulatively over \(\mathbb F_5\). Thus the units are independent modulo fifth powers in the full unit lattice. By Dirichlet's rank 19 their index is prime to five. The final independent column proves that \(a\) cannot be a fifth power times a global unit. If \(I^5=(z)\), then \(a/z^5\) would be a unit, a contradiction. Hence \(I\) has order exactly 25.
3. The plus field is the degree-ten real cyclotomic subfield at 25, with certified class number one by \(\mathtt{bnfcertify}(\mathtt{bnfinit}(\mathtt{polsubcyclo}(25,10),1))\). The anti-invariant upper bound above now implies that \(I\) generates the whole 5-primary class group. Its sole prime above 5 is trivial in that component. At the unramified rational prime 131 one has \(131\equiv6\bmod25\), \(\chi_{-479}(131)=1\), giving the tower generator over the quartic field. Select its Frobenius \(s\) from \(\mathtt{nfgaloisconj}(b)\) by reducing \(s(x)-x^{131}\) at a prime over 131. The prime is coprime to the power-basis index. A proposed principal generator for \(s(I)/I^{21}\) gives exactly that ideal on recomputing its Hermite normal form.

Norm to the quartic base is onto and its tame character is the one already detected at level zero. The tame action on the cyclic group of order 25 is its Teichmüller lift, since its order divides four. The source module therefore surjects onto this first-layer cyclic module, with \(\gamma\) acting by 21. At \(e\equiv11\bmod20\), \(6^{-e}=21\bmod25\), so the strict group contains a cyclic subgroup of order 25. Combining this with the earlier upper bounds proves the four exact orders claimed above. On \(e\equiv411\bmod500\) the possibilities are 25 and 125. The other weights \(e\equiv11\bmod100\) also retain the lower bound 25. The first-layer group is cyclic, so its reduction modulo five also sharpens the generator count to \(r=1\) on this branch. [Exact ideal-arithmetic and class group documentation](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html-stable/General_number_fields.html)

A second-layer certificate resolves this entire branch. In the degree-100 field use the first 49 signed logarithm rows at 8981251 (generator 3) and row zero at 26943751 (generator 6). Direct modular evaluation and fraction-free elimination give determinant **825 modulo 3125**, of valuation two. The anti-invariant split class group at level two therefore has order at most 25. Its norm onto the certified cyclic group of order 25 at level one is surjective by total ramification. Adjacent-level control now gives \(M=C_1\) on the active character, including the actual action of \(\gamma\) by 21 modulo 25. Consequently the full Case I column for conductor 479 is **zero for \(e\equiv1\bmod4\), cyclic of order 25 for \(e\equiv11\bmod20\), and cyclic of order five for \(e\equiv3,7,15,19\bmod20\)**. This resolves the earlier critical upper and lower alternatives by a second argument.

## Closing the conductor-3763 branch at three

For comparison with the unrestricted odd-prime arithmetic, the entire previously unresolved conductor-3763 column also stabilizes. This prime lies outside Case I since \(2\mid2e\). The real quadratic residual field is \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{11289})\). Use the real relative layers \(E_n=FQ_n\) and the quadratic involution over \(Q_n\). Choose the split primes \(3555222193,6814175869,10961935093\) and their respective primitive roots \(7,2,2\). In layer two take the first seven of the nine periodic rows at the first prime and row zero at each of the other two. The determinant is **15849 modulo 19683**, of 3-adic valuation three. In layer three take the first 25 of the 27 rows at the first prime and row zero at the other two. This gives **3510 modulo 19683**, again of valuation three. Both were reproduced by the finite-field products, discrete logarithms and exact determinant calculation described in the unit-index manuscript. The third-layer value provides an extra check. [Quadratic relative residue construction](AlpogeSelmerUnitIndex.md)

In the certified first layer, the cyclic 3-class group has order 27. Its quadratic involution acts by inversion, since it does so on the base quotient of order nine and a cyclic 3-group has only the two possible involutory scalar actions. The prime above three is unique, hence contributes no anti-invariant class on localization. Norm from the second layer is onto by total ramification, and its anti-invariant part has order at most 27 by the determinant. Thus \(C_2\to C_1\) is an isomorphism and full tower control gives \(M=C_1\). With the previously certified first-layer action \(\gamma=19=4^6\bmod27\), the full group for every positive odd \(e\) has order \(\gcd(27,19-4^{-e})\), computed using inverses modulo 27. It is **cyclic of order three for \(3\nmid e\), nine for \(e\equiv0,6\bmod9\), and twenty-seven for \(e\equiv3\bmod9\)**. In particular all formerly unresolved weights \(e\equiv21\bmod54\) have exact order 27. [Certified first-layer arithmetic and generator orientation](AlpogeSelmerTowerControl.md)
