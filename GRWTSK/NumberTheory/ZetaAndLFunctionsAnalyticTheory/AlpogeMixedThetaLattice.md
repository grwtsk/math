# Mixed Fourier coefficients and the primitive theta lattice

For a principally polarized Raynaud uniformization over a complete discrete valuation ring \(R\), the valuation of an ordinary theta section can be read in the abelian coefficient bundles, even when the extension by the torus is nonsplit as a group. Requiring integral sections after all normalized torsion translations gives a smaller lattice. Its elementary divisors are determined by the period form and the dimension of the abelian quotient. This gives a comparison with the primitive frame used to define the integral chart. All statements below concern an existing polarized uniformization and its compatible integral chart. The construction of such charts for an entire host is a further input.

## Valuing the actual coefficient bundles

Write \(K=\operatorname{Frac} R\), \(v(\pi)=1\), \(G/R\) for the smooth semiabelian model with abelian generic fibre \(A\), \(T\) for its split torus on the special fibre, \(X\) for the free character group of rank \(t\), and \(B/R\) for the abelian scheme in the Raynaud data. Put \(b=\dim B\); the relative dimension of \(G\) is \(b+t\). The principal polarization identifies the period lattice with \(X\). Its data include \(c:X\to B(R)\), a rigidified symmetric principal line bundle \(M\) on \(B\), and the generators \(\psi(\alpha)\) in the duals of \(c(\alpha)^*M\) on the generic point. Write \(e\) for the identity section of \(B\). Line bundles on the base in the following formula are pulled back to \(B\). For every \(k\ge1\), the actual four terms in the source coefficient bundle are
\[
 M_{k,\alpha}=M^{k-1}\otimes t_{c(\alpha)}^*M\otimes(c(\alpha)^*M)^{-1}\otimes e^*M.
\]
Thus the coefficient \(s_\alpha\) lives in a translate-twisted \(M^k\). This is the content with respect to the \(R\)-lattice \(H^0(B,M_{k,\alpha})\). Indeed \(B\) is regular, its geometric special fibre integral, and horizontal prime divisors see no poles of a generic global section. The criterion for regularity on a normal scheme in codimension one proves the assertion. The lattice is finite free by properness and torsion freeness. On the generic fibre the twist by \(\operatorname{Pic}^0\) is a translate of \(M^k\) after algebraic closure. Riemann–Roch on a principally polarized abelian variety gives rank \(k^b\) (higher coherent cohomology of an ample bundle vanishes). Flat extension of the base commutes with global sections. ## The period form and the ordinary lattice

Put \(Q(\alpha)=v(\psi(\alpha))\) in the dual integral line. The rigidification, symmetry and cube generators are units on the integral base. Taking valuations of the specified evenness and bimultiplicativity identities gives \(Q(-\alpha)=Q(\alpha)\) and the symmetric bilinear pairing \(B_Q(x,y)=Q(x+y)-Q(x)-Q(y)\). Indeed the defining four factors of \(\tau(x,y)\) have valuation \(B_Q(x,y)\), and its bimultiplicativity gives \(Q(0)=0\). Consequently \(B_Q(x,x)=2Q(x)\). The positivity condition of the degeneration data gives \(Q(x)>0\) for nonzero lattice vectors. The rational quadratic form is therefore positive definite over the reals: density gives positive semidefiniteness, and the kernel of a singular rational matrix has a nonzero rational vector. Under a period \(\lambda\), translation on \(B\) identifies the coefficient at \(\alpha\) with that at \(\alpha+k\lambda\), up to the comparison generator. Its restriction at zero is \(\psi(\lambda)^k\tau(\lambda,\alpha)\) times the \(k\)-th power of the integral rigidification. The isomorphism of bundles follows from the theorem of the square, since the degree-zero twist is multiplicative in the lattice index and translation by \(c(\lambda)\) shifts \(M^k\) by the \(k\)-th power of the twist at \(\lambda\), up to the constant line. Scale its unique rigidified isomorphism to the stated value at zero. The preceding divisor argument then gives
\[
 v_B(s_{\alpha+k\lambda})=v_B(s_\alpha)+kQ(\lambda)+B_Q(\lambda,\alpha).
\]
Hence \(d_u=v_B(s_\alpha)-Q(\alpha)/k\) depends only on \(u=\alpha\bmod kX\), including the value \(+\infty\). Set
\[
 m_u=\min_{\alpha\bmod kX=u}\frac{Q(\alpha)}k,\qquad
 W(s)=\min_{u\in X/kX}d_u.
\]
The minimum defining \(m_u\) exists by positive definiteness, \(m_u\ge0\), and \(m_0=0\). The source requirement NuGe at \(r\) is \(W(s)\ge r\). For the ordinary valuation in the given model one obtains
\[
 v_G(s)=\operatorname{ord}_{G_s}(s)=\min_\alpha v_B(s_\alpha)
                 =\min_u(d_u+m_u).
\]
Here is a direct check that the middle equality sees the primitive integral frame. Use the Fourier expansion in the completion of \(G\) along the special fibre. The map to the corresponding thickening of \(B\) is the split-torus torsor specified in the uniformization, with tautological generators of character weights and an invertible comparison from the model line to the pullback of \(M\). Over the generic point of \(B_s\) trivialize the torsor and the finitely many integral bundle frames that occur in the leading reduction. Quadratic growth makes the leading sum finite, and all nonzero leading terms have distinct Laurent characters. In the Laurent polynomial algebra over the function field they are linearly independent, in every residue characteristic. Frame changes multiply the summands by units. Thus the order of the sum is the least coefficient order. One can first clear a common denominator and read this argument on successive thickenings up to the first nonzero reduction. The source expansion holds there, and multiplying both sides by a common scalar shifts both orders equally. The middle equality also follows by evaluating the nonarchimedean theta function at the Gauss point over the origin of the skeleton. ## Torsion lifts, CM pullback and the chart germ

Work over a finite extension splitting the finitely many torsion lifts under consideration, keeping \(v(\pi)=1\). A lift \(g\) of a point in \(A[k]\) has \(kg=\iota(y)\) for \(y\in X\). Its image \(b_g\) on \(B\) extends by properness. For the trivialization \(g.s(\alpha)\) in the biextension line at \((b_g,c(\alpha))\), the multiplication law for lifts and the integral cube comparison give
\[
 k\, v(g.s(\alpha))=v(\tau(y,\alpha))=B_Q(y,\alpha).
\]
Indeed successive addition of \(g\) multiplies the \(k\) trivializations using the integral biextension isomorphism at the extended points, and the resulting period has the displayed \(\tau\) trivialization. The coefficient at index \(\alpha+y\) after translation arises from \(t_{b_g}^*s_\alpha\). Its comparison generator at zero is \(g.s(\alpha)\) times the fixed lifting generator \(w\), up to the rigidification. With the extra scalar \(\zeta\) for the normalized lift, the divisor comparison gives
\[
 d'_{u+\bar y}=d_u+C,\qquad C=v(w)+v(\zeta)-Q(y)/k .
\]
The valuation of \(w\) uses the integral line at \(b_g\). Taking minima yields \(W(U_gs)=W(s)+C\). For a finite-order normalized lift on the theta space, applying its positive order to any nonzero section forces \(C=0\). This uses equality of integer multiples in the rational value group and works also when the residue characteristic divides the order. ## Exact local elementary divisors

Over a splitting field for the torsion points the preceding identities imply
\[
 W(s)=\min_{T\in A[k]} v_G(U_Ts).
\]
In fact the inner minimum for a translation of component \(y\) is \(\min_u(d_u+m_{u+\bar y})\). Every \(\bar y\in X/kX\) occurs. Over the algebraic closure the semiabelian extension above \(B\) is divisible on points: take a \(k\)-th root on \(B\), lift it, and correct the discrepancy by a \(k\)-th root on the torus. Applying this to the period \(\iota(y)\) produces the required preimage; the finitely many points and lifts descend to a finite extension in characteristic zero. The least \(d_u\) is exposed by translating its index to zero. Consequently \(W\ge0\) defines the common integral lattice for all moves, and is itself stable under them. Scalar ambiguities in products of normalized lifts have valuation zero, as their induced shifts of \(W\) are zero. On \(K\) the same formula is interpreted by intersection with the generic section space over \(K\).

Choose a minimum representative \(\alpha_u\) in every residue class and set \(H_u=H^0(B,M_{k,\alpha_u})\). The map from global generic sections to their coefficients at these representatives is injective by the functional equation and uniqueness of expansion. Both sides have dimension \(k^{b+t}\): the global principal line in degree \(k\) has that dimension, and the \(k^t\) coefficient spaces each have dimension \(k^b\). It follows that the coefficients at the minimum representatives vary independently. The ordinary integral lattice becomes \(\bigoplus_u H_u\); inside it the moves-integral lattice becomes \(\bigoplus_u\pi^{\lceil m_u\rceil}H_u\). Thus the quotient over the original discrete valuation ring has elementary divisors and length
\[
 \bigoplus_{u\in X/kX}(R/\pi^{\lceil m_u\rceil}R)^{k^b},
 \qquad \ell_R=k^b\sum_{u\in X/kX}\lceil m_u\rceil .
\]
This expression is valid for any split polarized data as above over \(K\), even when the moves are tested after extension. If the input data are themselves changed to a ring of ramification index \(h\), its new normalized minima are \(h m_u\), and one must round **after** multiplying by \(h\). This local index measures a difference between two integral lattices in the same geometric fibre. A global Petersson covolume involves additional data. [Bijective uniformization in degree \(k\)](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/ReciprocityTheory/ReductiveGroups/ArithmeticGeometry/ExtensionDescent/DegExp.lean#L132-L145) · [Lan, section 4.3, polarized degeneration data](https://www.kwlan.org/articles/cpt-PEL-type-thesis-revision-dense.pdf)

For rank one with \(Q(n)=A_0 n^2\), the nearest representatives of an odd level \(k=2m+1\) are \(0,\pm1,\dots,\pm m\). Before rounding, \(\sum m_u=A_0(k^2-1)/12\); at even \(k\) the unpaired endpoint gives \(A_0(k^2+2)/12\). For \(Q(n)=n^2,\ k=3,\ b=1\), the minima are \(0,1/3,1/3\), giving six copies of \(R/\pi R\). In the coefficient block of class one a primitive ordinary section has \(W=-1/3\). Translating to the zero class displays the loss after ramified extension with \(v(\pi)=1\).

A distinct check uses the good elliptic curve \(B:y^2=x^3-x\) over \(\mathbb Z_5\) and the section \(P=(0,0)\). For \(M=\mathcal O(O)\) the twist \(t_P^*M/M\) is the nontrivial two-torsion class \(\mathcal O(P-O)\). Translation sends \(x\) to \(-1/x\) and \(y\) to \(y/x^2\), interchanging \(P\) and \(O\). The denominators here are horizontal, and the transformed section of the translated bundle has unchanged vertical content. This checks the coefficient-bundle issue separately from any claimed realization of a specified monodromy form. Conversely \(z^{q-1}-1\) has zero evaluations at all points of \(\mathbb F_q^\times\) but has primitive Laurent coefficients for \(q>2\). Evaluations at residue-field points cannot prove the valuation equality above.
