# Formal degree in the real-place Haar measure of the theta lift

grwtsk contributors

Let \(N=p+q\), \(M=N(N-1)/2\), and let \(m\) be a Haar measure satisfying the archived `Lev.IsMinf` on \(U(p,q)\). For a discrete series with regular Harish-Chandra parameter \(\lambda\), and any nonzero Hilbert vector \(w\), the real-place factor is
\[
 \frac{\|w\|^4}{\int_{U(p,q)} |\langle w,\pi(g)w\rangle|^2\,dm(g)}
   = \frac{\prod_{a<b}|\lambda_a-\lambda_b|}{2^N\pi^{N(N+1)/2}}.       \tag{1}
\]
The calculation below obtains the constant independently from the unweighted matrix ball and uses the classical Harish-Chandra discrete series classification. This is one local factor in the arithmetic argument. The holomorphic test representation used to fix the constant imposes no holomorphicity condition on the representation in (1).

## The two Haar measures

Write \(dX\) for ordinary Lebesgue measure in the imaginary diagonal entries and the complex upper off-diagonal entries of the Lie algebra. The lower entries are prescribed by the signs of the Hermitian form. In the complexification \(X+iY\), a diagonal pair has absolute real Jacobian one. For an off-diagonal pair the map sends \(z=a+ib,w=c+id\) to \(z+iw,s(\bar z+i\bar w)\), with \(s=\pm1\). Up to reordering real coordinates the two blocks send \((a,d)\mapsto(a-d,s(a+d))\) and \((b,c)\mapsto(b+c,s(c-b))\). Their absolute joint determinant is four. Thus the archive's additive Haar whose square pushes forward to standard complex matrix volume is \(2^M dX\). Since its prescribed group-to-algebra density is \(2^{-M}\), the left Haar \(m\) has raw density \(dX\) at the identity. The exponential has identity derivative there. Smoothness of Haar density and the shrinking norm balls justify computing the defining limit in this chart. For \(U(j)\to S^{2j-1}\), use the first-column map. At the identity its horizontal coordinates are the first imaginary diagonal and the \(j-1\) complex entries below it. Conjugation and signs preserve ordinary real volume. The fibre coordinates are exactly those of \(U(j-1)\). Invariance and this computation at one point give round sphere measure on the quotient when the fibre has raw Haar. Consequently the compact raw volume is

 V_N=\prod_{j=1}^N \frac{2\pi^j}{(j-1)!},\qquad V_0=1.

The sphere area follows by integrating the standard complex Gaussian radially. The compact probability Haar thus has density \(1/(2^M V_N)\) against the archive's additive Haar. The shared density in `CDHaar.trick` and uniqueness of multiplicative Haar scale imply \(m=V_N\,cd_{p,q}\) in every signature. ## Calibrating one discrete series

Suppose \(p,q>0\). On the ball \(\mathcal B=\{Z\in M_{p,q}(\mathbb C):I_q-Z^*Z>0\}\), write \(gZ=(aZ+b)(cZ+d)^{-1}\) and \(Y=gZ\). Differentiating gives \(X\mapsto(a-Yc)X(cZ+d)^{-1}\). The block determinant identity \(\det(a-Yc)\det(cZ+d)=\det g\) follows by block elimination on \(g\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&Z\\0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Since \(|\det g|=1\), the real Jacobian is \(|\det(cZ+d)|^{-2N}\). Preservation of the Hermitian form gives
\[
 I-Y^*Y=(cZ+d)^{-*}(I-Z^*Z)(cZ+d)^{-1}.
\]
The group acts transitively since the columns of \((Z;I)\) span a negative \(q\)-plane and an orthonormal frame extends to an indefinite unitary basis. The origin stabilizer is \(K=U(p)\times U(q)\). Its tangent block complement maps to \(B\in M_{p,q}\) with derivative the identity. Disintegrating \(m\) with Haar probability on \(K\) therefore gives the quotient density \(V_pV_q\det(I-Z^*Z)^{-N}dZ\).

Use unweighted square-integrable holomorphic functions on the ball, with action \(f(Z)\mapsto\det(c'Z+d')^{-N} f(g^{-1}Z)\), where the primed blocks belong to \(g^{-1}\). The Jacobian and the cocycle law prove unitarity and the representation law. Strong continuity follows first on polynomials by dominated convergence for \(g\) near the identity and then by density, obtained by the homogeneous expansion on this bounded circular domain. Rotation averaging realizes evaluation at zero as inner product against the constant, up to its norm. Consequently orthogonality to every translate of the constant forces a function to vanish at every point. The constant is cyclic, and its \(K\)-character line has dimension one because rotation separates homogeneous degrees. Every commuting orthogonal projection is therefore zero or the identity. This proves irreducibility.

For the normalized constant \(v\), the modulus squared of its coefficient is \(|\det d'|^{-2N}=\det(I-Z^*Z)^N\) with \(Z=g0\), since \(d'=d^*\). It cancels the density exactly. To calculate the remaining Euclidean volume, let \(J_{p,q}(a)=\int_{\mathcal B}\det(I-ZZ^*)^a dZ\) for integral \(a\ge0\). With the previous columns \(Y\) fixed, substitute the last column \(z=(I-YY^*)^{1/2}u\). The complex unit ball condition is \(\|u\|<1\), its Jacobian is \(\det(I-YY^*)\), and the determinant gains \(1-\|u\|^2\). The radial beta integral gives
\[
 J_{p,q}(a)=J_{p,q-1}(a+1)\,\pi^p a!/(p+a)!.
\]
Iterating from \(a=0\) yields \(J_{p,q}(0)=\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}\pi^p j!/(p+j)!\). Hence
\[
 \int_G |\langle v,\pi(g)v\rangle|^2dm=V_pV_q J_{p,q}(0)=V_N.
\]
The nonzero square-integrable coefficient puts this irreducible representation in the discrete series. For a compact signature the trivial representation gives the same calibration directly, including \(N=0\).

## Which parameter the archive specifies

Atiyah and Schmid's Theorem 8.1 gives the formal degree proportional to the product of absolute positive-root pairings, with the same Haar constant in all chambers, and parametrizes the discrete series up to the compact Weyl group. Theorem 8.5 gives the compact highest weight \(\Lambda=\lambda+\rho_n-\rho_c\) with multiplicity one. All other compact highest weights differ by a nonzero sum of positive roots in the chamber of \(\lambda\). These assertions apply to \(U(p,q)\) by the finite central covering \(U(1)\times SU(p,q)\to U(p,q)\) for \(N>0\). The circle acts by scalars on each irreducible. Any closed subspace stable under the semisimple factor is then stable under the whole covering group, and the compact central fibre contributes the same volume to every squared coefficient. Differences of coordinates remove a common central shift. Rank one has no roots and follows directly from its compact volume. [Atiyah and Schmid, *A geometric construction of the discrete series for semisimple Lie groups* (1977), pp. 41–42, Theorems 8.1 and 8.5](https://www.math.utah.edu/~ptrapa/math-library/schmid/atiyah-schmid.pdf)

The archive orders coordinates by decreasing parameter, fixes the compact order on equal signs, and minimizes \(\|\mu+2\rho_c\|^2\). For the classical representation this minimum is uniquely achieved at \(\Lambda\), since \(\Lambda+2\rho_c=\lambda+\rho\) pairs strictly positively with every positive root. Adding a nonzero positive-root sum increases the squared norm. Conversely, sorting the coordinates of \(\Lambda+2\rho_c\) recovers the chamber, since \(\lambda+\rho\) and \(\lambda\) are regular in the same chamber. Subtracting that chamber's \(\rho\) recovers the parameter. This proves the identification for interleaved signatures as well.
