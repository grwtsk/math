## The complete frame fibre

Put \(R=\mathcal O_E/I\) and \(D_I=D/ID\). A lift of a fixed lower marking on an abelian scheme amounts to choosing an \(R\)-linear frame of the \(I\)-annihilated summand of its \(p\)-torsion, with model \(D_I\). Indeed the upper marking has source
\[
 D/(\mathfrak b I)D=(D/\mathfrak bD)\oplus D_I .
\]
Raising the upper marking to the \(p\)th multiple recovers the lower one. The old primary component of the lift is therefore fixed, since multiplication by \(p\) on it is invertible. On the new half the condition is exactly that the marking be a frame. To check this last equivalence against the source's stronger full Tate-marking condition, start with a compatible Tate marking locally. At paired primes over \(p\), Cartier duality recovers the action on the opposite half as the inverse adjoint. Every change of frame lifts on the chosen half to integral invertible matrices over the local DVRs: lift entries in any basis; the determinant remains a unit. Use their inverse adjoints on conjugate factors, and the identity at all other primes. This constructs a compatible symplectic Tate marking for every frame without changing the old data. Conversely the full Tate isomorphism exhibits the upper marked half as an isomorphism. The divisor-one auxiliary frame imposes no condition. Over characteristic zero the torsion groups are finite étale, so this description works after étale localization and commutes with extension of the base.

There is no extra polarization, section or differential-frame choice over a fixed framed point. A polarized automorphism fixing the old half acts trivially on the full \(M\)-torsion by duality, since it respects the \(\mathcal O_E\)-action and \(\mathfrak b\) and \(\bar{\mathfrak b}\) are coprime. Its action on homology has finite order by positive definiteness of the Rosati involution on the endomorphism algebra. Congruence to the identity modulo an odd prime dividing \(M\) makes it the identity: for a matrix \(1+\ell^t B\) with \(t\ge1\) and \(B\not\equiv0\bmod\ell\), raising to \(\ell\) raises the exact depth by one, and raising to an exponent prime to \(\ell\) preserves the depth. The polarized rigidified bundle comparison is unique when the underlying line bundles agree, because a global unit on a proper geometrically connected abelian fibre is constant and rigidification fixes that constant. The source's condition identifying the theta bundle with the pullback of its polarization biextension along \((1,w_0)\) fixes even the possible symmetric two-torsion ambiguity: a line in \(\mathrm{Pic}^0\) has trivial associated biextension. Under forgetful pullback the given tangent eigenframes and theta section are then carried along uniquely.

For a presentation of the cover using the source map \(d.m\), its cartesian comparison identifies the underlying polarized abelian scheme and its \(\mathcal O_E\)-action; the two marking compatibility equations then identify the induced forgetful map by the fine moduli property. It follows that \(X_2\times_X X_2\cong X_2\times G\) for the actual forgetful cover, where \(G=\operatorname{GL}_R(D_I)\) acts by precomposition on the new frame. This is an isomorphism of sheaves: the unique element comparing two frames is their composite with the inverse of the first, defined locally and compatible on overlaps. In particular one may use this full fibre relation for descent. If \(I=\prod v^{h_v}\) and \(q_v=|\mathcal O_E/v|\), the degree is \(\prod_v q_v^{(h_v-1)r^2}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(q_v^r-q_v^j)\), by reduction of invertible matrices to the residue fields. ## The finite character and the sign in the Fourier transform

Use the source norm-one finite idèle character \(\xi\), trivial on rational norm-one elements and on norm-one integral idèles congruent to one modulo \(m\), nontrivial on the latter congruence subgroup at level \(M\). Its norm-one values have finite order and lie in \(K\). For \(t\in R^\times\), lift it to units on the chosen half above \(p\). Make a norm-one idèle \(j(t)\) by putting the inverse conjugate on the complementary half and one away from \(p\). Different lifts differ by a level-\(m\) congruence, so
\[
             \chi(t)=\xi(j(t)),\qquad \psi(g)=\chi(\det g)                 \tag{1}
\]
are well-defined characters over \(K\). Every norm-one idèle congruent to one modulo \(M\) is an integral unit and agrees modulo \(m\) with one such \(j(t)\); hence \(\chi\) is nontrivial. The determinant is onto, since \(D_I\) is free over a product of local rings and one may use a diagonal element. Formula (1) is independent of this choice of basis.

Write \(Y=D/\bar I D\) for the Fourier-dual half. The phase in the source's finite pairing is \(e(x,q)=\zeta_p^{\sum_j E_F(x_j,c_I q_j)}\), where the Chinese remainder scalar \(c_I\) is one modulo \(I\) and zero modulo \(\bar I\). The perfect Weil pairing on the two opposite halves makes it independent of representatives. The adjoint \(g^*\) on \(Y\) satisfies \(e(x,gq)=e(g^*x,q)\). Note that this is the adjoint of the frame change, whereas the *isometry* acts on the complementary half by its inverse adjoint. For \(n\ge r\), choose a basis tuple \(x_*\) in the first \(r\) slots of \(Y^n\). On these slots take the orbit of the ordered basis and put \(h(g^*x_*)=\psi(g)^{-1}\), zero on nonbases; ignore the last \(n-r\) slots. This is well-defined since the action on ordered bases is free, and the opposite multiplication order for adjoints has no effect on the character. It satisfies the support, normalization and character equations for the pinned finite test. Its values are algebraic integers.
\[
  \widehat h(q)=\sum_{x\in Y^n}h(x)e(x,q),
  \qquad \widehat h(gq)=\psi(g)\widehat h(q).
\]
The extra old-level factor \(\zeta_M^{\operatorname{pair}_M(x_0,q)}\) is unchanged, because the deck group fixes \(q\) modulo \(\mathfrak b\). For any integral or field-valued input transformed by the same character rule, the full weight \(w\) obeys \(w(g^{-1}q)=\psi(g)^{-1}w(q)\). For \(n<r\) the host does not require the pinned spanning test; the covariance calculation still holds for any specified weight satisfying that rule. [Actual finite transforms and the cover tie](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L187-L226) · [Class-weight equivariance](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/SpanningFamilies/DatEqZero.lean#L31-L45)

Let \(T_q(y)\) be the inner sum of normalized torsion translates evaluated at a framed point with upper marking \(y\), including the source harmonic polynomial. The normalized roots and translates are functorial and root choices do not change it by the [marked torsion calculation](AlpogeTorsionTrace.md). Consequently \(T_q(yg)=T_{gq}(y)\); this is an equality before taking jets, so it holds for every degree. The Fourier sum \(S(y)=\sum_q w(q)T_q(y)\) transforms by \(\psi(g)^{-1}\). All normalizing denominators involving \(N\), the lattice and the marking module are common to both sides. A cover factor transforming by \(\psi\) therefore cancels this multiplier exactly, with **no division by the degree of the cover**. ## The coefficient-field phase from the rational Hermitian space

On a geometric connected component with marked adelic representative \(h\) in the unitary group of \(V\), set
\[
                          c(h)=\xi(\det h_f).                              \tag{2}
\]
The actual uniformization uses rational changes \(\gamma\) carrying the completed reference lattice and its half marking to the period lattice. Equation (2) has the same value on \(\gamma h\), since \(\det\gamma\) is rational of norm one. On the right the upper stabilizer preserves the completed lattice and fixes the chosen half modulo \(m\). Its determinant is a unit at every finite place and is one modulo the chosen half. Being norm one, it is also one modulo the conjugate half. Thus the upper stabilizer is killed by \(\xi\). A right change of the new frame by \(g\) has determinant equal to \(\det g\) in \(R\); the same unit and congruence argument now gives the ratio \(\psi(g)\) in (1). To check the orientation, under precomposition the images of the basis vectors in the marking are given by the columns of \(g\), so the new adelic representative is \(h\tilde g\). This proves the deck ratio for (2) without assuming the source's phase tie. For the component indexing used here, the elementary double-coset description follows directly from the complex PEL lattice description: varying the compatible complex structure with fixed integral marked Hermitian lattice gives the connected domain \(\prod U(a,b)/(U(a)\times U(b))\). Changing the rational isometric identification of the lattice gives left multiplication and changing the integral basis preserving the marked level gives right multiplication. Descriptions in distinct rational isometry classes can be treated separately. In the selected rational genus the reference reading is normalized to one. On other genera one may choose a separate base lattice to define (2) by relative transport. These choices do not change the deck character. It is necessary to prove that this complex component function is defined over \(K\). Its values lying in \(K\) alone does not prove this: the two geometric points of \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q(i)\) over \(\mathbb Q\) cannot be assigned the separate rational values \(1,-1\) by a rational function. Here there is a direct test using the host's explicit marked CM elliptic curve condition at level \(m\). For every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/K)\) it gives a complex elliptic curve \(C\) with lattice homothetic to \(\mathcal O_k\) and a group isomorphism between its conjugate and itself respecting every labeled point of \(C[m]\). Over a coefficient field containing the ray class field \(k_{(m)}\) this condition also follows from the main theorem of complex multiplication: the idèle of \(\sigma\) is a principal idèle times an integral unit congruent to one modulo \(m\). Rescale the CM lattice by its principal factor; its induced level marking is the identity. The archive uses precisely this marked condition, which it obtains through Weber values, as an input in its field package. This argument retains that field hypothesis and makes no estimate of the ray field's discriminant or its contribution to (M). For a geometric component choose an \(E\)-rational subspace \(V_+\subset V\) of dimension \(a\) positive at all the distinguished real places, with negative orthogonal complement \(V_-\) of dimension \(b\). To construct it, choose an orthogonal-signature basis at each real place and approximate the first \(a\) vectors simultaneously by vectors of \(V(E)\) in the corresponding coordinates. Positive definiteness is open and these independent columns give the desired span. In the definite cases use the whole or zero subspace. Give these summands respectively the two complex structures induced by \(k\), with the sign making the Riemann form positive. This gives a CM point in the prescribed period domain. Its actual homology lattice \(L\) need not split as an orthogonal integral sum. Its intersections with the two rational summands have full total rank, so their direct sum has finite index. On each rational summand the complex torus with lattice in that summand is isogenous, as a complex group, to a power of \(C\): a \(k\)-basis identifies its lattice up to commensurability with a sum of copies of \(\mathcal O_k\), using conjugate coordinates on \(V_-\). Thus the abelian variety of the CM point admits a rational identification with a product of copies of \(C\), carrying its rational polarization to a positive Hermitian matrix over \(k\). This last assertion follows also from the product polarization and \(\operatorname{End}^0(C)=k\). The \(E\)-action is given by matrices over \(k\) in these product coordinates.

Conjugating the product and then identifying its factors by the isomorphism in the marked CM condition respects every rational matrix just described. Indeed the degree-one group isomorphism preserves the canonical elliptic polarization, and \(\sigma\) fixes \(k\); the eigenvalue on the tangent line determines an elliptic endomorphism in characteristic zero. The integral Tate module of \(C\) is free of rank one over \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_k\). The resulting transport on all its torsion therefore acts by a unit \(u\in\widehat{\mathcal O}_k^\times\), with \(u\equiv1\bmod m\) because the marking is fixed. Under the rational identification, the completed lattice of the conjugate point is carried from \(\widehat L\) by \(B=\operatorname{diag}(u\,1_{V_+},\bar u\,1_{V_-})\). One can check this even at primes dividing the degree of the chosen isogeny. Choose a split product sublattice of finite index in \(L\); the additional quotient points specify a subgroup of the torsion of that product. Its conjugate is exactly the image of this subgroup under \(B\), so the conjugate overlattice is \(B\widehat L\). The same computation on division representatives transports the marking. Reversing the orientation of the comparison replaces the transport by its inverse, with the same character computation.

The map \(B\) is a similitude of multiplier \(u\bar u\). Multiplication by the central integral unit \(u^{-1}\) preserves the transported lattice and acts trivially on its marking modulo \(m\). Consequently the conjugate marked class can be represented using the genuine unitary transport
\[
             U=u^{-1}B,\qquad \det_E U=(\bar u/u)^b.                       \tag{3}
\]
The arithmetic identification of the rational polarizations in the previous paragraph justifies using the same rational Hermitian space here. Equation (3) preserves its form because the summands are orthogonal. Although \(U\) need not stabilize the original lattice, its determinant is an integral norm-one unit congruent to one modulo \(m\) everywhere. Hence \(\xi(\det U)=1\). If the original component was represented by \(h\), the marked lattice of the conjugate point is represented by \(Uh\), up to the two harmless stabilizers already identified. The characteristic-zero PEL moduli space is smooth (the complex period domains modulo the torsion-free level groups are smooth), and its geometric connected components are open and closed. Galois permutes those components. Checking the value of (2) at the CM point in each one proves \(c(\sigma Z)=c(Z)\) for every component \(Z\). In particular every level set of the finite-valued function (2) descends: its characteristic idempotent is Galois invariant, and faithfully flat Galois descent gives the corresponding open-and-closed subscheme over \(K\). Multiplying the idempotents by the prescribed values in \(K\) gives the regular function \(c\) over \(K\). Independent phase construction on a different rational genus is legitimate because the polarized rational isogeny comparison preserves that genus as a rational space; if several local genera occur, the explicit isometry \(U\) preserves the local isometry type. This argument only needs invariance of the **value** under the transport (3). For example, for a split local Hermitian space in a basis on one half in which the positive projector has a denominator \(1/\ell\), the matrix \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&(v-1)/\ell\\0&v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), with \(v\) a unit unequal to one modulo \(\ell\), has unit determinant and fails to preserve the standard lattice. Its inverse adjoint defines the other half. Thus concluding that every component is fixed merely from determinant integrality would lose a genuine distinction.

## Integral effect and scope

Multiplying the marked section by the phase (2) yields an invariant for the entire fibre relation. Faithfully flat descent now gives the lower-level section on the interior in the coefficient field \(K\), for every input and harmonic differential for which the upper-level algebraic section has been constructed. The harmonic bundle can be tested on its frames because the descended correction has weight zero. On each relevant adelic component (2) realizes the archived determinant reading with the single root \(c_0=1\) for this chosen normalization. If a preassigned cover section is used, the deck ratio alone determines its quotient by (2) only up to a function on lower components. Matching a preassigned convention for the **whole** projected operator still requires its specified common root and the remaining local and archimedean comparisons.

The finite order of \(\xi\) on norm-one idèles gives \(c^t=1\) for some fixed positive integer. At every finite valuation of a component, \(c\) therefore has valuation zero, even when the residue characteristic divides \(t\) or \(|G|\). On a normal integral model a componentwise rational function of finite order and its inverse are both integral, and are regular units. For valuations checked after an extension of DVRs, the valuation of a scalar from the base is multiplied by the ramification index. Thus the lower reading inherits exactly the normalized valuation bound on the upper reading, including the chart loss from the [torsion trace](AlpogeTorsionTrace.md). The same observation applies to a determinant computed in a common local frame from these descended sections. It does not establish the missing divisibility of the individual upper readings on general semiabelian charts, the index of their image in the true cusp lattice, or the comparison of its covolume with the Petersson metric. The all-embedding class-sum, type-projection and smaller-field comparison also remain required for (M). [Archival smaller-field rational-span target](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/OneMapT.lean#L87-L112)

For the pinned spanning-family test there is also an exact archimedean check on its size. Over the finite product of local rings \(R\), any spanning ordered \(r\)-tuple in a free rank-\(r\) module is a basis, by the determinant criterion. Thus the normalization and covariance force the preceding choice of \(h\) on its support once the reference basis is specified. Put \(s=|Y|=|R|^r\). There are exactly \(|G|s^{n-r}\) tuples on which \(|h|=1\). Orthogonality of the perfect finite pairing gives
\[
    \sum_{q\in (D_I)^n}|\widehat h(q)|^2=s^n |G|s^{n-r}.
\]
Indeed the inner sum over \(q\) in the expanded square vanishes for unequal indices by translation by an element having nontrivial character ratio. Since \(h\) ignores the last \(n-r\) slots, \(\widehat h\) vanishes unless the last \(n-r\) entries of \(q\) vanish. Cauchy–Schwarz on this support gives the normalized bound
\[
               s^{-n}\sum_{q\in(D_I)^n}|\widehat h(q)|\le\sqrt{|G|}.         \tag{4}
\]
The old marking variables only repeat these absolute values, because their phase has modulus one. Dividing by the cardinality of the full marking module gives the same bound (4) for the actual weight. It is uniform in the column count and holds at every complex embedding.

If the determinant character on each local factor of \(R\) is primitive, the mean in (4) equals \(|G|/|R|^{r^2/2}\). After choosing dual bases, the first \(r\) Fourier variables form a square matrix \(Q\). Over one factor \(\mathcal O_v/v^h\), reduce a singular \(Q\) to Smith form. A diagonal unit acting on a nonunit row fixes it if that unit lies in \(1+v^{h-1}\) for \(h>1\); over the residue field use any unit on a zero row. Primitivity supplies one such unit with nontrivial character. Covariance forces the transform to vanish there. On invertible \(Q\) its absolute value is constant by transitivity. The square identity gives the absolute value \(s^{n-r}|R|^{r^2/2}\) on those \(|G|\) points and proves the stated mean. Formula (4) holds for the other possible source characters as well. Since \(I\bar I=(p)\), one has \(|R|=p^{[E:\mathbb Q]/2}\). Thus the logarithmic cost from (4) is at most \(r^2[E:\mathbb Q]\log(p)/4+\tfrac12\sum_{v\mid I}\sum_{j=1}^r\log(1-q_v^{-j})\). All summands in the correction are negative. This gives the marking-weight contribution to the archimedean triangle estimate; the norms of the geometric readings themselves still need to be bounded.
