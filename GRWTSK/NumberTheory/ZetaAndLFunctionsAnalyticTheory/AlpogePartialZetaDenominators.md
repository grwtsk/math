# Local norm bounds for the partial zeta denominators in the toric estimate

grwtsk contributors

Let \(F\) be any totally real field of degree \(d\), let \(k=2s\) with \(s\ge1\), and let \(z_C=\zeta_F(C,1-k)\) for an ordinary ideal class \(C\). For a rational prime \(\ell\) put \(g_\ell=\gcd_{w\mid\ell}e(F_w/\mathbb Q_\ell)\). In the archive's normalization we have
\[
 c_E(\ell)=\max_C\max\{0,-v_\ell(z_C/2^d)\}
       \ \le\ d\,v_\ell(2)+m_\ell(k g_\ell),\qquad
 m_\ell(n)=\min_{\substack{a\ge2\\(a,\ell)=1}} v_\ell(a^n-1).
\]
A zero rational value contributes zero. Since \(g_\ell\mid d\), this implies the stated **LocalBound**. In particular the smoothing exponent is \(k\) at every prime unramified in \(F\), even if its residue degrees are large. We also derive the complete **SumBound** with the original cutoff \(dk\ge16\). Every odd denominator prime exceeding \(k+1\) must therefore divide the field discriminant. Taking \(F\) to be the maximal real subfield of each CM field \(E\) yields the source's **ZetaDenominators**, without importing the archive's special-value theorem or any RH statement. The analytic inputs below are classical continuation, the published Shintani cocycle integrality theorem, class field theory and Chebotarev. [Original variables and quantifiers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/ZetaValueIdeals.lean#L13-L62)

## A principal smoothing prime with prescribed norm

At finite modulus one, the narrow ideal classes above \(C\) partition the integral ideals in \(C\). Summing their partial zeta functions gives the archive's Dirichlet series on \(\Re z>1\), with absolute convergence, and a continuation holomorphic off \(1\). Its value at \(1-k\) is uniquely determined by the identity theorem. Charollois, Dasgupta and Greenberg prove rationality of the narrow values and integrality of their smoothed differences. Concretely, if \(\mathfrak q\) is a prime ideal of prime norm \(q\), the difference of the partial value at the translated class and \(q^k\) times the original partial value lies in \(\mathbb Z[1/q]\). For a totally positive principal \(\mathfrak q\) the translate fixes **every** narrow class. It follows at once, after summation, that
\[
                 (1-q^k) z_C\in\mathbb Z[1/q]\quad\text{for every } C .
\]
This also recovers rationality directly since \(1-q^k\ne0\). We use the refined integral result, including at the coefficient prime 2: the preliminary cocycle argument alone permits an extra factor of \(2d\) in the denominator. [Charollois–Dasgupta–Greenberg, *Integral Eisenstein cocycles on GLn, II*, Theorems 1, 2 and 4.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0954v1) · [Charollois–Dasgupta, *Integral Eisenstein cocycles on GLn, I*, Theorem 1 and its refinement qualification following Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.3050v2)

Fix \(\ell\). Write \(U=\mathbb Z_\ell^\times\) and let \(\mathcal N_\ell=\prod_{w\mid\ell}N_{F_w/\mathbb Q_\ell}(\mathcal O_{F_w}^\times)\subseteq U\). For every \(b\in\mathcal N_\ell\), every \(h\ge1\), and every finite set of unwanted rational primes, there is a totally positive principal prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of degree one above an allowed \(q\ne\ell\) such that \(q\equiv b\bmod\ell^h\). To see this, choose a tuple of local units with product of norms \(b\). By the Chinese remainder theorem there exists \(\alpha\in\mathcal O_F\) agreeing with that tuple modulo \(\ell^h\mathcal O_{F_w}\) for each \(w\); adding a sufficiently large positive integer multiple of \(\ell^h\) makes \(\alpha\) totally positive. The determinant description of norm gives \(N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)\equiv b\bmod\ell^h\). Choose \(\mathfrak q\) in the narrow ray class of \((\alpha)\) of modulus \(\ell^h\mathcal O_F\) and all real places. The ray class field and Chebotarev give positive density for this class among prime ideals of \(F\). The prime ideals of absolute residue degree at least two have Dirichlet density zero, since the sum of their reciprocal norms converges. We may thus choose \(\mathfrak q\) of prime norm, avoiding the specified primes. Now \(\mathfrak q=(\alpha\gamma)\) with \(\gamma\) totally positive and congruent to 1 at all \(w\) modulo \(\ell^h\mathcal O_{F_w}\). Taking norms gives the required congruence with its positive sign. [Milne, *Class Field Theory*, v4.03, Ch. V §§1, 3 and Ch. VI](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf)

## The ramification indices replace the global degree

Let \(K_w\) be the maximal unramified subextension of \(F_w/\mathbb Q_\ell\). Every \(u\in U\) is the norm of a unit of \(K_w\). Indeed the norm of finite residue fields is surjective. Having matched a unit modulo \(\ell^j\), multiplying a lift by \(1+\ell^j t\) changes its norm modulo \(\ell^{j+1}\) by the factor \(1+\ell^j\operatorname{Tr}(\bar t)\). The residue trace is surjective for a finite separable extension of finite fields, including when \(\ell\) divides its degree. Completeness gives a unit of the desired norm. Viewed in \(F_w\), such an element has absolute norm \(u^{e_w}\), by transitivity. Bézout coefficients for the finite set of \(e_w\)'s and inversion of units now give \(U^{g_\ell}\subseteq\mathcal N_\ell\). The gcd need not itself be the ramification index of any place.

If \(\ell\nmid g_\ell\), this inclusion is an equality. Modulo \(\ell\), the norm of an integral unit at \(w\) is the residue-field norm raised to \(e_w\). This follows by filtering \(\mathcal O_{F_w}/\ell\) by powers of its maximal ideal: multiplication by the unit acts on each of the \(e_w\) residue-field quotients by the same scalar. Thus the product of reductions is in \((\mathbb F_\ell^\times)^{g_\ell}\). Raising to \(g_\ell\) is an automorphism of \(1+\ell\mathbb Z_\ell\) by Hensel's lemma. It follows that every element of the product of norm groups is itself a \(g_\ell\)-th power. In the wild case the inclusion suffices.

Choose \(a\ge2\) prime to \(\ell\) attaining \(r=m_\ell(k g_\ell)\), which exists by well ordering and \(a=\ell+1\) as a possible input. Apply the prime construction with \(b=a^{g_\ell}\) and \(h=r+1\). Then \(v_\ell(q^k-1)=r\) exactly. The integral smoothed values give \(v_\ell(z_C)\ge-r\) for every nonzero \(z_C\) simultaneously. Dividing by \(2^d\) proves the asserted bound. Since \(d=\sum_{w\mid\ell}e_w f_w\), \(g_\ell\mid d\); for \(n\mid n'\), the divisibility \(a^n-1\mid a^{n'}-1\) gives \(m_\ell(n)\le m_\ell(n')\). This establishes the original degree bound as well. In the tame case just discussed, \(r\) is the smallest smoothing valuation available from the local norms; the zeta values themselves may have smaller denominators.

## Closed formulas and the full logarithmic budget

For \(n>0\), \(h\ge1\), the inequality \(h\le m_\ell(n)\) holds exactly when the exponent of \((\mathbb Z/\ell^h)^\times\) divides \(n\). Indeed every unit has an integer representative at least 2 prime to \(\ell\), and the condition is that each representative satisfies \(a^n=1\bmod\ell^h\). For odd \(\ell\) this group is cyclic of order \((\ell-1)\ell^{h-1}\). At \(2\) its exponent is \(2^{h-2}\) for \(h\ge3\), as the units have decomposition \(C_2\times C_{2^{h-2}}\). At \(h=1,2\) the exponents are 1 and 2. Since the original \(n\) is positive even, this gives
\[
 m_\ell(n)=
 \begin{cases}
  0 & \ell>2,\ \ell-1\nmid n,\\
  1+v_\ell(n)& \ell>2,\ \ell-1\mid n,\\
  2+v_2(n)& \ell=2 .
 \end{cases}
\]
These are the three minimum equalities required by **LocalBound**, including the dyadic offset. The proof uses finite unit-group exponents in place of the archive's minimum-valuation calculation. [Mathlib Carmichael prime-power formulas](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ArithmeticFunction/Carmichael.lean#L123-L166)

For \(n=dk\), the odd exceptional primes form \(P(n)=\{\ell>2:\ell-1\mid n\}\). Hence support is finite and exponentiating the entire original log sum gives
\[
 \prod_\ell\ell^{c_E(\ell)}
 \ \le\ 2^{d+2} n \prod_{\ell\in P(n)}\ell .
\]
Here \(n\) bounds the product of the prime powers coming from \(v_\ell(n)\), including \(v_2(n)\). There are at most \(n/2\) odd integers from \(3\) through \(n+1\), so \(\sum_{\ell\in P(n)}\log\ell\le(n/2)\log(n+1)\). For \(n\ge16\) we have \(d+2\le n\) and \(4\log 2\le\log(n+1)\), giving \((d+2)\log2\le(n/4)\log(n+1)\); also \(\log n\le(n/4)\log(n+1)\). Summing yields the required \(n\log(n+1)\). This accounts for the finite-support and real-logarithm clauses with their stated threshold. The more precise local bound can be inserted before taking this coarse sum.

## Arithmetic checks and the toric consumer

For \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), \(k=2\), the ordinary class group is trivial and \(\zeta_F(-1)=1/12\). Here \(5\) is inert and unramified. The degree-only formula allows a factor of 5 because \(4\mid dk\), whereas the local norm bound proves integrality at 5. The totally positive \(3+\sqrt2\) has prime norm 7, and \((1-7^2)\zeta_F(-1)=-4\). For \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), the same degree and weight give \(\zeta_F(-1)=1/30\) and \(g_5=2\); the allowed factor of 5 actually occurs. The totally positive \((7+\sqrt5)/2\) has prime norm 11 and \((1-11^2)\zeta_F(-1)=-4\). Both class groups are trivial by Minkowski's bound. The values follow from \(\zeta_F(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_D)\) and \(L(-1,\chi_D)=-\frac D2\sum_{a=1}^D\chi_D(a)B_2(a/D)\) for \(D=8,5\), respectively. These computations distinguish residue degree from ramification index.

At the pinned source's three-input toric denominator theorem, only existence and identification of the rational \(z_C\) are used directly from **ZetaDenominators**. Its two lattice hypotheses separately supply the bound for each normalized CM lattice sum. The numerical size of \(c_E\) enters the subsequent aggregate budget, and its precise normalization also occurs with coefficient 12 in **betaBound_vp**. The source's special-value route removes Euler factors over \(\ell\) from the Deligne–Ribet congruences and uses translates by positive rational integers of norm \(a^d\). The argument here starts with undepleted classes, constructs a principal smoothing prime, and uses the norms of arbitrary local units. It does not establish **LatIntRam**, **LatIntUnr**, independence of the CM periods, the Selmer estimates or (M).
