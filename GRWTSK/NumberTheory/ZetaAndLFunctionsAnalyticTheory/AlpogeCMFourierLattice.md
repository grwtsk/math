# The CM lattice and its Hilbert Eisenstein jets at all degrees

grwtsk contributors

Assume the full **AllLat** data. Thus \(E/\mathbb Q\) is abelian Galois and CM, its different over \(E^+\) is the unit ideal, \(2\mid\operatorname{disc}E\), and \(d>4\) is prime. The CM type \((\phi_i)_{i\in I}\) has at least two members, has product one on every finite-order element of \(E^\times\), and sends one common square root of \(-d\) to \(i\sqrt d\) at every member. Take any \(s\ge1\) and prescribed rational partial-zeta values \(z(C)\) at \(1-2s\). The nonzero proper base conductor has exponents at most two at split places over odd rational primes unramified and completely split in \(E\). The elliptic period pair spans precisely the integral elements of the embedded quadratic field. At each prime \(\ell\), let \(Q\) be any finite set of split \(F\)-places prime to the conductor and away from \(\ell\), and let the arbitrary finite idele \(y\) have components one at every place above \(\ell\). All valuation embeddings and all permissible period roots are allowed. Write \(F=E^+\), \(g=[F:\mathbb Q]\ge2\), \(k=2s\ge2\), \(h=\sum_i\kappa_i\), \(w=kg+2h\), and \(e_Z=\max_C(0,-v_\ell(z(C)/2^g))\), omitting zero values in the maximum. Valuations on algebraic numbers use the specified embedding and \(v_\ell(\ell)=1\). Put \(D=|\operatorname{disc}\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})|\), \(m=v_\ell(D)\), \(\delta=v_\ell(\operatorname{disc}F)/g\), and \(t=v_\ell(2\operatorname{Im}\tau/\sqrt d)\), where \(\tau=(-1+\sqrt{-d})/2\) for \(d\equiv3\bmod4\), and \(\tau=\sqrt{-d}\) otherwise. Thus \(m/2-t=\frac12{\bf1}_{\ell=d}\). Write \(a_v=v_{\mathfrak p_v}(\mathfrak f)\), \(e_v=e(v/\ell)\), \(f_v=f(v/\ell)\), and \(v(i)\) for the prime of \(F\) induced by the chosen \(\ell\)-adic embedding composed with \(i:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb R\). Define
\[
 B_\ell=e_Z+s\,v_\ell(\operatorname{disc}F)
       +k\sum_{v\mid\ell} f_v(a_v-e_vm/2)_+
       +\sum_i\kappa_i\big(\delta+|a_{v(i)}/e_{v(i)}-m/2|-t\big).
\]
For the actual lattice \(\Lambda_E=\mathrm{lattice}(\mathrm{condQ}(\mathfrak f,Q),y)\), the normalized lattice sum satisfies
\[
 v_\ell\big(\mathrm{beta}(E,d,s,\kappa,\Lambda,S(0))\big)\ \ge\ -12 B_\ell .       \tag{1}
\]
More precisely **JetInt** holds with the same periods \(a_i=\Omega,\ b_i=2\pi/\Omega\) at every \(i\) and \(\ell\), for any \(\Omega^{12}=\mathrm{latDisc}(\Lambda)\). The jet and (1) include algebraicity. We use the classical functional equation for the partial zeta functions of the totally real field and the integrality of singular moduli. These inputs do not assert zero-freeness in the critical strip. The proof below concerns the lattice sum with \(\mathrm{OneOver}(\ell,y)\); transport to the general class term with its character and modulus factors is a further step. ## Two elementary divisors at every allowed prime

Write \(\theta\in E\) for the element sent to \(\tau\) by every \(\phi_i\). Then \(E=F(\theta)\). Locally let \(A=\mathcal O_{F_v}\), \(B=\mathcal O_E\otimes_{\mathcal O_F} A\). This quadratic algebra over the DVR \(A\) is finite étale, including the split case, by the relative different hypothesis. The completion of \(\Lambda_E\) here is exactly \(A+\mathfrak p_v^{a_v}B\). Indeed the order generated by \(\mathcal O_F\) and \(\mathfrak c\mathcal O_E\) is their additive sum, and this full lattice has finite-adelic closure equal to the product of its completions by the Chinese remainder theorem. Multiplication by the finite idele and intersection with \(E\) recover the corresponding fractional lattice with those completions, by clearing denominators and the same theorem. The factors of \(Q\) are prime to \(\ell\) and \(y=1\) at *all* places above \(\ell\). [Order and adelic lattice](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/EntireEqOfHalfPlane.lean#L65-L76) · [Different and conductor support](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/IsFinSetSplitPlacesCoprimeConductor.lean#L15-L33)

Choose an \(A\)-basis \(1,\eta\) of \(B\); \(B/A\) is torsion-free since \(F_v\cap B=A\) along the scalar embedding. Write \(\theta=t_v+u_v\eta\). The discriminant of \((1,\theta)\) is \(-D\), and the étale basis has unit discriminant, so \(v_{\mathfrak p_v}(u_v)=e_vm/2\). Choose a positive integer \(M\) prime to \(\ell\) clearing the away-from-\(\ell\) denominators of the following full coordinate lattice. Set \(n=MD\) and
\[
 L=\{(r,q)\in F^2:r\theta+q\in n\Lambda_E\}.
\]
Writing the two coefficients in \(1,\eta\) shows that at \(v\)
\[
 r\in\mathfrak p_v^{e_vm/2+a_v},\qquad q+t_v r\in\mathfrak p_v^{e_vm}.
\]
In particular the lattice is integral there, so \(M\) can indeed be chosen with \(L\subseteq\mathcal O_F^2\) everywhere. It contains \(N\mathcal O_F^2\) for some positive integer \(N\), which may be enlarged to include an auxiliary prime-to-\(\ell\) torsion order \(M_0>1\). Its exact elementary exponents \(\alpha_v\le\beta_v\) are the sorted pair \(\{e_vm/2+a_v,e_vm\}\). This derives the inclusion on the specified source lattice before any passage to cusp coordinates. [Source scaled-coordinate construction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/MemPassScaled.lean#L24-L48)

At every integral modular transform \(L'\) of \(L\), let \(\mathfrak b\) be its first-coordinate projection and \(V\) its vertical ideal. Locally their exponents \(J_v,K_v\) satisfy \(\alpha_v\le J_v\le\beta_v\), \(K_v+J_v=\alpha_v+\beta_v\). Projection of the elementary-divisor inclusions gives the first inequalities, and the determinant gives the equality. The [Lean proof](AlpogeCMFourierLattice.lean) computes the vertical generator directly by Bézout elimination. Write \(\mathfrak a=\mathfrak D_F^{-1}V^{-1}\), with \(\mathfrak D_F\) the different of \(F/\mathbb Q\). The cusp frequencies \(\xi\in\mathfrak a\mathfrak b\) then satisfy
\[
 v_\ell(i\xi)\ge-\delta-(\beta_{v(i)}-\alpha_{v(i)})/e_{v(i)} .
\]
Indeed the different exponent divided by \(e_v\) is \(\delta\) since \(F/\mathbb Q\) is Galois. Also the cusp factor \(\omega_{\mathfrak a}^k=(|\operatorname{disc} F|^{1/2}\operatorname N\mathfrak a)^k\) has valuation \(-s\,v_\ell(\operatorname{disc} F)-k\sum_v f_v K_v\). These are exact local computations before taking the worst cusp, in contrast to a single rational level bound.

## Normalized Fourier expansion, including weight two

Here and below set \(T=2\pi i\). Split the projection \(L'\to\mathfrak b\) as an \(\mathcal O_F\)-module map by projectivity of the fractional ideal \(\mathfrak b\). Its second coordinate is multiplication by \(\lambda\in F\). Thus the rows are \((b,b\lambda+c)\), \(b\in\mathfrak b,\ c\in V\). Take the source sum of \(\prod_i(i(b)z_i+i(b\lambda+c))^{-k}\) over nonzero rows modulo simultaneous units. Its Fourier expansion after multiplication by
\[
       c_K/(T^{kg}\omega_{\mathfrak a}^k),\qquad
       c_K=((k-1)!)^g|\operatorname{disc}F|^{(k-1)/2},
\]
has constant \(2^{-g}\zeta_{[\mathfrak a]^{-1}}(1-k)\). Its coefficient of the totally positive \(\xi\) is \(e^{2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda\xi)}\) times the sum of \((|\operatorname N\alpha|/\operatorname N\mathfrak a)^{k-1}\) over \(\alpha\in\mathfrak a,\ b\in\mathfrak b,\ \alpha b=\xi\) modulo reciprocal units. Each summand is an integer power of an integral ideal norm. The sum is finite since \((\alpha)\mathfrak a^{-1}\) divides \((\xi)(\mathfrak a\mathfrak b)^{-1}\). The phases are roots of unity. [Archive normalization and explicit summands](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/OmegaFactorization.lean#L85-L110) · [Deligne–Ribet, Hilbert Eisenstein expansions, §6](https://math.berkeley.edu/~ribet/Articles/deligne-ribet.pdf)

For \(k>2\) first sum over \(c\) by Poisson summation on \(V\) for each nonzero \(b\) modulo units. The dual lattice is \(\mathfrak a\), and the inverse covolume is \( |\operatorname{disc} F|^{1/2}\operatorname N\mathfrak a\). In one real coordinate the transform at \(\alpha_i\ne0\) is zero unless \(\alpha_i b_i>0\); closing the contour gives \(T^k|\alpha_i|^{k-1}/(k-1)!\) times the exponential with frequency \(\alpha_i b_i\), since \(k\) is even. The frequency zero integral vanishes by a primitive. Multiplication gives exactly the coefficient above. The rows with \(b=0\) give \(\operatorname N(V)^{-k}\zeta_{[\mathfrak D_F\mathfrak a]}(k)\). The usual partial-zeta functional equation sends the class \(C\) to \([\mathfrak D_F]C^{-1}\). Its real gamma-factor ratio \(\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(1-k)/\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(k)=T^k/(2(k-1)!)\) gives the displayed constant. Absolute convergence for \(k>2\) follows by choosing unit representatives with \(|i b|\asymp |\operatorname N b|^{1/g}\) by the unit theorem. The sum of absolute values over \(c\) is bounded for large \(b\) by \(O(|\operatorname N b|^{1-k})\) by comparison with boxes in the full lattice \(V\); summation over the balanced representatives converges for \(k>2\). The same bounds are uniform on compact subsets of the product of upper half-planes.

For \(k=2\) initially multiply each summand by \(\prod_i |i(b)z_i+i(b\lambda+c)|^{-2\sigma}\) at \(\Re\sigma>0\). In a coordinate with nonzero imaginary displacement \(b_i\operatorname{Im}z_i\), scaling and translating the zero-frequency integral yields a constant
\[
 J(\sigma)=\int_{\mathbb R}(u+i)^{-2}(u^2+1)^{-\sigma}\,du
\]
times \(|b_i\operatorname{Im}z_i|^{-1-2\sigma}\); reflection \(u\mapsto-u\) gives the same constant for either sign. \(J\) is holomorphic near zero and \(J(0)=0\). Summing over nonzero \(b\) modulo units contributes an ideal partial zeta at \(1+2\sigma\), times an entire scale, with at most a simple pole. The product \(J(\sigma)^g\) therefore kills this term at zero for \(g\ge2\). The \(b=0\) part is regular there.

For the nonzero frequencies the \(\sigma\)-continuation and the deformation derivatives can be taken normally. Here are details of the uniform bound. Keep \(z\) in a small compact neighborhood with imaginary parts bounded below. In each Poisson integral shift the real line by the sign that damps the Fourier exponential, through a fixed small fraction of \(|b_i|\). Both linear factors from the squared norm have zeros outside this strip, so use analytic logarithms there continuous from the original real-line product. The absolute integrand without the exponential is bounded, after real translation and division of the variable by \(|b_i|\), by \(C |b_i|^{-2-2\Re\sigma}(1+|u|)^{-2-2\Re\sigma}\), uniformly for \(\sigma\) sufficiently close to zero. This gives an exponential \(e^{-c\sum_i|\alpha_i b_i|}\) times a polynomial in \(|\operatorname N b|+1\). The balanced \(b\)'s of bounded size form a finite set and their number in a ball grows polynomially. The dual lattice has a positive minimum in Euclidean norm. Hence the double sum over the nonzero dual lattice and balanced \(b\) converges normally. The same argument allows independent small complex variations of \(z_i\) and the initially conjugate variables: the two poles remain on opposite sides of the contour. In the zero mode the analytic extension in those variables is the continuation of the explicit imaginary-part factor times \(J(\sigma)^g\), by uniqueness from the real slice. Differentiating a smooth family of these compact parameter estimates is justified on smaller neighborhoods by Cauchy estimates. At \(\sigma=0\) this gives the same holomorphic Fourier series as above. This local continuation suffices for every source \(g,S\) satisfying the defining relations by uniqueness; occurrence of the continued value at zero is the only continuation property used here. [Weight-two source continuation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/HeckeWeightTwoContinuationOfFunctionalEquation.lean) · [Source partial-zeta equation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/PublishedTheorems/Divisors₂/PartialZetaFunctionalEquation.lean)

## Holomorphic jets at every cusp and evaluation on the CM period

Take a nonzero division point of order \(M_0\) prime to \(\ell\), with \(M_0\mid N\), and use the source correction \(C=(G_2+\wp_P)/T^2\) in unscaled elliptic coordinates. Its Fourier coefficients at every transformed marked point are integral at \(\ell\). Indeed the Tate expansion of \(G_2+\wp_P\), divided by \(T^2\), is \(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z} u q^j/(1-uq^j)^2\), where \(u=\zeta q^{a/N}\). Expand using \(x/(1-x)^2=\sum_{n\ge1}n x^n\) and invariance under \(x\mapsto x^{-1}\). A constant \(\zeta/(1-\zeta)^2\) occurs only when the fractional exponent is zero and \(\zeta\ne1\) has prime-to-\(\ell\) order, so \(1-\zeta\) is a unit. The formula follows by summing the partial fractions of the lattice \(\wp\) function one period direction at a time; its subtracted constant is the holomorphic \(G_2\). [Exact torsion correction and convention](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/ZetaActRefl.lean#L31-L46)

For the diagonal elliptic argument \(z\), define the holomorphic jet \(H_\mu\) as in **hfun**: in each variable differentiate \(\mu_i\) times along the displacement of the column \((z,1)\) by \(u_i\,[T^2C(z)(z,1)-T(1,0)]\), multiply the lattice sum by \(c_K\) and divide by \(T^{kg+2|\mu|}\). These form a finite permuted family of scalar modular forms of weight \(kg+2|\mu|\) as the lattice and marked point vary in their integral modular orbit. Indeed \(\gamma(z,1)=j(\gamma z,1)\) with \(j=c z+d'\) for bottom row \((c,d')\); the corrected function at the corresponding mark transforms as \(C(\gamma z)=j^2 C(z)-cj/T\). This follows from the weight-two \(\wp\) law and the quasi-period law for \(G_2\) (equivalently modularity of \(G_2-\pi/\operatorname{Im}z\)). Thus the image of the displacement vector is \(j^{-1}\) times the new displacement vector. Homogeneity of the kernel at each embedding yields the indicated weight. Finiteness follows from fixing \(N\mathcal O_F^2\subseteq L\subseteq\mathcal O_F^2\) and \(N P=0\).

At the cusp a frequency \(\xi\) contributes products of one-variable polynomials in \(C\) and \(i\xi\), homogeneous of order \(\mu_i\), with integer coefficients. Indeed in the deformed Fourier term the factor in each variable has shape \((1+c'u)^{-k}\exp(Au/(1+c'u))\) after removing the constant exponential. Its \(n\)-th derivative at zero has coefficient \((-1)^{n-j}\binom nj(k+j)_{n-j}\) on \((c')^{n-j}A^j\). This follows by expanding the exponential first and differentiating the remaining binomial series. Upon dividing by \(T^{2n}\) the two inputs are \(C\) and \(-i(\xi)\), where \(i\) here denotes the real embedding. At the constant frequency only the \(C^n\) term remains. The normal convergence of the positive-frequency series permits differentiation, and finitely many totally positive frequencies occur at fixed trace since the Fourier lattice is discrete. Roots of unity from translations are integral. Therefore every coefficient at every cusp of \(H_\mu\) has denominator exponent at most
\[
 U_\mu=e_Z+s v_\ell(\operatorname{disc}F)+k\sum_v f_v\beta_v+
             \sum_i\mu_i r_i,\qquad
 r_i=\delta+|a_{v(i)}/e_{v(i)}-m/2|.
\]
This proof keeps the original embedding label also for a modular transform; the integral \(2\)-by-\(2\) matrix acts on the row coordinates only. [Exact jets and integer coefficients](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/HasLevelStd.lean#L59-L90)

These cusp bounds hold for \(H_\mu(\tau)/\omega^{kg+2|\mu|}\), where \(\omega=\Omega/T\). To prove this, multiply the entire finite orbit by an algebraic scalar of valuation \(U_\mu\), possible since the bound is rational. For each resulting form \(h_j\) of weight \(w'\) take \(h_j^{12}/\Delta^{w'}\). The monic polynomial having these as roots has invariant weight-zero coefficients, holomorphic except at the cusp. They are polynomials in the modular invariant \(j\) since \(X(1)\) has coordinate \(j\) with a simple pole there. Their Fourier coefficients are in the valuation ring: the normalized \(\Delta=q\prod(1-q^n)^{24}\) has invertible leading coefficient. Comparing leading Laurent terms successively with \(j=q^{-1}+744+\cdots\) puts all the polynomial coefficients in that ring. The singular modulus \(j(\tau)\) is integral, hence the specialized roots are algebraic and integral there; \(\omega^{12}=\Delta(\tau)\) identifies the normalized value. For a zero component or zero specialized value interpret the valuation as \(+\infty\). This argument uses closure under the finite modular orbit and the *same* valuation in every Fourier series. It requires no Galois stability of the constituent modular form. [Archive finite-family evaluation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/SzValueOfFamily.lean#L17-L39)

At the CM point the difference between the true conjugate direction, rescaled by \(v_0=\pi/\operatorname{Im}\tau\), and the marked holomorphic direction is \((v_0-T^2C)(\tau,1)\), since \(v_0(\bar\tau-\tau)=-T\). Adding a scalar multiple of the base column to a direction acts on the \(n\)-th derivative of a function homogeneous of degree \(-k\) by the same binomial law above: factor \(1+u c'\) from the column and replace the remaining parameter by \(u/(1+u c')\). After division by \(T^2\omega^2\) the extra scalar is \(-X(P)=-(s_2+\wp_P)/\Omega^2\), using \(s_2=G_2(\tau)-\pi/\operatorname{Im}\tau\) for the period pair \(1,\tau\). The underlying lattice is \(\mathcal O_K\) by **IsIntLattice**, irrespective of the supplied basis.

For completeness \(v_\ell(X(P))\ge-r_i\) at every embedding in this argument. At primes outside \(2d\) the bound \(v_\ell(X)\ge0\) suffices. This follows from the good-reduction coordinate and isogeny trace in the [dyadic CM calculation](AlpogeDyadicCMTrace.md), which apply also to prime-to-\(\ell\) torsion at odd primes using a good model after finite extension. More explicitly \(Y=\wp/\Omega^2\) has \(12Y\) integral and \(Y(P)-Y(Q)\) integral for nonzero prime-to-\(\ell\) torsion. For \(\alpha\) of norm \(n\) prime to \(\ell\), \((\alpha^2-n)X-(\alpha^2-1)Y\) is integral by the trace formula. For \(\ell\nmid 6d\) take \(\alpha=\sqrt{-d}\). At \(\ell=3\), if \(3\mid d+1\) the same trace works; otherwise take \(1\pm\sqrt{-d}\), whose conjugate traces eliminate \(Y\) with coefficient \(4d\sqrt{-d}\) a unit. At \(\ell=d\), take \(1+\sqrt{-d}\), where the coefficient of \(X\) has valuation \(1/2\) and \(Y\) is integral. At 2 the maximal-order calculation proves integrality for \(d\equiv3\bmod4\). For \(d\equiv1\bmod4\) use the trace for \(\sqrt{-d}\), \(4Y\) integral, and \(2\mid d+1\), giving \(v_2(X)\ge-2\). The tower discriminant formula gives \(\delta=\delta_\ell(E)\ge m/2\), using the relative unit different and \(K\subset E\). If \(m>0\) the conductor is prime to \(\ell\), so \(r_i=\delta+m/2\), large enough for both ramified bounds. This explains the need for the improved dyadic calculation when \(m=0\). [Archimedean-to-CM correction](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ImportsRestLast.lean#L23-L38)

Let \(J_\kappa\) be the jet in direction \((\bar\tau,1)\), with the \(c_K\) prefactor, multiplied by \(v_0^h/(T\omega)^w\). It is a sum over \(\mu\le\kappa\) of \(H_\mu(\tau)/\omega^{kg+2|\mu|}\) times \((-X(P))^{h-|\mu|}\) and a product of integer binomial coefficients and rising factorials. Each summand has valuation at least \(-U_\kappa\), since \(v_\ell(X(P))\ge-r_i\) in each of the missing factors. This establishes the estimate through every derivative order using a single marked point at this prime.

## Return to the undivided derivatives and original beta

Writing \(n x=r\theta+q\) identifies \(\phi_i(nx)+u_i\overline{\phi_i(nx)}\) with \(i(r)(\tau+u_i\bar\tau)+i(q)(1+u_i)\). Thus
\[
 \frac{(2\pi/\sqrt d)^h\,\operatorname{iterD}(u,g_0)(0)}{\Omega^w}
       = n^{kg}(2\operatorname{Im}\tau/\sqrt d)^h J_\kappa ,
\]
where \(g_0\) is any source **IsDefSum** and \(u\) has counts \(\kappa\). At \(k>2\) this follows termwise by absolute locally uniform convergence. At \(k=2\) use the same deformation before taking the continuation at \(\sigma=0\): the positive scalar norm powers introduced by \(n\) and the variable column scalings are analytic and equal to one at zero. The continued sum is finite there, also after deformation, as proved above. In the half-plane of convergence the holomorphic Wirtinger derivative with the conjugate deformation held fixed produces \((-1)^h\prod_i(k+\sigma)_{\kappa_i}\) times the source sum **IsLatSum** at \(\sigma\), with the prefactor \(c_K\). The local uniform continuation permits this differentiation before specializing, giving \((-1)^h c_K\prod_i(k)_{\kappa_i}S(0)\) for the actual derivative. This handles repeated and mixed derivatives in their specified order. Since \((k-1)!(k)_j=\Gamma(k+j)\), the twelfth power of the left side is precisely **beta**, with \(\mathrm{latDisc}(\Lambda)=\Omega^{12}\); its sign disappears. Subtracting \(kgm+ht\) from \(U_\kappa\) gives \(B_\ell\) because \(\sum_v e_vf_v=g\) and \(\beta_v-e_vm=(a_v-e_vm/2)_+\). This proves (1). [Defining deformation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/DefiningCoordinates.lean#L12-L47) · [Gamma and period constant](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/PairingDivisors/StatementH.lean#L46-L51) · [Original beta](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Divisors/EntireEqOfHalfPlane.lean#L93-L112)

For \(a_i=\Omega,\ b_i=2\pi/\Omega\), the undivided derivative in **JetInt** equals the left side above times \((\sqrt d)^h\). Its period tests reduce to \(a_i/\Omega=1\) and \((a_i b_i\sqrt d/(2\pi))^{-1}=1/\sqrt d\). On the conductor support all places above \(\ell\) are completely split, \(\ell\nmid 2D\operatorname{disc}E\), and \(c_\mathrm{vp}=A=\max a_v\). Thus \(B_\ell=e_Z+k\sum_v a_v+\sum_i\kappa_i a_{v(i)}\le e_Z+kgA+hA\). Away from that support \(c_\mathrm{vp}=\delta\), and \(B_\ell=e_Z+s g\delta+h(\delta+\frac12{\bf1}_{\ell=d})\); the extra \((\sqrt d)^h\) pays the last indicator. The chosen \(a_i,b_i\) are nonzero, work simultaneously for every defining function, every multi-index and every ordering of its derivatives, and make no choice depending on their degree. Both estimates give the original undivided **JetInt** bound \(e_Z+kg c_\mathrm{vp}+h c_\mathrm{vp}\). At \(c_\mathrm{vp}=0\) formula (1) gives precisely the exponent \(12e_Z\) of **LatIntUnr**. At \(c_\mathrm{vp}\ne0\), (1) is bounded by the original **LatIntRam** budget \(12(e_Z+kg c_\mathrm{vp}+h(2c_\mathrm{vp}+1+{\bf1}_{\ell=2}+\frac12{\bf1}_{\ell=d})+w a_0)\). To recover the precise **ValGe_vp** predicate, its exponent must be uniform in the valuation embedding. Use the original source bound, or take the maximum of (1)'s denominator exponents over the embeddings. For each \(n>0\) and integer \(b\) with \(b/n\) at least that uniform bound, \(\ell^b\mathrm{beta}^n\) is integral at every prime above \(\ell\) of a number field containing it. Its denominator ideal can thus be cleared by a rational integer prime to \(\ell\), exactly **IntAt**. The embedding-specific stronger estimate (1) may differ between embeddings. Thus within the manuscript proof both lattice suppliers apply at their actual quantifiers. The global character transport, evaluation of the sum over classes, and the other theta-lift estimates in (M) require separate proofs.
