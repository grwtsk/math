# Frobenius signs for a fixed family of CM curves at inert primes

Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field of fundamental discriminant \(-d\), with \(d\equiv7\pmod {16}\), and let \(H\) be its Hilbert class field. We compare the actual elliptic formal group in the short parameter \(t=-x/y\) with the Honda logarithm \(L(X)=\sum_{r\ge0}X^{p^{2r}}/p^r\) used in the local theta calculation. The question changes according to whether a curve or a tangent unit can depend on the inert place. The following arguments use CM, good reduction and finite extensions of number fields independently of any zeta zero hypothesis.

## Checking the first nonlemniscatic example

For each such \(K\), primes \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) inert in \(K\) have positive density. Indeed \(K\ne\mathbb Q(i)\), and the two restrictions specify an element of the biquadratic compositum. The lemniscatic curve has ordinary reduction there by the CM reduction theorem. A good quadratic twist has the same geometric height. In particular the reduction of \(y^2=x^3-x\) has 8 points over \(\mathbb F_5\), whereas a curve of good reduction with CM by \(\mathcal O_{-23}\) is supersingular there. An invertible change of integral formal parameter preserves the degree of the first nonvanishing term of the reduced multiplication by 5. Thus the fixed lemniscatic model cannot represent this local supplier.

For \(d=23\) insert \(J=125Y\) in the Hilbert class polynomial
\[
 H_{-23}(J)=J^3+3491750J^2-5151296875J+12771880859375.
\]
After division by \(5^9\) the result is \(g=Y^3+27934Y^2-329683Y+6539203\). Modulo 5 it is \((Y-1)(Y^2+2)\). The simple root lifts uniquely to \(Y\in1+5\mathbb Z_5\). The equation \(27YB^2=4(1728-125Y)\) has a unique root \(B\equiv1\pmod5\) by the same criterion. For \(E:y^2=x^3+5x+B\) the actual denominator of the short-curve formula is
\[
 D=4\cdot5^3+27B^2=6912/Y,\qquad j=1728\cdot500/D=125Y.
\]
An extra factor of 125 in the denominator of the source exchange's intermediate simplification must be deleted. Here \(D\) is a unit. The class polynomial identifies the geometric CM order in characteristic zero. Write \(u^2=2\) in the unramified quadratic completion. The unit \(3u\) has norm \(-18\equiv2\pmod5\) and is a nonsquare. The twist has reduced equation \(y^2=x^3-u\) over \(\mathbb F_{25}\). The untwisted curve has trace zero over \(\mathbb F_5\), so its square Frobenius is \([-5]\). The twist has Frobenius \([5]\) and 16 points. The modular arithmetic and the point count can also be checked directly. ## Positive normalization at a finite collection of places

Fix a good short model over \(H\) with geometric endomorphism ring \(\mathcal O_K\). At a rational inert prime \(p\ge5\), the unique prime of \(K\) over \(p\) is principal. Its Artin symbol in \(H/K\) is trivial. Thus at every place over such a prime the completion of \(H\) has integer ring \(O_2=\mathbb Z_{p^2}\) and residue field of order \(q=p^2\). Discard the finite set of bad primes of the chosen model. Its geometric CM endomorphisms descend over \(H\) since their characteristic-zero tangent multipliers lie in \(K\) and the differential on endomorphisms is injective. Specialization then places \(K\) inside the endomorphism algebra commuting with Frobenius. The curve is supersingular by Deuring; the centralizer of a quadratic field in its quaternion algebra is that field. Consequently the \(q\)-Frobenius \(\pi\) is in \(\mathcal O_K\) and \(\pi\bar\pi=p^2\). Inertness implies \((\pi)=(p)\), and the only units of \(\mathcal O_K\) are \(\pm1\). Thus \(\pi=[\pm p]\). [Deuring reduction and CM isogeny action, §1](https://www.math.u-bordeaux.fr/~damienrobert/pro/publications/reports/2013-04-class_poly_g2.pdf)

For a unit \(\tau\), twist the two coefficients by \(\tau^2,\tau^3\). Over the quadratic extension adjoining its square root the isomorphism sends \((x,y)\) to \((\tau x,\tau^{3/2}y)\). A nonsquare residue changes the descent cocycle of Frobenius by \([-1]\), and hence reverses the sign; a square leaves it fixed. At an arbitrary **finite** set of good inert places, prescribe a nonzero residue of the appropriate square class at each place. Chinese remaindering in \(\mathcal O_H\) realizes every prescribed sign pattern with one global twist. This prescription can include different places over the same rational prime. The twist may change the data at other places.

If the sign is positive then the full equality \([p](t)\equiv t^q\pmod p\) holds in the short parameter: this is the \(q\)-power endomorphism on the special fiber. The linear coefficient in characteristic zero is \(p\), so the Lubin–Tate strict comparison for the uniformizer \(p\) gives an integral isomorphism from the stated Honda group over \(O_2\) with tangent one. For negative sign one instead has \([p](t)\equiv-t^q\). Comparison in degree \(q\) would require the tangent \(b\) to satisfy \(b=-b^q\) modulo \(p\). No unit of \(O_2\) satisfies that equation. No strict isomorphism is possible even after extending the residue field. However a **unit-tangent** comparison already exists over the unramified quartic ring \(O_4\): strictly compare to the nonsquare twist, then transport back by the displayed isomorphism, which on parameters multiplies by \(\sqrt\tau\). The tangent has trace zero under the nontrivial automorphism of \(O_4/O_2\). Among finite unramified extensions of \(O_2\), degree two is minimal, and every even relative degree suffices. Odd degrees are excluded by iterating \(b^q=-b\). This gives a bounded field of definition for the Honda comparison for these good \(O_2\)-models. [Lubin–Tate comparison and source period cosets, §§8, 12](AlpogeEllipticCubicalB4.md)

For the example, the single *global* twist parameter \(3\sqrt{-23}\) has norm \(9\cdot23\). At each inert prime \(p\ge5\), the quadratic character of its residue over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) equals the Legendre symbol of its norm. Thus it reverses the original sign at **all** inert \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) and preserves it at all inert \(p\equiv3\pmod4\). Indeed \((23/p)=-(-1/p)\). The identification at 5 may take \(\sqrt{-23}\equiv u\). This sign rule applies to good models defined over the degree-two local field; the square class is measured in that residue field. In particular it does not globalize the displayed Hensel short equation over the Hilbert class field. In the cubic field \(F=\mathbb Q(j)\), its required \(B^2=(500/27)(1728-j)/j\) has norm \(-687695111488/96702579\), by evaluating the monic class polynomial at 1728 and 0. Neither this norm nor its quotient by \((-23)^3\), equal to \(29899787456/51155664291\), is a rational square. If \(B\) were in \(F(\sqrt{-23})\), squaring its expression in the quadratic basis would force it into either \(F\) or \(\sqrt{-23}F\), contrary to these norm tests. At 37, the class root \(3+3u\) for \(u^2=-23\) has \(N((1728-j)/j)=13\pmod {37}\), a nonsquare. Adjoining the indicated square root there enlarges the residue field to degree four and kills the degree-two nonsquare character. The local test at 5 prescribes a different place.

There is nevertheless an explicit global curve realizing the local repair at 5. Put \(b=(500/27)(1728-j)/j\) in the cubic class field generator's field \(F=\mathbb Q(j)\). The curve \(E_*:y^2=x^3+5b x+b^2\) is defined over \(F\). At the chosen embedding into \(\mathbb Q_5\) it is the twist of the original short model by \(B\), since \(B^2=b\). As \(B\in1+5\mathbb Z_5\), this twist is locally trivial. The global twist of \(E_*\) over \(H\) by \(3\sqrt{-23}\) then gives the same positive local isomorphism class as in the example: the ratio of the two local twist parameters can be chosen congruent to 1.

For every inert \(p\ge5\) away from the finite bad or ramified set, complex multiplication identifies \(\operatorname{Gal}(H/\mathbb Q)\) with the generalized dihedral group of the class group. Here the class polynomial has degree three, the class group has order three, and every element outside that cyclic subgroup acts as a transposition on the three class roots. Equivalently, complex conjugation acts on ideal classes by inversion, so each such element acts on them as \(x\mapsto a-x\) in \(\mathbb Z/3\). It fixes a unique class. There is consequently a place of \(F\) over \(p\) of residue degree one. At a place of \(H\) extending it, the good curve \(E_*\) has \(p^2\)-Frobenius \([-p]\) by trace zero over \(\mathbb F_p\). Thus **one fixed global curve** over \(H\), the displayed twist of \(E_*\), supplies the positive sign at a place over every such inert \(p\equiv1\pmod4\), and hence an all-orders strict Honda comparison at those places. This specifies the field, model and choice of place; it makes no simultaneous assertion about periods or a fixed embedding chosen in advance.

## Why a fixed finite list cannot supply all positive signs

Fix any nonempty finite family of characteristic-zero elliptic curves with geometric CM by \(\mathcal O_K\), with coefficients in number fields. This includes any preselected algebraic twists of the curves. There is a common finite Galois extension \(M/\mathbb Q\) containing all their fields and \(H\), and a positive-density set of inert rational primes such that at **every** place of \(M\) above **each** of these primes all the curves have good reduction over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) with Frobenius \([-p]\).

To prove this, use the principal CM lattice \(\mathcal O_K\). Its conjugate lattice is itself, so \(j_0=j(\mathcal O_K)\) is real algebraic in a chosen complex embedding. Choose a short curve \(E_0\) with that invariant over the real subfield \(F=\mathbb Q(j_0)\subset\mathbb R\). Here \(j_0\notin\{0,1728\}\). Every lattice in the family is homothetic to a fractional ideal of \(K\), commensurable with the principal lattice. The resulting complex isogenies \(E_0\to E_i\) are algebraic over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\): their finite kernels consist of algebraic torsion points and the isomorphisms from the quotients are algebraic. First include every conjugate of the finitely many coefficient tuples of the curves. This is still a finite family, all with CM by the same abstract order, since conjugation preserves the geometric endomorphism ring. Choose the isogenies from the fixed principal curve to this enlarged family and take a finite Galois \(M\) containing all coefficients and maps. Exclude finitely many ramified or bad primes, including divisors of the degrees of the isogenies. Write \(c\) for the restriction of complex conjugation to \(M\). It fixes \(F\) pointwise and acts nontrivially on \(K\).

Chebotarev gives primes with Frobenius conjugate to \(c\), of Dirichlet density \(|[c]|/[M:\mathbb Q]>0\). For each prime select a place with Frobenius exactly \(c\) by conjugating the place. Its residue field has degree two, and the residue field of \(F\) there is \(\mathbb F_p\), since \(c\) fixes its elements. The rational prime is inert in \(K\). The reduction of \(E_0/\mathbb F_p\) is supersingular, hence its integer trace is divisible by \(p\). Hasse's bound forces trace zero for \(p\ge5\). The Frobenius equation now gives \(\pi_p^2=[-p]\), exactly the Frobenius after passage to \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). The reduced isogenies commute with this Frobenius and are surjective, so the same scalar acts at the selected place on each curve of the enlarged family. For another place \(gW\), reduce the conjugate curve \(g^{-1}E_i\) at \(W\) and transport by \(g\). The induced residue-field isomorphism commutes with the \(p^2\)-power map and transports the integer scalar to itself. This proves the assertion at all places above the prime. Enlarging the family or adding finitely many auxiliary twists merely changes the finite extension to which the argument applies. [Chebotarev density theorem, Theorem 28.9](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2021fa/LectureNotes28.pdf)

In particular for a family over \(H\) the residue field at the contracted place of \(H\) is already \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\). Even if one permits a choice from that finite family at each place, positive Frobenius fails for the whole family at every place above these primes. The finite-window twist and the place-dependent comparison over \(O_4\) explain how this conclusion coexists with an integral local supplier. The actual arithmetic inequality fixes the global theta kernels, characters, periods and Rallis factors. Transporting them through a change of global curve requires a separate comparison. In the source's completed maximal unramified field a place-dependent unit is allowed, as in the existing construction of the sigma compensation. The obstruction concerns the stronger fixed normalization over \(O_2\).
