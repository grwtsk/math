## A square formula for the finite trace

Work on the rank-one **source** model with target \(L'\). Its period lattice \(\Lambda\) is stable under \(\mathcal O_K\) because its periods are generated by the fixed \(K\)-ideal of elliptic periods times embedded vectors in the half-different Hom lattice. The induced CM type gives one Hermitian complex space \(V\) coming from a \(K\)-vector space at the chosen embedding of \(K\). Let \(h\) be any \(K\)-rational unitary change preserving this rational lattice span and the Gaussian sign, and suppose \(A_h=1-h^{-1}\) is invertible. Put \(D_h=\Lambda\cap h\Lambda\). The ratio \(c_h\) of pullback by \(h^{-1}\) to \(J_{\Lambda,h\Lambda}\) on the nonzero theta line has the coherent fixed-point expression
\[
  c_h=\frac{|\det_{\mathbb C}A_h|}{\det_{\mathbb C}A_h}\,
     \frac{1}{\sqrt{|G_h|}}\sum_{\ell\in G_h} q_h(\ell),\qquad
  G_h=\Lambda/A_hD_h,\qquad
  q_h(\ell)=\alpha(\ell)\exp[-\pi H((A_h^{-1}-1/2)\ell,\ell)].
\]
Here \(A_hD_h\subseteq\Lambda\) since \(D_h\subseteq h\Lambda\cap\Lambda\). Both the index and the positive square root in this expression matter. The pullbacks of the positive theta line bundle under the two isogenies agree on the intersection, and the local coherent Lefschetz denominator is \(\det A_h\); the finite trace and its normalization are derived in §17 of the theta calculation. This is a shared geometric input of the present proof. [Coherent fixed-point calculation](AlpogeEllipticCubicalB4.md) · [Normalization and orientation](AlpogeAllPlaceThetaNormalization.md)

Write \(b(x,y)=q_h(x+y)/(q_h(x)q_h(y))\). This is a bicharacter. The phase is even and \(q_h(0)=1\), hence \(q_h(nx)=q_h(x)^{n^2}\). It is nonsingular: the isometry gives absolute value \(\sqrt{|G_h|}\) for its unnormalized sum, whereas expanding its squared absolute value and summing the characters gives \(|G_h|\,|\operatorname{rad}b|\) whenever the sum is nonzero. The group \(G_h\) is a finite \(\mathcal O_K\)-module, and its polar pairing satisfies
\[
                          b(ax,y)=b(x,\bar a y)
                           \quad(a\in\mathcal O_K).
\]
Indeed, on the lattice the polarization of the sign \(\alpha\) is the exponential of \(\pi i\) times the integral alternating Hermitian pairing, by the theta translation law. This pairing is balanced by Hermitian adjointness. The two cross terms of the exponential of \(H((A_h^{-1}-1/2)\ell,\ell)\) obey the same adjointness because \(A_h\) commutes with \(K\); in the second cross term this uses conjugate linearity in the other argument. These identities descend to the finite quotient. [Original Hermitian form and multipliers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/GlobalSchurBounds/GeneralLinearActions/IsDiscriminantTwelfthRootOfIdealLattice.lean#L71-L97) · [Homogeneity and square-root normalization](AlpogeKernelJetBlockDescent.md)

Orthogonally decompose \(G_h\) into its primary subgroups. On the 2-primary subgroup the two dyadic idempotents give \(X\oplus Y\); they act through the finite ring modulo a sufficiently high power of 2. Since they are exchanged by conjugation and have product zero, the balance identity gives \(b(X,X)=b(Y,Y)=1\). Nonsingularity makes \(b\) a perfect pairing between \(X\) and \(Y\), in particular \(|X|=|Y|\). The restrictions \(a=q_h|_X,d=q_h|_Y\) are characters and their evenness gives \(a^2=d^2=1\). Thus \(q_h(x+y)=a(x)d(y)b(x,y)\). For the unique \(x_0\) such that \(b(x_0,\cdot)=d^{-1}\), character orthogonality evaluates the sum as \(|Y|a(x_0)\). Its normalized square is one; both linear characters on the isotropic summands have been retained.

For odd \(p\), diagonalize the nonsingular quadratic module into cyclic Jordan summands of orders \(p^k\). A cyclic summand has form \(e(t x^2/p^k)\) for a unit \(t\); classical quadratic Gauss evaluation gives normalized square \(1\) if \(k\) is even, and \(\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right)\) if \(k\) is odd. The dependence on \(t\) disappears upon squaring. Write \(\chi_4(n)=1\) or \(-1\) as the odd part of \(n\) is \(1\) or \(3\bmod4\), extended multiplicatively to positive rationals. Multiplying the primary evaluations gives the exact identity
\[
 \left(|G_h|^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell\in G_h}q_h(\ell)\right)^2
       =\chi_4(|G_h|).
\]
The split, balanced condition at 2 is essential to this formula. For the nonsplit finite quadratic module \(\mathbb Z/2\) with \(q(1)=i\), the normalized square is \(i\). [Strömberg, Proposition 2.11 and Lemmas 3.4–3.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.0202)

## Comparison at a transverse scalar translate

Every automorphism \(\gamma\) under consideration has \(\det_{\mathbb C}\gamma=1\) on the rank-one source Hom space. Indeed \(\gamma,\gamma^{-1}\) preserve the full lattice \(L'\), so their algebraic determinants are integral and the principal determinant ideal is the unit ideal. Unitarity gives \(z\bar z=1\) for \(z=\det_E\gamma\). Evaluate the pinned principal-ideal identity **IsChi** at \(z\) and \(1\); it gives \(\prod_\tau\tau(z)=1\), which is the complex determinant on \(V\). This uses the given character rather than inferring a CM-type product from a norm-one condition alone. [Principal-ideal and stabilizer calculation](AlpogeKernelDeterminantUnit.md)

Choose a norm-one \(u\in K^\times\) with \(u\ne1\) such that \(u\gamma\) has no eigenvalue 1. There are infinitely many choices by \((1+t\sqrt{-d})/(1-t\sqrt{-d})\) for \(t\in\mathbb Q\). Compare \(h=u\gamma\) and \(j=uI\). Since \(\gamma\Lambda=\Lambda\), one has \(h\Lambda=j\Lambda\) and \(D_h=D_j=D\). The normalized operator \(J_{\Lambda,h\Lambda}\) in the two traces is literally the same, including its square-root index. On the original line \(\gamma\) acts by a scalar \(\lambda\), choosing pullback by \(\gamma^{-1}\); commutativity with \(uI\) gives \(c_h=\lambda c_j\). There is no triangle scalar in this comparison.

For \(r=\dim_\mathbb C V\), unitarity gives \(\overline{\det A_t}=(-1)^r\det(t)\det A_t\) for \(t=h,j\). Their determinants agree because \(\det_\mathbb C\gamma=1\). Thus \(\det A_h/\det A_j\) is real. Moreover it lies in the chosen complex image of \(K\): for the rank-one source space the determinant of \(1-u^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\) is the embedding of \(N_{E/K}(\det_E(1-u^{-1}\gamma^{-1}))\), using all embeddings over the fixed embedding of \(K\). The same holds with \(\gamma=1\). Since \(K\) is imaginary quadratic, the ratio is a nonzero rational number \(s\). It follows from real covolumes that
\[
 \frac{|G_h|}{|G_j|}
 =\frac{[\Lambda:D]|\det_{\mathbb C} A_h|^2}
        {[\Lambda:D]|\det_{\mathbb C} A_j|^2}=s^2 .
\]
The normalized Gauss sums consequently have the same square, as \(\chi_4(s^2)=1\). The two archimedean factors also have the same square by the determinant identity. Dividing the exact trace formula gives \(\lambda^2=1\). The orientation with \(\gamma\) in place of \(\gamma^{-1}\) gives the same assertion. This proof includes eigenvalue 1 with arbitrary multiplicity, including \(\gamma=1\). [CM determinant identification and reference correspondence, §17](AlpogeEllipticCubicalB4.md)

For the standard source lattice of rank \(N\), the \(N\) independent Hom columns tensor the rank-one section, so their eigenvalue is \(\lambda^N=1\) for every original even \(N\). For the actual source lattice \(L\) the normalized intertwiner between the two principally polarized lines commutes with this stabilizer by reindexing its finite sum; both period lattices are preserved. Uniqueness of the nonzero section up to scalar then gives the same action on the selected **eNat**, independently of its scalar normalization. Both member and reference twists use the same argument. The proof of trivial **Stab0** in the archive concerns the initial class index 0; it does not supply any of these target stabilizer identities at \(i+1\). ## Decorated stabilizers in the three finite kernels

The vacuum eigenvalue computed above is common to all three kernels. The additional factors in the finite Schwartz datum depend on the column index. At a tame character prime, write the reduction of a level element as a block upper triangular matrix with invertible diagonal blocks \(A,D\). The ordinary datum vanishes unless the matrix is integral and its lower left block reduces to zero. On this support its multiplicative weight is the zero extension of the character of the upper left determinant. Left multiplication leaves the support invariant in both directions, and the determinant character contributes
\[
                  \chi(\det g)^{-1}\chi(\det A)=\chi(\det D)^{-1}.
\]
This equality multiplies the original coefficient and also holds when that coefficient vanishes. At a flag prime the support is expressed in the two prescribed flag orders. In each unipotent chain the new simple-superdiagonal entries are the sums of the two old entries: the two diagonal entries equal one and the entries below the diagonal vanish. There is no simple-root character across the break between the chains. Multiplication of the remaining diagonal blocks gives the product of their determinant characters; the adjacent-stage determinant twist restores the last block in this product. Applying the inverse level element proves support invariance off the cell. ## Formal scope

The companion module proves the finite split character sum with arbitrary isotropic characters, its normalized square for an even character, the unitary fixed-point determinant identity, its fixed-field ratio at equal determinant and the block determinant character cancellation. The first two declarations retain the perfect pairing as an explicit equivalence to additive characters. The manuscript identifies that pairing from the actual split dyadic CM action. Its geometric identification and the finite-kernel comparisons above are analytic source comparisons. The five Lean declarations contain no imported RH theorem. [Lean statements and proofs](AlpogeKernelStabilizerPhase.lean)

The module built and passed fresh kernel replay. All five public declarations use only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` in their transitive axiom reports. Independent finite controls evaluated 872 odd-modulus Gauss squares and 124 signed split dyadic Gauss sums in exact cyclotomic rings, including a separate nonsplit counterexample, 1,100 signed-permutation comparisons in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})\) and 2,000 tame character calculations over \(\mathbb F_5\). These finite examples test the sign, determinant and support conventions. Independent review of the complete geometric argument remains open.
