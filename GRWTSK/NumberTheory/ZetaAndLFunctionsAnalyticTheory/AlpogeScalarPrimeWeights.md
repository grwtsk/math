# Pointwise vanishing for scalar Selmer groups at signed residue classes

grwtsk contributors

Write \(G_m=K_{4m}(\mathbb Z)\) for \(m\ge1\). For an odd prime \(p\), let \(h=(p-1)/2\) and let \(r=h/\gcd(h,m)\) be the order of the residual character \(\omega^{-2m}\). We obtain three pointwise results for the **whole** primary group.

* If \(h\mid m-1\) or \(h\mid m+1\), then \(G_m[p^\infty]=0\).
* If \(a\ge1\), \(2a<p-1\), and \(h\mid m+a\), then \(G_m[p^\infty]\ne0\) requires \(p\mid\operatorname{num}(B_{2a})\). In particular this whole group vanishes if \(p>(2a+1)^{4a+1}\). This gives a zero range \(a\le c\log p/\log\log p\) at large \(p\), uniformly in the weight.
* Fix \(R\ge1\) and \(L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2R+1)\). If every prime factor of \(m\) is congruent to either sign modulo \(L\), then **every** coefficient prime of residual order \(r\le R\) gives \(G_m[p^\infty]=0\). This holds for arbitrary products and powers of those prime factors. For each \(0<\delta<1/5.18\) and all sufficiently large \(X\), at least \(\gg_\delta X^\delta\) prime weights \(m\le X\) have this vanishing simultaneously through \(r\le c_\delta\log X\).

At even source weight \(e=2m\), these orders are the terms of the trivial-character row in Alpöge’s CorG over minimal coefficients. Thus they contribute zero to any admissible finite prime sum. The proofs do not require a bound on the exceptional specialization lengths of a class tower. The other residual orders at these weights, weights outside the indicated families and the general host remain open. ## The mirror and the signs of the weight

Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\mu_p)\), \(\Delta=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\), and write \(A\) for the \(p\)-primary class group. Lim’s Corollary 4.3, with \(F_{\mathrm{base}}=\mathbb Q\), depth one and twist \(i=2m\), gives zero \(p\)-rank if \(h\mid m\). Otherwise the rank is that of the \(\theta=\omega^{-2m}\) part of \(A\). The unique prime over \(p\) is principal and its full decomposition group eliminates the local correction. Consequently the presence of any \(p\)-torsion depends only on \(m\bmod h\). Each \(G_m\) is finite, so zero rank implies full primary vanishing. When \(h\mid m-1\) the rank equals that at \(m=1\), and Rognes proved \(K_4(\mathbb Z)=0\). This also covers \(p=3\). [Lim, v2, Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00168v2#page=8) · [Rognes, K4(Z) is the trivial group, Topology 39 (2000), 267–281](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0040-9383(99)00007-5)

Suppose now \(\theta\ne1\), and put \(\eta=\omega\theta^{-1}\). A nonzero \(\theta\)-quotient of the elementary unramified class group gives a nonzero \(\eta\)-line of unramified Kummer radicals. Indeed the radicals are characters of the quotient with values in \(\mu_p\), which accounts for both the inverse and the cyclotomic twist. An unramified radical has all ideal valuations divisible by \(p\), so dividing its principal ideal’s exponents by \(p\) gives a class in \(A[p]\). The kernel is represented by units. In a CM field the quotient of a unit by its complex conjugate is a root of unity. Since \(p\) is odd, the odd part of units modulo \(p\)th powers comes from roots of unity and has only type \(\omega\). Our \(\eta\) is odd and distinct from \(\omega\), so \(A[p]^\eta\ne0\) is necessary. [Kummer reflection and class group map](AlpogeSelmerReflection.md) · [Gras, Théorèmes de réflexion, JTNB 10 (1998)](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.234/)

For every \(b\) between 1 and \(p-1\), the integral Stickelberger element
\[
 (b-\sigma_b)\frac1p\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}j\sigma_j^{-1}
   =\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\lfloor bj/p\rfloor\sigma_j^{-1}
\]
annihilates the ideal class group. On the \(\eta\)-component modulo \(p\), this acts by the sum with \(\sigma_j^{-1}\) replaced by \(\eta(j)^{-1}\). If \(h\mid m+1\) and \(p\ge5\), then \(\theta=\omega^2\), \(\eta=\omega^{-1}\). At \(b=2\) the eigenvalue equals \(u=\sum_{j=(p+1)/2}^{p-1}j\) modulo \(p\). Direct summation gives \(8u+4p=3p^2+1\). Hence the eigenvalue is \(1/8\) in \(\mathbb F_p\), ruling out all \(p\)-torsion. This proves the other sign. [Stickelberger, Ueber eine Verallgemeinerung der Kreistheilung, Math. Ann. 37 (1890)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01721360)

## A necessary Bernoulli divisor at every weight

Put \(k=2a\), where \(2\le k<p-1\) and \(h\mid m+a\). Now \(\eta^{-1}=\omega^{k-1}\), and the Stickelberger eigenvalue is
\[
       u_b=\sum_{j=1}^{p-1}\lfloor bj/p\rfloor j^{k-1}\pmod p .
\]
We give the calculation for every multiplier, since the choice \(b=2\) sometimes loses information. The least positive residues \(bj-p\lfloor bj/p\rfloor\) permute the integers from 1 to \(p-1\). Expand their \(k\)th powers modulo \(p^2\) and sum, writing \(S_k=\sum j^k\). This yields
\[
 k b^{k-1}p\,u_b\equiv(b^k-1)S_k\pmod{p^2}.
\]
With the convention \(B_1=-1/2\), Faulhaber’s formula gives \(S_k=(B_{k+1}(p)-B_{k+1})/(k+1)\equiv p B_k\pmod{p^2}\). Indeed \(p\ge k+3\), and the Bernoulli recurrence gives \((j+1)!B_j\in\mathbb Z\) by induction. All denominators in the expansion are therefore prime to \(p\). After dividing by \(p\) we obtain
\[
                    k b^{k-1}u_b=(b^k-1)B_k\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p .
\]
For a primitive root \(b\), \(b^k-1\ne0\). Thus all the annihilator tests vanish exactly when \(p\mid\operatorname{num}(B_k)\). This is the necessary direction of Herbrand’s theorem applied after reflection. It supplies no converse for the even component. [Herbrand, Sur les classes des corps circulaires, 1932, §4](https://www.numdam.org/item/JMPA_1932_9_11__417_0/)

For the size bound, the same recurrence gives \(|B_j|\le j!\). Inductively, the sum of the bounds for the terms of \(B_j=-\sum_{i<j}\binom{j+1}{i}B_i/(j+1)\) is at most \(j!\sum_{t=2}^{j+1}1/t!\le j!\). Euler’s even zeta-value formula gives \(B_k\ne0\) at positive even \(k\). Consequently
\[
  1\le |\operatorname{num}(B_k)|\le (k+1)!\,k!\le(k+1)^{2k+1}.
\]
This proves the stated zero range. For example, take \(a\le\log p/(8\log\log p)\) for sufficiently large \(p\). Then \(2a+1\le\log p\) and \((4a+1)\log(2a+1)<\log p\). For each fixed conductor, adding multiples of its conductor half preserves the offset condition at arbitrarily large positive weights. [Mathlib’s pinned Faulhaber identity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/NumberTheory/BernoulliPolynomials.lean#L152-L180) · [Mathlib’s pinned even zeta-value identity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ZetaValues.lean#L432-L450)

## Eliminating every small residual order simultaneously

For every odd \(p\), put \(d=\gcd(h,m)\), so \(p=2rd+1\) and \(d\mid m\). If every prime divisor of \(m\) is congruent to a sign modulo \(L\), the same is true for each divisor \(d\), including \(m\) itself. Suppose \(2\le r\le R\) and \(d>1\). If \(d\equiv1\pmod L\), the integer \(2r+1\) divides \(p\), with \(1<2r+1<p\). If \(d\equiv-1\pmod L\), the integer \(2r-1\) divides \(p\), again a proper divisor. Both contradict primality. Hence \(d=1\), \(h=r\), and \(m\equiv\pm1\bmod h\) because \(h\mid L\). The two sign results prove vanishing. Order one was already handled by Lim. In particular this argument treats the potentially wild exceptional weights of the order-five and order-eight branches whenever \(R\ge8\). [Comparison with the exceptional weight classes for fixed residual orders](AlpogeOcticSelmerBound.md)

For prime weights alone, the dichotomy is immediate: \(d\) is 1 or \(m\), so any prime candidate of order \(r\) is \(2r+1\) or \(2rm+1\). For fixed \(R\), Dirichlet’s theorem gives infinitely many primes in each of the two sign classes. To obtain a growing range at individual weights, let \(m_R\) be the least prime congruent to \(-1\) modulo \(L\). The residue is coprime to \(L\). Xylouris’s Theorem 1.1 gives \(m_R\le C L^{5.18}\) with an absolute \(C\). The elementary Chebyshev bound gives \(\log L\ll R\). In detail, the extra prime powers in \((n,2n]\) each contribute a prime factor to \(\binom{2n}{n}\), by the factorial valuation formula. Thus the difference in the logarithms of the two least common multiples is at most \(2n\log2\), and summation over dyadic endpoints proves the bound. Since \(m_R\ge L-1\to\infty\), this yields infinitely many prime weights with \(r\le c'\log m_R\) all eliminated pointwise. A fixed power \(m_R^t\) has the same conclusion with constant \(c'/t\). The estimates need no zero hypothesis. [Xylouris, Acta Arithmetica 150 (2011), Theorem 1.1, p. 66](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)

The growing-order count also follows from the same least-prime theorem. Fix \(0<\delta<1/5.18\), and choose \(\epsilon>0\) with \(5.18(\delta+2\epsilon)<1\). The Chebyshev estimate allows \(R=\lfloor c_\delta\log X\rfloor\) with \(L\le X^\epsilon\) at large \(X\). Bertrand’s theorem gives a prime \(q\in[X^\delta,2X^\delta+2]\), coprime to \(L\) since \(q>2R+1\). For every nonzero residue \(a\bmod q\), the Chinese remainder theorem gives a reduced residue congruent to \(a\bmod q\) and \(-1\bmod L\). Its least prime is at most \(C(Lq)^{5.18}\le X\) for large \(X\). These primes are distinct as \(a\) varies, giving at least \(q-1\) eligible weights. All these weights share the same residual-order cutoff. This count gives no pointwise upper bound on orders above that cutoff.
