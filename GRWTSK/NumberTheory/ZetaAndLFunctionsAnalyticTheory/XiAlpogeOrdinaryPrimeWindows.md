# Deconstructing Alpöge's arithmetic input for ordinary-prime blocks and Weil positivity

Local source attribution: grwtsk contributors.

There are two useful points at which to enter the proof. A version using only ordinary primes inherits the original zeta ramp estimate through an explicit positive correction. For the earlier GRWTSK statistic $D_N=S_{2N}-S_N$ there is instead an exact signed pairing. These are ways to use, compare, or independently replace an arithmetic estimate in Alpöge's proof. A completed route using his decisive arithmetic estimate retains that dependence.

## Where the original and shorter proofs can be cut

The November 20 archive defines the untwisted zeta statistic by
\[
 G(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)(x/n-1)-x\log x+(1+\gamma)x .
\]
For a family at a nonprincipal level, its statistic $\mathrm{Gm}$ adds weighted nonprincipal-character terms. At the cyclic level generated by the principal character this extra set is empty. The geometric and character estimates still have to be supplied. In the zeta exposition, the main inequality (M) and the two auxiliary character estimates (B) and (C) supply an upper estimate throughout arbitrarily late intervals $[Y,Y^{3/2}]$, for each positive exponent allowance. One choice of auxiliary primes must work over the whole interval. The analytic window theorem then excludes right-half-plane zeros and reflection gives RH. ## A directly transferable ordinary-prime ramp

There is also a positive answer if the block kernel may be changed. Retain Alpöge's linear ramp and remove the higher prime powers with their full centering constant. Put
\[
 C=\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{\log p}{p^k}
   =\sum_p\frac{\log p}{p(p-1)},\qquad
 G_{\rm pr}(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p\,(x/p-1)-x\log x+(1+\gamma+C)x.
\]
This uses each ordinary prime once. Let $H(t)=\sum_{p^k\le t,\ k\ge2}\log p$. Then for $x>1$,
\[
 G_{\rm pr}(x)-G(x)
    =x\int_x^\infty H(t)t^{-2}dt
    =(2+o(1))\sqrt x .
\]
Indeed an atom of mass $\log p$ at $p^k$ contributes $\log p\min(1,x/p^k)$ to the integral after the factor $x$. These are exactly the terms of $Cx-\sum_{p^k\le x,\ k\ge2}\log p(x/p^k-1)$. Tonelli applies by positivity, and $C$ converges by comparison with $\sum_m\log(m)/m^2$. The ordinary prime number theorem gives $H(t)=\theta(\sqrt t)+O(t^{1/3}\log t)\sim\sqrt t$; the higher-power bound uses Chebyshev's estimate for each of at most $\log_2 t$ exponents. Integration from $x$ to infinity yields the constant two. Chebyshev's upper bound alone suffices for the uniform error $O(\sqrt x)$.

Consequently an upper bound $G(x)\le x^{1/2+\epsilon/2}$ on all points of a sufficiently late window transfers to $G_{\rm pr}(x)\le x^{1/2+\epsilon}$ on that same window. The converse needs no allowance since $G\le G_{\rm pr}$. Thus the family of one-sided power-window assertions at every positive exponent allowance is equivalent for these two observables. The linear term $Cx$ is essential. This identifies an ordinary-prime consumer of Alpöge's genuine ceiling even before applying the zero-exclusion theorem. It changes the test kernel, retains the upstream arithmetic dependence, and does not assert the sign of the original doubling blocks.

The error also preserves the Mellin poles in the open half-plane $\Re s>1/2$. On $\Re s>1$ the Mellin transform on $[1,\infty)$ of $G_{\rm pr}-G$ is $C/(s-1)-[\sum_{p,k\ge2}(\log p)p^{-ks}]/[s(s-1)]$. The higher-power sum converges normally on compact subsets of $\Re s>1/2$, and its value at one is $C$, removing the apparent pole. The [native Lean ramp module](XiAlpogeOrdinaryRamp.lean) proves the exact prime-power decomposition and the two-way window transfer from summability for every exponent above one half. The exact leading coefficient two is obtained by the paper argument.

## Finite source at the physical scale

The unit-coefficient windows can be tested with no infinite centering constants and no prime above the scale. Set $M=\lfloor x\rfloor$ for $x\ge2$ and $H_M=\sum_{j=1}^M1/j$. Define
\[
 G_{\rm fin}(x)=\sum_{p\le x}(\log p)\left(\frac{x}{p-1}-1\right)
                 +x[1+H_M-\log(x(M+1))].
\]
Put $C_M=\sum_{p\le M}\log p/[p(p-1)]$. Since $1/p+1/[p(p-1)]=1/(p-1)$, direct subtraction gives
\[
 0\le G_{\rm pr}(x)-G_{\rm fin}(x)
   =x\{\gamma-H_M+\log(M+1)+C-C_M\}
   \le\frac{x}{M}\bigl[1+2(\log M+1)\bigr].
\]
Indeed the integral comparison for the harmonic series gives $H_M-\log(M+1)\le\gamma\le H_M-\log M$, and thus the first difference is at most $1/M$. For the second use $1/[p(p-1)]\le2/p^2$ and the decreasing positive function $(\log y)/y^2$ on $y\ge2$. Its integral on $[M,\infty)$ is $(\log M+1)/M$. Thus the elementary upper error is $O(\log x)$, smaller than $\sqrt{x}$. Partial summation and Chebyshev even give $C-C_M=O(1/M)$. The established two-way window equivalence therefore also holds for $G_{\rm fin}$, with arbitrary small exponent slack. This observable is a finite sum of logarithms of ordinary primes and a finite harmonic correction at the actual scale. It is a different test from the rational doubling-block observable.

## A criterion with integer comparisons inside each window

For an integer $n\ge2$ put $L_n=n!$ and write the following positive rational as $A_n/B_n$ with positive integers $A_n,B_n$:
\[
 E_n=\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n\prod_{j=1}^n\left(1+\frac1{nj}\right)^n .
\]
Let
\[
 Z(n)=\sum_{p\le n}\left(\frac{n}{p-1}-1\right)\log p+
             n\log\frac{E_n}{n(n+1)}.
\]
The elementary bound $0\le u-\log(1+u)\le u^2/2$ for $u\ge0$ gives, term by term,
\[
 0\le 1+H_n-\log E_n\le (1+\zeta(2))/(2n).
\]
Consequently $0\le G_{\rm fin}(n)-Z(n)\le(1+\zeta(2))/2$. The comparisons with $G_{\rm pr}$ and $G$ above give $Z(n)-G(n)=(2+o(1))\sqrt n$. The weaker uniform bound $|Z(n)-G(n)|=O(\sqrt n)$ already follows from Chebyshev.

For rational $\theta>1/2$ set $K_{n,\theta}=\lceil n^\theta\rceil$. This is an integer computable by integer powering and root comparison. The assertion $Z(n)\le K_{n,\theta}\log n$ is **exactly** the integer inequality
\[
 \left(\prod_{p\le n}p^{\,L_n(n/(p-1)-1)}\right) A_n^{\,nL_n}
   \ \le\ n^{L_n K_{n,\theta}}\bigl[B_n n(n+1)\bigr]^{nL_n}.
\]
Any common multiple of the positive integers through $n$ can serve as $L_n$. In particular $p-1$ divides $L_n$ and all prime exponents are nonnegative. Exponentiating the displayed logarithmic assertion after multiplication by $L_n$ and clearing denominators gives precisely the stated inequality. The [native product theorem](XiAlpogeRationalRampWindow.lean) verifies this exact log-to-rational comparison for any integer threshold, using $L_n=n!$. The [center theorem](XiAlpogeRationalRampCenter.lean) bounds the error at the actual Euler–Mascheroni constant. This comparison counts each ordinary prime through $n$ once. The integer products can be large.

**Window equivalence.** The family of untwisted real assertions
\[
 \forall\eta>1/2\ \forall y_0\ \exists Y\ge y_0
      \forall x\in[Y,Y^{3/2}],\quad G(x)\le x^\eta
\]
is equivalent to the following discrete family: for every rational $\theta>1/2$ and every $y_0$ there exists $Y\ge y_0$ such that the stated integer comparison holds at **every integer** $n\in[Y,Y^{3/2}]$. The start may equivalently be an integer $M$: the inner range is then exactly $M\le n$ and $n^2\le M^3$. One can restrict the quantified exponents to $(1/2,1)$ because larger exponents only relax sufficiently late inequalities.

For the forward implication choose $1/2<\eta<\theta$. On the resulting sufficiently late window the upper bound on $G$ and the uniform $O(\sqrt n)$ comparison imply $Z(n)\le K_{n,\theta}\log n$. For integer starts take $M=\lfloor Y\rfloor$, having chosen $Y$ at least one above the requested lower limit. Since $M^{3/2}\le Y^{3/2}$, the only potentially uncovered integer is $n=M$. For that point the unit-distance estimate for $G$ proved below gives $G(M)\le G(Y)+O(\log Y)$, which the very same exponent slack absorbs. Conversely fix $\eta>1/2$ and a rational $1/2<\theta<\eta$. In any sufficiently late window pick for each $x$ an integer $n$ inside the same closed interval with $|n-x|\le1$; this includes both endpoints because the interval length exceeds two. The ramp $G$ is continuous and piecewise differentiable, with derivative $O(\log x)$ on $[x-1,x+1]$ for large $x$ by $\sum_{m\le x}\Lambda(m)/m=O(\log x)$. Thus
\[
 G(x)\le \lceil n^\theta\rceil\log n+O(\sqrt n+\log x)\le x^\eta
\]
uniformly on that sufficiently late window. The exponent slack absorbs both the logarithm and the unit-scale interpolation error. No estimate across gaps between the supplied windows was used. This is an arithmetic rewriting of the full window supplier, not an independent proof of that supplier.

The [end-to-end Lean equivalence](XiAlpogeFiniteWindowCriterion.lean) proves the displayed three-halves window equivalence for the centered von Mangoldt ramp. Its finite side uses an executable natural number: for $b>0$ it finds the least $m$ with $n^a\le m^b$ and proves that $m=\lceil n^{a/b}\rceil$. The proof uses the [finite proper-power correction](XiAlpogeFiniteRampBounds.lean) and the [score bounds](XiAlpogeFiniteRampScore.lean)
\[
              G(n)-4\ \le\ Z(n)\ \le\ G(n)+\sum_{p,\ k\ge2}\log p\min(1,n/p^k).
\]
The [unit-distance estimate](XiAlpogeRampOscillation.lean) proves $|G(v)-G(u)|\le9(v-u)\sqrt v$ for $1\le u\le v$. This weaker local estimate already permits [rounding both window endpoints](XiAlpogeIntegerWindows.lean) with arbitrarily small exponent slack. These local theorems establish the equivalence without an RH or window-existence hypothesis. Alpöge's arithmetic proof of the estimate and the window-to-RH argument are separate source results at the pins above.

A failure of Weil positivity would have a concrete consequence here. One negative value of the complete Weil form on a test square would contradict RH. By the analytic window implication and the equivalence above, there would then be a fixed rational $\theta>1/2$ and a fixed $L$ such that **every** integer start $M\ge L$ has a failing integer comparison somewhere in $M\le n$, $n^2\le M^3$. This follows by negating the quantifiers. In the affirmative direction an independent proof needs whole passing windows for each exponent arbitrarily far out; a finite list of passing windows supplies only those instances.

## Exact intermediate identity

For integers $N\ge2$, put
\[
 W_N(y)=y^{-2}\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N}(1-y^{-2})^j/j,\quad
 F_N(y)=yW_N(y)/\log y,\quad
 R_N=\sum_{p}\sum_{k\ge2}W_N(p^k)/k .
\]
Let $D_N=S_{2N}-S_N$ denote the harmonic discrepancy in the [ordinary-prime criterion](XiBernoulliOrdinaryPrimeBias.md). Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad D_N=\int_1^\infty G(y)F_N''(y)\,dy-R_N.\quad}
\]
Indeed on $(1,\infty)$ the distributional second derivative of $(y/n-1)_+$ is $\delta_n/n$, and $(y\log y)''=1/y$. Hence $yG''=\sum_{n\ge2}\Lambda(n)\delta_n-dy$. Pair $G''$ against $F_N$. At $p^k$ the coefficient is $\Lambda(p^k)/\log(p^k)=1/k$, accounting for the whole signed prime-power correction. The continuous term is exactly $\int_1^\infty W_N(y)dy/\log y$. At $1$, $F_N=O_N((y-1)^N)$ and $F_N'=O_N((y-1)^{N-1})$; at infinity $F_N=O_N(1/(y\log y))$, $F_N'=O_N(1/(y^2\log y))$ and $F_N''=O_N(1/(y^3\log y))$. Chebyshev's estimate and partial summation give $G=O(y\log y)$ and $G'_+=O(\log y)$. Both endpoint terms in integration by parts vanish, and the integrals converge. The affine term $(1+\gamma)y$ contributes zero to this pairing. An arbitrary affine function can likewise be subtracted from $G$.

The [finite ramp pairing](XiPrimeBlockRampPairing.lean) formalizes the finite integration and endpoint terms. Passing to infinity and the asymptotic estimates below are paper arguments.

The curvature $F_N''$ takes both signs. Indeed $\int_1^\infty F_N''=0$ by the endpoint limits, and $F_N$ is positive inside with zero values at both ends, so $F_N''$ cannot vanish identically. By continuity each sign occurs on an open interval. Thus the precise requirement for literal block descent is
\[
              \int G(F_N'')_+ - \int G(F_N'')_- \ \le\ R_N ,
\]
where $a_\pm=\max(\pm a,0)$. A source ceiling for $G$ controls the first term on its own window; the second uses the lower side of $G$, and the integrals also reach outside that window. Positivity of $W_N$ alone does not supply those two controls.

## Scale of the signed pairing

For any fixed $0\le\sigma<2$,
\[
             \int_1^\infty y^\sigma|F_N''(y)|dy
                   \ll_\sigma N^{-1+\sigma/2}/\log N .
\]
Here is a proof with both ends of the integration range controlled. Write $x=\log y$, $B(s)=\sum_{j=N+1}^{2N}(1-e^{-s})^j/j$ and $H(x)=B(2x)/x$, so that $F_N''(y)=y^{-3}(H''-3H'+2H)(x)$. For $N\ge4$, all four derivatives of orders $0,1,2,3$ of $B$ are $O(\exp(-N e^{-s}/4))$ for $s\ge0$. For order zero this follows termwise. For the others put $A=(1-e^{-s})^N$. Then $B'=A(1-A)$, $A'=P_1$ and $A''=-P_1+2P_2$, where $P_i$ denotes the probability of exactly $i$ successes with success probability $e^{-s}$ in $N$ independent trials. Writing $z=N e^{-s}$ gives $P_i\le z^i e^{-z/2}/i!$ for $i=1,2$. Differentiating $B'$ proves the bounds. On $0\le x\le1$, use $H=\int_0^2 B'(vx)dv$ to get an exponentially small bound also for the first two derivatives of $H$. On $x\ge1$, direct differentiation of $B(2x)/x$ bounds their sum with $|H|$ by $C x^{-1}\exp(-N e^{-2x}/4)$. In the resulting integral against $e^{(\sigma-2)x}dx$, the interval $[1,(\log N)/4]$ contributes $O_\sigma(e^{-\sqrt N/4})$. On the remaining ray use $x^{-1}\le4/\log N$ and substitute $z=N e^{-2x}$; the resulting integral is bounded by a constant times $N^{-1+\sigma/2}\Gamma(1-\sigma/2)/\log N$.

Consequently a **global two-sided** upper bound for $|G(y)|$ of order $O_\epsilon(y^{1/2+\epsilon})$ for every $\epsilon>0$ would give $D_N\le O_\epsilon(N^{-3/4+\epsilon})$ through this identity, after adjusting $\epsilon$. More weakly, this estimate on the signed integral itself only at the specified [sparse starts](XiPrimeBlockMellin.md) suffices for the GRWTSK Mellin zero exclusion because $R_N\ge0$. These are conditional implications. Their premises are stronger in coverage or different in signed content from Alpöge's one-sided window assertion. A direct GRWTSK proof could instead bound the signed integral by the actual reserve there.

The negative curvature also contributes at the natural scale. For $v$ in a compact subinterval of $(0,\infty)$,
\[
 \tfrac12 N^{3/2}\log N\,F_N''(\sqrt N v)\ \longrightarrow\ K''(v),
 \qquad K(v)=v^{-1}\int_1^2 e^{-t/v^2}\,dt/t.
\]
Indeed the defining sums for $B(2\log(\sqrt N v))$ and their first two $v$-derivatives converge uniformly on each such subinterval to those of the integral by Riemann sums and differentiation of $(1-1/(Nv^2))^j$ with $1<j/N\le2$. The factor $\log N/[2\log(\sqrt N v)]$ tends to one with its first two derivatives tending to zero. Since $K$ is positive inside, vanishes at both ends and has derivative tending to zero at both ends, $K''$ takes both signs. Integrating on a fixed compact interval of each strict sign and using the upper bound gives
\[
 \int_1^\infty y^\sigma(F_N''(y))_\pm\,dy
       \asymp_\sigma N^{-1+\sigma/2}/\log N
       \qquad(0\le\sigma<2)
\]
for both choices of sign. Under a square-root bound for the absolute value of the ramp, the worst-case error from the negative-curvature part has the same order as the prime-square reserve. Its actual signed contribution depends on the arithmetic values of the ramp.

## Size of the reserve

The ordinary prime number theorem gives
\[
 R_N\sim \frac{\Gamma(3/4)}2
       \left(\int_1^2 t^{-7/4}\,dt\right)\frac{N^{-3/4}}{\log N}.
\]
For the squares put $Y=N^{1/4}$ and $u=p/Y$. On compact $u$ intervals, $N W_N(p^2)$ tends uniformly to $u^{-4}\int_1^2 e^{-t/u^4}dt/t$. The measures $(\log Y)/Y\sum_p\delta_{p/Y}$ tend weakly on compact subintervals of $(0,\infty)$ to $du$. To control tails use $NW_N(p^2)\le u^{-4}e^{-1/u^4}$. For $p<\sqrt Y$ this is exponentially small in $Y$; for $p\ge\sqrt Y$ the Chebyshev prime count on dyadic intervals gives a summable majorant, of order $a^{-3}e^{-1/(16a^4)}$ on $a\le p/Y<2a$. Thus the square contribution times $N^{3/4}\log N$ tends to twice the integral of the limiting profile. The gamma integral yields the coefficient displayed above. For $k\ge3$ the number of powers $p^k\le z$, with multiplicities, is $O(z^{1/3}\log(2z))$. Summing $y^{-2}e^{-N/y^2}$ over dyadic shells $y\asymp2^h\sqrt N$ bounds the remaining contribution by $O(N^{-5/6}\log N)$. This proves the asymptotic.

In particular, controlling the limsup of $N^{3/4}\log N\int G F_N''$ strictly below this explicit coefficient at the sampled starts would prove strict descent there. The transfer and the reserve evaluation leave that signed inequality unproved.

## What the shorter proof measures at a prime square

Let $\lambda_{d,q}(n)=\Lambda(n)(1-(n/d))\mathbf1_{n\equiv1\bmod q}$, where $(n/d)$ is the Legendre symbol. From the shorter source's definition of its centering constant $c_{d,q}$,
\[
 x R_{d,q}(x)=\sum_n \lambda_{d,q}(n)(x/n-1)_+
               -\frac{x\log x}{\varphi(q)}-c_{d,q}x .
\]
In the same sense as the untwisted identity above, pairing the second derivative against $F_N$ gives
\[
 \int_1^\infty y R_{d,q}(y) F_N''(y)\,dy
 =\sum_{p^h\equiv1\bmod q}\bigl(1-(p^h/d)\bigr)\frac{W_N(p^h)}{h}
      -\frac1{\varphi(q)}\int_1^\infty\frac{W_N(y)}{\log y}\,dy .
\]
The boundary estimates used above remain valid since $0\le\lambda_{d,q}\le2\Lambda$. The source's arithmetic inequality is one-sided in this specific remainder and retains its progression and twist. If $p\ne d$, then $(p^{2j}/d)=1$ for $j\ge1$. The coefficient in front of every such even power is zero. If $p=d$, the multiplier is one and, for this specialization, $d^2\equiv1\bmod 18480$. Thus the signed projection of this one remainder does not contain the full positive prime-square contribution of the ordinary GRWTSK block. This discrepancy exists even before one discusses numerical error. ## A sparse set of auxiliary twists suffices in the shorter proof

Fix $q=18480$, $a=9239$, and $1/2<\beta<1$. Let $\mathcal A_\beta$ be any set of primes $d\equiv a\pmod q$ on which, for each $d$ separately, there is $x_0(d,\beta)$ such that
\[
 R_{d,q}(x)\le x^{\beta-1}\qquad(x\ge x_0(d,\beta)).
\]
Here $R_{d,q}$ is exactly the shorter proof's $\mathrm{primeRemainder}$, with the weights $\Lambda(n)(1-(n/d))\mathbf1_{n\equiv1\bmod q}(1/n-1/x)$ for $n\le x$, and with the source's subtraction $\log(x)/\varphi(q)+c_{d,q}$.

**Claim.** If $\sum_{d\in\mathcal A_\beta}(\log d)/d=\infty$, all characters modulo $q$ are zero-free in $\Re s>\beta$ away from $s=1$. The weaker assumption that the counting function of $\mathcal A_\beta$ is not $O(X^{1-(\beta-1/2)/18})$ also suffices. The sets and thresholds may vary with $\beta$. Taking a sequence $\beta\downarrow1/2$ and the principal character gives ordinary RH via the functional equation. The criterion concerns all sufficiently large $x$ for each selected auxiliary prime.

**Proof.** Put $\delta=(\beta-1/2)/9$. For each fixed finite $H\ge0$, the source's $\mathrm{zero\_density\_quadTwist}$ bounds the number of bad primes $d\le X$ by $O_{q,\beta,H}(X^{1-\delta/2})$. A bad $d$ admits some Dirichlet character $\xi$ modulo $q$ whose quadratic twist by $d$ has a zero in $\Re s\ge\beta$, $|\Im s|\le H$. The source's congruence hypotheses hold since $8\mid q$, $a\equiv7\bmod8$ and $\gcd(a,q)=1$. In the dyadic shell $2^j<d\le2^{j+1}$ the contribution of bad primes to $\sum(\log d)/d$ is $O((j+1)2^{-j\delta/2})$. Summing the convergent geometric tail proves that the bad primes have finite weight. Either size assumption therefore supplies a $d\in\mathcal A_\beta$ outside the bad set. The weighted condition implies the counting condition by this same dyadic estimate applied to $\mathcal A_\beta$.

Given $\rho\ne1$ with $\Re\rho>\beta$, take $H=|\Im\rho|$ and use this $d$. For the transform of $R_{d,q}(e^v)$ the shorter proof's residue formula in this box becomes $m(s)/(\varphi(q)s(1-s))$ away from $s=0,1$, where $m(s)$ is the sum of the vanishing orders of the pole-removed $L$-functions of all characters modulo $q$. The selected twists contribute zero to these orders. The residues on the positive real segment to the right of $\beta$ are nonnegative as in the source. Its one-sided Landau theorem, using the bound for this particular $d$, forces the residue at $\rho$ to vanish. Thus $m(\rho)=0$, and each of the nonnegative orders in that sum vanishes. For the principal character, the extra Euler factors $\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-\rho})$ are nonzero in $\Re\rho>0$. This transfers exclusion to ordinary zeta.

This is a paper refinement of the source's choice of $d$. Its geometric proof supplies the arithmetic upper estimate for all the eligible primes. A GRWTSK-only proof through this cut would have to supply the eventual arithmetic estimates on a sufficiently large subfamily by independent means. ## The Weil and Hilbert–Pólya conclusions

A proof of the ordinary-prime window assertion feeds the same zero-exclusion step as the original ramp, through the comparison above. Once all the zeros are on the line, the full Weil explicit formula writes each smooth compact convolution square as a sum of absolute Fourier squares with the actual multiplicities. The diagonal operator on the sum of multiplicity spaces realizes those ordinates. This constructs the operator after the arithmetic estimate has done the work. An operator constructed independently from primes and proved to have this spectrum would require a further theorem. For the earlier fixed doubling blocks, the repository's [conditional prime-square bias](XiBernoulliOrdinaryPrimeBias.md) gives eventual strict descent once RH has been obtained. Deriving that descent directly from arithmetic still requires control of the signed curvature pairing.
