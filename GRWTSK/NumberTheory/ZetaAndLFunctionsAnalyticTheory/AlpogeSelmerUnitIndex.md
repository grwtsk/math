# Finite residue certificates for strict Selmer groups on real character fields

grwtsk contributors

At every odd coefficient prime, a finite matrix over a residue ring can certify that the full strict Selmer group in a tame character branch vanishes at every weight on that branch. Below we construct its columns from rational conductors. Six quadratic initial characters give complete vanishing columns in Case I at five. A relative version bounds class groups in layers of degrees up to 100 with one auxiliary prime. The unrestricted sum in CorG still requires upper bounds when the residual class component survives. ## A class-number test with all residual conductors allowed

Fix an odd prime \(p\). Let \(F\) be the field of a nontrivial even residual character \(\theta=\bar\psi^{-1}\omega^{-e}\), interpreted by its Teichmüller lift. Then \(G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)=\langle s\rangle\) is cyclic of order \(D\) prime to \(p\), and \(F\) is totally real. Write \(U=\mathcal O_F^\times/\{\pm1\}\) and \(I=\ker(\mathbb Z[G]\to\mathbb Z)\). For each nontrivial rational orbit of characters with kernel \(H\), write \(f_H\) for the conductor of \(F^H\), and put
\[
 u_H=N_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_{f_H})/F^H}(1-\zeta_{f_H}),\qquad
 e_H=D^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\left(\sum_{\ker\chi=H}\chi(g^{-1})\right)g.
\]
The inner sum is a rational algebraic integer. Using multiplicative group-ring exponents set \(v=\prod_H (u_H^{1-s})^{D e_H}\). Each quotient \(u_H^{1-s}\) is a unit. For a prime power conductor this follows from the unique prime above its rational prime in the cyclotomic field. For other conductors \(\Phi_{f_H}(1)=1\). The lattice \(C=v^I\subset U\) has full rank and
\[
                         v_p([U:C])=v_p(h_F).                              \tag{1}
\]

Indeed use the embedding indexed logarithms \(\log|g(x)|\). The determinant of the resulting convolution by the logarithms of \(v\) on the augmentation space is the product, in absolute value, of its nontrivial character transforms. Using inversion in the indexing only permutes characters. In integral coordinates obtained by dropping one embedding, a basis of \(I\) gives determinant of absolute value one, so that this determinant equals the regulator of \(C\). For \(\ker\chi=H\), the idempotent keeps the \(H\)-factor alone, multiplication by \(D\) gives \(D\), and inflation to \(G\) gives \(|H|\). The difference \(1-s\) gives a factor of absolute value \(|1-\chi(s)|\). Summing the logarithms of the norm defining \(u_H\) gives the primitive even logarithmic character sum at \(f_H\). Its absolute value is \(\sqrt{f_H}|L(1,\bar\chi)|\), by the Fourier series for \(\log|1-z|\) and the primitive Gauss sum. Thus every transform is nonzero. The conductor–discriminant formula and the analytic class-number formula identify the product of the last quantities with \(2^{D-1}h_F R_F\). In the remaining product all factors \(D|H|\) divide a power of \(D\), and \(\prod_{\chi\ne1}|1-\chi(s)|=D\). Dividing by \(R_F\) gives (1). Separating conductors avoids spurious zeros from imprimitive Euler factors. This direct argument uses the classical circular-unit method. Its subgroup and index factors are specified here. [Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, 2nd ed., Chapters 3–4 and 8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-1934-7) · [Sinnott, *On the Stickelberger ideal and the circular units of an abelian field*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0089530)

Fix \(b\ge1\). At rational primes \(\ell\equiv1\bmod\operatorname{lcm}(f_F,p^b)\), choose a residue embedding and a generator of \(\mathbb F_\ell^\times\). Reduction and discrete logarithm modulo \(p^b\) define a homomorphism on \(U\), since \(-1\) has trivial image. Choose \(D-1\) such homomorphisms and evaluate them on a basis of \(C\) obtained from \(I\). Their matrix \(B\) factors as \(VA\bmod p^b\), where \(A\) is the inclusion matrix in any basis of \(U\). Hence if \(\det B\ne0\bmod p^b\), then \(v_p(h_F)<b\). In particular a nonzero determinant modulo \(p\) kills \(S_e[p]\) by the finite-class-group description whenever \(\theta\) is locally nontrivial at \(p\). Every element of \(S_e\) has finite \(p\)-power order, so the whole group is zero. The same test applies to every positive weight giving the same residual character. [Finite Selmer identification and local hypotheses](AlpogeSelmerResidual.md)

Allowing different auxiliary primes makes the test complete for the valuation of the *whole class number*. Indeed put \(L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\operatorname{lcm}(f_F,p^b)})\). The kernel of \(F^\times/F^{\times p^b}\to L^\times/L^{\times p^b}\) vanishes. It injects by inflation–restriction into \(H^1(\operatorname{Gal}(L/F),\mu_{p^b})\), which vanishes because central complex conjugation acts as minus one. A root of a unit in \(F^\times\) is itself a unit. Thus Kummer duality for \(L(U^{1/p^b})/L\) realizes every functional on \(U/p^bU\) by an automorphism, after choosing a generator of the roots of unity. This finite extension is normal over \(\mathbb Q\) since the unit lattice is stable. Chebotarev gives a rational prime split in \(L\) and a prime above it with the specified Frobenius for each coordinate functional. The power-residue map at that prime gives the desired row up to an invertible scalar. Together these rows make \(V\) invertible. This argument supplies no bound at the original source scale on the least such primes or on \(h_F\). A nonzero class number component also requires its own action and local splitting checks to produce a strict class.

The same construction isolates the rational orbit of the faithful residual character, even when proper subfields have nontrivial \(p\)-class groups. Let \(e_*\) denote its rational idempotent, and put \(C_*=v^{I\cap e_*\mathbb Q[G]}\), \(U_*=U\cap e_*(U\otimes\mathbb Q)\). Then
\[
 v_p([U_*:C_*])=\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_p}
                 e_*(\mathrm{Cl}(F)\otimes\mathbb Z_p).
\]
To see this, apply (1) to every subfield \(F_k\) of degree \(k\mid D\). After tensoring with \(\mathbb Z_p\) all rational orbit idempotents are integral. The unit and ideal-class lattices from \(F_k\) identify with the invariants of its fixing subgroup \(J\) by norm and inclusion, whose composites are \(|J|\) and the relative trace. For units modulo signs this calculation also holds after tensoring. The construction of \(C\) is compatible on these invariants up to \(p\)-units. Indeed lift a group-ring coefficient from \(G/J\) by summing over its fibers. This gives the invariant augmentation lattice over \(\mathbb Z_p\), since \(|J|\) is invertible. Evaluating it on \(v\) raises each retained orbit factor to an additional power \(|J|\) from the trace, and replacing the multiplier \(k\) of the smaller orbit idempotent by \(D\) costs another factor \(D/k\). Orbit factors with nontrivial restriction to \(J\) are killed by that trace. These two factors are \(p\)-units. Thus the equality for \(F_k\) compares the sums of orbit lengths for all character orders dividing \(k\). Möbius inversion at \(D\) gives the displayed equality. The lattice \(I\cap e_*\mathbb Q[G]\) has rank \(\varphi(D)\), and can be computed by integer linear algebra. A nonsingular probe matrix of that size modulo \(p\) suffices for the full strict vanishing. It need not determine the separate lengths for distinct \(p\)-adic irreducible summands inside the orbit.

## Removing the prime-to-degree restriction in tested layers

Suppose \(E\subset\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)\) is totally real abelian and \(\tau\) is an involution in its group. Write \(K=E^{\langle\tau\rangle}\), \(t=[K:\mathbb Q]\). Assume \(m\) has two distinct prime divisors and that each character of \(E\) with \(\chi(\tau)=-1\) has primitive conductor divisible by every prime of \(m\). Put \(u=N_{\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)/E}(1-\zeta_m)\). Let \(C^-\) be generated by the \(t\) translates of \(u^{1-\tau}\) indexed by coset representatives. Let \(U^-=\ker(N_{E/K}:U_E\to U_K)\), with units taken modulo signs. For every odd \(p\), even if \(p\mid t\),
\[
                 v_p([U^-:C^-])=v_p(h_E/h_K).                              \tag{2}
\]

In coordinates given by one embedding per pair the difference vectors \(g-g\tau\) are an integral basis of the anti-invariant lattice. As before the determinant of the logarithms is \(\sqrt{|D_E/D_K|}\prod_{\chi(\tau)=-1}|L(1,\chi)|\). Indeed the norm from conductor \(m\) down to the primitive conductor gives \(1-\zeta_{f_\chi}\) by the cyclotomic distribution relation, since only exponents of primes already dividing that conductor increase. Write \(R^-\) for the determinant of a basis of \(U^-\) in these coordinates. Adding embedding rows in pairs in the total regulator gives \(2^{t-1}R_KR^-=[U_E:U_K U^-]R_E\). The quotient has exponent dividing two since \(x^2=N(x)(x/\tau x)\). Applying the two analytic class-number formulas gives the exact comparison \([U^-:C^-]=2^{2t-1}(h_E/h_K)/[U_E:U_K U^-]\), proving (2). At odd \(p\), norm and extension on class groups split off the plus summand, so (2) measures the total anti-invariant class length. Localizing at \(p\) can only decrease it. The residue matrix argument now applies to the translates generating \(C^-\). A bound at one deep level bounds classes seen at that level. Passing from a finite level to the full Selmer group requires further control. [Adjacent-level control and the full weight comparison](AlpogeSelmerTowerControl.md)

## Exact arithmetic in Case I

For \(p=5\), \(\psi=\chi_{-d}\), and odd \(e\), the two possible residual fields agree. Their degree-four field is cut out by \(\chi_{-d}\omega\) or its inverse. Adjoin the degree-\(5^n\) cyclotomic layer to obtain \(E_n\). Its group is cyclic of order \(4\cdot5^n\); take \(\tau\) its unique involution. Characters nontrivial on \(\tau\) have odd exponent on the tame quartic factor, hence their conductors contain five and all prime divisors of \(d\). Here \(-d\) is fundamental, \(d\equiv3\bmod4\), and \(5\nmid d\). Formula (2) applies to \(m=d\,5^{n+1}\), including the branch with nontrivial wild degree. Both residual characters belong to the anti-invariant summand.

For each of the following primitive initial characters the **full group at five vanishes for every positive odd weight**. Each row gives a split prime, its primitive root, and the determinant at layer zero modulo 3125. The result is a unit at five. Nontrivial local inertia is automatic. These initial conductors are coprime to 3, 5 and 7, so the auxiliary prime, auxiliary class number and next prime in the source are 7, 1 and 3 respectively. Five is outside the exceptional set and \(4\nmid2e\). The initial character can be used as its own host character. To reproduce the certificates, put \(P=p^b\), \(b=5\) at five and \(b=9\) at three, and put \(z=g^{(\ell-1)/m}\), \(w=g^{(\ell-1)/P}\) in \(\mathbb F_\ell\). At five let \(T=5^n\). For each unit \(a\bmod m\), multiply \(1-z^a\) into the bucket indexed modulo \(4T\) by \(\log_2(a\bmod5^{n+1})+2T\,\mathbf1_{\chi_{-d}(a)=-1}\). Write \(L_i=\log_w((B_i/B_{i+2T})^{(\ell-1)/P})\) for \(0\le i<2T\), extending by \(L_{i+2T}=-L_i\). The matrix is \((L_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<2T}\). At three use \(m=3763\cdot3^{n+1}\) and index by the sign of \(\chi_{11289}(a)\) together with \(a\equiv\pm4^i\bmod3^{n+1}\). Take the positive-to-negative ratio at each \(i\) and extend \(L_i\) periodically with period \(3^n\). The two different boundary signs reflect the actual Galois actions. All 13 determinants were reproduced by PARI/GP 2.17.4 and separately by integer trial division, modular powers, a complete discrete-log table and fraction-free Bareiss elimination in Python. For example the base row at \(d=47\) has \((L_0,L_1)=(3055,3102)\), giving \(-3055^2-3102^2\equiv821\bmod3125\).

A failed choice of auxiliary prime has no converse implication. For \(d=47\), \(\ell=2350001\) and \(g=6\) give \((2995,2840)\) and determinant \(1875\bmod3125\), divisible by \(5^4\), although the other prime in the table proves that the relative class length is zero.

There is additional information on the previously unresolved progression \(e\equiv21\bmod54\) for \(p=3,d=3763\). The strict group is **cyclic of order either 27 or 81** for every \(e\equiv21,75\bmod162\). Indeed its cyclotomic class module \(M\) over \(\mathbb Z_3[[T]]\), \(T=\gamma-1\), has first-layer coinvariants cyclic of order 27, with action 19 as computed in the preceding manuscript. Write \(V=M/3M\). In characteristic three the first-layer relation is \(T^3\), so \(\dim_{\mathbb F_3} V/T^3 V=1\). Nakayama implies \(V/TV\ne0\), and then \(TV=T^3V\). Applying Nakayama again to the finitely generated \(TV\) gives \(TV=0\). Thus \(M/3M\) has dimension one. Every finite abelian 3-quotient of the compact module \(M\) is generated by the image of one fixed lift of that basis, by ordinary finite-group Nakayama. The lift topologically generates \(M\), so every finite twisted group in its character dual is cyclic. The existing first-layer action gives a subgroup of order 27 on this progression. The primitive special values \(L(1-j,\chi_{-3763})\) have 3-adic valuation four at \(j=87,141\), by the exact generalized Bernoulli formula, checked separately by integer moments and rational Bernoulli-polynomial evaluation. The critical comparison gives the full upper bound 81 when \(2\cdot3^4\mid e+j\). These two congruences give precisely the stated progressions. The earlier critical value at \(j=33\) gives an upper bound \(3^6\) on \(e\equiv1425\bmod1458\). The actual orders on these finer progressions and the unrestricted third branch remain open.
