# The archimedean Fock factor of the normalized theta lift

Authors: grwtsk contributors.

The archimedean polynomial factor in Alpöge's Rallis scalar is exactly the product of the hook lengths of the selected positive and negative rows, with one copy per complex embedding. We derive it from complex Gaussian moments and compute the entire Gram pairing of the corresponding translated polynomials. This identifies the scalar for every rank, stage and coefficient-field embedding in the original `DeltaNorm` predicate. The full adelic norm requires additional matrix coefficients, Haar volumes and finite-place factors. ## One flag of corner minors

The archived Fock pairing is **linear in the first argument**,
\[
 [P,Q]_F=\sum_\alpha P_\alpha\overline{Q_\alpha}\prod_j\alpha_j!.
\]
For independent standard complex Gaussians \(X_j\), this equals \(\mathbb E[P(X)\overline{Q(X)}]\). The one-variable density is \(\pi^{-1}e^{-|z|^2}\,dx\,dy\). Angular integration kills unequal powers. Under \(t=|z|^2\), the remaining integral is \(\int_0^\infty t^k e^{-t}dt=k!\). Independence gives the stated factorials. This fixes the scale of every homogeneous degree. [Source definition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/FockGram/StatementFock.lean#L9-L15)

Let \(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_l\ge0\), \(d_k=\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}\), \(\lambda_{l+1}=0\), and write
\[
   \Delta_\lambda(X)=\prod_{k=1}^l \det(X_{1:k,1:k})^{d_k}.
\]
Only the first \(l\) rows and columns occur, so start with an \(l\)-square Gaussian matrix. The empty flag has norm one. For \(l>0\) perform column Gram–Schmidt, \(X=UR\), choosing a positive real diagonal in the upper triangular \(R\). The null set of singular matrices does not affect the integral. For each \(k\) the first \(k\) columns of \(UR\) only use the first \(k\) columns of \(U\). Thus
\[
 |\Delta_\lambda(X)|^2=|\Delta_\lambda(U)|^2\prod_{i=1}^l R_{ii}^{2\lambda_i}.
\]
Conditionally on the preceding columns, the residual of column \(i\) in their orthogonal complement is a standard complex Gaussian in dimension \(l-i+1\). Its squared norm has density \(e^{-t}t^{l-i}/(l-i)!\), by convolution of independent exponential variables. Its conditional law is independent of the preceding columns, giving independence of the successive squared norms. The joint law of \((U,R)\) is invariant under \((U,R)\mapsto(VU,R)\) for each fixed unitary \(V\), by unitary invariance of \(X\) and uniqueness of this triangular factorization. Averaging over probability Haar measure shows that \(U\) is Haar and independent of \(R\). The radial contribution is therefore \(\prod_i (\lambda_i+l-i)!/(l-i)!\).

For the angular contribution, take the tensor of \(d_k\) copies of the unit highest vector \(e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_k\) in each \(\bigwedge^k\mathbb C^l\), with the exterior basis orthonormal. Its matrix coefficient at \(U\) is the indicated product of leading determinants, up to conjugation depending on the inner-product convention. This unit vector belongs to the irreducible Cartan component of highest weight \(\lambda\). Indeed all tensor weights are obtained from the total highest weight by subtracting sums of positive roots, and the total highest weight space is the unique tensor line of the highest vectors. Complete reducibility yields exactly one summand with that highest weight. Averaging its rank-one orthogonal projector over \(U(l)\) gives the identity divided by the dimension, by Schur's lemma and the trace. The highest-vector matrix coefficient consequently has squared Haar norm \(1/\dim V_\lambda\). The unitary Weyl dimension formula gives \(\dim V_\lambda=\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j+j-i)/(j-i)\), also obtained from the alternating-determinant character formula by specializing its variables to one. Since \(\prod_{i<j}(j-i)=\prod_i(l-i)!\), the total squared Fock norm is
\[
             H_\lambda=\frac{\prod_i(\lambda_i+l-i)!}
                      {\prod_{i<j}(\lambda_i-\lambda_j+j-i)} .                 \tag{1}
\]
The highest-weight and dimension theorem used here concerns finite-dimensional representations of the compact unitary group. The source's archived proof instead applies a Capelli differential identity to multiply by successive minors. [Highest-weight theorem and unitary dimension formula, §§11.5–11.6](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~sengupta/notes/finitegrouprepresent%20%20.pdf) · [Archived differential calculation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/FoundationProofs/PolarisationOperatorApplyVariable.lean#L490-L509)

To compare (1) with the actual Young-diagram product, put \(b_i=\lambda_i+l-i\), and let \(\lambda'_j\) count the rows reaching column \(j\). For \(1\le j\le\lambda_i\), the hook length of cell \((i,j)\) is \(b_i-y_j\), where \(y_j=j-1+l-\lambda'_j\). These \(y_j\) are strictly increasing integers in \([0,b_i)\). No \(b_k\) equals \(y_j\): when \(k\le\lambda'_j\) we have \(b_k>y_j\), and otherwise \(b_k<y_j\). The \(l-i\) occupied positions \(b_k<b_i\) and the \(\lambda_i\) positions \(y_j\) thus exhaust the \(b_i\) possibilities. Multiplying distances from \(b_i\) gives the row's hook product times \(\prod_{k>i}(b_i-b_k)=b_i!\). This proves the stated hook comparison including repeated parts and zero rows. [Original corner weights and quotient](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/TorsionBlocks/BottomMinor.lean#L9-L42) · [Young-diagram definition in the scalar](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/BlockTheory/DivisorBase/TwistedLevelValues.lean#L34-L56)

## The polarized matrix coefficient

Let \(X\) have \(n\) rows and \(m\) columns of independent standard complex Gaussians, and let \(l\le\min(n,m)\). Allow arbitrary \(l\times n\) matrices \(A,C\) and \(m\times l\) matrices \(B,D\). Then
\[
 [\Delta_\lambda(AXB),\Delta_\lambda(CXD)]_F
       =H_\lambda\,\Delta_\lambda(AC^*)\,\Delta_\lambda(D^*B).                \tag{2}
\]
For the diagonal pairing first assume the two frames have full rank. Write \(A=L U\) with \(L\) lower triangular and \(UU^*=I_l\), and \(B=V R\) with \(R\) upper triangular and \(V^*V=I_l\). Extend these orthonormal rows and columns to unitary bases. Then \(UXV\) is a standard square Gaussian block, since left and right unitary transformation preserves the joint standard Gaussian distribution. All leading determinants multiply by the corresponding leading determinants of \(L\) and \(R\). The squared diagonal factors are the leading determinants of \(AA^*\) and \(B^*B\), proving (2) on the diagonal. Polynomial continuity extends it to all ranks. To polarize, the difference of the two sides is holomorphic polynomial in the entries of \(A,B\) and antiholomorphic polynomial in those of \(C,D\). Its zero diagonal gives a polynomial identity in the real and imaginary parts of \(A,B\). The change of complex variables from those parts to a variable and its formal conjugate is invertible; hence every coefficient vanishes. This step determines the order \(D^*B\) as well as \(AC^*\), including when either leading minor vanishes. [Triangular leading-block calculation](AlpogeFockCornerGram.lean)

Read the archived variables \((\tau,j,i)\) as the entry in **row** \(i\), **column** \(j\) of \(X_\tau\). For its positive signature \(p\) the source takes the leading \(k\) rows in the first \(k\) columns. For its negative signature \(q\), it takes the bottom \(k\) rows **in increasing order**, with columns \(p+1,\ldots,p+k\). Reversing the bottom rows converts these to leading nested minors with signs \((-1)^{k(k-1)/2}\). Each sign appears twice in a pairing of translates of the same flag and cancels. Column sets of the two factors and variable sets of distinct \(\tau\) are disjoint. Hence their Gram factors multiply even when their sets of row labels overlap. [Original variables, minors and product](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/IndefiniteReal/LocalNewformFunction.lean#L288-L305)

More explicitly, use the actual polynomial action \(T_u=\mathrm{fockGL}(m,w,B_0)(u)\), \(g_u=B_0^{-1}uB_0\) componentwise, and \(d(u)=\prod_\tau\det(u_\tau)^{-c_\tau}\). Let \(\Delta^+_a(M)\) use leading principal minors and \(\Delta^-_a(M)\) trailing principal minors with the same successive weight differences. For the true polynomial \(\delta\), (2) gives its complete orbit Gram matrix
\[
 [T_u\delta,T_v\delta]_F =
 d(u)\overline{d(v)}
 \prod_\tau H_{a^+_\tau}H_{a^-_\tau}
   \Delta^+_{a^+_\tau}\!\left(g_{u,\tau}^{-1}(g_{v,\tau}^{-1})^*\right)
   \Delta^-_{a^-_\tau}\!\left(g_{u,\tau}^{\mathsf t}(g_{v,\tau}^{\mathsf t})^*\right).       \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the positive columns in the archived substitution are multiplied on the left by \(g_u^{-1}\), the negative columns by \(g_u^{\mathsf t}\). The first and second row frames in (2) are their leading or reversed trailing rows as appropriate. The column frames select the disjoint variables. The character factor outside the substitution appears exactly as in (3). Bilinearity with conjugation in the second argument extends this formula to arbitrary sums in the source's span of translates. In particular it preserves the original norm on each diagonal translate when the conjugated frame action is unitary. Arbitrary invertible changes of frame are covered by (3) without an isometry assumption. [Actual substitutions and determinant twist](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/TranslateMemCoefLatFn.lean#L34-L77) · [Original harmonic orbit span](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/TranslateMemCoefLatFn.lean#L76-L94)

In the source's chosen basis, put \(g_{u,\tau}=\operatorname{diag}(t_{\tau,1},\ldots,t_{\tau,n})\). Each positive corner scales by the product of the first \(k\) inverse diagonal entries; each negative corner scales by the product of the last \(k\) diagonal entries. Multiplication by the successive weight differences telescopes. The determinant character subtracts \(c_\tau\) at every site, including sites in neither flag. In the range \(p_\tau+q_\tau\le n\) of the source's Weight.Good condition, this gives the exact weight of the actual polynomial
\[
       T_u\delta=\left(\prod_{\tau,j} t_{\tau,j}^{-w.\mathrm{lam}_{\tau,j}}\right)\delta .
\]
In particular the signs of the dual weight and the determinant shift are fixed on each diagonal site, also for nonunitary rational frames. ## Return to the Rallis scalar

For each stage \(i\), embedding \(\sigma\) and real-place label \(\rho\), the source dimensions are \(m=\operatorname{rank} W_i\), \(n=\operatorname{rank} V_i\), with \(p+q=m\le n\). The selected positive and negative weights are decreasing partitions even after the coefficient embedding reindexes the label. On the positive block the strictly decreasing integral \(\mathrm{lamAt}\) drops by at least one at each step, canceling the unit increase of the affine correction in the floor. On the negative block its order is reversed and its sign negated, with the opposite unit correction. Taking floors and then their nonnegative parts preserves these inequalities; at the end of each block the source extends the rows by zero. Thus setting \(u=v=1\) in (3) proves precisely
\[
 [\mathrm{deltaAtEmb}(D,i,\sigma),\mathrm{deltaAtEmb}(D,i,\sigma)]_F
      =\prod_\rho\mathrm{hookOf}(p_\rho,a^+_\rho)\,
                    \mathrm{hookOf}(q_\rho,a^-_\rho).
\]
Since the rows are decreasing, sorting them in \(\mathrm{hookOf}\) preserves them. Each factor is a positive integer. Taking its logarithm gives the source's full \(\mathrm{mainTerm}\) for these embeddings with no approximation. This verifies the polynomial factor multiplying the global norm, including the two separate hook products and the absence of any extraneous square or archimedean volume. For the full archimedean matrix-coefficient clause of the inner-product formula one must also identify the oscillator action on the specified global theta test and the matrix coefficients of the discrete series of \(U(p,q)\) at **every** real group element. The polynomial action in (3) is the archived action on the coefficient side. The argument does not supply those discrete-series coefficients, the formal degree for the prescribed Haar normalization, or the global theta pairing. These are distinct obligations in the original scalar.
