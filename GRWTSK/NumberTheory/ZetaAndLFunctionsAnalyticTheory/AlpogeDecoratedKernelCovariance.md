## Ordinary finite Fourier integral

At a row prime \(P\) put \(q=q_P\), let \(R_P\) be the valuation ring in the number field, and write \(D=D_P\). At every prime in the two selected row sets, \(q\) is a uniformizer with residue field \(\mathbb F_q\). Thus
\[
        \{j/q^A:0\le j<q^{A+B}\}
\]
is a transversal of \(q^{-A}R_P/q^BR_P\). One proof successively removes the residue in \(\{0,\ldots,q-1\}\) and divides by the uniformizer, starting with \(q^A x\). Distinct representatives have distinct residues modulo \(q^{A+B}\). This uses both ramification index one and residue degree one. Let \(k\) be the cardinality of the source's free matrix entries **FC**. In \(X(x,t)=\mathrm{xx1}(x,t)\), the first rectangle equals the distinguished map \(x\), the auxiliary staircase has diagonal ones and zeros below it, and exactly the free entries are filled by \(t\). The integrand is
\[
 F_x(t)=\Phi(X(x,t))\,\psi(-\ell(X(x,t))),\qquad
 \ell(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}X_{n+j,n'+j+1}.
\]
Here \(c'=c+1\), \(n,n'\) are respectively target and source ranks and \(\Phi=\mathrm{omPhi}\) includes the actual frame normalizations. The sum at \((A,B)\) carries the weight \(q^{-Bk}\) and **XiAt** also includes the reciprocal of the exact target stabilizer volume **volN**. That latter scalar is independent of \(x\) with the two class representatives fixed. For admitted representatives the local support and entrywise period of \(\Phi\) are contained in \(q^{-m}\operatorname{Mat}(R_P)\) and contain \(q^m\operatorname{Mat}(R_P)\), respectively. At flag primes this follows by undoing the diagonal lattice scaling of the flag coordinates and using the single residue reduction of the integral matrix; the admitted frames preserve the scaled lattices. At a tame prime the source and target lattices are sandwiched by the standard lattice and its stage-dependent dilate. Transporting an integral matrix with its residue test through these inclusions requires at most the stated chain depth \(m\). Nonzero terms also satisfy \(q^D\ell(X)\in R_P\). In the flag lattices the source exponent of auxiliary column \(j+1\) exceeds that of target row \(j\) by exactly one, and \(D=1\). At tame primes the sandwich bounds give at most the target stage for these entries, within the chosen \(D\). The actual \(\psi\) is multiplicative under addition of arguments in \(q^{-D}R_P\) and trivial on \(R_P\). These bounds justify every use of its character law on nonzero terms. The same support and periodicity hold in the free coordinates. Indeed they are actual entries of \(X\), and \(X(x,t+q^m v)-X(x,t)\) is \(q^m\) times a free matrix integral when \(v\) is integral. Its \(\ell\)-value is integral as well. For \(A,B\ge m\), refining \(B\) by one gives exactly \(q^k\) lifts of each class, canceling the change in the weight. Increasing \(A\) adds only zero terms after identifying the original cosets. Thus the original selected cutoff can be deepened freely. The supplied constants have \(m\le M\le\min(A,B)\) in this standard construction. Now let \(r'\) be a target parabolic lift restricting to \(g'\). Left multiplication sends \(X(x,t)\) to \(X(g'x,Tt)\), where \(T\) reads the new free entries and is affine bijective since the inverse lift preserves the same staircase. On its trailing block the simple-superdiagonal of a product adds: in position \((j,j+1)\) only the summation indices \(j,j+1\) contribute. For the rectangular block append the last unit row, and pad the target multiplier with a last identity coordinate. The absent final simple-root entry of the target multiplier contributes zero. Its lower-left block vanishes, so the distinguished rectangle cannot affect this calculation. This gives \(\ell(r'X)=\ell(X)+n_1(r')\) including the endpoint; the analogous right multiplication uses the whole source block. The determinant of either lift is that of its distinguished diagonal block. The inverse Fourier phase therefore introduces \(\psi(n_1(r'))^{-1}\). A general \(T\) need not preserve the rectangular box. Take the framed Hom lattice \(H\) of maps carrying the actual source period lattice to the target lattice, in the frames used by the integral. Choose \(b\ge0\) with \(q^b\operatorname{Mat}(R_P)\subset H\subset q^{-b}\operatorname{Mat}(R_P)\); both inclusions follow by clearing the finitely many denominators of the frames and their inverses. The support lies in \(H\), and shifts from \(qH\) preserve \(\Phi\) by the transported residue definition. The lifted multiplication \(\sigma\) preserves \(H\) in both directions. Write \(Uu=X(0,u)-X(0,0)\) and take
\[
 M_0=q^{B'}R_P^k\;\subset\; M_* = \{u:q^{-D}Uu\in H\}
        \;\subset\; M_1=q^{-A'}R_P^k,
 \qquad B'\ge b+D,\quad A'\ge b .
\]
Choose \(A',B'\) at least the original cutoffs as well. Differences satisfy \(U(Tt-Ts)=\sigma U(t-s)\), so \(T\) acts bijectively on the whole ambient quotient by \(M_*\). The integrand is constant on \(M_*\)-cosets: \(Uu=q^Dz\), \(z\in H\), and \(D\ge1\) gives a \(qH\) perturbation and integral Fourier increment. Both the untransformed support and the preimage of the transformed support lie in \(M_1\), since the lifted maps and their inverses preserve \(H\) and the original free coordinates are among the entries. In the finite quotient \(M_1/M_0\) every coarse class of \(M_1/M_*\) has \([M_*:M_0]\) representatives. Sum first on these fibres. The induced ambient bijection preserves the sum on the supported coarse classes even when it moves \(M_1/M_*\) as a set. The same constant multiplicity and the same Haar weight occur on both sides. This supplies the required exact change of variables before returning to the original cutoff. ## The coupled coset and the terminal Fourier factor

The first coupled column \(i=m_2=m-2\) evaluates the finite datum at the joined matrix \([x\mid y]\) in the specified frame. The possible \(y\)'s form a coset \(Y\) with the following local bounds and periods in the frame of its underlying lattice. At flag primes \(y\in q^{-1}R_P^n\) with periods \(R_P^n\); at tame primes \(y\in q^{-M}R_P^n\) with periods \(q^M R_P^n\). At a tame prime, \([0\mid z]\in q^M\operatorname{Mat}(R_P)\) for every such period, and the depth-\(m\) residue datum is unchanged. At a flag prime the auxiliary source column corresponds to the line scaled by \(q\). For \(z\in R_P^n\), the map \([0\mid q^{-1}z]\) carries the scaled source lattice to the target lattice, hence \([0\mid z]\in qH\). This gives the integral period at this prime even though the uniform matrix bound by itself gives only \(q^m\). At all primes a matrix perturbation in \(q^BR_P\) preserves the joined datum as \(B\ge m\). This independently accounts for the uses of **psiInv** and **psiInvX**. A target stabilizer in this column fixes the point *and* sends \(Y\) to itself. Therefore the chosen representative and its image differ by precisely the allowed local period. The transformed joined matrix has both columns moved; the residue law for its target lift contributes the prescribed level character and the inverse determinant twist. At \(i=m_2+1\) split the matrix into its projection and last row \(\eta\) in the framed **XiTop**. The scalar **ftY** is exactly
\[
  C_P\,1_{b\eta\in R_P^n}\,\psi(-\eta y),\qquad
 (b,c,C_P)=
 \begin{cases}
 (1,q,1)&P\text{ a flag prime},\\
 (q^M,q^M,q^{-Mn})&P\text{ a tame prime}.

Here \(cy\in R_P^n\). For \(\delta\in cR_P^n\) the indicator is unchanged on its support and off it. On the support \(bc\,\eta y\) is integral because it is the sum of products of the integral entries of \(b\eta,cy\). Also \(\delta y\) is integral because \(\delta/c,cy\) are integral. Thus the restricted character law applies at depth one and \(2M\) respectively and the factor is invariant under \(\eta\mapsto\eta+\delta\). A change of \(y\) by \(bR_P^n\) is likewise invisible on support. The factor \(q^{-Mn}\) is retained, in agreement with the volume of \(q^M R_P^n\) for unit volume of \(R_P^n\). The local denominator sum in this argument is sharp for a character specified only on \(q^{-D}R_P\). For \(q=2,M=1,D=1\), take \(\eta=y=1/2,\delta=2\). The arguments change from \(-1/4\) to \(-5/4\). ## Return to the two complex form spaces

The three local identities are multiplied with the common **phiT** indicator. At the auxiliary \(T(u)\) primes, a fixed decorated point preserves the lattice and decoration conditions defining this indicator. Its additive period there contains \(T(u)\) times the local Hom lattice. Taking
\[
             c_B=T(u)\left(\prod_{P\in\mathrm{rowSG}}q_P\right)^B
\]
also gives the required additive periods at all row primes. In the terminal column this uses \(B\ge M\) for the Fourier phase and \(B\ge1\) for the projected residue. Distinct flag and twist prime sets account once each for their level character; the product identity multiplies coefficients, so no nonvanishing assumption on an indicator is necessary. With \(\gamma\) fixing the target, set \(z=\det_E\gamma\) and let \(k\) be the product of the target level characters. Every local passive determinant is the inverse determinant of the corresponding local right frame. The resulting active law is
\[
              \Xi(\gamma x)\prod_{P\in\mathrm{rowSG}}\mu_{P,i}(z)=k\,\Xi(x).
\]
[Period modulus](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/Columns/FiniteSumNonzeroLargeDepth.lean#L1108-L1116) · [Three selected branch identifications and periods](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/SatakeParameters/ClassSumTermLawStabiliserTarget.lean#L25-L72)

Since \(\gamma\) and its inverse preserve the target lattice, \(z\) is a norm-one global unit. In the tied Hecke character, the factors on units outside the row set are trivial at the unramified places. Evaluating its principal idèle identity gives
\[
 P=\sigma_0\!\left(\prod_P\mu_{P,i}(z)\right)
      =\prod_\tau \tau(z)^{-e_{i,\tau}}
      =\prod_\tau \tau(z)^{-s_{i,\tau}}.
\]
In the last step \(e_{i,\tau}=s_{i,\tau}+N_i/2\), and the principal ideal character **IsChi** gives \(\prod_\tau\tau(z)=1\). The finite-label period just proved allows the sum over its actual translation quotient to be reindexed under \(x\mapsto\gamma x\). The signed translation transports the Gaussian factor exactly by unitarity, and the selected even-rank vacuum is invariant by the split-dyadic comparison. Hence the transformed theta function is \((\sigma_0(k)/P)\Theta\). The output determinant shift restores \(P\), giving the level character \(\sigma_0(k)\) required by **LawAt**. In particular the determinant shift cannot in general be dropped just because \(\det_\mathbb C\gamma=1\): the exponents \(s_{i,\tau}\) can vary with \(\tau\). ## Independent formal and finite checks

The companion's four public theorems prove uniform multiplicity for refined finite additive quotients, invariance of a supported sum under a moving cutoff, the simple-root product formula and exact invariance of the truncated Fourier factor over any field with an integral subring. They elaborate under the pinned root toolchain, passed fresh kernel replay, and their transitive axiom reports contain only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The source-specific identifications and global complex statement are manuscript arguments. The full archive-specific **KFi** predicate has no fresh native proof in this module. Independently, exact finite arithmetic checked 36 normalized moving-cutoff sums, 1,890 simple-root values and 4,608 unequal-denominator Fourier tests, including nine cases where weakening the domain of a partially specified character loses the period.
