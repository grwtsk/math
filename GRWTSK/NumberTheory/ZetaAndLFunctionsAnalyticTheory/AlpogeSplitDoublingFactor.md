# The unramified split factor in the theta-lift norm

grwtsk contributors

At a split finite place the two standard Euler factors and the normalizing polynomial can all be recovered by counting lattices. The same count bounds both Satake multisets in the exponent required by `scalar_bound_lowC`. The input is the source's spherical Hecke eigenvector and its bounded matrix coefficient. The global theta inner-product formula remains a separate hypothesis. ## The local integral

Let \(v\) split in the CM extension \(E/F\), choose \(w\mid v\), and write \(k=F_v\simeq E_w\), \(O=\mathcal O_k\), \(\pi\) for a uniformizer and \(q=|O/\pi O|\). Use the split chart of the self-dual lattice, with \(L=O^r\), \(G=\mathrm{GL}_r(k)\), \(K=\mathrm{GL}_r(O)\), \(r\ge1\). Give \(G\) Haar measure with \(\operatorname{vol}(K)=1\). Write \(c(g)=\ell(\rho(g)x)\) for the bounded spherical coefficient of the source, with Satake parameters \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_r\). The vector \(x\) is \(K\)-fixed and the coefficient is left \(K\)-invariant. The unramified twist on the graph is \(z=\chi_w(\pi)^{-1}\), with \(|z|=1\). For \(\Re s>r/2-1\), we obtain
\[
 \int_G \phi_s(\iota(g,1))c(g)\,dg
 =c(1)\,
 \frac{\prod_{j=1}^r(1-q^{-(2s+j)})}
 {\prod_{i=1}^r(1-z\beta_i q^{-(s+1/2)})(1-(z\beta_i)^{-1}q^{-(s+1/2)})}.
 \tag{1}
\]
When \(c(1)=0\) the integral vanishes identically; in this case interpret the right side as the identically zero rational function wherever a denominator vanishes. The archived local statement uses \(\Re s>r\) and Lean's total division. For \(c(1)\ne0\), the proof below gives nonvanishing of every displayed denominator throughout the larger half-plane. At the second place \(w'\mid v\) the source has the reciprocal twist and inverse Satake multiset. Thus (1) gives exactly \(c(1)L_v(s+1/2)/d_v(s)\). A coset \(gK\) is the full lattice \(M=gL\) and has mass one. Let \(I=L\cap M\), \(A=\operatorname{length}_O(L/I)\), \(B=\operatorname{length}_O(M/I)\). On \(M\oplus L\) the intersection with the diagonal is \(I\), and the quotient by that intersection maps by subtraction onto \(M+L\). Relative to the original doubled lattice, the diagonal and quotient determinants have absolute values \(q^{-A}\) and \(q^B\). Their ratio is the Siegel height \(q^{-A-B}\), while \(\operatorname{ord}\det g=A-B\). Thus the normalized graph section is \(U^A V^B\) with \(U=zq^{-(s+r/2)}\), \(V=z^{-1}q^{-(s+r/2)}\). This also gives its value at the identity and fixes the sign of the unramified twist. ## Counting the intermediate lattice

For a function on full lattices let \(D_U f(L)=\sum_{N\subseteq L}U^{\ell(L/N)}f(N)\), restricting to full sublattices. Define \(H_V f(N)=\sum_{M\supseteq N}V^{\ell(M/N)}f(M)\). In their composition, the intermediate \(N\) runs over every full sublattice of \(I\). Length additivity gives the coefficient of \(f(M)\) as \(U^A V^B Z_r(UV)\), where
\[
 Z_r(T)=\sum_{N\subseteq I}T^{\ell(I/N)}=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-q^jT)^{-1}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed upper triangular column Hermite representatives with diagonal \(\pi^{e_i}\), \(1\le i\le r\), have \(r-i\) entries in row \(i\) independently reduced modulo \(\pi^{e_i}\). They are unique after fixing representatives for each residue ring: successively project onto the last coordinate, choose a lift of its ideal generator, and reduce the earlier coordinates from the bottom upward using the previously chosen columns for the kernel. Their number is \(\prod_i q^{(r-i)e_i}\) and their colength is \(\sum_i e_i\). The same series counts superlattices by duality. Both one-sided sums on bounded functions converge absolutely for \(|U|,|V|<q^{-(r-1)}\). In this range their composition is absolutely convergent, including after taking absolute values of both weights and bounding \(f\) by its supremum. Taking \(N=I\) in that positive double sum bounds the absolute sum of the graph section as well.

Let \(T_j f(L)\) sum over \(\pi L\subseteq N\subseteq L\) with \(\dim(L/N)=j\), using residue-field dimension. The source gives \(T_j c=q^{j(r-j)/2}e_j(\beta)c\) on all lattices by translating its vector identity. This suffices to compute \(D_Uc\). Formally in \(U\),
\[
 D_U\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j q^{j(j-1)/2}U^j T_j=1.
 \tag{3}
\]
For a fixed endpoint \(M\subseteq L\), the intervening lattice \(N\) satisfies \(M\subseteq N\subseteq L\) and \(\pi N\subseteq M\). The possible \(N/M\) are the subspaces of \((L/M)[\pi]\). If its dimension is \(h\), the remaining coefficient after removing \(U^{\ell(L/M)}\) is \(\sum_j{h\brack j}_q(-1)^jq^{j(j-1)/2}=\prod_{i=0}^{h-1}(1-q^i)\). To see the polynomial identity, count \(j\)-dimensional subspaces according as they lie in a fixed hyperplane or meet it in dimension \(j-1\). The second class has \(q^{h-j}\) lifts for each intersection, yielding the Gaussian binomial recurrence and the product by induction. The coefficient is one at \(M=L\) and zero otherwise, since every nonzero finite-length module over \(O\) has nonzero \(\pi\)-torsion. [Original minuscule Hecke operators](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SatakeDualWeyl/Statement.lean#L195-L208) · [Actual eigenvalue and `IsSatVecGL` quantifiers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/Foundations/IsSatVecUOfIsSphericalU.lean#L86-L117)

The factor multiplying \(D_U c\) in (3) is \(\prod_i(1-q^{(r-1)/2}\beta_i U)\), since \(j(j-1)/2+j(r-j)/2=j(r-1)/2\). For superlattices the elementary operator in degree \(j\) equals \(T_r^{-1}T_{r-j}\), by sending \(N\supseteq L\) to \(\pi N\subseteq L\). The unit \(e_r(\beta)=\prod_i\beta_i\) gives the same polynomial with each \(\beta_i\) inverted, and the dual lattice argument gives (3) with the reversed inclusions. Therefore \(D_U H_Vc(L)\) is \(c(1)\) divided by the two respective polynomials as a formal series at \(L=O^r\). Comparing with (2) supplies the inverse factor \(Z_r(UV)^{-1}\). Substituting \(U,V\) gives (1). As an analytic identity, multiply through by the two polynomials first; absolute convergence extends this identity throughout the stated region even if \(c(1)=0\).

For \(c(1)\ne0\), (2) bounds \(|D_Uc(L)|\le\|c\|_\infty Z_r(|U|)\) in \(|U|<q^{-(r-1)}\). The polynomial times this holomorphic series equals the nonzero constant \(c(1)\), so none of its roots lies in that disk. The superlattice series gives the second inequality in
\[
  |\beta_i|\le q^{(r-1)/2},\qquad |\beta_i^{-1}|\le q^{(r-1)/2}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The public local interface `ParamBoundOn` asks only for exponent \(r/2\). Formula (4) recovers the sharper exponent of `scalar_bound_lowC` directly from the bounded coefficient. The trivial spherical representation attains the upper bound: its one-sided series equals (2), whose nearest pole is at \(q^{-(r-1)}\).

## The split-place contribution to the error

At the Rallis point \(2s=t\), the source has \(t\ge r+5\). Put \(h=(t+2-r)/2\). Each of the \(2r\) twisted parameters in (1) has absolute value at most \(q^{-h}\) after the shift. The inverse normalizer is \(\prod_{j=1}^r(1-q^{-(t+j)})\), also the probability that a uniformly random \(r\times(r+t)\) residue matrix has independent rows: after \(i\) independent choices its row span has \(q^i\) elements. Hence the normalized local factor \(F_v\) in (1) with \(c(1)=1\) satisfies
\[
 -\log|F_v|\le 2r q^{-h}+4q^{-(t+1)}.
\]
Indeed \(\log|1-x|\le |x|\) and \(-\log(1-y)\le y/(1-y)\) for \(0\le y<1\); summing the latter over \(j\) gives the displayed bound since \(q\ge2\). If \(f=[F:\mathbb Q]\) and \(B(d)=2^{-d}(1+2/(d-1))\), the sum of these losses over good split places is at most \(2rfB(h)+4fB(t+1)\). There are at most \(f\) places above each rational prime \(p\), each of norm at least \(p\), and \(\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-d}\le B(d)\) by integral comparison. Absolute convergence of the logarithms also follows from \(|\log|1-x||\le |x|/(1-|x|)\). The two places of \(E\) above a split \(v\) already occur in the \(2r\) standard factors. The scalar itself uses `quadSign D v`, which is one exactly when two primes lie over \(v\). The \(v\)-part of `dOff` is the product of \((1-q^{-i})^{-1}\) over the half-open integer interval \(t<i\le n_V=r+t\), precisely the reciprocal of the numerator in (1). Its `eulerOff` product separately indexes primes of \(E\) outside the chosen set and all \(r\) entries. In the scalar comparison the estimate above is restricted to good split places outside `finS R t`, the image of `R.Pg ∪ R.Q t`. A level or twist place requires its own test vector and measure normalization. Inert places have a unitary spherical Hecke algebra and different Tate factors. The ramified contributions, the identification of the genuine global theta matrix coefficient with the factorizable local integrand, and the Petersson covolume in (S6) remain open here. For comparison, the classical doubling computation gives the normalized unramified quotient, and Liu's even-rank unitary formula shows the splitting character in its Tate denominator becomes trivial at a split place. A central cell has mass one. At trivial twist and trivial spherical coefficient the shell masses in degrees zero through six are \(1,6,20,54,134,318,734\), agreeing with \((1-X^2)(1-2X^2)/((1-X)^2(1-2X)^2)\).
