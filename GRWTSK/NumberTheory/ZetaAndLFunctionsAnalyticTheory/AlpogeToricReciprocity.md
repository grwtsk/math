# Artin transport of the CM lattice values and their toric ratios

grwtsk contributors

Let \(K\) be the imaginary quadratic field with the specified embedding \(\iota\) and element \(\delta^2=-d\), \(d>0\). Let \(E\) be CM, unramified at every finite prime over its maximal real subfield \(F\), with the CM type \(\Phi=(\phi_i)\) consisting of the embeddings over \(\iota\). Then \(E=FK\) and \(m=[F:\mathbf Q]=|\Phi|\ge2\): if \(F=\mathbf Q\), its imaginary quadratic extension would have unit absolute discriminant. Use the source conductor \(c=\mathfrak f\prod_{q\in Q}q\), its order \(\mathcal O_c=\mathcal O_F+c\mathcal O_E\), and \(L_y=E\cap y\widehat{\mathcal O}_c\) for an arbitrary finite idele. Write \(k=2s\), \(s\ge1\), \(h=\sum_i\kappa_i\), \(w=km+2h\) and
\[
 X_{\Omega,\kappa}(S)=\operatorname{cmConst}(|\mathrm{disc}F|,d,k,\kappa) S/\Omega^w .
\]
The argument uses the classical main theorem of complex multiplication for elliptic curves, rationality and the functional equation of totally real partial zeta values, the characteristic-zero modular q-expansion principle and Hecke continuation. It also uses the analytic Fourier and jet calculation detailed in [the lattice manuscript](AlpogeCMFourierLattice.md) and the Euler coefficients in [the class transport manuscript](AlpogePicardClassTransport.md). We spell out which parts extend to the broader field and conductor hypotheses of the present problem. ## Artin direction, differential and torsion coordinates

Take the archimedean component to be one. The archive's Artin map sends a uniformizer to the **inverse** of arithmetic Frobenius in each unramified abelian quotient. Its **IsSymbol** condition inverts the map, so that \(g|_{K^{\rm ab}}=\mathrm{rec}_K(t)\) for the arithmetic reciprocity map. The equality of maps follows on every finite abelian quotient by uniqueness of global reciprocity (or density of unramified Frobenius classes together with approximation in a ray class group). A classical reference using inverse-arithmetic Artin is Milne, Theorem 9.17. Apply that theorem with idele \(t^{-1}\). The reflex norm for the elliptic type is the identity. It supplies a uniformization of the conjugate curve with lattice \(\mathfrak a=K\cap t^{-1}\widehat{\mathcal O}_K\), and the torsion map is multiplication by \(t^{-1}\) between the finite adelic quotients. Concretely \(x\bmod\mathcal O_K\) maps to the unique class \(x'\bmod\mathfrak a\) satisfying \(t x'-x\in\widehat{\mathcal O}_K\); existence follows by additive approximation at the finitely many involved places. This includes the action of the unit part of \(t\) on the level. S. Milne, *Complex Multiplication*, 2020, Remark 9.15 and Theorem 9.17](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CM.pdf#page=80)

For any \(\Omega_0\ne0\) use the Weierstrass curve of the lattice \(\Omega_0\iota(\mathcal O_K)\), in the model \(Y^2=4X^3-G_2X-G_3\) with differential \(dX/Y\). Here the symbols \(G_2,G_3\) in the equation denote the Weierstrass invariants of weights four and six. On the analytically uniformized conjugate curve the conjugate algebraic differential pulls back to \(\Omega_1\,dz\) for a nonzero scalar, since invariant holomorphic differentials on the additive uniformizing line are constant. In these normalized coordinates the elliptic functions of weights 2 and 3 are \(\wp_{\mathfrak a}(z)/\Omega_1^2\) and \(\wp'_{\mathfrak a}(z)/\Omega_1^3\). This follows from the principal parts in the differential coordinate and the short Weierstrass equation; the absence of a quadratic term fixes the constant in the first coordinate. Their values at all transported nonzero torsion points are the conjugates of the analogous source coordinates. The resulting weights four and six give the conjugated invariants as well. Any ordered period basis for the resulting lattice now supplies **TorsTransport**, including the normalized derivative. This proves the manuscript version of **PeriodEx** even when the initial \(\Omega_0\) is arbitrary. The geometric theorem applies over \(\mathbf C\) and takes the automorphism on the algebraic model; no continuous action of \(g\) on analytic functions or on a period is being used. The weight-two correction also has an algebraic transport calculation. For an embedded fractional ideal lattice \(\Lambda\) write
\[
 S_2(\Lambda)=\lim_{v\downarrow0}\sum_{0\ne z\in\Lambda}z^{-2}|z|^{-2v}.
\]
For \(\Lambda=b(\mathbf Z\tau+\mathbf Z)\), \(b>0\), its value is \(b^{-2}(G_2(\tau)-\pi/\Im\tau)\), where here \(G_2(\tau)\) is the holomorphic weight-two Eisenstein sum. Let \(u=\iota(\delta)\); it belongs to the lattice multiplier order, and \(u^2=-d\), \(|u|^2=d\). Split the convergent regularized sum of the overlattice \(u^{-1}\Lambda\) into its \(d\) cosets modulo \(\Lambda\). After subtracting the unshifted sum in each nonzero coset, its limit is \(\wp_\Lambda(P)\) by the defining difference series. Indeed the large-vector differences are bounded uniformly by a constant times \(|z|^{-3}\) for \(0\le v\le1/2\). Homothety on the other side gives
\[
 -2d\, S_2(\Lambda)=(u^2-|u|^2)S_2(\Lambda)=
       \sum_{0\ne P\in u^{-1}\Lambda/\Lambda}\wp_\Lambda(P).                 \tag{1}
\]
Torsion transport carries this kernel bijectively to the target kernel since multiplication by \(t^{-1}\) commutes with \(u\). Dividing by \(\Omega_j^2\) proves the conjugacy of the two normalized nonholomorphic corrections. When \(d=1\) the empty sum gives zero on both sides. The classical value of the regularization also follows by the one-variable Poisson formula, retaining the zero-mode limit; in weight two and dimension one it contributes \(-\pi/\Im\tau\). ## The actual lattice jet, including the finite level action

For \(j=0,1\) take positively oriented period bases \((b_j\tau_j,b_j)\) of \(\mathcal O_K,\mathfrak a\) respectively, using the embedding. One may take \(b_j\) to be the positive generator of the rational intersection of the fractional ideal. This generator is primitive in the rank-two lattice, and extends to an oriented basis. Thus \(b_0=1\), \(b_1\in\mathbf Q_{>0}\). The covolume ratio is the fractional-ideal norm, giving
\[
 b_1^2\Im\tau_1=|t|_{K,f}\Im\tau_0.
\]
Indeed a local uniformizer occurring in \(t\) enlarges the inverse ideal by the residue norm. Also \(2\Im\tau_0/\sqrt d\in\mathbf Q\), since \(\tau_0\in\iota(K)\). Let \(y_0=y\), \(y_1=y\,\mathrm{up}(t)^{-1}\). Choose \(n>0\) clearing the coordinates of \(L_{y_0}\) in \(\mathcal O_F\otimes\mathcal O_K\). Choose \(N\ge3\) such that its rows \(M_0=\{(r,q): r\tau_0+q\in n L_{y_0}\}\) contain \(N\mathcal O_F^2\) and are contained in \(\mathcal O_F^2\); here and below the basis elements in these row descriptions are identified with their preimages in \(K\). The map \(t^{-1}\) between the completed elliptic lattices has an invertible row matrix in the two chosen bases, hence a matrix \(A\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf Z/N)\). Tensoring with \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_F\) shows that \(M_1=\{(r,q): b_1(r\tau_1+q)\in n L_{y_1}\}\) is the inverse image modulo \(N\) of \((M_0\bmod N)A\). The completion identity follows locally from \(\widehat L_{y_1}=t^{-1}\widehat L_{y_0}\) and also holds above conductors and discriminants. Tensor here uses the free rank-two basis in \(K\); the larger orders of \(E\) at ramified primes do not change the chosen ambient row lattice. The same matrix sends labels of \(N\)-division points on the elliptic curves to the transported labels. [Archive lattice and finite-level matrix calculation](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/MemMulMatSmulOneIff.lean)

Write \(T=2\pi i\), \(\omega_j=\Omega_j b_j/T\), choose a marked point \(P\ne0\) of order dividing \(N\) and use its transported mark on the second curve. In period coordinates \((\tau_j,1)\) put \(C_P=(G_2+\wp_P)/T^2\). For a multi-index \(\mu\) denote by \(H_\mu\) the normalized holomorphic lattice jet: differentiate the normalized weight-\(k\) Hilbert Eisenstein sum along the column displacement \(T^2C_P(\tau,1)-T(1,0)\) in variable \(i\), with count \(\mu_i\), and divide by the total power \(T^{km+2|\mu|}\) (the undivided sum carries \(c_K=((k-1)!)^m|\mathrm{disc}F|^{(k-1)/2}\)). The full-level family with its translated rows and marks is a holomorphic elliptic modular family of weight \(km+2|\mu|\). The chain rule gives this weight since the anomalous term in \(C_P\) cancels the derivative of the fractional linear transformation. The positive-frequency and constant Fourier formulas and weight-two Hecke continuation are given in the [analytic lattice calculation](AlpogeCMFourierLattice.md). That calculation of the expansions uses total reality, degree at least two, a nonzero fractional-ideal projection and kernel, and rational partial zetas at \(1-k\). These hold here; the abelian-field and valuation assumptions from its later denominator estimates play no role. For \(k=2\) the offending zero Fourier mode contains a factor vanishing to order \(m\) times a partial zeta with at most a simple pole. This is why the dimension condition matters.

We need the exact descent action on this family. If an automorphism takes \(\zeta_N\) to \(\zeta_N^a\), conjugation of coefficients at the standard cusp transforms the rows and mark by \(\mathrm{diag}(1,a)\), and permutes real embedding labels according to \(g\). To check it, fix the first coordinate \(r\) of a row. Its permissible second coordinates form a coset \(q(r)+V\) of the vertical ideal. Multiplication of those residues by \(a\) permutes \(V\bmod N\) and replaces \(q(r)\) by \(a q(r)\) modulo \(V\). Poisson summation introduces exactly the phases \(\exp(2\pi i\operatorname{Tr}(\xi q(r)))\), with \(\xi\) in the trace dual of \(V\), of order dividing \(N\). The ideal norms and counts in the Fourier expansion are rational, including the removed cusp normalization \((|\mathrm{disc}F|^{1/2}\operatorname N(V^\vee))^k\); the partial-zeta constant is rational by Shintani. Powers of embedded frequencies in the derivatives go to the permuted embedding, because the coefficients of their finite jet polynomials are integers. For the marked term the Tate parameter is \(\zeta_N^{v_2}q^{v_1/N}\); its expansion \(\sum_{j\in\mathbf Z} u q^j/(1-uq^j)^2\) gives exactly the same diagonal action on the second label. At other level bases apply this calculation after transporting both rows and mark.

For precision about descent, at level \(N\ge3\) the modular form is a section of the invariant-differential line on the smooth modular curve with ordered basis of division points. Its values divided by \(\omega_j^{km+2|\mu|}\) evaluate that section in the differential frame of the scaled Weierstrass curve. Over the complex numbers this is the analytic modular-form/section correspondence; the factor \(T\) in \(\omega_j\) comes from \(d\log(e^{2\pi iz})=T\,dz\) on the Tate parameter. Algebraicity and the stated conjugate family follow by the q-expansion principle on each component: applying \(g\) to the algebraic section sends the Tate root-of-unity marking to the marking just computed. Equivalently use a number-field basis of the space of sections and a finite injective collection of Fourier coefficient functionals to descend a form whose coefficients lie in that field. Conversely equality of expansions forces equality of sections. The modular action of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z)\) supplies the basis changes within a component; reduction onto \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf Z/N)\) is surjective. The diagonal action accounts for the remaining components by the Weil pairing. Thus evaluation commutes with conjugation on the actual elliptic pair with its level structure, transferring \(M_0,P\) to \(M_1,PA\). This supplies the weight-by-weight transport of all the normalized holomorphic jets. [N. For clarity the final period normalization can be read off without choosing a twelfth root after conjugation. Define
\[
 B_j=\frac{\pi/\Im\tau_j-T^2 C_{P_j}(\tau_j)}{T^2\omega_j^2}
     =-\big(S_2(b_j(\mathbf Z\tau_j+\mathbf Z))+\wp_{b_j(\mathbf Z\tau_j+\mathbf Z)}(P_j)\big)/\Omega_j^2 ,
\]
where the mark on the right denotes the actual point in the period lattice with scale \(b_j\). By (1) and **TorsTransport**, \(g(B_0)=B_1\). Write \((u)_n=u(u+1)\cdots(u+n-1)\), with \((u)_0=1\), and set
\[
 J_j=\sum_{\mu\le\kappa_j}\prod_i
  \left[(-1)^{\kappa_{j,i}-\mu_i}{\kappa_{j,i}\choose\mu_i}
       (k+\mu_i)_{\kappa_{j,i}-\mu_i}\right]
  B_j^{h-|\mu|}\, H_{\mu,j}(\tau_j)/\omega_j^{km+2|\mu|}.
\]
The indices \(\kappa_0,\kappa_1\) are the input weights \(\kappa,\kappa'\) of **IsConjWt**. Every \(g\) fixing \(K\) permutes the embeddings above \(\iota\); the transported weights have the same sum and the same product of gamma factors. The preceding descent, relabeling each index, gives \(g(J_0)=J_1\). To recover the lattice sum itself observe that the true conjugate direction multiplied by \(\pi/\Im\tau\) differs from the marked direction by \((\pi/\Im\tau-T^2C_P)(\tau,1)\). For a homogeneous function of degree \(-k\), adding this multiple of its base column replaces the affine parameter \(u\) by \(u/(1+Bu)\) and contributes \((1+Bu)^{-k}\). Its Taylor coefficients are precisely the displayed binomial and rising factors. Direct differentiation of the continued lattice kernel gives the additional \((-1)^h\prod_i(k)_{\kappa_i}\), which combines with \(((k-1)!)^m\) to give the source gamma factors. The rescaling of each original row into coordinates \((r,q)\) contributes \((n/b_j)^{km}\); the normalized period \(\omega_j\) supplies \(b_j^{km+2h}\). Consequently for any source witnesses \(S_j\) at \(L_{y_j}\),
\[
 X_{\Omega_j,\kappa_j}(S_j(0))
   =(-1)^h n^{km}\left(b_j^2\,2\Im\tau_j/\sqrt d\right)^h J_j.
\]
This is the source's normalized value, including its discriminant, gamma and area factors. Taking conjugates on the right at \(j=0\) and using the covolume ratio yields
\[
 g\big(X_{\Omega_0,\kappa}(S_0(0))\big)
   =|t|_{K,f}^{-h} X_{\Omega_1,\kappa'}(S_1(0)).                         \tag{2}
\]
The proof of (2) needs only the stated torsion transport, even if no Artin symbol was specified. With \(\Omega_0^{12}=\mathrm{latDisc}(\Lambda_0)\), the first normalized curve has algebraic coefficients: its discriminant is one, and its singular modulus is algebraic, so the cube of the weight-four coefficient and square of the weight-six coefficient are algebraic. Its division points are algebraic as well. The same jet evaluation proves algebraicity of \(X_{\Omega_0,\kappa}(S_0(0))\). ## What survives division by the base toric sum

Put \(A=\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal O_c)\), \(a=[\mathrm{up}(t)]\) in \(A\), \(D(y)=\chi_f(y)|y|_{E,f}^{s}\). Impose the archive's hypotheses that \(g\) fixes all \(\phi_i(E)\) and all \(D(y)\); the weights therefore stay fixed. Normalize the class coordinates by
\[
 b_{j,q}=X_{\Omega_j,\kappa}(S_{y_q}(0))/D(y_q) .
\]
They are independent of the representatives by the principal homothety. In (2) one has \(D(y\,\mathrm{up}(t)^{-1})=D(y)/D(\mathrm{up}(t))\), giving
\[
 g(b_{0,q})=C_t b_{1,q a^{-1}},\qquad
 C_t=|t|_{K,f}^{-h}D(\mathrm{up}(t))^{-1}.
\]
The scalar \(C_t\) is nonzero and depends on neither auxiliary level nor twist. For \(\psi\in A^\vee\) the weighted sum uses \(\psi(q)^{-1}\). Replacing \(q\) by \(v a\) after conjugation pulls out \(({}^g\!\psi)(a)^{-1}\). Apply the same comparison to the base conductor \(\mathfrak f\), trivial twist and \(Q=\varnothing\). The period at each end and the entire \(C_t\) cancel upon division, despite the change of class group. With \(T_0\) the untwisted base-conductor value and \(T_\psi\) the given twisted sum at \(c\), this gives exactly
\[
 r_\psi=T_\psi/T_0\in\overline{\mathbf Q},\qquad
 g(r_\psi)=({}^g\!\psi)(a)^{-1}r_{{}^g\!\psi}.                          \tag{3}
\]
Each character factor has finite order. Finiteness of the class group, existence and uniqueness of the full lattice witnesses, and algebraicity of the finite factors follow from the [principal-power and Hecke calculations](AlpogePicardClassTransport.md). Those proofs use finiteness of the order quotient, the split conductor support, and nonzero angular infinity type. They allow the source's arbitrary \(E\) here. No action on the unnormalized, possibly transcendental value \(T_0\) is required. As a sign check, when \(t\) is principal the class translation vanishes but the two unnormalized sides may still have different factors. [Actual quotient, two distinct conductor groups and fixed-coefficient hypotheses](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/DivisorProofs/ToricRatioConjugatesOfTransport.lean#L62-L101)

In fact every \(T_\psi\ne0\). The local Euler polynomial calculation (4) in the preceding manuscript gives \(T_\psi=L(s,\chi\psi)\prod_{v\mid c}P_{a_v,b_v}(Q_v^{-s})\) for the archive character convention. The polynomials have constant coefficient one and roots of modulus \(Q_v^{-1/2}\); each factor is nonzero at \(s\ge1\). For \(s>1\) the ordinary Euler product is absolutely convergent and nonzero. At \(s=1\) let \(\eta=\chi\psi\) and delete a common finite set \(S\) of ramified Euler factors from the three functions below. The unitary character \(\eta^2\) has nonzero angular infinity type because \(s+\kappa_i>0\) at every embedding. Both \(L^S(u,\eta)\) and \(L^S(u,\eta^2)\) are holomorphic at one by Hecke continuation; the omitted factors are invertible there. For real \(u>1\),
\[
 \zeta_E^S(u)^3 |L^S(u,\eta)|^4 |L^S(u,\eta^2)|\ge1 .
\]
In the logarithmic Euler series each local coefficient is proportional to \(3+4\cos\theta+\cos2\theta=2(1+\cos\theta)^2\ge0\). A zero of \(L^S(u,\eta)\) at one would give vanishing order at least four against a pole of order three, a contradiction. This proves the needed endpoint for all twists. Since the source base character has exact specified conductor, its \(Q=\varnothing\) correction factors equal one. In particular the required \(T_0\) is nonzero, completing (3) with the quantifiers of **ToricRatioConjugates**. The nonvanishing here concerns the half-plane boundary of unitary Hecke L-functions. [Tate, *Fourier Analysis in Number Fields and Hecke's Zeta-Functions*, Chapter IV](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~alexk/2023S572/Tate1950.pdf)

For \(s>1\) an explicit two-sided archimedean bound also follows. Write \(d_v=\deg P_{a_v,b_v}\), which is \(2a_v\) for \(b_v=0\) and \(2(a_v-b_v)\) otherwise. The ordinary unitary Euler factors lie between \((1+Nw^{-s})^{-1}\) and \((1-Nw^{-s})^{-1}\) in absolute value, including an omitted ramified factor of one. Thus
\[
 \frac{\zeta_E(2s)}{\zeta_E(s)^2}\prod_{v\mid c}(1-Q_v^{1/2-s})^{d_v}
 \le |r_\psi|\le
 \frac{\zeta_E(s)^2}{\zeta_E(2s)}\prod_{v\mid c}(1+Q_v^{1/2-s})^{d_v}.
\]
At one this bound degenerates. The endpoint nonvanishing proof gives no such uniform positive lower constant as the character and field vary.

## The field generated by the ratios

The fixed coefficient field can be taken finite. Let \(L_0\) be the field generated by all \(\phi_i(E)\) and \(D(y)\) over \(K\), using the given complex embedding. The principal values of \(D\) lie in the normal closure of \(E\) by their infinity-type formula; the values on the order units are one. There are finitely many remaining representatives, and taking a principal power makes each value algebraic. This proves finiteness. Put \(L=L_0(\mu_{\exp A})\). Let \(K_A/K\) be the finite abelian class field of the kernel of the continuous map \(t\mapsto [\mathrm{up}(t)]\), extended trivially at infinity. Its target is the **image** in \(A\); the kernel contains \(K^\times\) and is open since the order units are open. Use arithmetic reciprocity to identify \(H=\mathrm{Gal}(LK_A/L)\) with the subgroup of this image fixing \(K_A\cap L\).

For \(g\) over \(L\), (3) reads \(g(r_\psi)=\psi(a_g)^{-1}r_\psi\), with \(a_g\in H\). Such automorphisms of algebraic numbers extend to automorphisms of \(\mathbf C\); the finite idele giving their restriction to \(K^{\rm ab}\) exists by class field theory, since the archimedean factor of the imaginary quadratic field is connected. Thus \(r_\psi\in LK_A\). Since every ratio is nonzero, its stabilizer inside \(H\) is exactly \(\ker(\psi|_H)\). Consequently
\[
 L(r_\psi)=(LK_A)^{\ker(\psi|_H)},\qquad
 [L(r_\psi):L]=|\psi(H)|,\qquad
 \operatorname{minpoly}_L(r_\psi)=X^{|\psi(H)|}-r_\psi^{|\psi(H)|}.
\]
All the ratios together generate \(LK_A\), since the characters of \(A\) separate \(H\). Choose one extension to \(A\) of each character of \(H\) (successively adjoining a cyclic generator and extracting the required root of unity extends any character). The chosen nonzero ratios are an \(L\)-basis of \(LK_A\). Indeed applying \(\sum_{h\in H}\nu(h)h\) to any linear relation extracts \(|H|\) times the coefficient of its \(\nu\)-term by finite-character orthogonality. Their number equals the extension degree. Their sum generates a normal basis, by conjugating the sum and inverting this same character table.

There is also a descent constraint over \(L_0\) before adjoining roots of unity. Put \(B_q=b_{0,q}/X_{\Omega_0,\kappa}(T_0)\) and \(M_{xy}=B_{xy^{-1}}\). The characters diagonalize this convolution matrix with eigenvalues \(r_\psi\). Its determinant is nonzero. For any \(g\) fixing \(L_0\), the ratio of the class coordinate and the base value transforms by \(B_q\mapsto B_{q a_g^{-1}}\). This last formula uses normalized class values at the two different periods; their underlying ratio is unchanged when the same nonzero period factor is removed at numerator and denominator. Translation permutes the rows, so \((\det M)^2\in L_0^\times\). This retains information on the whole family even where an individual ratio belongs to a larger field. Replacing the exact stabilizer by the full Picard group would overlook both capitulation and the intersection with the coefficient field.

## Conductor of the quadratic order seen by the Artin classes

The scalar extension map in (3) has a smaller explicit domain. Write \(D_K=|\mathrm{disc}K|\), \(a_v=\operatorname{ord}_v c\), and \(e_v=e(v/p)\). Its precise order on \(K\) is
\[
 \mathcal O_c\cap K=\mathbf Z+f_K\mathcal O_K,\qquad
 f_K=\prod_p p^{n_p},\qquad
 n_p=\max\left(0,\max_{v\mid p,\ a_v>0}
           \left\lceil\frac{a_v}{e_v}-\frac{v_p(D_K)}2\right\rceil\right),
\]
with the empty maximum contributing zero. To see this, fix an integral generator \(\theta\) of \(\mathcal O_K\), so \((\theta-\bar\theta)^2=\mathrm{disc}K\). An element of \(\mathcal O_{K,p}\) has the unique form \(u+b\theta\), \(u,b\in\mathbf Z_p\). At each conductor place the local algebra of \(E/F\) is split. Its two components evaluate this element at the two conjugate images of \(\theta\) in \(F_v\). They coincide modulo \(\mathfrak p_v^{a_v}\) exactly when \(e_v v_p(b)+e_v v_p(D_K)/2\ge a_v\), including at dyadic places. At the other places integrality suffices. Conversely membership in \(\mathcal O_c\) already forces integrality in \(K\), proving equality. Applying membership to a unit and its inverse gives the corresponding exact intersection of local order-unit groups at every prime.

It follows that the homomorphism from finite \(K\)-ideles in (3) factors as the extension of invertible ideals
\[
 \mathrm{Pic}(\mathbf Z+f_K\mathcal O_K)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal O_c).
\]
Its kernel consists exactly of the classes whose extensions are principal over the indicated order. Thus \(K_A\) is a subfield of the quadratic ring class field of conductor \(f_K\); capitulation can reduce the conductor of this subfield further. The image is anticyclotomic. Indeed \(t\bar t\) is a rational finite idele, which is a rational number times a tuple in \(\widehat{\mathbf Z}^{\times}\); these map respectively to a principal idele and order units. Complex conjugation therefore acts by inversion on the class group image. The degree of \(K_A/K\) lies between
\[
 \frac{h_K}{|\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)[m]|}\quad\text{and}\quad
 \frac{h_K f_K}{[\mathcal O_K^\times:(\mathbf Z+f_K\mathcal O_K)^\times]}
       \prod_{p\mid f_K}\left(1-\frac{(\mathrm{disc}K/p)}p\right),
\]
