# One-time Hamburger positivity and the escape of negative heat-moment degrees

Fix **any** \(\tau>0\) and nonnegative integer \(k\). Set \(a_j=(-1)^jH^{(j)}(\tau)\) using the arithmetic heat trace given by real-axis Laplace inversion. Then RH is equivalent to the single ordinary Hamburger condition
\[
 (a_{k+i+j})_{0\le i,j<d}\succeq0\quad\text{for every }d\ge1.
\]
Only the one shift \(k\) and the one time \(\tau\) occur. For \(\tau\le1.69\) all individual entries are known to be positive by the [prime explicit formula and the finite-height input](XiHeatPrimeEnvelope.md). There is a further unconditional result in the other order of quantifiers: **for each fixed \(\tau>0\) and each fixed \(d\), the matrix is positive definite for every sufficiently large shift \(k\)**. Hence if RH fails, each shift has a finite witness of negative energy but the smallest witnessing degree tends to infinity with the shift.

## Existence of the one-time measure

Under RH the moments are those of \(\sum_{g>0}m(g)g^{2k}e^{-\tau g^2}\delta_{g^2}\). Conversely, the displayed condition gives a finite positive Hamburger measure \(\nu\) on the real line with moments \(a_{k+j}\). This use of the Hamburger theorem requires no second matrix and imposes no support hypothesis. Since \(H\) is analytic for positive real part, Cauchy estimates on circles of each radius \(r<\tau\) bound \(|a_{k+j}|\le C_{r,k}j! r^{-j}\) by applying the estimate to the fixed derivative \(H^{(k)}\). Integrate the even Taylor series for \(\cosh(cx)\) for \(0<c<r\). Monotone convergence gives \(\int e^{c|x|}\,d\nu<\infty\). Its bilateral Laplace transform therefore agrees near zero with \(F(v)=(-1)^kH^{(k)}(\tau+v)\) by equality of Taylor coefficients. [Schmüdgen, *Ten Lectures on the Moment Problem*, Lecture 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12698)

Split off the part of the transform over \(x\ge0\), analytic throughout \(\Re v>0\) and at zero. The remaining Taylor series for the integral over \(x<0\) has nonnegative coefficients and analytic continuation along the entire positive half-axis, via \(F\) minus the first part. Its convergence radius is infinite. To see this directly, if a power series with nonnegative coefficients has a finite positive radius \(R\) and analytic continuation to a neighborhood of \(R\), expand at \(R-\epsilon\) inside that neighborhood. For small \(\epsilon>0\) the new series converges at \(R+\epsilon\). Binomial expansion and monotone summation give convergence of the original series there, a contradiction. Consequently the negative-support transform represents its integral for every \(v>0\). Since \(F(v)\to0\) by Gaussian decay of the true divisor, positive mass on any \(( -\infty,-\epsilon]\) is impossible. Thus the representing measure is supported on \([0,\infty)\).

Take one more Laplace transform in \(v\) for \(\Re z>0\). Using \(\lambda=(g+i(\Re\rho-1/2))^2\) as in the [normalization](XiHeatSaddleBounds.md) gives
\[
 \int \frac{d\nu(x)}{z+x}
 =\sum_{\Im\rho>0}\frac{m\lambda^k e^{-\tau\lambda}}{z+\lambda}.
\]
The right side is meromorphic with a nonzero residue at each distinct \(-\lambda\). The exponentially decaying weights give normal convergence away from its discrete poles. The left side is analytic off the nonpositive real ray. Uniqueness of continuation rules out every nonreal rate. Since \(g>0\), this puts every nontrivial zero on the critical line. In the affirmative case the measure is determinate by its exponential moment, hence each atom \(x\) has mass \(m x^k e^{-\tau x}\). The positive integer \(m=e^{\tau x}x^{-k}\nu(\{x\})\) recovers the analytic multiplicity.

Conditionally, this gives a spectral construction using only the arithmetic sequence to specify the measure and its atoms. Take the Hilbert sum, over positive atoms, of two copies of \(\mathbb C^m\), and use eigenvalues \(\pm\sqrt{x}\) on the maximal square-summability domain. Finite supports give density and the conjugate diagonal resolvents give self-adjointness. The divisor identification then gives \(\sum m/x<\infty\); the inverse is Hilbert–Schmidt. Pairing the two factors of the regularized determinant gives \(\prod(1-z^2/x)^m=\xi(1/2+iz)/\xi(1/2)\) by the even Hadamard product. The operator prescription uses the measure determined by the arithmetic moments. Its positive existence in every degree is still the unproved premise. ## Fixed matrix size at large shift

In the explicit formula take \(n=k\), \(t=\tau\) and \(s=\sqrt{k/\tau}\). For indices \(0\le j\le2d-2\), multiply the test \(h(u)=u^{2k}e^{-\tau u^2}\) by \(((u^2-s^2)/(2s))^j\). This is an entire even test. Its zero sum is the exact centered and scaled moment of order \(j\) with weights \(m\lambda^ke^{-\tau\lambda}\). At the positive saddle \(u=s+y\) the new coordinate is \(y+y^2/(2s)\). At the negative saddle \(u=-s+y\) it is \(-y+y^2/(2s)\). Reflection gives the same limiting real integral from the two saddles. On either shifted half-line the additional factor is bounded by \(C_{j,\tau}(1+|\Re u\mp s|)^{2j}\) for \(s\ge1\), using the sign of that half-line. It preserves the integrable Gaussian domination, the bounded prime contribution on the height-one shift and the negligible pole cost. Dividing the zero sum by \(W L\log(s/(2\pi))/(2\pi)\), the archimedean calculation therefore gives the entrywise limit
\[
  L^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R} y^j e^{-2\tau y^2}dy,\qquad L=\sqrt{\pi/(2\tau)} .
\]
For fixed \(d\) the limiting matrix is positive definite by integrating the square of each nonzero real polynomial against the positive Gaussian. Its smallest eigenvalue is positive. Entrywise convergence in this finite dimension preserves positivity for all sufficiently large shifts. The affine polynomial change of variable is invertible and gives a congruence back to the original Hankel matrix. This proves the unconditional assertion. It concerns heat-weighted powers of the unbounded rates. The inverse-power compact-node pencil has a different large-shift behavior. [Gaussian polynomial integrability, strict positivity and change of variable in Lean](XiTiltedHeatMomentCriterion.lean) · [Source for the moving-saddle normalization](XiGaussianPrimeProfile.md)

The size can also grow. There is a constant \(c_\tau>0\) such that positivity holds for every sufficiently large \(k\) and every \(d\) satisfying \(d\log(d+1)\le c_\tau\log\log k\). Normalize the test polynomial \(Q\), of degree less than \(d\), to have squared norm one in the probability Gaussian \(L^{-1}e^{-2\tau y^2}dy\). Its monomial coefficients and those of its derivative are bounded by \(A=\exp(C_\tau d\log(d+1))\), with a constant that may increase between bounds. Indeed expand in the orthonormal polynomials \(H_j(\sqrt{2\tau}\,y)/\sqrt{2^j j!}\), whose expansion coefficients for \(Q\) have modulus at most one. The explicit Hermite coefficients \(j!(-1)^r(2\sqrt{2\tau})^{j-2r}/(r!(j-2r)!)\) before normalization give the bound. Integrals of \((1+|y|)^{4d+8}\) against any fixed centered Gaussian are likewise at most \(A\), by integration by parts. [Hermite polynomial coefficients](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5.E13)

On the height-one Fourier contour the total prime error is at most \(A\) in units of \(WL/(2\pi)\). The centered coordinate has size at most \(C_\tau(1+|y|)^2\) and (2) of the prime envelope supplies the Gaussian. On the real positive half, the difference of \(Q(y+y^2/(2s))^2\) and \(Q(y)^2\) has absolute value at most \(s^{-1}A(1+|y|)^{4d+4}\) by the polynomial derivative bound and the mean value theorem. Reflect the negative half. Truncate at \(|y|\le k^{1/16}\). The local exponent error is \(O_\tau(k^{-5/16})\). Outside the truncation, spend half the majorizing Gaussian on the tail and integrate the polynomial against the other half. The error, including the logarithmic digamma weight, is at most \(A(1+\log s)e^{-c'_\tau k^{1/8}}\). The pole has an additional polynomial factor at most \(A(1+s)^{2d}\), absorbed by its original exponential decay in the asserted range. Together these estimates bound the normalized quadratic form from below by
\[
 \log(s/(2\pi))\bigl(1-O_\tau(k^{-1/4}A)\bigr)-O_\tau(A).
\]
All constants are uniform over the Gaussian unit sphere in those degrees. Choosing \(c_\tau\) sufficiently small makes \(A\le(\log k)^{1/2}\), proving the growing-size assertion. Any negative polynomial witnesses along large shifts must eventually have degree at least a positive time-dependent multiple of \(\log\log k/\log\log\log k\).

As a finite check, the [arithmetic fixture](../../../scripts/verify_xi_heat_prime_profile.py) encloses the first three centered leading minors of the actual matrix at shift 3,600 and time one. Every enclosure is positive, with the full tail included. Entrywise positivity alone fails on a four-rate model. Take one copy of rate 1, six of 5 and one each of \(4\pm i\). At time one the six real summands dominate the conjugate pair at every order since \(6>2e\) and \(5>|4+i|\). But the cubic \(P(x)=(x-1)(x-5)^2\) kills the real atoms and its energy is \(2\Re(e^{-(4+i)}P(4+i)^2)<-1\). This model tests the distinction between signs and square positivity.
