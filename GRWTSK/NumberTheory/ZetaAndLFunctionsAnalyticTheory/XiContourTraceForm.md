# Zeta contour moments and a positive metric on each finite spectral window

Let \(X(z)=\xi(1/2+iz)\). A rectangle symmetric about the real axis, with horizontal sides at imaginary parts \(\pm h\), \(h>1/2\), and vertical sides at real parts \(c\pm w\), captures the entire nontrivial divisor at heights in its horizontal interval. Require \(X\ne0\) on its boundary. Put \(u=(z-c)/w\). The following data use values of an analytic function on the contour, without a zeta-zero list:
\[
 s_k=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint u^k\frac{X'(z)}{X(z)}\,dz,\quad
 d=s_0,\quad B=(s_{i+j})_{0\le i,j<d}.
\]
The matrix is real and symmetric. Positive semidefiniteness of \(B\) certifies that every enclosed zero has real ordinate. Positive definiteness certifies simplicity as well. An empty window has the empty matrix. The residue theorem proves \(s_k=\sum m_a a^k\), where \(a\) are the normalized ordinates and \(m_a\) their analytic multiplicities. A factor holomorphic and nonvanishing on a neighborhood of the entire closed rectangle leaves these integrals unchanged. Delves and Lyness used such contour moments and Newton identities to locate analytic zeros. The Hermite–Sylvester criterion supplies the reality test. [Delves and Lyness, *Mathematics of Computation* 21 (1967), 543–560](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1967-0228165-4) · [Nathanson, 2019, v2, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01745v2)

## What the negative directions count

For a polynomial \(p\) with complex coefficients, its coefficient vector has quadratic form \(\sum m_a p(a)\overline{p(\bar a)}\). Evaluation of polynomials of degree less than the number of distinct nodes surjects onto their complex value vectors by interpolation. Each real node thus gives one positive square. A conjugate pair gives the Hermitian block with diagonal zero and both off-diagonal entries equal to the common multiplicity. It has one positive and one negative direction. All remaining dimensions in the size-\(d\) matrix are the null space of evaluation. In particular the negative index counts distinct conjugate pairs, while the nullity counts repeated copies beyond the first copy of each distinct node. For a direct negative witness assign values \(+1,-1\) on one pair and zero elsewhere. It has form \(-2m_a\). The [Lean proof](XiContourTraceForm.lean) constructs the interpolating polynomial and proves both directions of the positivity criterion, even allowing unequal positive weights in the real part of the form.

All multiplicities of nonreal pairs can also be counted. Recover the monic polynomial \(P\) of degree \(d\) from \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\) by Newton's recursion, with \(P=1\) for an empty window. Starting at \(P_1=P\), iterate \(P_{j+1}=\gcd(P_j,P_j')\), stopping at 1. Each \(g_j=P_j/P_{j+1}\) is the square-free product of the nodes of original order at least \(j\). Construct its Hermite matrix from its own Newton sums. The sum of their negative indices counts nonreal pairs **with** multiplicity.

On any square-free layer put \(B_1=(s_{i+j+1})\) and \(B_0=(s_{i+j})\), using the layer's own sums of weight one. Evaluation in the monomial basis gives \(B_0=V^{\mathsf T}V\) and \(B_1=V^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(a)V\) over the complex numbers, with invertible Vandermonde matrix \(V\). Hence \(\det(tB_0-B_1)/\det B_0=g(t)\). The identity also holds for distinct nodes with nonzero weights by inserting their diagonal weight matrix. When \(B_0=CC^{\mathsf T}\) is positive definite, \(C^{-1}B_1C^{-\mathsf T}\) is an explicit real symmetric realization of the normalized positions. Its coefficients require only the moments. A sign change of the pencil determinant on each of \(\deg g\) disjoint intervals certifies an eigenvalue in each, independently of the approximations used to propose the intervals.

The exact polynomial operations give a mathematical construction in repeated-root cases. Deciding an exact vanishing discriminant from finite-precision data alone can require additional evidence.

Fixed-degree tests lose information at a near collision. Take distinct real nodes \(r_1,\ldots,r_b\), a different real number \(a\), positive unit weights on the real nodes and equal positive weights \(m\) on \(a\pm i\delta\). At \(\delta=0\) the leading block of size \(b+1\) is positive definite by interpolation. For all sufficiently small positive \(\delta\) that block remains positive definite even though the size-\(b+2\) matrix has one negative direction. Quantitatively, put \(p(x)=(x-a)\prod_j(x-r_j)\) and use the Euclidean coefficient norm. At coalescence its coefficient vector spans the null space. The matrix is analytic in \(v=\delta^2\). For its simple eigenvalue at zero, the differentiated eigenvector equation gives the initial slope by the Rayleigh quotient on that vector. Since \(p(a+i\delta)=i\delta p'(a)+O(\delta^2)\), the negative eigenvalue is
\[
 -\frac{2m\,p'(a)^2}{\|p\|_{\rm coeff}^2}\,\delta^2+O(\delta^4).
\]
The bounded local mass test in the [exterior-probe construction](XiPoissonCellCertificate.md) and the contour test have different conditioning near a collision. A fixed numerical tolerance on the smallest Hermite eigenvalue cannot ensure positivity.

## The operator determined by these moments

Choose a locally finite partition of the height axis into bounded intervals of uniformly bounded length with zero-free vertical sides. This choice can be made using the input analytic function. In each band \((n+1/4,n+3/4)\), search rational cuts in parallel and certify nonvanishing on the segment \(|\Im z|\le1\) by interval bounds on shrinking subdivisions. Only finitely many cuts in a band meet a zero. A cut separated from all zeros has positive minimum modulus on the compact segment, so exhaustive refinement with increasing precision terminates. One cut per band covers every height with consecutive rectangles. The classical local zero count bounds each matrix size by \(O(\log(2+|n|))\). For example, Jensen's formula in the disk of radius 4 about \(2+i n\) bounds the count in the concentric disk of radius 3 by \(O(\log(2+|n|))\), using the polynomial growth from Euler–Maclaurin and \(|\zeta(2+in)|\ge1/\zeta(2)\). At large \(|n|\) the disk contains no pole. Consecutive intervals between the prescribed bands fit inside the inner disk together with the full critical strip.

For each nonconstant square-free layer \(g\) take the finite real algebra \(E=\mathbb R[u]/(g)\). It has a unique real algebra involution \(b\mapsto b^\natural\) for which \(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb R}(b b^\natural)>0\) for all nonzero \(b\). Here the trace is the trace of multiplication. Indeed the Chinese remainder theorem and real polynomial factorization give a product of copies of \(\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbb C\). An involution permuting primitive idempotents would give trace zero for a moved idempotent times its image. It fixes each factor, acts identically on real factors, and must conjugate each complex factor since the identity would give a negative square on \(i\). These choices give strict positivity. Thus this inner product exists unconditionally, including in the presence of nonreal zeros.

Complexify the real Hilbert space with inner product \(\operatorname{Tr}(a^\natural b)\). On each block let \(N\) multiply by \(c+wu\). Its adjoint multiplies by \(c+wu^\natural\), so \(N\) is normal. Take the orthogonal sum on its maximal domain and put \(A=\Re N\), the self-adjoint direct sum of the real parts. These definitions use contour integrals, finite polynomial arithmetic and a metric characterized inside the real algebra. Factorization is used to verify their spectra. A real factor supplies one eigenline and a complex factor supplies the two conjugate eigenlines. The layers restore every analytic multiplicity. The normal operator has the complete xi divisor, and \(A\) has all its real projections with multiplicity. Finite-block vectors are a dense core. Since the imaginary parts have modulus less than \(1/2\), \(N-A\) is bounded on the common domain. The real projections stay away from zero by discreteness and the absence of real nontrivial zeta zeros. The classical zero count gives Hilbert–Schmidt inverses for both operators. Pairing opposite zeros in the order-one Hadamard product, evenness and normalization at zero give \(\det_2(I-zN^{-1})=X(z)/X(0)\) throughout the plane. The all-plane determinant of \(A\) agrees exactly when the sum of negative indices vanishes in every window. One nonzero negative index produces a nonreal zero absent from the determinant of the self-adjoint operator.

The [earlier planar construction](XiJetDivisorMeasures.md) obtains the same normal spectrum and [real projection](XiSechProjectionTransport.md) by limits of local jet measures. Both use the analytic function xi. Here a finite list of contour moments gives the exact index and multiplicity decomposition on each window. An independent uniform positivity proof for the resulting finite forms is still required.

## Validated contour calculations

The [calculation](../../../scripts/verify_xi_contour_traces.py) uses rectangles of half-width \(1/2\) and half-height \(9/16\). It integrates the logarithmic derivative against all required monomials by complex ball quadrature. At the positive heights tested here the gamma, elementary and power factors of xi are holomorphic and nonzero throughout, so zeta alone gives its moments. The reality of the moments follows from reflection of the enclosed divisor. The program checks the imaginary enclosures, recovers the unique integer in the zeroth moment ball and verifies strict positivity of every leading principal determinant. [Johansson, *Numerical integration in arbitrary-precision ball arithmetic*, 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07942)

Here are the tail bounds used by the integrator. On \(|v|\le2\), let \(s=1/2+i(c+v)\), \(c>4\). Euler–Maclaurin at 1 through \(B_4\) gives main terms \(1/(s-1)+1/2+s/12-s(s+1)(s+2)/720\); the remainder is bounded by \(|s(s+1)(s+2)(s+3)|/[720(\Re s+3)]\) since the periodic fourth Bernoulli polynomial has modulus at most \(1/30\). Here \(\Re s\ge-3/2\) and \(|s-1|>1\). These bounds give \(|\zeta(s)|\le(c+10)^4\). Multiplication by \(e^{iLv}\) multiplies this bound by at most \(e^{2|L|}\) and leaves every moment unchanged. Denote the resulting bound by \(B\). [Euler–Maclaurin expansions of zeta](https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.2.iii)

Cauchy's bound on the Taylor coefficients is \(|a_j|\le B2^{-j}\). After retaining \(J\) coefficients, the function and derivative errors on \(|v|\le1\) are at most \(B2^{1-J}\) and \((J+1)B2^{1-J}\). The code encloses both in rectangular complex balls and refuses quadrature neighborhoods outside that disk. At large heights it forms coefficients from the discrete Fourier transform of \(J\) unit-circle samples. Each normalized transform coefficient of index \(j\) differs from \(a_j\) by at most \(B2^{-j}/(2^J-1)\), by summing \(a_{j+J}, a_{j+2J},\ldots\). The low-height calculation uses 128 Taylor coefficients directly. The high-height calculation uses 320 circle samples with \(L=14\). Each circle argument is enclosed at 512 bits and zeta is evaluated at the exact dyadic midpoint. The argument error is at most \(1/8\); the argument itself lies less than \(5/4\) from the disk center. Thus the segment to the midpoint has a radius-\(1/2\) neighborhood inside the radius-two disk. The sample error from this replacement is at most \(2(c+10)^4\) times the argument error, before applying the exponential. Whole-ball zeta samples at the high points could have bounds too large to certify any moments. Recentring the polynomial on eight subsegments per edge also avoids loss of precision from evaluating long polynomials on a wide interval. The implementation retains 50 shifted coefficients and sums absolute value bounds for every omitted coefficient and its derivative on each patch.
