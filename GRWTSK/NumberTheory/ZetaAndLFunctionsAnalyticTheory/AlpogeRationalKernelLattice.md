# Rational kernel coordinates and their integral denominators

For a fixed rank and fixed diagrams, one integer matrix recovers the archived target pairing on every flag-minor product from the normalized target polynomial. Its denominator is independent of the place and the functional. At a single site, its rank modulo a prime is the dimension of the simple module of the specified highest weight. The arithmetic consumer is the value-group clause of **KerEnt**. ## The extraction matrix

Fix a word \(u\), a step \(i<m\), target rank \(N=N(i+1)\), and the source diagrams \(\nu_s=\mathrm{nuD}(D,u,i)_s\). Their column heights fit the target rank. Let \(P_J(X)\) be the product of the minors with initial rows and increasing column lists \(J\), over all columns at all sites. Only rows up to \(N\) occur. Let \(S_a=P_{J_a}\) be the semistandard products. Integral straightening and their distinct monic diagonal pivots give a free basis of the integral span which stays independent after every base change. Setting \(P_J=\sum_a A_{aJ}S_a\) and ordering the semistandard choices first gives \(A=[I\ U]\) with integral \(U\). Every separate column choice appears in \(A\), even when several give identical products. [Straightening in the source variables](AlpogeOrbitFlowCorollary.md#integral-straightening-and-the-bounded-expansion) · [Lax, Propositions 4.3–4.5 and Theorem 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.02744#page=8)

Use one matrix \(X_s\) and one matrix \(Y_s\) for each site. Write \(\Delta_h\) for the leading principal minor of size \(h\). Applying Cauchy–Binet to each column of height \(h_{s,c}\), and multiplying, gives
\[
 E(X,Y)=\prod_{s,c}\Delta_{h_{s,c}}(X_sY_s)
       =\sum_J P_J(X)P_J(Y^{\mathsf T}).
\]
The transpose accounts for the archived frame-coordinate ordering. For any functional \(\phi\) in the \(X\)-variables put \(x_a=\phi(S_a)\). Let \(b\) be the coordinates of \(F(Y)=\phi(E(X,Y))\) in the basis \(S_a(Y^{\mathsf T})\). Then
\[
 G=AA^{\mathsf T}=I+UU^{\mathsf T},\qquad b=Gx,\qquad
 \phi(P_J)=(A^{\mathsf T}x)_J .
\]
The real quadratic form of \(G\) is \(|v|^2+|U^{\mathsf T}v|^2\), so \(D_\nu=\det G\) is a positive integer. The identity \(D_\nu x=\operatorname{adj}(G)b\) holds over every commutative ring. This recovers the values on the flag span independently of how \(\phi\) was extended to all polynomials. [Archived Cauchy–Binet expansion and transposition](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/UnitaryGroupMassFormula/GramInjectivity/InstFintypeColChoice.lean#L17-L60)

At a level-free place \(w\), take the embedding \(f=i_L(w):L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C_p\). For each target class \(c'\) and each \(v'\), take its actual polynomial representative \(Q=\mathrm{secC}(v')\) over \(L\). Write the rational frame matrices as \(B_\tau=f(B'_\tau)\). The normalized target is the scalar extension of
\[
 Y\longmapsto \left(\prod_\tau\det(B'_\tau)^{-s^{\rm Div}_{i,\tau}}\right)
                          Q\big((B'_\tau Y_\tau)_\tau\big).
\]
The source defines the polynomial weight condition by right upper triangular substitution, \(Q(Y\beta)=\chi_\nu(\beta)Q(Y)\). Substitution \(Y\mapsto B'Y\) preserves this law. Applying scalar diagonal matrices one site at a time gives homogeneity of degree \(|\nu_s|\) at each site because \(L\) is infinite. Transposing changes the right upper triangular law to Green's left lower triangular law. His Theorem (4.8f) identifies this semi-invariant space with the bideterminant module, over the coefficient field itself. For several sites, expanding in a basis for the homogeneous polynomials at the other sites preserves each separate covariance relation; iterating gives the tensor product of the prescribed bideterminant spans. Thus the normalized polynomial has coordinates \(b\in L^r\). Conversely any such coordinates give a functional on the flag span by \(x=G^{-1}b\), which extends linearly to the full polynomial ring. Its frame evaluation is the required polynomial by Cauchy–Binet. This also recovers the archived characteristic-zero range assertion **CanOnto** for arbitrary matrices in the completion: first expand \(Q\) in transposed bideterminants over \(L\), then apply left multiplication by those matrices, which retains the right triangular covariance over the completion. This argument works for singular matrices as well. The chosen right inverse **canInv** agrees with every preimage on the flag span, since \(G\) is invertible there. Therefore \(\mathrm{prTC}(v')_J=f((A^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}b)_J)\) for every \(J\). Taking norms gives the required arithmetic equality. If the polynomial coefficients lie in a fractional ideal \(I\) of the local valuation ring, the monic pivots give \(b_a\in I\), so \(D_\nu\phi(P_J)\in I\) for every \(J\). When \(p\nmid D_\nu\), integral recovery follows. Conversely, the expansion of \(E\) sends integral flag values to integral polynomial coefficients at every prime. The least universal integer clearing the basis coordinates is the exponent of \(\mathbb Z^r/G\mathbb Z^r\), because \(tG^{-1}\) is integral exactly when \(t\mathbb Z^r\subseteq G\mathbb Z^r\).

## Which primes can occur?

For one site of shape \(\lambda\), reduce \(G\) modulo \(p\) and extend scalars to \(k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). The flag-product span in \(X\) is Green's bideterminant module \(D_{\lambda,k}\), with the reduced semistandard basis. Evaluations at \(X\in\mathrm{GL}_N(k)\) span its entire dual. Otherwise a nonzero element of the polynomial span would vanish on all invertible matrices, a Zariski-dense open subset. Hence the image of \(G\) is the span of \(E(g,Y)\) for all invertible \(g\). After transposing \(Y\), these are exactly the right translates of \(H(Y)=\prod_c\Delta_{h_c}(Y)\), since principal minors are invariant under transpose. This \(H\) is Green's canonical bideterminant \((T_l:T_l)\). Its translates generate the unique simple submodule \(L(\lambda)\) of \(D_{\lambda,k}\). It follows that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_k G=\dim_k L(\lambda),\qquad
 p\mid\det G\ \Longleftrightarrow\ D_{\lambda,k}\text{ is reducible}.
\]
For \(p>|\lambda|\), the symmetric group algebra in degree \(|\lambda|\) is semisimple by Maschke's theorem. The Schur algebra, its commutant on tensor space, is then semisimple. The unique simple submodule of \(D_{\lambda,k}\) is consequently the whole module. Across independent sites the matrices \(A\), before reordering columns, and \(G\) are tensor products of the single-site matrices. Integral recovery follows at every \(p>\max_s|\nu_s|\). Empty diagrams contribute the matrix \([1]\). [Green, *Polynomial Representations of* \(\mathrm{GL}_n\), second edition, (2.6c,e), (4.4b), (4.5a) and (5.4c,d)](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-46944-3)

For one row of length \(m\), \(G\) is diagonal with entries \(m!/\prod_j\alpha_j!\) for \(\sum_j\alpha_j=m\), the multiplicities of each monomial among the ordered choices. At \(N=2,m=p\), take the functional equal to \(1/p\) on \(X_1^{p-1}X_2\) and zero on the other monomials. Its frame polynomial has sole coefficient \(1\), while its selected flag value has valuation \(-1\). This shows the universal prime threshold can be attained. Whether these degrees appear in a particular arithmetic word is a further question.

For the two-column shape \((2,1)\), write \(x_i=X_{1i}\) and \(P_{ij}=x_iX_{2j}-x_jX_{2i}\). Each nonstandard choice is \(x_kP_{ij}\) for \(k<i<j\). It equals \(x_iP_{kj}-x_jP_{ki}\). These two standard products carry that triple of distinct letters, so different nonstandard choices have disjoint supports. Consequently \(U^{\mathsf T}U=2I\), and
\[
 (3I-UU^{\mathsf T})G=3I.
\]
For each triple, \(G\) restricts on the corresponding pair to the block with diagonal entries \(2,2\) and off-diagonal entries \(-1,-1\). All other basis entries have identity blocks. Hence \(D_\nu=3^{\binom N3}\), the optimal common denominator for \(N\ge3\) is \(3\), and reduction at \(3\) has nullity exactly \(\binom N3\). This calculation also checks the modular criterion in a case involving a genuine straightening relation.
