## A uniform polynomial bound on all shallow terms

Let \(Y\ge2\) be a real number. At each prime choose any integer \(a_p\ge1\) with \(p^{a_p}\le Y\), and retain any subset of its prime-to-\(p\) host characters. Then for **every weight and tower level**,
\[
 \sum_p \log p\sum_\psi \operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O} S[p^{a_p}]
 \ \le\ NY\log Y + N^2Y^3\log(8NY^2).                                      \tag{1}
\]
The chosen characters and even their coefficient fields may vary with \(p\). The right side is independent of the weight. In particular, taking \(Y=(Nt)^B\) for fixed \(B>0\) pays a polynomial of the shape required by CountI for all the truncated contributions. It pays the full contributions on every retained term of exponent at most \(p^{a_p}\). Summing over \(e<t\) costs at most a further factor \(t\).

Here is the local step that allows residual invariants at \(p\). For \(a=a_p\), let \(E=L_\psi(\mu_{p^{a+1}})\), where \(L_\psi\) is cut out by the characteristic-zero character. Write \(M=A[p^a]\), a trivial module on \(G_E\). Every class in \(S[p^a]\) has a lift to \(H^1(\mathbb Q,M)\) by the multiplication exact sequence. At \(p\) any representing cocycle for this lift has the form \((\rho(g)-1)y\) with \(y\in A\), since its image with divisible coefficients is zero. Now \(p^a y\) is invariant under \(G_{\mathbb Q_p}\). Choose a preimage in inertia of \(1+p\) under the cyclotomic character, and raise it to \(d\) to kill \(\psi\). Lifting the exponent for odd \(p\) gives
\[
 v_p\big((1+p)^{-de}-1\big)=1+v_p(e).
\]
Consequently \(p^{a+1+v_p(e)}y=0\). At a place above \(p\) in \(E\), the finite character is trivial and the cyclotomic character lies in \(1+p^{a+1}\mathbb Z_p\). Its \((-e)\)th power differs from 1 by a multiple of \(p^{a+1+v_p(e)}\), so the lift is split there. At each other finite prime its restriction on inertia over \(E\) is zero because the full coefficient action there is trivial and the original restriction is a coboundary. Thus all lifts in question restrict to homomorphisms factoring through \(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_E[1/p])\). Including the one extra cyclotomic layer avoids assuming that the invariant correction at \(p\) vanishes.

Put \(D=[E:\mathbb Q]\le d p^{a+1}\) and \(\Delta=\mathrm{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\). The kernel of global restriction is \(H^1(\Delta,M)\). Its order is at most \(|M|\). Indeed restriction to the \(p\)-Sylow subgroup is injective by transfer, and that Sylow embeds into the cyclic \(p\)-part of \((\mathbb Z/p^{a+1})^\times\) because \(d\) is prime to \(p\). A crossed homomorphism on a cyclic group is determined by one value. Since \(M\cong(\mathbb Z/p^a)^f\) additively, the number of homomorphisms from the class group into \(M\) is at most \(h(E)^f\), by elementary divisors. This proves
\[
 (\log p)\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O}S[p^a]
      \le a\log p+\log h(E).
\]
For the sharper Case I estimate one can use the finite character field of the coefficient modulo \(p^a\), of degree at most \(d p^a\) and conductor dividing \(Np^a\), without the inflation cost. The local nontriviality used in that improvement was checked separately. [Case I finite-level description](AlpogeSelmerResidual.md)

Minkowski gives an integral ideal of norm at most \(2^D\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}E|}\) in every class. The number of ideals of norm at most \(T\) is at most \(T^2\zeta_E(2)\), with \(\zeta_E(2)\le\zeta(2)^D<2^D\) by the Euler factors. All character conductors divide \(Np^{a+1}\), so conductor–discriminant gives \(h(E)\le(8Np^{a+1})^D\). There are at most \(N\) characters per prime, at most \(Y\) primes, and \(p^{a+1}\le Y^2\). These inequalities give (1). This argument includes primes dividing the host conductor and all depths satisfying the cut.

There is also a full-depth polynomial subrange that allows nonzero reflected eigenspaces. Suppose \(\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}\ne1\). Let \(F_1\) be cut out by \(\bar\rho\) and let \(p^h\) be the exponent of the \(p\)-primary group \(C=\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_{F_1}[1/p])[p^\infty]\), allowing \(h=0\). If \(v_p(e)\ge h\), then the entire strict group is already killed by \(p^h\), and its order is the order of
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Gal}(F_1/\mathbb Q)}
       \big(C,(K/\mathcal O)(\widetilde{\bar\rho})\big),                      \tag{4}
\]
where the lift of the residual character is the Teichmüller lift. Indeed the quotient of \(\rho\) by this lift is the \((-e)\)th power of the principal-unit part of the cyclotomic character. It is trivial modulo \(p^{1+v_p(e)}\). Thus all finite coefficient fields up through depth \(h+1\) equal \(F_1\) and their actions are the reductions of the same character. The exact finite-layer identification from the companion residual descent applies. Homomorphisms from \(C\) at depths \(h,h+1\) have the same order, since \(p^h C=0\). Equality of these consecutive kernels rules out any element at larger depth. For \(h=0\) the residual Hom is zero and the same conclusion holds.

For every fixed host and weight, the total unrestricted-depth contribution of all terms satisfying these hypotheses is at most \(N^2 e\log(8Ne)\). Only \(h\ge1\) can contribute, so \(p\mid e\). The contribution per character after normalization is at most \(\log h(F_1)\le d p\log(8Np)\), by the same Minkowski estimate, using conductor dividing \(Np\). Sum over at most \(N\) characters and use \(\sum_{p\mid e}p\le e\) for \(e\ge2\); the latter follows by induction from \(u+v\le uv\) for distinct prime factors. At \(e=1\) every retained term vanishes. In any fixed nontrivial local residual weight class modulo \(p-1\), both \(F_1\) and \(h\) are fixed. Chinese remaindering supplies infinitely many positive weights in that class divisible by \(p^h\). Formula (4) is an exact full-depth calculation at all of them. Neither the degree nor the weight need be bounded by a fixed power of the window's small exponent. [Finite-layer descent and stabilization](AlpogeSelmerResidual.md)

## Reflection gives a test at every depth

Suppose \(\bar\rho\ne1\) globally, and set \(F=L_\psi(\mu_p)\), a CM field of degree prime to \(p\). Its group is \(G\), and \(\eta=\omega\bar\rho^{-1}=\bar\psi\omega^{e+1}\) is odd. Then
\[
 \dim_k S[p]\le
 \dim_k\big(\mathrm{Cl}(F)[p]\otimes_{\mathbb F_p}k\big)^{\eta}
 +\mathbf 1_{\bar\rho|_{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}=1}.                                   \tag{2}
\]
The superscript denotes the character eigenspace. In Case I the indicator is zero, since the square of the finite character on inertia is trivial and \(p-1\nmid2e\). [Original prime and character cases](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/HeightAndVanishing/Cases.lean#L9-L40)

To prove (2), the global lift to \(H^1(\mathbb Q,A[p])\) is unique because \(A^{G_{\mathbb Q}}=0\). Its local value at \(p\) lies in the connecting image of \(A^{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}/p\), of dimension at most one by the principal-unit calculation above, and zero when the residual action is locally nontrivial. On the kernel of this local map restriction to \(F\) is injective by transfer. It gives everywhere unramified characters, split above \(p\), with \(G\)-type \(\bar\rho^{-1}\) in the scalar-valued dual. Multiplication by the cyclotomic line identifies these with a subspace of the \(\eta\)-part of \(F^\times/F^{\times p}\otimes k\) by Kummer theory. An unramified Kummer extension requires every valuation of its radicand to be divisible by \(p\), as otherwise the valuation group increases. Sending a radicand whose principal ideal is \(\mathfrak a^p\) to \([\mathfrak a]\) gives a map to \(\mathrm{Cl}(F)[p]\). Its kernel consists of unit classes. The odd part of the units modulo \(p\) comes entirely from roots of unity: for any unit \(u\), all conjugates of \(u/\bar u\) have absolute value one, and \(2\) is invertible modulo \(p\). These roots of unity give just the character \(\omega\), different from \(\eta\) because \(\bar\rho\ne1\). Taking eigenspaces is exact since \(p\nmid|G|\). This proves the claimed injection on the locally split part. [Gras, *Théorèmes de réflexion*, JTNB 10 (1998), 399–499, for the general reflection framework](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.234/)

There is an integer floor-sum test for the mirror obstruction. Let \(m=\operatorname{lcm}(\mathrm{cond}\,\psi,p)\). For \(b>0\) coprime to \(m\) compute in \(k\)
\[
 u_b=\sum_{1\le j<m,\ (j,m)=1}\lfloor bj/m\rfloor\,\eta(j)^{-1}.              \tag{3}
\]
If any \(u_b\ne0\), the mirror dimension in (2) is zero. In particular the **full strict group vanishes** when the local residual action is nontrivial. This conclusion follows at every depth, since a continuous cocycle into the discrete torsion module has finite image and any nonzero torsion group contains a class of order \(p\). When the local action is trivial this test only bounds the residual dimension by one.

Indeed Stickelberger's annihilation theorem on \(C=\mathbb Q(\mu_m)\) applies to the integral element
\((b-\sigma_b)\sum (j/m)\sigma_j^{-1}\). Its coefficient at \(\sigma_j^{-1}\) is \((bj-(bj\bmod m))/m\). The norm from \(C\) to \(F\) is surjective on the odd sign of the \(p\)-part of ideal classes. To check the possible defect, write \(H_F\) for the Hilbert class field. Every class acting trivially on \(H_F\cap C\) has a prime representative splitting completely in \(C/F\) by Chebotarev applied to \(H_F C/F\); hence it is a norm. The Galois action on \(\mathrm{Gal}((H_F\cap C)/F)\) is trivial since \(C/\mathbb Q\) is abelian. Therefore this quotient cannot obstruct the odd component. Projecting the annihilator to \(G\) now acts on the mirror eigenspace by (3). This proof works when \([C:F]\) is divisible by \(p\). [Stickelberger, *Ueber eine Verallgemeinerung der Kreistheilung*, Math. Ann. 37 (1890), 321–367](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01721360)

For the odd quadratic character of conductor seven and \(p=5\), the two possible odd residues of \(e\) modulo four yield \(u_2=4,3\) respectively. The local residual inertia character is nontrivial in both cases. At \(p=17\), take the real dyadic character of conductor 32 specified by \(\psi(5)=2\in\mathbb F_{17}\) and \(\psi(-1)=1\). For the even weights \(2,4,6,8,10,12,14,16\) the pairs \((b,u_b)\) are \((3,2),(3,1),(5,9),(3,8),(3,11),(3,14),(3,1),(3,5)\). At the final weight the residual inertia character is trivial, but \(\psi(17)=2^4=-1\), so the local residual representation is still nontrivial. This proves full vanishing at every positive even weight for this character, by periodicity of the residual test. Running all four conjugate characters at this split prime gives certificates in 30 of 32 character–weight cells. For \(\psi^3,e\equiv2\bmod16\) and \(\psi^7,e\equiv8\bmod16\) the residues are zero for every unit representative \(1\le b<544\). A zero test does not determine the strict group. A further check with \(p=3\) and the odd quadratic character of conductor 107 gives zero for all 212 unit representatives modulo 321, consistent with the nonzero example on the associated multiquadratic host. [Strict opposite-sign example](AlpogeSelmerGrowth.md)

The last case gives an explicit nonzero rational-character row of (4). Let \(p=3\), \(\psi=\chi_{-107}\) and \(e\) be odd. The residual product \(\bar\rho=\chi_{-107}\omega\) cuts out \(F_1=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{321})\), and its inertia character at three is nontrivial. The integral norm calculation in the preceding linked example proves \(C\cong\mathbb Z/3\). The nontrivial quadratic automorphism acts on \(C\) by inversion because a class times its conjugate is principal. Thus \(S[3]\cong\mathbb Z/3\) for every odd weight, and **the entire group is exactly \(\mathbb Z/3\) whenever \(e\equiv3\bmod6\)**, by (4) with \(h=1\). This gives one explicitly nonzero summand with normalized length one in each such weight, including when the primitive initial character is chosen to be \(\chi_{-107}\) and both moving primes grow. The other strict conditions at primes dividing the host modulus are included in the finite-layer comparison. This prime satisfies \(p-1\mid2e\), outside Case I.

## Large dyadic-order characters at each fixed odd prime

For fixed \(p,q,d_0,\chi\), where \(d_0\) is the moving quadratic prime, there is a finite dyadic level after which every newly appearing prime-to-\(p\) host character has \(S=0\) **at every positive weight of its parity**. This includes weights for which the residual cyclotomic character is trivial. To make the level statement precise, let \(F_0\) contain \(\mu_p\) and the maximal prime-to-\(p\) subextension of the compositum of the fixed initial character field and the two fixed quadratic fields. Take \(F_0\) itself abelian of degree prime to \(p\), and put \(F_s=F_0\,\mathbb Q(\mu_{2^{s+2}})^+\). Every prime-to-\(p\) character of the host factors through the corresponding compositum by abelian primary decomposition. Here “new” means nontrivial on \(\mathrm{Gal}(F_s/F_{s_0})\). The assertion quantifies uniformly over \(s\ge s_0\), \(e\) and the character once \(p,q,d_0,\chi\) are fixed.

Washington's cross-prime theorem makes the \(p\)-class numbers of the \(F_s\) bounded. Beyond any initial repetitions these are the layers of a cyclotomic \(\mathbb Z_2\)-extension of an abelian base. Extension of ideal classes is injective on the \(p\)-parts, because norm after extension multiplies by a power of two. Bounded orders therefore become constant, and extension is then surjective as well. Every relative tower automorphism fixes these extended classes. Enlarge \(s_0\) so that \(\mathrm{Gal}(F_\infty/F_{s_0})\) is contained in a decomposition group above \(p\). Indeed the image of \(p\) in the real dyadic Galois group has infinite order (no positive power of \(p\) is \(\pm1\) in \(\mathbb Z_2\)), so its closed subgroup has finite index. The projection from \(F_\infty\) to the pure dyadic tower has finite kernel, which preserves openness of the decomposition subgroup. This reasoning includes a prime ramifying in \(F_0\).

On an automorphism in this relative group detected by the new character, \(\omega\) is trivial and \(\bar\psi\) is nontrivial since its order is prime to \(p\). The local and global invariant corrections therefore both vanish. Restricting the residual strict lift to \(F_s\) now gives a class-group character as in the proof of (2), with target of nontrivial relative type and source on which the relative group acts trivially. It vanishes. This kills the entire strict group, including any high powers. For characters trivial on both moving quadratic factors the same proof uses only the fixed initial character field and \(\mu_p\), giving a threshold independent of both moving primes. For any **fixed finite** set of coefficient primes the maximum of these thresholds works. Washington's result here furnishes no uniform level when those primes and the two moving factors vary together. [Washington, *The non-p-part of the class number in a cyclotomic* \(\mathbf Z_p\)*-extension*, Invent. Math. 49 (1978), 87–97, theorem on p. 87](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01399512)

There is an explicit unrestricted-order consequence with varying \(p\). Take a real character \(\psi\) solely from the dyadic tower, \(p-1\mid e\), and
\[
 p<10^9\quad\hbox{or}\quad p\not\equiv\pm1\pmod {32}.
\]
If \(\psi(p)\ne1\), then \(S=0\). Indeed \(\bar\rho=\bar\psi^{-1}\), with nontrivial local Frobenius, and its residual field is precisely one of the real dyadic layers, whose class number is prime to \(p\) by Fukuda–Komatsu. For \(\psi=1\) and \(p-1\mid e\) the global and local cyclotomic images coincide and a residual lift can be corrected to be unramified everywhere over \(\mathbb Q\), giving the same vanishing. In particular, **for every \(p\equiv3,5\bmod8\), every real dyadic character of every order, and every positive multiple \(e\) of \(p-1\), the entire strict group vanishes**. A prime in either congruence class topologically generates the real dyadic tower: \(v_2(p^2-1)=3\) and \(v_2(p^{2^j}-1)=j+2\) for \(j\ge1\). Thus its Frobenius is detected by each nontrivial character. At \(p=3\) the conclusion applies to all positive even weights. On the source rows this gives a zero budget for these characters even with simultaneously unbounded weight, prime and dyadic order, independently of the moving primes. [Fukuda and Komatsu, *Weber's class number problem in the cyclotomic* \(\mathbb Z_2\)*-extension of* \(\mathbb Q\)*, III* (2011), main theorem](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793042111004782) · [Global-to-local correction for the trivial residual character](AlpogeSelmerResidual.md)

## Verification and the remaining global estimate

The formal companion proves the pairing character identity, vanishing of a character against trivial tower action, descent of a fixed action through a surjection, the kernel/rank bound, evaluation of an annihilator on an eigenvector, the integral floor coefficient, the principal-unit valuation and denominator cancellation, and both explicit conductor-seven residues. These are algebraic and computational ingredients. The class-field and Kummer identifications, arithmetic bounds (1) and (2), the analytic application of Washington and Fukuda–Komatsu, and the final source statements remain unformalized here. [Lean companion](AlpogeSelmerReflection.lean)

For the full CountI or ThmHV budget a prime can exceed every chosen polynomial cutoff, and a nonzero reflected eigenspace allows contributions beyond every shallow layer covered by that cutoff. The weighted sum of these deep contributions uniformly in the moving characters, weights and odd primes remains open. A first arithmetic test for a remaining cell is the inverse character congruence (3); when it vanishes, the higher class-group layers or a separate quantitative bound are necessary.
