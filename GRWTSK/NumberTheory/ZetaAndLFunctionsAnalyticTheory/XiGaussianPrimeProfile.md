# The arithmetic oscillation of high heat derivatives

Fix \(t>0\), \(s=\sqrt{n/t}\), \(W=s^{2n}e^{-n}\), \(L=\sqrt{\pi/(2t)}\). Let \(H_n=(-1)^nH^{(n)}(t)\) for the real ξ heat trace. As \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{2\pi H_n}{LW}=\log\frac{s}{2\pi}
 -2\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\sqrt m}
 e^{-(\log m)^2/(8t)}\cos(s\log m)+o(1).
\]
The prime series is absolutely and uniformly convergent in the saddle height. The bounded oscillation survives after subtracting the growing logarithmic density. The exact order sampling and the mean square are given in the [sampling argument](XiHeatPhaseSampling.md). The underlying unconditional identity is [Carneiro and Milinovich, Lemma 2.2](https://www.math.bas.bg/mathmod/Proceedings_CTF/CTF-2023/files_CTF-2023/05-Carneiro-Milinovich.pdf).

Use the test \(h(u)=u^{2n}e^{-tu^2}\) of the [prime envelope](XiHeatPrimeEnvelope.md). Its normalized two-saddle approximation is \(G(u)=e^{-2t(u-s)^2}+e^{-2t(u+s)^2}\). On the positive half-axis use \(z=\sqrt t(u-s)\), allowing a bounded imaginary part. There
\[
 h(u)/W=\exp(2n\log(1+z/\sqrt n)-2\sqrt n z-z^2).
\]
For \(|z|<\sqrt n\) the exponent differs from \(-2z^2\) by at most \((2/3)|z|^3/(\sqrt n-|z|)\), by the absolutely convergent log series. On the complement of a bounded central interval, (2) in the prime envelope gives an integrable Gaussian bound uniform in large \(n\) on each fixed horizontal strip, since \(0\le\sqrt{u^2+b^2}-|u|\le b\) for real \(u\) and shift of magnitude \(b\). Reflection gives the second saddle. Thus on \(\Im u=-1\) the integral of \(|h/W-G|\) tends to zero. Shifting the Fourier contour bounds its prime-sum error by this integral times \(\sum\Lambda(m)m^{-3/2}<\infty\). The Gaussian Fourier integral gives \(\widehat G(v)=2L e^{-v^2/(8t)}\cos(sv)\). The pole tends exponentially to zero. [Translated Gaussian Fourier integrals in Lean](XiGaussianPrimeProfile.lean)

For the archimedean term an absolute \(o(1)\) after subtracting \(\log(s/(2\pi))\) is required. One can truncate at \(|\Re z|\le n^{1/16}\). The exponent error there is \(O_t(n^{-5/16})\). Outside, a Gaussian majorant bounds the discarded integral even with the weight \(1+\log(2+|u|)\), by \(O_t((1+\log s)e^{-c n^{1/8}})\). Inside, \(D(u)=\log(s/(2\pi))+O_t(n^{-7/16})\) by the digamma sum-integral estimate. Applying this on both real saddles proves the formula including its absolute remainder.

## Uniform arithmetic constants for small positive times

For \(n>10^{21},0<t\le1.69\), the same approximation after scaling \(w=\sqrt t(|u|-s)\) has the following uniform bounds. On \(|w|\le12\) on the real axis or on either shifted half-line of height one, \(|z|\le14\), and the exponent error is less than \(6\cdot10^{-8}\). At \(|w|>12\) the modulus bound for the shifted test is at most \(e^{2t}e^{-9w^2/16}\); the other Gaussian, centered across the origin, has negligible integrated mass there and on the central interval (its nearest scaled distance on that half-line is \(\sqrt n\)). Integrating the two central relative errors against \(e^{2t-2w^2}\) and the tails against these envelopes gives \(\sqrt t\int_{\Im u=-1}|h/W-G|\,|du|<2\cdot10^{-5}\). These estimates apply for arbitrarily small \(t\). The factor \(\sqrt t\) cancels the scale in \(L\).

On both real central intervals \(|w|\le12\), \(|u|/s\ge1-12/\sqrt n\). For \(s\ge1.5\cdot10^{12}\) the digamma lower bound gives \(D(u)\ge\log(s/(2\pi))-10^{-8}\). The relative real mass on those intervals is at least \(1-10^{-6}\) in units of \(2L\), accounting for both the exponent error and the omitted Gaussian tail. Outside, \(D\ge-7\) and the real Gaussian tail makes the negative contribution smaller than \(10^{-8}\). The pole error is below \(10^{-8}\) by the pole bound in the [prime envelope](XiHeatPrimeEnvelope.md). The prime error after normalizing by \(L\) is at most \(2\cdot10^{-5}\sqrt{2/\pi}\sum\Lambda(m)m^{-3/2}<0.0002\), using \(\Lambda(m)\le\log m\) and comparison with \(\int_1^\infty \log(2x)x^{-3/2}dx<6\). This proves the uniform lower bound used for the all-order time interval. The fixture also evaluates the actual divisor at \(t=1,n=3600\) using all ordinates below 100 and a rigorous omitted-tail bound. Its normalized value is 3.3948356663, against a prime-profile prediction near 3.3935589063. This separate finite comparison checks the sign and scale in the formula. At the high sample \(s=10^{15}+10\), \(t=1.69\), the order \(n=169(s/10)^2\) is an exact integer. The same estimates give a two-sided enclosure of the actual normalized derivative. On the real central intervals the digamma comparison has the same absolute error in both directions and the mass differs from one, in units of \(2L\), by less than \(10^{-6}\). On the complement \(|D(u)|\le 42+|w|\) at this height by the same sum-integral bound for \(|u|\ge1\), and monotonicity of its real part below one. Multiplying this bound by the Gaussian tail gives an error below \(10^{-8}\). Consequently the absolute error about the finite cosine sum is at most the positive prime-tail estimate plus 0.001. This high-order calculation uses the two real saddles and the prime column alone.
