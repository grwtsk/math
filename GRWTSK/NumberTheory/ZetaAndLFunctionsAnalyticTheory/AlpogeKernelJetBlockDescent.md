## The ambiguity lies in one fixed cyclotomic field

On a self-dual period lattice use \(R_\Lambda(x)=\alpha(x)U_x\), where \(\alpha(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{round}(c\,\mathrm{Re}\,H(x,x))}\). The triangular and fixed-point trace calculations for the actual normalized interchange operators \(J_{\Lambda,\Gamma}\) give finite quadratic sums. Their phases have an additional symmetry. For a triangular phase, conjugating the three signed translations by \(F(z)\mapsto F(-z)\) carries their product at \((x_0,x_2,x_1)\) to the product at \((-x_0,-x_2,-x_1)\). This conjugation preserves the section space on the common intersection because its sign is even. Both products are scalars, so \(q(-x)=q(x)\). For the fixed-point phase this follows directly from \(\alpha(\ell)\exp(-\pi H((A^{-1}-1/2)\ell,\ell))\), with \(A=1-g^{-1}\). Write \(b(x,y)=q(x+y)/(q(x)q(y))\). At \(y=-x\) evenness gives \(b(x,x)=q(x)^2\), hence \(q(nx)=q(x)^{n^2}\) for all integers \(n\). If the quadratic sum \(S\) is nonzero, translating by any member of \(\operatorname{rad} b\) gives \(q=1\) there. Thus \(q\) induces a homogeneous nonsingular finite quadratic module on \(G/\operatorname{rad} b\), with its additive form in \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) obtained from the roots of unity. Milgram's formula gives \(S/|S|\in\mu_8\), including at 2. On the group of order two the choice \(q(1)=i\) gives the primitive eighth root \((1+i)/\sqrt 2\) after normalization. The radical contributes its positive integer order. The isometries of the principally polarized lines ensure that the sums used here are nonzero. This argument needs evenness: on \(\mathbb Z/5\), \(q(x)=\exp(2\pi i(x^2+x)/5)\) has the same polarization as the unshifted quadratic form, but its normalized sum includes the nontrivial fifth root arising by completing the square. ## The target action on a single theta line

Let \(G_{L'}\) be the group of rational unitary automorphisms preserving \(L'\). Use the action on arguments \(Z\mapsto\gamma Z\) as in the stabilizer test. In source rank one with source lattice \(\mathcal O_E\) the target period lattice \(\Lambda_1=\Lambda(\mathcal O_E,L')\) is preserved by this group. Its theta space is a line: the inverse half-different makes the alternating form perfect on this lattice, and the positive theta line bundle on the complex torus consequently has precisely one holomorphic section up to scalar. The source's half-different lattice, trace-dual Hermitian Hom pairing and integrality of the quadratic sign give precisely this principal polarization. Choose a nonzero section \(h\). The Hermitian Gaussian and the even sign are preserved by \(\gamma\), so \(h(\gamma Z)=\lambda_{L'}(\gamma)h(Z)\). Evaluating at one point where \(h\ne0\) proves independence of the scalar from the section and multiplicativity in \(\gamma\). The character kills \([G_{L'},G_{L'}]\). For the standard source lattice \(\mathcal O_E^N\), specifying a Hom matrix is equivalent to specifying \(N\) columns independently from \(L'\). Multiplication by the half-different and passage to the period lattice respect this finite direct sum. The Hermitian forms in different column spaces are orthogonal. On lattice vectors the quadratic quantity inside **round** is integral, so the sign of a sum of columns is the product of their signs. Consequently \(\prod_{j=1}^N h(Z_j)\) is a nonzero section of the correct theta line. Its eigencharacter is \(\lambda_{L'}^N\). For an arbitrary self-dual source lattice \(L\), the normalized intertwiner from \(\Lambda(\mathcal O_E^N,L')\) to \(\Lambda(L,L')\) is an isometry on the section line. Both lattices are preserved by every \(\gamma\in G_{L'}\). Changing \(x\) to \(\gamma x\) permutes the finite quotient in the signed average, with identical Gaussian and sign factors, so the operator commutes with this stabilizer. The normalized chosen **eNat** at \((L,L')\) belongs to the resulting line and is nonzero. Its proportionality scalar cancels on comparing with its own pullback. Therefore for *every* such \(L,L'\)
\[
                    e_{L,L'}(\gamma Z)=
                      \lambda_{L'}(\gamma)^N e_{L,L'}(Z).
\]
This argument uses no equality between the independently normalized vacua at two rationally moved lattice pairs. In particular \(e_{L,L'}(-Z)=e_{L,L'}(Z)\) for every even \(N\), since \(-I\) is an involution. Odd-degree jets at a self-opposite label vanish; if a translate is normalized by the lattice-pair factor, this factor is common to both sides. ## Exact terminal twist-prime congruences

At a terminal twist prime write \(O=\mathcal O_{E,P}\), \(q=q(P)\), \(M\ge1\). The selected \(q\) is a uniformizer by \(q\in P\setminus P^2\). The integral matrix \(g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) acts on a column \((x,y/q^M)\), with \(x\in O^{N'-1},y\in O\). The first output must be integral and reduce to \(\bar A\bar x\); the last output must differ from \(y/q^M\) by \(q^M O\). For these requirements to hold for every column it is necessary and sufficient that
\[
      b\in q^{M+1}O^{N'-1},\qquad c\in q^MO^{1\times(N'-1)},\qquad
      d-1\in q^{2M}O .
\]
After clearing denominators the two errors are \(by\) modulo \(q^{M+1}\), and \(q^Mc x+(d-1)y\) modulo \(q^{2M}\). Taking \(x=0,y=1\) and \(x=e_j,y=0\) proves necessity; substitution proves sufficiency. The uniform original depth \(2M\) implies all three and cannot be uniformly reduced: \(d=1+q^{2M-1},x=0,y=1\) fails. For the source's product of a residue-class function of the first rows and a terminal Fourier function of the last row, the first factor therefore sees precisely \(\bar A\) and the second factor is unchanged on support. In detail, a perturbation of its covector by \(q^M O\) pairs integrally with the allowed \(q^{-M}\)-integral argument, so the additive character is trivial. The source's coefficient-domain assumption \(D(P)\ge2M\) ensures its character is defined even on the original pairings. For support equality in both directions one needs the same transformation property for \(g^{-1}\), supplied by the original level subgroup. The ordinary residue-block character and the other branches still have to be evaluated.
