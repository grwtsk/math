# Simultaneous cyclotomic specialization of the auxiliary ray theta elements

grwtsk contributors

For the canonical auxiliary coordinate character in Alpöge's archive, the ray-class construction yields its specified coordinate tie. For every twist index satisfying **NonzeroIndexPlace**, assume the source's coefficient integrality **adm9** for \(N_0\), its pair-deletion identity **adm10**, and the two theta hypotheses of **SetHyp**: the empty-level sums are nonzero and each \(\lambda\) has an admissible integer \(s_\lambda\). For every \(\varepsilon>0\) and finite forbidden set, there is a nonempty conjugation-stable auxiliary support and one ray character satisfying **PsiOk**, **partD**, and simultaneously for every \(\lambda\)
\[
 -N_0(i)\ \le\ v_\ell\!\left(\mathrm{thEval}_{Q,\lambda}
                    (\psi^{\pm1})\right)\ \le\ c_0(i,\lambda).
\]
All the displayed evaluations are nonzero. Both signs work, including the source's prescribed sign at each \(\lambda\). One conjugate pair suffices. Alternatively, for any \(r\ge1\) and prescribed open normal \(J\subset G_E\), one can use the archive's \(r\) left and \(r\) right pairs satisfying **SlopeTestRemainingChoices** and **Steps** for the same \(J\). For the nonempty choices described here this sharpens the upper bound \(c_0+\varepsilon\) in **partC**. The statement uses ordinary class field theory, Chebotarev, and the two named theta inputs. It imposes no Selmer determinant estimates. ## Recovering the actual canonical coordinates

Fix \(D,i\), put \(X=D.\mathrm{ntw}\), \(E=D.\mathrm{host}.E\), \(O=X.O(i)\), \(\ell=X.l(i)\), and \(M\ge1\). Write \(N=\ell^M\). Form the Kummer control field \(L/E\) and admissible family from the ray character construction. The source's canonical map takes, on each pair \(U=\{P,cP\}\), a choice \(d_U\) from **all** continuous anticyclotomic \(C_N\)-characters fully ramified to order \(N\) at both members, unramified elsewhere, and trivial on decomposition over \(\ell\). It uses the trivial character if this set is empty. At every admitted split \(P\) the ray construction proves that the set is nonempty. On any conjugation-closed \(Q\) and \(P\in Q\), the \(P\)-coordinate of the source map is therefore exactly \(d_{\{P,cP\}}\), even at a nonadmitted \(P\), where the same definition still applies. The family \(P\mapsto d_{\{P,cP\}}\) satisfies **IsCoord** on *every* such \(Q\), and **AdmFam** with the same Kummer open subgroup and exceptions on the admitted primes. This establishes **DelUTie** for the canonical realization independently of any claim that our concrete ray character was the one selected by classical choice. The archive installs that canonical map in the **withAll** series construction, where the universal-coordinate equality is reflexive. A record satisfying only the six general character laws permits the identically trivial map; at a nonzero index such a prescription cannot have the required tie, since split degree-one primes splitting in any proposed finite control extension exist outside every finite set. In particular, on a union of \(m\) distinct admitted pairs the image in the \(2m\) coordinates is **exactly** the subgroup constant on each pair, of order \(N^m\). The two coordinates at conjugate places are identical because the canonical choice depends on the unordered pair. Inertia at one representative of each pair maps onto its entire coordinate while killing the others. These \(m\) subgroups generate the claimed image. The character remains anticyclotomic as a function of its *Galois argument*. Confusing that action with inversion of the duplicate coordinate would, for instance, give the wrong order for a product of the two coordinate characters.

## Test places with unit deletion factors

Choose one place \(v\in X.pD(i)\), of residue characteristic \(p\ne\ell\). The image of its inertia under \(\delta=X.\mathrm{delta}(i)\) has order 15, and its norm is \(1\bmod15\), so \(p\notin\{3,5\}\). Choose a prime \(b\in\{3,5\}\) unequal to \(\ell\), and an inertia element whose \(\delta\)-value has order \(b\). The procyclic closure of the subgroup generated by this element is a product of its pro-primary components. Its pro-\(b\) component contains an element \(z\) with the same \(\delta\)-value, since the continuous map to the cyclic group of order \(b\) factors through this component. This \(z\) meets the source definition of **IsTameInertiaElementAtTwistPrime**. Its image in any finite quotient has \(b\)-power order, prime to \(\ell p\), and its \(\delta\)-value retains order \(b>2\) in the residue field of \(O\). For the latter assertion, roots of unity of order prime to \(\ell\) reduce injectively: if such a root reduces to 1 the geometric sum of its powers is a unit. Thus \(\delta(z)^2-1\) is a unit already in the source coefficient DVR. For each of the finitely many \(\lambda\), the residual Galois character \(\bar\lambda\) is continuous with finite image. At \(z\), the source relation says that \(\lambda(z)/\delta(z)^{\pm1}\), after coefficient transport, has \(p\)-power order. Its reduction is also an image of a pro-\(b\) element under a continuous character, hence has \(b\)-power order and is 1. Consequently \(\bar\lambda(z)=\bar\delta(z)^{\pm1}\ne1\) for **all** \(\lambda\). Let \(F/E\) be the composite of \(L\), the extension fixed by \(J\), and the fields cut out by the residual characters \(\bar\lambda\). As \(L\) is unramified at \(v\), \(z|_L=1\). Choose the left primes unramified in \(F\), with Frobenius conjugate to \(z|_F\). They split in \(L\), have norm \(1\bmod 2N\), and have \(\bar\lambda(\mathrm{Frob})=\bar\lambda(z)\). Chebotarev permits absolute residue degree one and avoidance of all forbidden places, rational ramification and previously selected pairs. For a right prime with label \(a\), let \(B/E\) be cut out by all the left canonical characters. Their inertia subgroups at the distinct left places generate \(\mathrm{Gal}(B/E)=C_N^r\); as \(F\) is unramified there, \(B\cap F=E\). In the resulting direct product prescribe the Frobenius \((e_a,z|_F)\). This gives the identity matrix of left-character values at the right primes and the **same** nontrivial residual \(\bar\lambda(z)\) at all right primes. Both lists satisfy the original **Steps** and avoidance requirements. One may instead take just one left prime if the two lists are unnecessary. Write \(Q\) for the union of the \(m=1\) or \(m=2r\) chosen pairs. With the trivial ray character, delete the pairs one at a time using **adm10**. For the representative of each pair let \(q\) be its norm and \(X_v=\lambda(\mathrm{Frob})\) the source unit. Modulo the maximal ideal the Euler factor becomes
\[
 (1-\bar X_v)(1-\bar X_v^{-1}),
\]
since \(q\equiv1\bmod\ell\) and \(q^{-s_\lambda}\equiv1\). Every factor is a unit by the Frobenius prescription. Writing \(a_\lambda=\sum_\delta X.\mathrm{th}(i,\varnothing,\lambda,\delta)\), this proves \(v_\ell(\mathrm{thEval}_{Q,\lambda}(1))=v_\ell(a_\lambda)=c_0(i,\lambda)\). The empty-level ray group itself may be nontrivial; the definition sums over all its elements.

## One conductor for all theta evaluations

Choose any units \(b_h\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\) for the \(m\) pairs and put \(d=\sum_h b_h d_h\), with one canonical coordinate per pair, in additive notation. This factors through \(X.\mathrm{RCG}(i,Q)\) by the source's Artin universal property; denote the factor by \(d\) again. Its inertia image at each member of \(Q\) is all of \(C_N\). For each \(\lambda\) define the actual polynomial over \(O\)
\[
 F_\lambda(T)=\sum_{\delta\in X.\mathrm{RCG}(i,Q)}
       \ell^{N_0(i)}X.\mathrm{th}(i,Q,\lambda,\delta)\,
                         (1+T)^{[d(\delta)]_N}.
\]
Here \([x]_N\) is the least nonnegative representative. Integrality follows coefficient by coefficient from **adm9**, since all the chosen levels are **QOk**. If \(e_O=\mathrm{ord}_O(\ell)\), then the nonzero constant term has discrete order
\[
 C_\lambda=e_O(N_0(i)+c_0(i,\lambda))\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.
\]
This number is known from the empty level and the denominator bound *before* choosing \(M\) or any primes. [Primitive roots and Artin universal property](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/LevelLetterCocycles/DataN.lean#L388-L403)

For an integral polynomial \(F\) with nonzero constant term of order \(C\), plot the points \((n,a_n)\) with \(a_n=\mathrm{ord}_O([T^n]F)\) for nonzero coefficients. At a positive real number \(s\) take the minimum of \(a_n+ns\). At most \(C\) distinct positive \(s\) can yield a tie. Indeed, at a tie take two minimizing degrees \(u<v\) and associate the intercept \(a_v\), an integer in \([0,C-1]\). For a larger tie \(t>s\), each minimizing degree \(w\) at \(t\) satisfies \(w\le u\), by adding the two minimizing inequalities at \(s,t\). Minimality at \(s\) then gives \(a_v<a_w\). This injects any prescribed collection of tied slopes into the \(C\) possible intercepts. At a nonexceptional \(s\), every argument of extended discrete order \(s\) gives an evaluation with order exactly the unique minimum, between 0 and \(C\), by the nonarchimedean triangle inequality. Scaling the extension valuation to restrict to \(\mathrm{ord}_O\) fixes these units throughout.

Choose \(j_0\ge1\) such that \(2mt/\varphi(\ell^{j_0})\le\varepsilon\), then take \(M=j_0+\sum_\lambda C_\lambda\). Only now choose the places and the polynomials. The exponents \(j_0,\ldots,M\) give distinct positive slopes \(e_O/\varphi(\ell^j)\); each is the extended discrete order of \(X.zk(i,j)-1\), also of its inverse minus 1. Together all polynomials exclude at most \(\sum_\lambda C_\lambda\) of these slopes. For a remaining \(j\), set \(\psi(\delta)=X.zk(i,j)^{[d(\delta)]_N}\). The exponent gives a homomorphism because \(\ell^j\mid N\). Dividing \(F_\lambda(X.zk(i,j)^{\pm1}-1)\) by \(\ell^{N_0(i)}\) now gives the corresponding source evaluation exactly, with the two valuation bounds above. Both inertia orders at each pair are \(\ell^j\), so **partD** has exact cost \(2mt/\varphi(\ell^j)\). The remaining **PsiOk** conditions follow from the canonical ray character and chosen primes. [Cyclotomic order and the exact tame cost](AlpogeTameConductorLength.md)

The constant-term bound is sharp. The product of the distinct shifted factors \(\Phi_{\ell^j}(1+T)\) for \(J\) chosen levels has constant term \(\ell^J\) and vanishes at every tested primitive argument for those levels. Dividing it by \(\ell^J\) shows why controlling only the augmentation, without a common coefficient denominator, would allow arbitrarily many failures.

## Dependencies and verification

The source proves **adm10** for an identified toric element using the pair-deletion law for class sums and Artin reciprocity. Its **adm9** supplier requires uniform bounded denominators for the period-normalized class terms over all admissible auxiliary levels. Neither the existence of a canonical ray character nor the level-zero nonvanishing proves that bound. The selected support here is subject to residual-unit Frobenius prescriptions. The existence of *one and the same support* giving the Selmer polynomial coefficient estimate for **partA** and **partAT** remains to be established. This argument supplies no such determinant, or proof of (M).
