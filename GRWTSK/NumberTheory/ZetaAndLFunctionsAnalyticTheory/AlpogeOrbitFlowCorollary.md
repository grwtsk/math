## Perturbing the original flow

Let \(p\ge5\), \(d\ge2\) even, and \(M(D)\) be the supremum of the source flow with capacities \(D_s\), on \(s\in\mathbf Z/d\). An edge \((s,i)\) for odd \(1\le i<d\) consumes \(p^i x_{s,i}\) at \(s\) and \(x_{s,i}\) at \(s+i\). All other variables in the source array vanish. Thus its feasible values coincide with those for this finite edge set, by restriction and extension by zero. Put \(F(D)=\gamma\sum_s D_s-M(D)\), with \(\gamma=p/(p^2-1)\). The original corollary takes \(k\ge3\), \(w_s>0\), \(0\le E_s\le4w_s\). Set \(K=k-1\), \(W=\sum_s w_s\). Its capacities are \(D_s=Kw_s+E_s\) off the marked set and \(D_s=(K-2)w_s+E_s\) on it, hence
\[
 (K-2)w_s\le D_s\le(K+4)w_s .
\]
[Original objective and its feasible values](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataParams%E2%82%82.lean#L51-L59) · [Original marked capacity conditions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/ProductZerothFirstParts.lean#L153-L176)

The zero flow is feasible. Summing all capacity inequalities counts \(p^i+1\ge p+1\) per unit of an edge value; in particular \(M(Kw)\le KW/(p+1)\). Multiplying a feasible array for \(D\) by \(K/(K+4)\) produces a feasible array for \(Kw\). Taking suprema and using the preceding bound gives
\[
 M(D)-M(Kw)\le \frac4K M(Kw)\le\frac4{p+1}W .
\]
Since \(\sum D_s\ge(K-2)W\), it follows that
\[
 F(D)\ \ge\ F(Kw)-\left(2\gamma+\frac4{p+1}\right)W
 \ \ge\ F(Kw)-\frac6pW.                                                \tag{1}
\]
The last inequality follows by clearing \(p(p^2-1)>0\), giving \(4p\ge6\). For every marked \(s\), the source's \(\mathrm{baseTerm}(s)\) is termwise at most \(\mathrm{topTerm}(s)\). For odd \(i<d_{\rm Top}\), the second row length only decreases the numerator; for odd \(i>d_{\rm Top}\) each positive part is at most the full first row length divided by \(p^i\). Both summands vanish at equality and at even \(i\). They are summable, being bounded by the geometric series with this same first row length. Applying (1) inside the original \(\mathrm{KvalGe}\) leaves the common marked \(-\gamma W_c\) terms and the original digit error unchanged. This proves \(\mathrm{Cor2}(6,C)\), and weakening both error constants gives \(\mathrm{Cor}(\max(6,C))\). The pinned comparison uses 11 for the weight error and 1 for the digit error. [Pinned comparison and separate constants](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/JetPolynomialLogs/LocalBoundElevenComparison.lean#L13-L67)

A two-cycle explains the limit of (1). Mark site 0, choose \(K>2\) integral and weights \((u,1)\), \(u=p(K+4)/(K-2)\). Set \(E=(0,4)\). Both flow values together cannot exceed the receiver capacity at site 1, and the edge from site 0 alone attains it in both programs. Their suprema are consequently \(K\) and \(K+4\). The coefficient of \(W/p\) needed in this example is
\[
 \frac{p}{u+1}\bigl(4+\gamma(2u-4)\bigr).
\]
Taking \(K\to\infty\) and then primes \(p\to\infty\) gives 6. All capacities can be made integral by scaling both weights and both errors by \(K-2\). The marked residual capacity can be represented by a two-row diagram with both rows equal to it; the receiver uses a one-row diagram. Choose sufficiently large rank and \(a\) to meet the Hida inequalities. Thus no smaller absolute constant works for this flow comparison over the source data. The other terms of the full corollary can create additional slack.

## The single-letter place

The source \(\mathrm{Loc1}\) starts with a two-site additive series \(\beta\) satisfying B1–B4, followed by the substitutions \(X_0=X,\ X_1=eX\) independently in every entry, with \(e\) a unit. Its B0 multiplies the merged exponent by \(\pm1/2\). The underlying \(\mathrm{JetLoc1}\) requires \(p\ge5\), so this scalar also has valuation zero. Its external rescaling \(ep_0\) has norm one. The one-letter Hida condition only requires \(p\le a\) and \(|\nu|\le a\). [Precise one-letter B0 and target](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/ProductZerothFirstParts.lean#L246-L276) · [Prime bound in the underlying record](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalRingCardinality/DataJet.lean#L36-L46)

Let \(\eta=1/(p-1)\) and \(\rho=1/(p^2-1)\). A nonzero degree-\(n\) coefficient of \(\beta\) has valuation at least \(\eta-\rho n\) unless its two opposite-parity exponents are individual \(p\)-powers, in which case the proven cost is at most one extra valuation unit. In that case the powers have unequal exponents, by the two-site torus comparison modulo \(p^2-1\), hence \(n\ge p+1\). In both cases the valuation is at least \(\eta-\gamma n\), since \(\gamma-\rho=1/(p+1)\). Units and the merging of variables preserve this bound in each degree. Taking the exponential loses at most \(m\eta\) for the factorial of \(m\) occurrences, so the resulting coefficients have valuation at least \(-\gamma n\). [Polarization, exceptional powers and their costs](AlpogeUnmarkedMinorFlow.md) · [Torus input on the additive coefficients](AlpogeHeightTwoTorusGain.md)

The inverse of the one-letter logarithm has coefficients of degree \(n\) of valuation at least \(-\rho(n-1)\), by triangular inversion as on the longer orbits. The integral factor after this substitution has degree-\(n\) valuation at least \(-\rho n\). Multiplying it by the exponential and undoing the unit \(ep_0\) preserves \(-\gamma n\). Each contributing minor monomial has total entry degree \(|\nu|\). Restoring its actual factorials by Legendre yields
\[
 v\!\left(\prod_j n_j!\,[X^{\boldsymbol n}]\mathrm{Th}\right)
 \ge \rho|\nu|-\eta\sum_j s_p(n_j)
 \ge \rho|\nu|-N_0N_1(1+\log_p a),
\]
since \(\eta-\gamma=\rho\) and \(0\le n_j\le a\). The integral minor coefficients and the ultrametric inequality now give precisely \(\mathrm{Loc1.ThmOne}(1)\), for every rank and every original Hida choice. Application to a particular place still requires its geometric B0–B4 witnesses. ## Integral straightening and the bounded expansion

At one site the source's \(\nabla_X(\nu,J)\) is the product of flag minors of a fixed shape. A column of height \(h\) uses the first \(h\) rows and the increasing list \(J_c\) of column indices. When a column has height \(>N\) there is no such list, so the span is zero; the empty diagram gives the span of the constant one. Otherwise the construction uses only the first \(N\) rows of the infinite-row polynomial ring. After transposing the matrix and numbering entries from one, these products are exactly the tabloid monomials of shape \(\nu\) on the alphabet \([N]\). The flag-minor straightening relation at an inversion of adjacent columns is an integral identity: the Laplace expansion of the determinant with overlapping rows, after column sorting, expresses the original monomial as a signed sum of earlier tabloids. This terminates because the shape has finitely many tabloids. Proposition 4.5 in Lax gives the precise strictly decreasing order and integer coefficients; hence the resulting identity holds over every commutative ring, including rings with zero divisors. [Source minors and the complement it asks for](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/SharpRestriction/Divisibility/ProductZerothFirstParts.lean#L78-L127) · [Lax, *Accessible proof of standard monomial basis for coordinatization of Schubert sets of flags*, Propositions 4.3–4.5 and Theorem 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.02744#page=8)

For the semistandard tableaux left after straightening, order the variables lexicographically by giving the earliest row and then the smallest column index priority. The dominant term of each minor is the diagonal, with coefficient \(+1\); choosing any later column in its first differing row yields a smaller monomial. The product for a tableau also has a monic diagonal term. A row's exponents in that monomial give the multiset of its entries. Sorting each row reconstructs the original semistandard tableau, so distinct semistandard products have distinct pivots. Subtract the appropriate coefficient times each standard product in descending pivot order. This finite triangular procedure gives a direct-sum decomposition of the entire polynomial module into the span of the standard products and the free span of all monomials outside their pivots. Arbitrary monomials involving subsequent rows pose no problem since there are only finitely many pivots. This supplies the precise \(\mathrm{MinorProdSpanHasCompl}\) over each coefficient ring. For any finite family of sites take products of the respective standard products. Disjoint variable sets again give distinct monic pivots. The archive separately phrases conversion of coefficient bounds as an implication from the complement property. [Archived complement interface](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/NablaSpans/MinorProdSpanHasCompl.lean#L10-L22) · [Archived implication](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/PeriodIntegrals/OrbitalBoundsConclusion/GenFrameSB.lean#L1-L6)

For an arbitrary input series \(\mathrm{Th}\) over \(\mathbf C_p\) and a given column choice \(I\), expand each source minor of \(XZ\) by Cauchy–Binet. The result is an integral sum of an \(X\)-flag minor times a \(Z\)-minor. Therefore applying the source's factorial-weighted linear functional in the \(Z\)-variables puts the output polynomial in the field-span of the products of \(X\)-flag minors. This includes the case \(h>N_0\) in which that determinant of the product is zero, even when the \(I\)-choice exists. Now suppose every coefficient of this polynomial has absolute value at most \(p^{-B}\). The monic triangular procedure takes as each successive standard coefficient its current pivot coefficient. Subtracting this scalar times a polynomial with integral coefficients preserves the same upper bound on every remaining coefficient. The final remainder vanishes because it belongs both to the field-span and to its pivot-free complement. All selected standard products are among the source's allowed column choices, so extending their coefficients by zero gives the required finitely supported expansion. In reverse, a bounded expansion gives the coefficient bound immediately by ultrametricity and integrality. This proves the source's \(\mathrm{Conclusion4}\) for all real \(B\), without rounding the threshold in the nondiscrete value group.
