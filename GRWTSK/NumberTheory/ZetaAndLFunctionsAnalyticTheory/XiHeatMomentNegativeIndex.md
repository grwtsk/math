# The negative index of the heat moments and the local arithmetic wave operator

Let \(K\in\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}\) count distinct off-line conjugate pairs of the rates \(\lambda=(g+i(\Re\rho-1/2))^2\) for \(g=\Im\rho>0\). Each such pair is one distinct off-line zero quadruple, irrespective of its analytic order. Fix arbitrary \(\tau>0\) and any integer \(k\ge0\). The supremum of the negative indices of the real polynomial forms
\[
  Q(P)=\sum_{\rho:\,g>0} m\,\lambda^k e^{-\tau\lambda} P(\lambda)^2
\]
over finite degrees is exactly \(K\). These are the Hankel forms of the [one-time arithmetic moments](XiTiltedHeatMomentCriterion.md). In particular, when \(K<\infty\) every sufficiently large finite section has exactly \(K\) negative eigenvalues. The previous inverse-power localization argument uses nodes in a compact disk. The unbounded heat-rate nodes require a density argument. [Compact-node comparison](XiNewtonMomentHilbertPolya.md)

## Density and the ideal that removes the negative directions

Write \(c_\lambda=m\lambda^k e^{-\tau\lambda}\) and use the positive norm \(\sum |c_\lambda||f(\lambda)|^2\) only to analyze the form. Polynomials are dense in this weighted sequence space. Indeed a continuous functional annihilating all polynomials gives coefficients \(b_\lambda\) with \(\sum |b_\lambda|^2/|c_\lambda|<\infty\) and \(\sum b_\lambda\lambda^j=0\) for all \(j\). The function \(\sum b_\lambda e^{-v\lambda}\) is analytic for \(\Re v>-\tau/2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz and Gaussian decay. On bounded imaginary parts the factors \(e^{|\Im v||\Im\lambda|}\) cost only an exponential linear in height. All its derivatives at zero vanish. Its Laplace transform on \(v>0\) therefore yields \(\sum b_\lambda/(s+\lambda)=0\) for \(s>0\). This normally convergent meromorphic sum has residue \(b_\lambda\) at each distinct pole. All the coefficients vanish. Symmetrization under conjugation shows that real polynomials are dense in the conjugation-invariant real subspace. The heat quadratic is continuous for this norm by Cauchy–Schwarz.

Each real rate is positive and gives a positive square. Each nonreal pair gives the real form \(2\Re(c_\lambda z^2)\) on the free complex coordinate \(z\), of real signature \((1,1)\). This bounds the negative index above. To obtain the lower bound, prescribe negative coordinate axes on any desired finite set of pairs, zero elsewhere, then approximate those finitely many vectors by real polynomials. Their Gram matrix remains strictly negative for sufficiently accurate approximation. The index result follows. The [Lean lemmas](XiHeatMomentNegativeIndex.lean) give the negative coordinate, change in quadratic value and finite-matrix error bound.

More precisely, the real polynomials \(q\) for which \(Q(qP)\ge0\) for every real polynomial \(P\) form exactly the ideal of polynomials vanishing at all nonreal rates. Repeat the density argument with weights \(c_\lambda q(\lambda)^2\) on their nonzero support; polynomial factors preserve exponential integrability. Thus if \(K\) is infinite, only the zero polynomial works. If \(K\) is finite, the unique monic generator \(q\) has degree \(2K\) and simple roots, one for every nonreal rate. Both the ideal and its generator are specified by the arithmetic Hankel moments. In this finite-index case the positive moment measure for \(Q(qP)\) has atoms at exactly the real rates. Dividing their masses by \(q(x)^2x^k e^{-\tau x}\) restores the true integer multiplicities. For each discarded root \(\lambda\), its multiplicity is recovered by dividing the residue of the one-time Stieltjes continuation at \(-\lambda\) by \(\lambda^k e^{-\tau\lambda}\). The diagonal real operator from the positive measure then gives the normalized ξ determinant divided by the finite product of \((1-z^2/\lambda)^{m_\lambda}\) over the discarded rates. This finite-index statement does not prove finiteness or vanishing of \(K\).

## A trace-class arithmetic operator on every finite interval

For **every** \(R>0\) take the integral operator \(A\) on \(L^2(0,R)\) with kernel \(V_\tau(x-y)\), defined from the real arithmetic heat data or the exact Gaussian prime formula. It is self-adjoint and trace class, and
\[
            n_-(A)=2K,\qquad \operatorname{Tr} A=R H(\tau).
\]
Thus the ordinary Fredholm determinant \(\det(I+zA)\) has exactly \(2K\) positive real zeros counted with multiplicity. This operator tests the missing spectral sign on an arbitrarily short interval. Its Fredholm determinant differs from the desired regularized ξ identity. [Arithmetic wave and target realization](XiHeatWavePrimeKernel.md)

To prove the formula write \(V_\tau(x)=\sum_\omega b_\omega e^{ix\omega}\) with the distinct frequencies \(\omega=\pm(g+id)\), \(b_\omega=m e^{-\tau\omega^2}/2\). Terms at conjugate frequencies have conjugate coefficients. The two exponentials in each rank-one kernel have product of \(L^2(0,R)\) norms at most \(R e^{R|\Im\omega|}\). Thus the series converges in trace norm and the trace is its integrated diagonal. The form on functions factors through evaluation \(f\mapsto \int_0^R f(x)e^{-ix\omega}dx\) and the Hermitian form on the weighted sequence space with norm \(\sum |b_\omega||z_\omega|^2\). The contributions are \(b_\omega z_\omega\overline{z_{\bar\omega}}\) or their simultaneous conjugate-index relabeling according to the Fourier convention; either convention gives one positive and one negative direction for each conjugate pair. Every real frequency gives a positive square. Each off-line rate pair yields two frequency pairs.

The evaluation map has dense range. An annihilator gives an entire exponential sum \(\sum a_\omega e^{ix\omega}\) vanishing almost everywhere on \((0,R)\), with \(\sum |a_\omega|^2/|b_\omega|<\infty\), after conjugating indices if needed. The identity theorem and its positive-half Laplace transform give the zero meromorphic function \(\sum a_\omega/(s-i\omega)\). Its residues force all \(a_\omega=0\). Finite negative subspaces can now be approximated as above. The compact self-adjoint spectral theorem identifies the resulting index with the eigenvalue count. The same proof using evaluation on any dense set of times instead of the integral implies that the negative indices of the nested matrices on the dyadic grids of \([0,R]\) increase to \(2K\). In particular the bounded-span all-size problem detects every off-line quartet even if the two-point test there passes.

At \(\tau=1/100\), \(R>0\), the trace of the negative part obeys the unconditional estimate
\[
  R^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(A_-)
   \le T^2 e^{-\tau(T^2-1/4)+R/2}\sum_{g>T} m/g^2,\qquad T=3\cdot10^{12}.
\]
Remove the positive trace-class sum over the verified real frequencies. The trace of the negative part is at most the trace norm of the remaining sum, by compression to the negative spectral subspace. Use the preceding rank-one bound and the monotonicity of \(g^2 e^{-\tau g^2}\). This estimate allows arbitrarily small negative eigenvalues. Exact finite-index operator theory for related indefinite kernels appears in the generalized Nevanlinna literature; the present compact interval uses the actual Fourier heat test and its Gaussian weights.
