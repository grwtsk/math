# The archimedean factor of the normalized theta norm

grwtsk contributors

Let \(n=\dim_E V\), \(r=\dim_E W\), \(u=n-r\), and let \((p,q)\) be the signature of \(W\) at a real place of the host field. Write \(\alpha_i,\beta_j\) for the two decreasing arrays of natural numbers in the source's \(\mathrm{aPlAt},\mathrm{aMiAt}\), indexed here from one. Set \(D_a=\prod_{k=0}^{a-1}k!\) and \(C_r=2^r\pi^{r(r+1)/2}\). Let \(d_\pi\) be the formal degree for the source's Haar measure \(\mathrm{Lev.IsMinf}\), \(L\) the lowest compact type, \(T\) the harmonic compact \(U(n)\)-type and \(H_\gamma\) the product of hook lengths of a partition. Then
\[
 \frac{\dim T\,\dim L}{d_\pi}\,H_\alpha H_\beta
 = \frac{C_r D_u}{D_nD_pD_q}
   \prod_{i=1}^p(\alpha_i+n-q-i)!\,
   \prod_{j=1}^q(\beta_j+n-p-j)!.                                      \tag{1}
\]
This gives the entire weight-dependent real factor of the archived scalar. In particular its ratio to the two hook products is bounded below independently of the sizes of the rows. The following derivation uses the arithmetic data before the claimed inequality or any conclusion on zeta zeros. ## Which coefficients are paired

Fix any full lift datum, including the embedding over \(K\), the characters, frames, level vector and intertwiner. For the two polynomial inputs of \(\mathrm{InnerProductFormulaC}\) put \(\phi_i=\mathrm{ptAt}(Q_i)\); the two cusp inputs are the values of arbitrary maps in \(\mathrm{Wall}\) on arbitrary lowest-type vectors. The source's up-lift pairs its theta kernel with the conjugated cusp form. Conjugating the [normalized global pairing](AlpogeGlobalDoublingPairing.md) therefore gives
\[
 \langle\mathrm{upLift}_1,\mathrm{upLift}_2\rangle_V
 =\nu(\mathcal F)\int_{G(\mathbb A_F)}
       (\omega(1,g)\phi_1,\phi_2)_X\,
       \overline{(R(g)F_1,F_2)_W}\,dg .
\]
The three parentheses here are linear in their first entry. On the additive quotient the covolume is one. The compact quotient on the left uses the given \(\nu\). Rapid cusp decay against the theta kernel gives convergence for every fixed source element. Cauchy–Schwarz on the compact definite quotient gives integrability of the two lifts' product. The absolute orbit estimate in the global derivation gives integrability of the displayed adelic integrand.

At finite places the principal level fixes both the cusp input and the modified oscillator test. At the chosen split primes it acts trivially on the residue data specifying the support and the characters of that test. At unmodified places the fixing condition is the lattice-vacuum law. Finite transformations commute with the real polynomial creation operators. The archived factorization into compact finite cosets and real Haar measures assigns each finite principal-level coset mass one. It therefore changes the integral into \(\int \sum_x Z(xb)\,d\prod m_\rho(b)\); Tonelli applied first to the absolute value gives the required finite integral of the sum of norms. The countable coset set can be infinite. Unimodularity and the independence of quotient integrals from the fundamental domain justify the original arbitrary choice of representatives, domains and orthonormal frames. ## Cancellation of the root products

The compact source coefficient has local highest weight \((\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_p,0^u,-\beta_q,\ldots,-\beta_1)\) up to a determinant twist or simultaneous duality. Indeed the first column flag transforms as the standard exterior powers and the last flag as their duals. In triangular coordinates the relevant minors transform by the diagonal characters of their respective flags, since adding earlier participating columns to a column fixes the determinant. The global upper triangular unipotent stabilizes the two extremal flags for these dual actions. Their exponents telescope to the displayed torus weight. The extremal tensor maps to this nonzero polynomial. With a nonzero finite test its Fock embedding is injective, and the irreducibility and trivial finite action of \(c.W\) identify the orbit it generates with the outer tensor product of these compact factors. Nonvanishing holds in the application to the scalar by the positive lattice norm. The zero-test case of the preceding coefficient identity follows directly by linearity. On the small group the lowest type has positive weights \(\alpha_i+n/2\) and negative weights \(-\beta_{q+1-j}-n/2\), up to a common scalar shift. In oscillator coordinates the opposite-row compact generator is \(n+N_++N_-\). This fixes the difference of the determinant twists of the two blocks even if the splitting shifts each individual weight. Equivalently the source computes these two arrays by removing the common twist in its geometric weight. The embedding over \(K\) fixes the imaginary quadratic generator, selecting the positive-first holomorphic chamber at every place. Writing \(\kappa\) for this compact weight, its Harish-Chandra parameter is \(\lambda=\kappa+\rho_c-\rho_n\). Here \(\rho_n=(q/2,\ldots,q/2;-p/2,\ldots,-p/2)\), while \(\rho_c\) is half the descending progression \(s-1,s-3,\ldots,1-s\) on each block of size \(s\). Consequently
\[
   \lambda_i^+ - \lambda_{q+1-j}^-
      = \alpha_i+\beta_j+n+1-i-j >0 .
\]
This is exactly the gap between rows \(i\) and \(n+1-j\) in the shifted Weyl numerator of the large compact group. Set \(x_i=\alpha_i+p-i\), \(y_j=\beta_j+q-j\). Let \(A=\prod_{i<i'}(x_i-x_{i'})\), \(B=\prod_{j<j'}(y_j-y_{j'})\), and \(C=\prod_{i,j}(x_i+y_j+u+1)\). The compact Weyl dimension formula gives \(\dim L=AB/(D_pD_q)\), while the Harish-Chandra degree formula for the raw Haar gives \(d_\pi=ABC/C_r\). The Haar constant follows by calibrating on the unweighted matrix ball with consecutive parameters. Its compact dual has total raw volume \(C_r/D_r\). Taking compact Haar probability in place of the prescribed raw measure would lose that volume.

The large compact Weyl coordinate list, up to common translation, is
\[
  (x_i+u)_{i=1}^p,\quad (u-1,u-2,\ldots,0),\quad
  (-1-y_j)_{j=q}^1.
\]
In the middle-to-bottom differences the last list is read in reverse order. For each top or bottom bead \(x\), the product of its distances to the middle list is \((x+1)\cdots(x+u)\). Writing \(M_x,M_y\) for the products over the corresponding beads, the large dimension is \(ABD_u M_xM_y C/D_n\). The Weyl product itself follows by evaluating the alternant character on \(\operatorname{diag}(1,z,\ldots,z^{n-1})\) and taking \(z\to1\). Each top hook row consists of distances from its bead to the vacant positions below it. Removing the distances to occupied beads from \(x_i!\) gives the hook row. Multiplying rows gives \(H_\alpha=\prod_i x_i!/A\) and similarly for \(H_\beta\). Substitution cancels \(A,B,C\), and \(x!\,(x+1)\cdots(x+u)=(x+u)!\) proves (1). Empty blocks give empty products equal to one. ## Uniform error in the norm

After dividing by \(H_\alpha H_\beta\), the weight-dependent numerator is \(AB M_xM_y\). Every within-block factor is at least its value at the zero partition because the rows are decreasing. The same holds separately for every factor in \(M_x,M_y\). The exact minimum over partitions is thus achieved at the vacuum and equals
\[
 \frac{C_r}{D_pD_q\prod_{i,j}(n+1-i-j)}\ \ge\ n^{-\binom r2}.
\]
There are exactly \(\binom r2\) factors in this denominator when the two consecutive Vandermondes are expanded, all at most \(n\); \(C_r\ge1\). As a check, in signature \((1,1)\) the vacuum value is \(4\pi^3/(n-1)\), also obtained by integrating the scalar Bergman coefficient on the unit disk. In a definite signature the vacuum gives \(C_r/D_r\).

For the actual eigenvalue \(l\) of the normalized composite on a nonzero finite eigenspace, retain the source's finite convolution identity and the volume/index-times-norm identity as explicit inputs. Put \(d_S=\prod_{v\in S}d_v\). The former contributes \(|P.H|\,\|f\|^2\,\mathrm{eulerOff}/d^{S}\); the level-adjusted adjoint divides by \(|P.H|\). Summation on an orthonormal basis of \(c.W\) gives its dimension. Of the two powers of the compact dimension in the adjoint pairing, one is removed upon returning to the Hilbert inner product \(\mathrm{ipAt}\). Finally \(\nu(\mathcal F)=[K_V:J]\,\mathrm{mass}(V)\) and \([K_V:J]\,\|f\|^2=d_S^{-1}\). The positive scalar is therefore \(\mathrm{mass}(V)|\mathrm{eulerOff}|/(d^{S}d_S)\) times the product of (1) over the real places. In particular
\[
 \log l\ \ge\ \mathrm{mainTerm}
   +\log\!\left(\frac{\mathrm{mass}(V)|\mathrm{eulerOff}|}{d^{S}d_S}\right)
   -\#\mathrm{Tau}\binom r2\log n .
\]
Positivity of the finite Euler factor in absolute value is required when taking this logarithm. The estimate introduces no weight-width loss. Discharging the simultaneous finite convolution identity on the entire prescribed eigenspace, the other terms of the finite error budget and the transition from the scalar to (M) are distinct mathematical obligations.
