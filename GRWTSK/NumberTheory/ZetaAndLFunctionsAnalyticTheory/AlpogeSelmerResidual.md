## Finite-level description in Case I

Let \(p>2\), let \(\mathcal O\) be the source coefficient DVR finite over \(\mathbb Z_p\), with uniformizer \(\pi\), residue field \(k\), fraction field \(K\), and let \(\psi\) be a finite character of conductor dividing \(N\), of order prime to \(p\). Write \(\rho=\psi^{-1}\chi_{\mathrm{cyc}}^{-e}\) for \(e\ge1\), \(A=(K/\mathcal O)(\rho)\), and \(M_a=A[\pi^a]\). In a host, \(\psi(-1)=(-1)^e\), so \(\rho(c)=1\) for complex conjugation. The strict group \(S'(A)\) consists of continuous cohomology classes unramified on inertia everywhere and zero on decomposition groups above primes dividing \(pN\). At all places over infinity, cohomology in positive degree of groups of order at most two vanishes on \(p\)-primary coefficients. Denote the finite abelian field cut out by the action on \(M_a\) by \(F_a\), with group \(\Delta_a\). It is totally real for the indicated parity, including when \(a>1\). [Twisted coefficient, strict length and restriction of levels](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/CharacterLevelBounds/ShaFamMono.lean#L14-L52) · [Local subgroups used by the source](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/AutomorphicVarieties/GaloisLibrary/SelmerTransport/ConjStableShaFam.lean#L92-L99)

Suppose the reduction \(\bar\rho\) is nontrivial on \(G_{\mathbb Q_p}\). For every integer \(a\ge1\) there is an isomorphism of \(\mathcal O\)-modules
\[
 S'(A)[\pi^a]\ \simeq\
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\Delta_a}\bigl(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_{F_a}[1/p]),M_a\bigr).       \tag{1}
\]
The domain of Hom has its ordinary Galois action. The target action is specifically \(\psi^{-1}\chi_{\rm cyc}^{-e}\) reduced modulo \(\pi^a\). Formula (1) uses the class group with the primes above \(p\) inverted.

Both global and \(p\)-local invariant groups of \(A\) vanish. Indeed, a local scalar with nonidentity reduction has \(\rho(g)-1\) a unit. The coefficient exact sequence identifies \(H^1(\mathbb Q,M_a)\) with \(H^1(\mathbb Q,A)[\pi^a]\), and does so locally at \(p\). For global restriction to \(F_a\), the kernel and transgression from inflation–restriction vanish. To see this, choose an element of \(\Delta_a\) with nontrivial reduction and take its prime-to-\(p\) component. It generates a central subgroup \(H\) of order prime to \(p\) with \(M_a^H=0\). Averaging kills its higher cohomology, so Hochschild–Serre gives \(H^i(\Delta_a,M_a)=0\) for every \(i\). The same argument holds for the image of the \(p\)-adic decomposition group. Therefore a finite-level cocycle class equals an equivariant character of \(G_{F_a}\), and the condition at \(p\) is precisely splitting above \(p\).

For \(r\ne p\) the action on inertia has finite order prime to \(p\). If the class over \(A\) is unramified, the restriction of its finite lift to \(I_r\cap G_{F_a}\) is an ordinary homomorphism into \(M_a\) whose image in \(A\) is zero: the whole inertia action there is trivial, since reduction is injective on prime-to-\(p\) roots of unity. Conversely, if the character over \(F_a\) is unramified, the restriction to \(I_r\) inflates from a finite prime-to-\(p\) group and is zero in cohomology. At primes \(r\mid N\), \(r\ne p\), its image in \(H^1(\mathbb Q_r,A)\) is already zero. If inertia acts nontrivially then \(A^{I_r}=0\). If it acts trivially, unramified \(H^1\) is \(A/(\rho(\mathrm{Frob}_r)-1)A=0\), since \(r^{-e}\) times a root of unity differs from 1 and any nonzero scalar acts surjectively on \(K/\mathcal O\). This verifies both directions of all local conditions. Global class field theory identifies the resulting abelian, everywhere unramified characters split above \(p\) with the right side of (1).

In the source's Case I, \(p\notin B_\chi\) and \(p-1\nmid 2e\). A host character's order divides a power of two times the order of the fixed initial character, so its order is prime to \(p\). Its inertia image at \(p\) has order at most two. Indeed \(p\) does not divide the fixed conductor. At the two moving primes the defining field includes only the respective quadratic character, and at any remaining odd prime it is unramified. The residual cyclotomic character on \(I_p\) surjects onto \(\mathbb F_p^\times\). If the two residual factors canceled on \(I_p\), then \(\omega^{2e}\) would be trivial, contradicting \(p-1\nmid2e\). Thus (1) applies to **every** Case I character, including when \(p=q\) or \(p=d\). It proves exact vanishing of the whole \(S'(A)\) whenever the indicated eigenspace at \(a=1\) is zero. Indeed every cohomology class is killed by a power of \(\pi\), as a continuous cocycle from a compact group into a discrete module has finite image. A nonzero class produces one killed by \(\pi\). ## An infinite family of vanishing columns

Suppose \(\psi\) factors through the tame cyclotomic character at \(p\), including \(\psi=1\). Then \(\rho\) factors through the \(p\)-adic cyclotomic quotient. Write \(\bar\rho=\omega^{-b}\), with \(b=e\) for \(\psi=1\); for a Teichmüller power \(\psi=\widetilde\omega^v\) take \(b=e+v\). Under host parity, \(b\) is even. The unique real subfield \(F_1\subseteq\mathbb Q(\mu_p)\) cut out by \(\bar\rho\) has degree \(m=(p-1)/\gcd(p-1,b)\). For \(m>1\), the hypothesis of (1) holds by total ramification. Its unique prime over \(p\) is principal: the norm of \(1-\zeta_p\) down to \(F_1\) generates that prime, since its residue degree in the upper extension is one. Thus the localization in (1) at \(a=1\) leaves the ordinary class group.

If \(m=1\), then the residual action is trivial. The global and local cyclotomic images on \(A\) are identical by total ramification of the cyclotomic tower. A class of \(S'(A)[\pi]\) lifts to \(H^1(\mathbb Q,M_1)\). Its local value at \(p\) lies in the image of \(A^{G_{\mathbb Q_p}}/\pi\) by the coefficient sequence. This group is exactly \(A^{G_\mathbb Q}/\pi\), so subtracting the corresponding global connecting class makes the lift zero at \(p\). At each other prime the action on inertia of the full module is trivial, so the residual lift is unramified there. Since \(\mathbb Q\) has class number one, this corrected character is zero. This correction step is essential when the residual action is trivial.

In every one of the degrees \(m=1,2,3,4,6\), one therefore has \(S'(A)=0\) for all \(e\) of the prescribed parity. For \(m=2\), \(F_1=\mathbb Q(\sqrt p)\) and \(p\equiv1\bmod4\). Minkowski gives an integral ideal of norm \(\le\sqrt p/2\) in every class. There are at most \(\sum_{n\le T}\tau(n)\le T^2\) such ideals for \(T\ge1\), since the number of ordered pairs of positive integers with product at most \(T\) is at most \(\lfloor T\rfloor^2\). Thus \(h(F_1)<p\). The real cubic conductor-\(p\) field has class number \(<p\) by Moser–Payan. In degree four, Moser's Theorem B bounds the integral quotient \(h(F_1)/h(\mathbb Q(\sqrt p))\) by \(p/4\). Both factors are prime to \(p\). For degree six we use Moser's prime-divisor bound for real cyclic quartic and sextic fields of prime conductor, also stated in Jakubec's introduction. One needs \(p\nmid h(F_1)\); a claim that the entire class number is \(<p\) in degree six is unnecessary. [Moser–Payan, 1981, cubic conductor-\(p\) class number](https://doi.org/10.2969/jmsj/03340701) · [Moser, Grenoble 1979–80, Theorems A and B](https://www.numdam.org/article/STNG_1979-1980__8__A3_0.pdf) · [Moser, Math. Nachr. 102 (1981), 45–52](https://doi.org/10.1002/mana.19811020105) · [Jakubec, 1994, introduction p. 263](https://www.dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/107512/ArchMathRetro_030-1994-4_3.pdf#page=2)

For \(\psi=1\), positive even \(e\) and \(p-1\mid 2e\), only \(m=1,2\) occur. This proves the source's PropZ for \(V\) itself, and CountZ follows by inclusion for \(V'\). For \(m=3,4,6\) it also removes summands inside Case I. There are concrete octic examples: Jakubec's theorem shows \(p\nmid h(F_1)\) for prime \(p=u^4+16\) when none of \(B_{v(p-1)/8}\), \(v=1,3,5,7\), has numerator divisible by \(p\). For \(p=17,97,641\) the four residues are respectively \((3,15,6,4)\), \((13,87,51,26)\), and \((508,319,323,194)\). They follow by \(B_0=1\) and the exact recurrence \(B_n=-(n+1)^{-1}\sum_{k<n}\binom{n+1}{k}B_k\) modulo \(p\), used only for \(n<p-1\). For each of these primes every positive twist with residual order eight and the stated parity gives a vanishing column. This use of the nondivisibility test does not require its converse. ## A growing range at all odd primes of prime-to-character order

For every abelian \(L\subseteq\mathbb Q(\mu_N)\), \(N\ge2\), \(D=[L:\mathbb Q]\), and \(e\ge1\), there is an absolute \(C_*\ge1\) such that
\[
 \sum_{p>2}\log|K_{2e}(\mathcal O_L)[p^\infty]|
 \ \le\ \exp\!\big((2eD)^{C_*}\log(2N)\big).                         \tag{2}
\]
Set \(k=2e\), \(P=\max(D+1,2k+2)\). For \(L\ne\mathbb Q\), Bayer-Fluckiger–Emery–Houriet's Theorem 1.1 bounds the logarithm of the part prime to all primes \(\le P\) by products of powers of \(2k+1,D,|\Delta_L|\) with fixed polynomial exponents in \(k,D\). The conductor–discriminant relation gives \(|\Delta_L|\le N^D\), as each character conductor divides \(N\). For \(L=\mathbb Q\) one can use \(\mathbb Q(i)\) and \(4N\) throughout: restriction in \(K\)-theory injects on all odd-primary parts since extension and restriction of scalars compose to multiplication by 2 for the free \(\mathbb Z\)-module \(\mathbb Z[i]\). For every odd \(p\le P\), pass to \(L'=L(\mu_p)\), totally imaginary, of degree at most \(Dp\) and conductor dividing \(Np\). Its degree over \(L\) is prime to \(p\). The extension is unramified outside \(p\) and infinity, and these archimedean places may be included throughout at odd \(p\). Restriction followed by corestriction in degree-two Galois cohomology is multiplication by \([L':L]\). Odd-primary Quillen–Lichtenbaum and the uniform LemmaK estimate thus bound each small-prime logarithm by \(\exp((2eDp)^{C_K} Dp\log(2Np))\) after increasing constants. Since \(p\le P\) is polynomial in \(eD\), increasing the absolute exponent \(C_*\) absorbs both this cost summed over small primes and the high-prime contribution. [Bayer-Fluckiger, Emery and Houriet, v3, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5953v3#page=1) · [Meng Fai Lim, v2, Corollary 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00168v2#page=6) · [Small-prime LemmaK argument](AlpogeSelmerTail.md)

For a character of order \(d\) prime to \(p\), pass to the degree-\(d\) abelian field \(L_\psi\) cut out by \(\psi\) in characteristic zero, distinct in general from the finite reduction fields in (1). Restriction in \(H^1\) is injective by transfer, since \(d\) acts invertibly on \(A\). The source's local conditions give a strict class at each place of \(L_\psi\) over \(p\) and an unramified class elsewhere. On \(L_\psi\) the finite character is trivial. Since \(\mathcal O\) is finite free over \(\mathbb Z_p\), scalar extension commutes with continuous cochains and strict kernels here (choose a finite integral basis). Poitou–Tate then bounds the normalized length \(V_n\) by the \(\mathbb Z_p\)-length of \(K_{2e}(\mathcal O_{L_\psi})[p^\infty]\). Indeed \(M\otimes_{\mathbb Z_p}\mathcal O\) for a finite \(\mathbb Z_p\)-module \(M\) has \(\mathcal O\)-length equal to its \(\mathbb Z_p\)-length times the ramification index, exactly the normalization in \(V_n\). This step bounds the full torsion groups, at every depth.

In particular, if both the largest weight and the largest character order are fixed, (2) is a polynomial in the conductor bound with exponent depending on those two fixed cutoffs. Summing over at most the host degree of characters and the fixed weights gives the polynomial term required by the counting estimates on all rows in this range, even before window selection.

On the source's selected square window, set \(\delta=\epsilon/(8C_*)\). Sum the opposite-sign character budget over any finite set of odd \(p\notin B_\chi\), all \(1\le e<t(x)\) satisfying \(e\le x^\delta\), and all relevant characters with order \(d\le x^\delta\). Then for sufficiently large \(X\) the total weighted sum is at most
\[
                              \exp(x^{3\epsilon/5}).                    \tag{3}
\]
To interchange the order of summation when the coefficient rings vary with \(p\), embed their finite groups of character values into the complex roots of unity of the same orders. At each \(p\) this embeds its characters into the complex character list of the single finite abelian host group, preserving kernels and degrees. The latter list has size at most \(4\,\mathrm{ord}(\chi)\,2^{s(x)}\), from the fixed character field, the real dyadic cyclotomic factor and the two moving quadratic fields. The list may therefore be enlarged uniformly before summing over \(p\), and (2) applies to each retained character field. The host modulus \(N=\mathrm{lcm}(2^{s(x)+2},\mathrm{cond}(\chi))qd\) satisfies \(\log(2N)\ll x^{\epsilon/4}\) on the window by the defining bound on \(\log(qd)\) and admissibility \(2^{s(x)}\le x^{A_1}\). Thus the logarithm of the right side of (2) is \(O(x^{2\delta C_*+\epsilon/4})=O(x^{\epsilon/2})\). The polynomial number of indices and characters is absorbed by (3). The threshold is independent of the admissible functions, the moving primes and the tested finite prime set. The estimate is valid throughout each qualifying window and uses no bound on the rational prime's size. ## A finite certificate for a specified Case I row

For fixed \(p,e,\psi\) in Case I, put \(b_a=|\operatorname{Hom}_{\Delta_a}(\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal O_{F_a}[1/p]),M_a)|\), \(b_0=1\). The first equality \(b_{a+1}=b_a\) certifies the full answer \( |S'(A)|=b_a\). Indeed, the identified groups on the left of (1) are nested. If every class killed by \(\pi^{a+1}\) is killed by \(\pi^a\), applying the same implication to successive multiples \(\pi z\) gives the result for every higher layer. All classes are torsion, and the full group is finite by restriction to \(L_\psi\) and Quillen–Lichtenbaum as above. Hence this stopping condition occurs for each such triple. In the case \(b_1=1\), the test begins at \(a=0\).

There is also a finite prime cutoff for a specified characteristic-zero host character and weight. With \(k=2e\), use the field \(L_\psi\), replaced by \(\mathbb Q(i)\) for \(\psi=1\); denote its degree by \(D\) and its absolute discriminant by \(\Delta\). Put \(P=\max(D+1,2k+2)\) and \(B=(2k+1)^{71k^4D^3}D^{293k^5D^5}\Delta^{528k^5D^4}\). If \(p>P\) contributes to \(S'(A)\) in Case I, it divides the even \(K\)-group of that field, so \(\log p\le B\) by Bayer-Fluckiger–Emery–Houriet, Theorem 1.1. Thus integers up to \(\max(P,3^B)\) suffice as candidate primes. Together with the increasing-layer test this describes a terminating exact calculation for individual rows by class groups, using unconditional certification of any class-group data. The cutoff grows too quickly to yield the required uniform estimate or a practical computation on the full rows. [Explicit exponents and cutoff, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5953v3#page=1)

## Checks on the critical sign and outstanding sum

The preceding manuscript's ThmBc estimate uses \((-1)^{e+1}\) as eigenvalue of **twisted** complex conjugation in \(H^1\); the present host character condition instead gives the action of \(\rho(c)\) above. Poitou–Tate pairs the degree-one coefficient \(A_e=\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p(-e)\) with \(\mathbb Z_p(e+1)\); the invariant map makes the resulting scalar pairing Galois invariant, and the dual of a sign is the same sign. All real-place Frobenii in \(E/E^+\) equal \(c\). Consequently the relative critical projector at \(j=e+1\) is exactly the ThmBc projector. For example \(e=1\) takes the plus projector on \(K_2\) while \(e=2\) takes the minus projector on \(K_4\). The scalar product of imprimitive matching-parity \(L\)-values is nonzero, with all omitted Euler factors nonzero at \(1-j\). The integer obtained after clearing denominators bounds the sum of valuations over **any** subset of odd primes: \(v_p(Z_j)\le v_p(U_j)\) separately and all \(v_p(U_j)\ge0\). Neither a bound on a real absolute value before denominator clearing nor the complementary idempotent would give this conclusion.
