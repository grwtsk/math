# Descent and integral traces in a marked theta sum

grwtsk contributors

Fix a stage of Levent Alpöge's construction, the coefficient field of its marked row, a unimodular class lattice \(\Lambda\subset E^n\) which is standard at every prime dividing the marking level \(M\), and an allowed odd \(N\) divisible by \(M\). Write \(A\) for the PEL abelian variety, \(L\) for its rigidified symmetric theta bundle, \(r=p_0+q_0\) for the \(E\)-rank of its homology, and \(P\in\mathrm{harmL}(\Lambda,w)\) for the prescribed harmonic polynomial. Its homogeneous degree is \(d=\mathrm{DF}(w)\) for nonzero \(P\), by summing the specified row degrees. The zero polynomial gives a zero reading. Let \(Z_\Lambda\) be the subgroup of \(A[N^2]^n\) used for the short sum, \(B_M=(\mathrm{LevQ})^n\) the finite marking module, and \(s\) the original theta section. For \(q\in B_M\) choose the marked division tuple \(t_q\) satisfying \([N]t_q=\mathrm{lev}(q)\) and supported on the prescribed half of the marking ideal. Up to the outer cover correction the analytic or geometric reading in a frame is
\[
 \frac{1}{\beta_H\,\mathrm{mIdx}\,|B_M|\,N^d}
 \sum_{q\in B_M}w(q)\sum_{z\in Z_\Lambda}
      \nabla_P\big(U_{t_q}U_z[N]^*(s^{\boxtimes n})\big).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]
Each \(U\) acts factor by factor by the normalized odd symmetric theta lift. The tensor-product notation includes the prescribed linear pairing against the multicolumn jet. In the class-sum version with \(\beta=1\), omit \(\beta_H\) and retain the coefficient \(\mathrm{ep}\) from the [class calculation](AlpogeThetaPeriodDescent.md). ## Descending the choices of geometric torsion points

In characteristic zero full \(N^2\)-torsion is finite étale. At every finite prime \(v\) of \(E\) use bases in which \(\Lambda_v\) has elementary divisors \(\pi_v^{a_j}\). Put \(h_v=v(N)\). The containment \(N\mathcal O_E^n\subset\Lambda\cap\mathcal O_E^n\) gives \(a_j\le h_v\). Self-duality gives \(a_j\ge-h_v\) since at the conjugate prime the elementary divisors are the negatives in dual bases. The defining test for \(Z_\Lambda\) kills each tuple by every \(N\mu\) with \(\mu\Lambda\subset\mathcal O_E^n\). Such matrices are integral after multiplication by \(N\); locally their rows span \(N\Lambda_v^\vee\). Localization is valid because the module of admissible rows is the dual of the finite projective lattice. In these bases the \(j\)-th torsion coordinate therefore ranges over \(A[\pi_v^{h_v-a_j}]_v\), with \(r\) copies in the \(v\)-adic homology lattice. The latter description follows from the rational complex lattice uniformization and tensoring its projective integral \(\mathcal O_E\)-homology with \(\mathcal O_{E,v}\). It extends to every geometric characteristic-zero fibre by descent to a finitely generated field and complex embedding. This proves the source cardinality \([\Lambda:N\mathcal O_E^n]^r=\mathrm{zCard}\). In opposite dual bases the two exponents paired by the Weil pairing sum to \(2h_v\), which is the exponent of the ambient \(N^2\)-torsion. The pairing on \(Z_\Lambda\) is consequently trivial. At self-conjugate primes the same argument uses a basis of the lattice and its Hermitian dual, so does not require diagonalizing a Hermitian form over a dyadic DVR. [Annihilator and lattice indices](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L43-L64) · [Definition of the enumerated subgroup](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L87-L94)

On an odd-order isotropic subgroup the symmetric lifts multiply. Indeed, commuting lifts multiply to a lift of the sum, the product has order dividing the odd exponent, and symmetry sends it to its inverse. Two lifts with both properties have scalar ratio of order dividing two and that odd exponent. The splitting on \(A[N]\) associated to the pullback \([N]^*L\) is symmetric for the rigidified identification with \(L^{N^2}\), and fixes \([N]^*s\). For a second admissible root \(t'_q\), the difference \(u=t'_q-t_q\) belongs to \(A[N]^n\) and is supported on the chosen marking half. The two halves of the marking ideal are coprime, and Rosati conjugation interchanges them. Thus the Weil pairing of two tuples supported on that same half is trivial: a Chinese remainder scalar acting as one on the first support has adjoint zero on the second. Moreover \(u\in Z_\Lambda\), since \(\Lambda\) is standard at the marking primes, where \(Z_\Lambda\) has exactly the above \(N\)-torsion components. It follows that \(U_u\) commutes with \(U_z\) and \(U_{t_q}\), and fixes the starting section. Hence every term of (1) is independent of the choice of marked root. The sum is invariant under permutation of the enumerated \(Z_\Lambda\). These are identities of sections. [Mumford, *On the equations defining abelian varieties I*, §2](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-82a4-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b) · [Source root and complete-group quantifiers](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Formalism/LocalToGlobal/ProjectiveLattices/ThShort.lean#L239-L263)

After pulling back the marked characteristic-zero family to a finite étale cover with these roots and labels, translations, rigidified multiplication pullbacks, and the finite sums are algebraic. The symmetry and odd-exponent equations define the normalized lifts uniquely after étale localization, so translations respect descent. On the double overlap the formulas just proved identify the two pullbacks. Faithfully flat descent for sections gives the tensor-section sum over the original **marked** coefficient field. With the marked harmonic jet operator defined over that field, the same holds for its reading. This argument over the root cover works over nonreduced characteristic-zero bases as well: finite étale labels split locally and the theta-group identities are equalities of morphisms. On a finite residue field at a prime of good reduction coprime to \(N\), geometric torsion labels group into Frobenius cycles and the descent uses precisely their conjugate terms. The trace of a residue-field orbit need not be divisible by its length. For example the trace of a root of \(X^2+X+1\) over \(\mathbf F_2\) is one. This explains why counting a Frobenius orbit cannot replace local divisibility of the summands. The further descent from the marked level using its deck character, the componentwise archimedean comparison and the rational cusp projection are distinct requirements.

## The two local factors in the primary torsion sum

Let \(V\) be a complete DVR of mixed characteristic \((0,\ell)\), and let \(G\) be the formal completion of the integral group at its identity with finite-height multiplication by \(\ell\). An \(\mathcal O_E\)-matrix \(D\) gives a homomorphism on \(G^n\). For the source's adapted factorization use letters \(B,C\) for its matrices named \(p,q\), set \(\mu=BC\) and suppose \(\mu^\dagger\mu=(kN)^2 I\) with \(k\) odd and prime to \(\ell\). The matrices \(B,C\) have isogenies on the formal groups. Indeed \(\mu^\dagger\mu=\mu\mu^\dagger=(kN)^2 I\) gives finite closed fibre for \(\mu\). Each factor has a two-sided quasi-inverse up to the same nonzero integer: for \(C\) take \(\mu^\dagger B\), for \(B\) take \(C\mu^\dagger\), and use cancellation over \(E\) for the other order. The complete local power series ring on source coordinates is finite over that on the target by completeness and the finite closed fibre. The target uniformizer and parameter sequence form a system of parameters in the regular source ring, hence a regular sequence. The resulting finite maps are flat. [Actual adaptation conditions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/PlaceAdaptation/AdaptStd.lean#L140-L196)

The local trace ideal on the coordinate ring for \(B\) is **exactly**
\[
 (J_B),\qquad J_B=\det(dB_0)=\prod_{\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}
                  \tau(\det B)^{\mathrm{mult}(\tau)}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Use integral invariant differentials on the formal groups. In those bases the matrix of the differential everywhere is its matrix at the identity. The ordinary Jacobian determinant in any formal coordinates is therefore \(J_B\) times a unit. The displayed product follows after scalar extension splitting the \(E\)-action on the tangent space, regardless of whether the eigenspace decomposition is integral. For the finite complete intersection its dualizing module is invertible and, under a local duality generator, the trace functional is the Jacobian times a unit. One can apply the finite syntomic different formula directly to a power series map: it is finite flat, its fibres are complete intersections, and its module of relative differentials is computed by the continuous Jacobian. For the last assertion, finite derivations of the finite algebra into a finite module over a complete Noetherian local ring are continuous by the Leibniz rule; the finite relative differential module thus has the presentation by the coordinate differentials. Local complete intersection of fibres also follows from the presentation by setting the target parameters in a regular power series ring, or by completing a smooth isogeny chart. Finally a duality generator \(t\) is onto: the source is finite free over the local target, its element one is primitive, and a linear functional taking one to one is a source multiple of \(t\). This proves the exact ideal, including attainment among all integral functions. It asserts divisibility for the particular theta function, whose individual trace can have larger valuation. On integral functions invariant under \(\ker C\), the trace for \(BC\) has image ideal \((m_C J_B)\), where \(m_C\) is the rank of the formal kernel of \(C\). To see this, pass to a finite extension splitting the generic kernel. Invariance under the split generic points implies scheme-theoretic invariance since the finite flat coordinate modules are torsion-free. The finite flat isogeny is a torsor under its kernel, so the equalizer criterion for faithfully flat descent identifies the invariant section with a pullback through \(C\). Transitivity of trace now gives the whole degree \(m_C\) before applying (2). No averaging by \(m_C^{-1}\) occurs. In the generic split fibre the trace is exactly the sum of translations appearing in (1). An integral rigidified change of line-bundle trivialization replaces each translate by a unit ratio and leaves this conclusion valid for the linearized sections.

For a chart coming from an abelian scheme with good reduction, this applies to the geometric tensor section. Let \(\mathcal L=L^{\boxtimes n}\). The rigidified symmetric difference \(D=\mathcal L^{(kN)^2}\otimes(\mu^*\mathcal L)^{-1}\) is in the dual abelian scheme: its polarization homomorphism vanishes by the Rosati identity \(\mu^\dagger\mu=(kN)^2\). Symmetry gives \(D^2=1\) in that dual. Since \(kN\) is odd, \(\mu^*\) is an automorphism on dual two-torsion, with inverse induced by its adjoint there. Thus \(D=\mu^*D'\) for a rigidified symmetric two-torsion line, and the bundle used on the source is genuinely \(\mu^*(\mathcal L\otimes D')\). Its kernel linearization is symmetric and agrees with the odd symmetric lifts by uniqueness on the generic fibre. On an odd-residue-characteristic formal chart the rigidified \(D'\) is multiplicatively trivial: its pullback under \([2]\) has the rigidified trivialization of \((D')^2\), and \([2]\) is an automorphism of the formal group. At residue characteristic two the formal kernel of every such odd isogeny is trivial. This deals with a possible two-torsion characteristic even for the scalar-free identity (2).

For each coset of the formal subgroup of the primary kernel the remaining sum is handled without an extra index loss. In the full \(\ell\)-primary group \(W\subset\ker(BC)\), write \(W_f\) for its formal elements and \(W_0=W\cap\ker C\). When the initial tensor is fixed by \(W_0\), it is constant under \(W_0\) in each translated orbit, by the isotropic splitting. The formal trace in a coset of \(W_f\) contributes \(|W_f\cap W_0|J_B\) times a descended integral germ. Decomposing \(W\) into cosets of \(W_0 W_f\) multiplies this by \(|W_0|/|W_0\cap W_f|\). The surviving factor is \(|W_0|J_B\). This is an equality of germs after the indicated pulled-back frames. In a general integral chart from a semiabelian model the trace proof applies to an integral linearizing gauge and regular translates wherever supplied. Establishing that gauge and regularity for every uniformization is a further geometric comparison. For \(\ell\nmid M\), the marking roots have prime-to-\(\ell\) order. After separating the prime-to-\(\ell\) part of \(z\), the unshifted \([kN]^*s\) is fixed by the requisite \(W_0\): the adapted equation \(g C=k_1 N I\), with \(k_1\) a unit at \(\ell\), puts \(W_0\) in \(A[N]^n\). Its action commutes with all the prime-to-\(\ell\) translations. To compare this lifted calculation to (1), pull the whole orbit of sections back under ([k]). Division by (k) permutes the primary subgroup (W). Pullback intertwines its normalized linearizations because it respects inversion, the odd-order condition and rigidifications, which uniquely specify these lifts. For the prime-to-(ell) translate one can choose a prime-to-(ell) division point by (k) on the generic fibre. Any scalar from its fixed bundle identification is common to the whole primary orbit and is a unit by integral extension of normalized lifts on an abelian model. The harmonic reading acquires (k^d), a unit. Thus this change of level preserves the valuation of the sum, including possible cancellation between terms. On the Tate module, \(|W_0|\) is the \(\ell\)-part of \(|N_{E/\mathbb Q}(\det C)|^r\), by Smith reduction. The columns of \(g\) span \(\Lambda\cap\mathcal O_E^n\) locally and \(gC=k_1 N I\), so that this integer is exactly the \(\ell\)-part of \(\mathrm{mIdx}\). Moreover \(BC=kN\gamma\), so locally \(B=(k/k_1)\gamma g\); \(\gamma\) takes \(\Lambda\) onto the standard lattice at \(\ell\). Formula (2) therefore has the valuation of \(\beta_H\) when \(\beta_H\) satisfies its determinant-ideal equation. If \(\beta_H=1\) by the archive's alternative definition, \(J_B\) is still integral. Applying the homogeneous harmonic differential to a \(BC\)-pullback multiplies by \((kN)^d\) and replaces \(P\) by its transform under \(\gamma\), which is integral in these coordinates. Indeed every generating linear form of \(\mathrm{symO}(\Lambda)\) transforms by the vector \(\gamma b\), integral locally for \(b\in\Lambda\); unitarity preserves the harmonic contractions. Canonical logarithmic gauges differ in exponential coordinates by conjugate-type quadratics killed by those contractions. For an isogeny pullback, the transformed gauge obeys the same polarization identities by the similitude law; equivalently the canonical real-analytic metric pulls back with curvature multiplied by \((kN)^2\), its rigidified normalized potential being determined up to the harmonic-null quadratic. Comparing the marked jets on the two sides thus uses the same normalization as in (1). The \(N^d\) and \(\ell\)-part of \(\mathrm{mIdx}\) cancel, even when \(\ell\mid N\). ## At a marking prime with good reduction

There is a direct argument at **every** good-reduction place where \(\Lambda\) is standard locally. This includes all marking primes. Let \(v(\ell)=1\) and write \(N=\ell^h N'\) with \((\ell,N')=1\). The primary part of \(Z_\Lambda\) is exactly \(A[\ell^h]^n\) by the local annihilator calculation. Write \(z=z_\ell+z'\), with \(z'\) prime to \(\ell\). Since \(z_\ell\in A[N]^n\), the first lift in (1) gives the same section for all \(z_\ell\). This uses \(U_z=U_{z'}U_{z_\ell}\), and applies even when \(t_q\) has \(\ell\)-power order, since \(U_{t_q}\) acts **after** \(U_z\). The inner sum supplies \(|A[\ell^h]^n|\); its valuation is that of \(\mathrm{mIdx}\). For globally standard \(\Lambda\) this first cancellation is exact at once over all primes.

The residual section for one column is a normalized translate of \([N]^*s\) by \(t_q+z'\). Its underlying section is the pullback of the translate of \(s\) by \(a=[N](t_q+z')\), up to the theta bundle identification. The order of the \(\ell\)-part of \(a\) divides \(M\), since \([N]t_q\) is a marking point and \(z'\) has order prime to \(\ell\). Choose a positive \(m\) killing all these \(a\)'s, with \(v(m)\le v(M)\). On an abelian model, after a finite splitting extension of the DVR, the torsion points extend by properness. The translated bundle differs from \(L\) by a line in \(\mathrm{Pic}^0[m]\), up to a base line. Its \([m]\)-pullback is trivial as a rigidified difference line because \([m]^*\) is multiplication by \(m\) on \(\mathrm{Pic}^0\). Choose generators of the base lines over the DVR. This makes \([m]^*t_a^*s\) an integral section \(H_m\) of \(L^{m^2}\). All normalized theta translations on the generic fibre of the original integral torsion sections extend with unit scalar: the bundle isomorphism exists integrally up to a base line; iterating an integral isomorphism around the torsion cycle multiplies by a global unit; a generic lift with finite order then differs from the integral isomorphism by a unit after splitting. The original theta generator stays nonzero on the special fibre, since for a principal polarization the degree-one global sections form a line and commute with base change. Pullbacks by finite flat isogenies and translations preserve this primitivity.

Now \([m]^*(U_{t_q}U_{z'}[N]^*s)\) and \([N]^* H_m\) correspond to the same translated section pulled back by \([mN]\). Their two bundle identifications differ by a scalar, since the generic abelian variety is proper and geometrically connected. The scalar is a unit by the preceding integral identifications, also seen from primitivity. The same argument takes the product over all columns before applying \(P\). The derivative chain rule for the scalar homotheties gives
\[
 m^d N^{-d}\nabla_P\big(U_{t_q}U_{z'}[N]^*s^{\boxtimes n}\big)
     =u\,\nabla_P(H_{m,1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes H_{m,n}),\quad v(u)=0.
                                                                  \tag{3}
\]
For scalar multiplication the chain rule follows directly in formal logarithmic coordinates: each derivative is multiplied by the integer, the pullback bundle power is multiplied by its square, and the rigidified symmetric power identification preserves the harmonic reading. This works for even \(m\) as a multiplication pullback. The lifts in the original sum still use its given odd \(N\). For the chosen integral chart let \(-\rho\) bound its harmonic evaluations on integral degree-\(d\) germs at all bundle powers used, in these valuation units. The archive takes the infimum of these valuations over integral harmonic polynomials and integral germs at each bundle power. The right side of (3) meets these tests because \(\Lambda\) is standard at \(\ell\). For the normalized short sum one obtains
\[
 v(\text{reading})\ \ge\ -\rho - d\,v(M)-v(|B_M|)
                 +\min_q v(w(q)).                                    \tag{4}
\]
The outer cover factor contributes its own valuation if present. Both \(\beta_H\) and the relevant class factor of the preceding paper are units at such a place. The finite Fourier weights are integral for an integral test: they are finite sums of integral coefficients times roots of unity. For a single class indicator the weight is a root of unity. The actual denominator \(|B_M|=M^{[E:\mathbb Q]rn/2}\) follows from the cardinality of the marking half on the rank-\(r\) projective homology and \(n\) copies. Thus \(|B_M|M^d\) clears the marking losses in (4), independent of the auxiliary \(N\), with the chart loss still visible. In degree zero \(\rho=0\) follows from evaluating an integral germ at the origin. The result in higher degrees preserves the actual chart valuation, so a proof of the requisite host bound for \(\rho\) can be inserted without changing any torsion count. The archive's sharper host chart estimate is \(\rho\le d\,v_\ell(|\operatorname{disc}E|)/(2[E:\mathbb Q])\) for the normalized valuation at conductor primes, with zero allowed away from them under its host hypotheses. Its proof requires integral eigenlifts for the canonical biextension gauge and a bound on paired contractions. Equation (4) by itself proves neither that estimate nor regularity of every translate on a semiabelian uniformization. A construction using the identity component must account for its translate.
