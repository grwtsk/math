## An integral self-dual trace basis fixed by the half-period

Here \(p\) is the residue characteristic, inert in the quadratic host, and \(n=\mathrm{addOrderOf}(h_P)>1\) is odd. The split bound implies \(p\ge5\). Each orbit of the site permutation has length \(n\), since it translates the finite abelian-group coordinate by \(h_P\) and preserves the sheet in this class. Write \(O_a=W(\mathbb F_{p^a})\) in the completed maximal unramified ring and let \(\sigma\) denote arithmetic Frobenius. There is a basis \(\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_n\in O_n\) of \(O_{2n}/O_2\) satisfying
\[
 \sigma^n\omega_j=\omega_j,\qquad
 \sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{2t}(\omega_j\omega_k)=\delta_{jk}.                 \tag{1}
\]
In particular the source's second basis \(\sigma^n\omega_j\) really is trace-dual and integral. No denominator \(n\) occurs. [Source basis and trace constraints](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/40e56bab026552e089d7afcfeff08d2defb9c005/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/OrbitLabellings/StdSeriesOrbitCoordinateChangeOfOddInert.lean#L217-L252)

To prove (1), take a power basis \(a_j\) of the residue field of \(O_n\) over \(\mathbb F_p\). The evaluation matrix \(V_{tj}=a_j^{p^t}\) is invertible by the Vandermonde determinant and separability. Frobenius cycles its rows, so \(\det V\) is fixed because the cycle length is odd. The residue trace Gram \(V^tV\) therefore has nonzero square determinant in \(\mathbb F_p\). A nondegenerate symmetric form of square determinant over this field admits an orthonormal basis. Indeed ordinary diagonalization works since 2 is invertible. For nonzero diagonal entries \(a,b\), the subsets \(a\{x^2\}\) and \(1-b\{y^2\}\) each have cardinality \((p+1)/2\), hence intersect. Thus each remaining subspace of dimension at least two represents 1 and splits off a norm-one line. The last coefficient is a square by the determinant criterion. Choose lifts to \(O_n\). Their Gram matrix \(G\) for the actual unramified trace is the identity modulo \(p\). If \(G=I+2E\) with \(E\in p^k M_n(\mathbb Z_p)\), replace the basis by the row transformation \(I-E\). Since \(E\) is symmetric, the new Gram is \(I-3E^2+2E^3\equiv I\pmod{p^{2k}}\). These changes converge to an invertible integral transformation. This gives a trace-orthonormal \(\mathbb Z_p\)-basis of \(O_n\).

Finite unramified base change from degree 1 to degree 2 preserves this basis, since \(\gcd(2,n)=1\): the residue degree of \(\mathbb F_{p^n}\mathbb F_{p^2}\) over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) is \(n\), the basis remains independent, and lifting modulo \(p\) gives an integral basis. On \(O_n\) the powers \(\sigma^{2t}\) run through all \(n\) embeddings. This proves (1) even if \(p\mid n\). In contrast, the residue Gram of the *single* vector 1 then vanishes, so a construction that starts by normalizing that vector need not work. For even \(n\) the residue embedding determinant changes sign under Frobenius and this square-discriminant argument fails.
