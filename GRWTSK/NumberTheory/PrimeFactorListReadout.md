# Complete output from a prime-list supplier

Vasily Ilin and contributors publish PrimeFactorLean under the MIT license. Commit `d26f6a704ba83c311601b47ec183f465a309c6f7` uses Lean 4.24.0 and Mathlib `f897ebcf72cd16f89ab4577d0c826cd14afaafc7`. Its `PrimeFactorLean.factor_total_correct` quantifies over every algorithm, configuration and nonzero natural number. It proves the executable returns `some ps`, every member of `ps` is prime and the product of `ps` is the original integer. This includes the empty list on 1. An auxiliary theorem proves partial correctness for the actual list returned by a specific call. [Provider theorem](https://github.com/Vilin97/PrimeFactorLean/blob/d26f6a704ba83c311601b47ec183f465a309c6f7/PrimeFactorLean/Proofs/Algorithms.lean) · [Exact output predicate](https://github.com/Vilin97/PrimeFactorLean/blob/d26f6a704ba83c311601b47ec183f465a309c6f7/PrimeFactorLean/Proofs/Core.lean) · [License](https://github.com/Vilin97/PrimeFactorLean/blob/d26f6a704ba83c311601b47ec183f465a309c6f7/LICENSE)

The common implementation uses a sound primality checker and a splitter that returns checked proper factors. Well-founded recursion follows both factors. If a splitter gives up, an exact trial-division fallback finishes the decomposition. This supports correctness of outputs even for configurations selecting SIQS or GNFS. Their running times, success probabilities, and the cost of carrying out the computation on a fixed multitape machine are outside the theorem.
