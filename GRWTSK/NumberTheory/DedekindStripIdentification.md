The root Mathlib class-number formula gives the positive limit of $(x-1)\zeta_F(x)$ as **real** $x\downarrow1$. In `right_residue_of_agreement` the equality on the convergent half-plane transfers that limit to the selected continuation. The value at the omitted point does not enter. A nonzero one-sided limit alone cannot classify a complex singularity. For example $g(s)=(1+\exp(-1/(s-1)))/(s-1)$ has unit real right-limit after multiplication by $s-1$, and an essential singularity. This example tests the need for a meromorphic or holomorphic regularization premise in a pole-order argument. [Mathlib's residue and positivity at the exact root pin](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d13f23b723b8a846827a245b89c10fc7d3f11612/Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/DedekindZeta.lean)

In `simple_pole_of_meromorphic_agreement`, add meromorphicity of the chosen continuation at $1$. A meromorphic function there either converges to a finite limit or has a pole. Apply this dichotomy to $(s-1)f(s)$. Its real right-limit is finite and nonzero, excluding a pole and identifying the full complex limit by uniqueness. Thus $(s-1)f(s)$ has order zero and $f$ has order $-1$. The theorem `meromorphic_of_principal_regularization` discharges the meromorphicity premise from an analytic regularization $R$ and $R(s)=(s-1)f(s)$ near $1$ inside the positive half-plane. Values assigned at the pole have no effect on the punctured germ.
