# Deterministic factoring via a bounded subgroup index

Authors: grwtsk contributors

## Source and mathematical return

Cogentic's deterministic factoring manuscript, §11.1, constructs a common exponent from a prime set, a cap and congruences. In [FactoringSubgroupSaturation.lean](FactoringSubgroupSaturation.lean), the namespace is `GRWTSK.IntegerFactorization.SubgroupSaturation`. The local argument specializes the prime residues to actual units in a Mathlib field. The inputs are the original composite $N$, a prime divisor $p$, a finite set $P$ of primes at most $B$ and an exponent $M$ with $a^M\equiv1\pmod N$ on $P$. The theorem consumes these mathematical objects without asking an algorithm to factor $N$ to find $p$. The conclusion is quantified separately for every such $p$. [Cogentic, November 25, 2026, Lemma 11.1 and proof of Theorem 1.3](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf)

| Local declaration | Statement and supplier |
| --- | --- |
| `closure_pow_eq_one`, `card_dvd_saturated` | The generators lie in the kernel of the power map on a commutative group. The generated subgroup is cyclic inside a finite cyclic ambient group. Its exponent equals its order. Lagrange gives order times index equal to the ambient order. |
| `index_dvd_lcm`, `multiplier_eq_primePowers` | A positive index at most $K$ divides $L_K=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,K)$. The same integer is the product of the maximal powers of all primes up to $K$. |
| `log_multiplier_le`, `multiplier_bits_le` | Mathlib's Chebyshev function gives $\log L_K\le (\log4+4)K$, a linear bound for the number of bits. This constant suffices for the resource order and is larger than the manuscript's sharper $2\log4$. |
| `prime_sub_one_dvd`, `prime_sub_one_dvd_of_congruences` | Reducing the original congruences modulo $p$ annihilates the subgroup generated by the given units. A bounded index yields $(p-1)\mid ML_K$. |
| `multichoose_le_subgroup_card`, `prime_sub_one_dvd_of_packing` | Prime multisets of fixed length $t$ have distinct integer products by unique factorization. When $B^t<p$ they remain distinct modulo $p$ and are in the generated subgroup. Thus $\binom{\#P+t-1}{t}$ is a lower bound for its order when $\#P>0$, with Lean using `Nat.multichoose` at all inputs. The integer comparison $p-1<\operatorname{multichoose}(\#P,t)(K+1)$ suffices for the index bound. |

Two failure cases identify the necessary hypotheses. When $p=5$, the one-term prime products $2$ and $7$ coincide modulo $5$ after the no-wrap inequality is removed. For $p=7$, generators $\{2\}$ and $M=3$ give a subgroup of index two; choosing $K=1$ leaves $6\nmid3L_1$. These examples also distinguish congruence annihilation from a common multiple of every $p-1$. An independent exact-integer check compared the conclusion to all 23,644 premise-satisfying tuples in a finite sample: odd primes $p$ through 43, $N=pq$ with $q\in\{3,5,7\}$ distinct, nonempty subsets of primes coprime to $N$ through a prime cap $B<\min(p,15)$, even $2\le M\le48$, $1\le t\le4$, and $1\le K<p$. All conclusions held.

To recover the manuscript's full deterministic bound one must supply, uniformly on its input domain, the admissible prime set, the annihilating exponent, its size, the quantitative comparison giving the cap, and a bit-costed implementation of all these operations. A proof that an integer has $O(K)$ bits is independent of the time to construct it. Lemmas 5.1 and 6.3, Propositions 9.3 and 10.1, and the final order-splitting algorithm retain their source-specific suppliers. The first admits an arbitrary dimensional algebraic and positivity problem; the second requires a uniform quadratic-field Burgess estimate. The probabilistic claims belong to the separate Las Vegas manuscript. [Source-local ledger](../ComputerScience/AlgorithmsInComputerScience/IntegerFactoringManuscripts.md)
