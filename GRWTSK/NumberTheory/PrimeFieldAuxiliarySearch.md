# Bounded discovery of an auxiliary prime

The mathematical input is the auxiliary-prime reduction in OpenAI's *Deterministic Polynomial Factorization over Prime Fields*, October 4, 2026, at commit `3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6`. The original problem factors a dense nonzero polynomial over the prime field of binary characteristic $p$. An integer factorization routine is never returned by this reduction. Let $L=\lceil\log_2 p\rceil$ and $B=20+(n+1)(L+1)$ for degree $n$. Only $n\ge2$ and $p>B^{200000}$ use the table, once for each prime $q\le n$. For such a $q$ the local predicate requires prime $\ell$, all four exclusions $\ell\notin\{2,3,p,q\}$, $\ell\equiv1\pmod {12q}$, and $p^{(\ell-1)/q}\ne1$ in $\mathbf F_\ell$. The congruence makes $(\ell-1)/q$ an integer. The small-characteristic, linear and constant-polynomial branches are outside this search. A constant polynomial has an empty irreducible factor list and retains its leading coefficient.

The scan tests candidates in increasing order, beginning at zero, and counts the successful test. Induction on the remaining budget proves that its returned value satisfies the predicate and every earlier candidate fails. Given existence up to $E$, the scan returns the least admissible prime after at most $E+1$ tests. Exhaustion excludes witnesses only in this interval. With $(p,q)=(5,2)$ the cap 72 exhausts 73 tests and the cap 73 finds 73 on test 74. The two finite examples check the predicate and boundary behavior even though the factoring algorithm takes the small-characteristic branch for $p=5$.

The scanner charges predicate calls. In a schoolbook bit implementation, trial division through $\ell$ candidates and repeated squaring modulo $\ell$ take polynomial time in $E$ and $\log p$. There are at most $n(E+1)$ candidate tests for the whole table. The parameters $c,d$ in the numerical bound must be fixed uniformly, with $c$ a multiplicative constant and $d$ an exponent. The published $d=20{,}000{,}000$ gives $400{,}000{,}100$ in the displayed substitution. The [new analytic existence proof](AuxiliaryPrimeAnalyticExistence.md) preserves the original strip hypothesis and strengthens the value bound to $d=1{,}000{,}000$, giving $20{,}000{,}100$ in this same algebraic substitution. A bound on the bit length of $E$ alone allows exponentially many candidates. Its final local discovery theorem proves the nuisance bound, cyclotomic congruence and uniform numerical closure, while retaining the explicit formula and specified Kummer field data as formal premises. A verified fixed-machine implementation of the predicate and its bit cost, full formal qualification of those premises and the original circuit/algebraic factoring stages remain separate. ### Quadratic output for an odd prime

The companion [quadratic search](PrimeFieldQuadraticSearch.lean) uses one executable Euler test. For a representative $0<z<p$, Euler's criterion equates rejection of $z^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}=1\bmod p$ with Legendre symbol $-1$. This excludes the zero class, which otherwise also fails the power equality. For $p\le T$, existence of a nonsquare in the finite field gives a successful representative among $0,\ldots,p-1$. For $p>T$, assume a source auxiliary prime $\ell\le E$ for $q=2$. The checked [reciprocity theorem](AuxiliaryPrimeNonresidue.lean) gives $z=\ell\bmod p$ with Legendre symbol $-1$, so $z<p$ and $z\le E$. A direct scan of ordinary representatives finds a nonresidue on both branches. Its budget is $p$ in the small branch and $\min(p,E+1)$ otherwise. Thus the expensive large-characteristic prime is never used as the numerical scan limit when the supplied cap is smaller. Specialize $T=B^{200000}$ and $E=c B^{20000000}$ with fixed $c$ for the paper's degree-two application; only the second branch consumes the analytic supplier.
