The strict threshold proof keeps the integer margin. Multiplying the multiplier's allowed error by $\sigma(x)$ costs at most $1/50$. Multiplying the half-unit threshold gap by $\tau>1$ yields a gap greater than $1/2$. The sign therefore survives perturbation, with multiplicativity transferring the decision to $mx$. This consumes an approximating coprime multiplier. Its uniform construction from the query input, including small-prime stripping, analytic supply, direct branches and bit cost, remains an independent obligation of Theorem 1.2.

For the unitary lower bound choose $p=\min\operatorname{Fac}(d)$. Coprimality excludes $p$ from the quotient, so $v_p(d)=v_p(w)$. Its entire power divides $d$. For the largest exponent, valuations give $a=k v_p(y)$. Once $k=a$, injectivity of a positive power on natural numbers gives $y=p$. A composite exponent such as $12$ causes several exact powers; choosing the first successful exponent can miss the prime base. The executable root search tests at most $\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor$ exponents. Its full implementation-level bit cost is open.

A finite hostile case is $w=12$ with candidates $0,1,2,3,4,6,12,13$. The unitary filter retains $3,4,12$, and chooses $3$. The least ordinary divisor $2$ is not a full component. When the answer set omits $3$, the proof supplies no coverage conclusion. Separately, at $x=5$, $g=6$, the coprime multiplier $8866$ has $\sigma(8866)=16128$ and yields a strict yes on the equality case. At $g=7$, $m=82$ has $\sigma(82)=126$ and yields a strict no. Both multiplier errors are below $1/500$. An independent finite check using exact rational arithmetic verified the two strict signs in all 148 cases satisfying the displayed approximation hypotheses for $1\le x<40$, $1\le g\le2x$, and $1\le m\le200$. Direct divisor enumeration checked 2,135 exact quotient updates for $2\le w\le150$, every nontrivial unitary divisor, and $0\le j\le4$.
