## Main statement and complete algebraic argument

Suppose Q(x,y)=S(x)+R(y+Y(x)), where Y is an actual linear map, R is
nonnegative, and S(0)=R(0)=0. Every finite negative dimension of Q equals
the corresponding finite negative dimension of S. Hence their indices in
ENat agree, even if the index is unbounded.

One direction uses the explicit injective lift x -> (x,-Y(x)); the
complementary energy is exactly zero. Conversely, project a negative frame
of Q to the first coordinate. Since R>=0, the projected S values remain
negative. The projection is injective: otherwise a nonzero difference of
frame coordinates maps to zero, contradicting S(0)=0 and strict negativity.
The proof works on arbitrary modules; no ambient finite-dimensional or
compact-resolvent theorem is being smuggled in.

The real bilinear specialization reuses
`GRWTSK.LinearAlgebra.SchurResidual.block_square_completion` from
`SchurResidualEnclosure.lean`, inspected at math commit
`6cab1fb0725c00a9d06b112ffc215e3523396c50`, blob
`5ca2219b009cf3d0184c050c77ef30f37c68b9e2`.
It assumes symmetry and nonnegativity of the high form a and an exact
linear solution map Y satisfying a(Y(u),v)=b(u,v) for every u,v. The Schur
diagonal is l(u,u)-b(u,Y(u)). The existing module proves the square identity;
this source proves the additional negative-index equality. No inverse
existence or domain-comparison theorem is claimed. This specialization counts
real dimensions; the generic completed-square theorem also permits K=C.

Companion declarations: `hasNegativeFrame_completedSquare_iff`,

The scalar margin follows from

    (1-eta)*[(1-alpha)*x^2-2*beta*x*y+(1-eta)*y^2]
      = [(1-eta)*y-beta*x]^2
        + [(1-alpha)*(1-eta)-beta^2]*x^2.

With eta<1 and alpha+beta^2/(1-eta)<=1 both terms on the right are
nonnegative. The index-zero consumer additionally requires the resulting
quadratic expression to be a true lower bound for every vector of the
specified full source domain. The head, coupling and complementary-space
bounds are not generated by naming them. For lower<=source<=upper, index
order reverses: index(upper)<=index(source)<=index(lower).
