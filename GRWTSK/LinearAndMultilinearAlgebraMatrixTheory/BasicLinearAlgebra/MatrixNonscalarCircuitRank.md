# Nonscalar product and quotient counts

Local attribution: grwtsk contributors.

OpenAI's November 24, 2026 manuscript *The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths* proves the nonscalar bound in the bilinearization lemma, labelled `lem:bilinearize`, and the finite square corollary, labelled `cor:tight`. The exact upstream Lean file `QuotientCircuit.lean` instead charges every product and quotient. The local refinement uses the same rational programs, correctness condition, formal derivative maps and coefficient tensor. Let $K$ be the rational function field in the left and right input entries over $F$. The classification is membership in the image of $F\to K$. A product is charged when both factors are outside that image. A quotient is charged when its divisor is outside that image. Valid quotients have a nonzero divisor in $K$. The derivative argument uses vanishing derivatives of field constants in one direction. In positive characteristic, nonconstant $p$th powers can also have zero derivatives and still meet the stated nonscalar classification.

For a register $p$, write $D_{xy}p$ for its mixed derivative and $D_L p,D_R p$ for its first derivative vectors. Induct over the program with one common list of rank-one matrices. Every mixed register derivative lies in their $K$-linear span. Constants and input registers have mixed derivative zero. Additions and subtractions take linear combinations of the old span. A multiplication by a scalar $c$ scales the old mixed derivative by $c$. For a nonscalar product $pq$, differentiating the product adds at most $D_Lp\otimes D_Rq$ and $D_Lq\otimes D_Rp$ to the common list.

For a quotient $h=p/q$, differentiate $hq=p$. This gives

$$D_{xy}h=q^{-1}\left(D_{xy}p-hD_{xy}q-(D_Lh\otimes D_Rq)_{xy}-(D_Lq\otimes D_Rh)_{xy}\right).$$

A scalar nonzero $q$ removes both first-derivative products and its mixed derivative, so no new term is charged. A nonscalar $q$ adds the two displayed matrices. The old register expansions retain both new coefficients at zero. Therefore $t$ charged gates supply at most $2t$ rank-one matrices for the mixed derivatives of every output simultaneously.

Formal correctness identifies the output mixed derivatives with the matrix multiplication coefficient tensor, using cyclic output indices $(k,i)$ and the actual output address $(i,k)$. The coefficients of the common list indexed by $(k,i)$ give its third factors. The resulting tensor has rank at most $2t$ over $K$. Specialization over the algebraic closure of $F$ preserves a decomposition of that length, as in the pinned `CircuitRankSpecialization.lean`. Combining that comparison with the exact square exponent and its exact square tensor rank inequality gives $n^{9/4}\le 2t$ for $n\ge2$. Taking the natural ceiling gives $\lceil n^{9/4}/2\rceil\le t$. The exponent step retains its separate verification scope.
